## Online News Act {#online-news-act .Title-of-Act}

[S.C.]{.abbr title="Statutes of Canada"} 2023, c. 23
:::

Assented to 2023-06-22

An Act respecting online communications platforms that make news content
available to persons in Canada
::::

:::: section
::: Enacts
His Majesty, by and with the advice and consent of the Senate and House
of Commons of Canada, enacts as follows:
:::

## [Short Title]{.HTitleText1} {#h-1412660 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Short title

**[[1]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-1 .sectionLabel}** This Act may be cited as
the [Online News Act](/eng/acts/O-9.3).

## [Interpretation]{.HTitleText1} {#h-1412663 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Definitions

- **[[2]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-2 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  following definitions apply in this Act.

  [[Commission]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[Commission]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means the Canadian
      Radio-television and Telecommunications
      Commission. ([Conseil]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

  [[covered agreement]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[covered agreement]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means, as applicable,

      - [(a)]{.lawlabel} an agreement that is entered into as a result
        of bargaining sessions referred to in paragraph 19(1)(a) or
        mediation sessions referred to in paragraph 19(1)(b); or

      - [(b)]{.lawlabel} an arbitration panel's decision that is deemed
        to be an agreement under section 42. ([accord
        assujetti]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

  [[digital news intermediary]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[digital news intermediary]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means an online
      communications platform, including a search engine or social media
      service, that is subject to the legislative authority of
      Parliament and that makes news content produced by news outlets
      available to persons in Canada. It does not include an online
      communications platform that is a messaging service the primary
      purpose of which is to allow persons to communicate with each
      other privately. ([intermédiaire de nouvelles
      numériques]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

  [[eligible]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[eligible]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} in relation to a news business,
      means that the business is designated under subsection
      27(1). ([admissible]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

  [[entity]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[entity]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} includes a corporation or a trust,
      partnership, fund, joint venture or any other unincorporated
      association or organization. ([entité]{.DefinedTermLink
      lang="fr"})

  [[Indigenous news outlet]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[Indigenous news outlet]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means an
      undertaking or any distinct part of an undertaking whose primary
      purpose is to produce news content and that

      - [(a)]{.lawlabel} is operated by an individual who belongs to an
        Indigenous group, community or people; and

      - [(b)]{.lawlabel} produces news content primarily for Indigenous
        peoples. ([média d\'information autochtone]{.DefinedTermLink
        lang="fr"})

  [[Indigenous peoples]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[Indigenous peoples]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} has the meaning
      assigned by the definition [[aboriginal peoples of
      Canada]{.dfn}]{.DefinitionRef} in subsection 35(2) of the
      [Constitution Act, 1982](/eng/Const/). ([peuples
      autochtones]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

  [[Minister]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[Minister]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means the Minister of Canadian
      Heritage or, if another federal minister is designated under
      section 5, that minister. ([ministre]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

  [[news business]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[news business]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means an individual or
      entity that operates a news outlet in Canada. ([entreprise de
      nouvelles]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

  [[news content]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[news content]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means content --- in any
      format, including an audio or audiovisual format --- that reports
      on, investigates or explains current issues or events of public
      interest and includes such content that an Indigenous news outlet
      makes available by means of Indigenous storytelling. ([contenu de
      nouvelles]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

  [[news outlet]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[news outlet]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means an undertaking or any
      distinct part of an undertaking whose primary purpose is to
      produce news content and includes an Indigenous news outlet or an
      official language minority community news outlet. ([média
      d'information]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

  [[official language minority community]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[official language minority community]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means
      English-speaking communities in Quebec and French-speaking
      communities outside Quebec. ([communauté de langue officielle en
      situation minoritaire]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

  [[official language minority community news outlet]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[official language minority community news
      outlet]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means an undertaking or any distinct
      part of an undertaking whose primary purpose is to produce news
      content and that produces news content primarily for an official
      language minority community. ([média d'information de communauté
      de langue officielle en situation minoritaire]{.DefinedTermLink
      lang="fr"})

  [[operator]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[operator]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means an individual or entity
      that, through any means, operates a digital news
      intermediary. ([exploitant]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Making available of news content

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} For the purposes of this Act, news content is made
  available if

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the news content, or any portion of it, is
    reproduced; or

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} access to the news content, or any portion of it,
    is facilitated by any means, including an index, aggregation or
    ranking of news content.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Freedom of expression

- **[[3]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-3 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} For
  greater certainty, this Act is to be interpreted and applied in a
  manner that is consistent with freedom of expression.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Journalistic independence

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} This Act is to be interpreted and applied in a manner
  that supports the journalistic independence enjoyed by news outlets in
  relation to news content produced primarily for the Canadian news
  marketplace, including local, regional and national news content.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Treatment of news content

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} This Act is to be interpreted and applied in a manner
  that is aimed at ensuring news content is made available by digital
  news intermediaries without undue manipulation or interference.

## [Purpose]{.HTitleText1} {#h-1412696 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Purpose

**[[4]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-4 .sectionLabel}** The purpose of this Act is
to regulate digital news intermediaries with a view to enhancing
fairness in the Canadian digital news marketplace and contributing to
its sustainability, including the sustainability of news businesses in
Canada, in both the non-profit and for-profits sectors, including
independent local ones.

## [Designation of Minister]{.HTitleText1} {#h-1412699 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Designation

**[[5]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-5 .sectionLabel}** The Governor in Council
may, by order, designate any federal minister to be the Minister
referred to in this Act.

## [Application]{.HTitleText1} {#h-1412702 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Application

**[[6]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-6 .sectionLabel}** This Act applies in respect
of a digital news intermediary if, having regard to the following
factors, there is a significant bargaining power imbalance between its
operator and news businesses:

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} the size of the intermediary or the operator;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} whether the market for the intermediary gives the
  operator a strategic advantage over news businesses; and

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} whether the intermediary occupies a prominent market
  position.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Duty to notify

- **[[7]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-7 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} If this
  Act applies in respect of a digital news intermediary, its operator
  must so notify the Commission.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Information required

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} An individual or entity that operates an online
  communications platform must, at the request of the Commission and
  within the time and in the manner that it specifies, provide the
  Commission with any information that it requires for the purpose of
  verifying compliance with subsection (1) or preventing non-compliance
  with it.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}List of digital news intermediaries

- **[[8]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-8 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  Commission must maintain a list of digital news intermediaries in
  respect of which this Act applies. The list must set out each
  intermediary's operator and contact information for that operator and
  specify whether an order made under subsection 11(1) or 12(1) applies
  in relation to the intermediary.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}[Statutory Instruments
  Act](/eng/acts/S-22)

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The [Statutory Instruments Act](/eng/acts/S-22) does
  not apply in respect of the list maintained under subsection (1).

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Publication

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} The Commission must publish the list on its website.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Broadcasting

**[[9]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-9 .sectionLabel}** This Act does not apply in
respect of a digital news intermediary that is a [[broadcasting
undertaking]{.dfn}]{.DefinitionRef} in respect of its
[[broadcasting]{.dfn}]{.DefinitionRef}, as those terms are defined in
subsection 2(1) of the [Broadcasting Act](/eng/acts/B-9.01).

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Telecommunications service providers

**[[10]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-10 .sectionLabel}** For greater certainty,
this Act does not apply to a [[telecommunications service
provider]{.dfn}]{.DefinitionRef}, as defined in subsection 2(1) of the
[Telecommunications Act](/eng/acts/T-3.4), when it is acting solely in
that capacity.

## [Exemptions]{.HTitleText1} {#h-1412724 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Exemption order

- **[[11]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-11 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  Commission must make an exemption order in relation to a digital news
  intermediary if its operator requests the exemption and the following
  conditions are met:

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the operator has entered into agreements with news
    businesses that operate news outlets that produce news content
    primarily for the Canadian news marketplace and the Commission is of
    the opinion that, taken as a whole, the agreements satisfy the
    following criteria:

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} they provide for fair compensation to the news
      businesses for the news content that is made available by the
      intermediary,

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} they ensure that an appropriate portion of the
      compensation will be used by the news businesses to support the
      production of local, regional and national news content,

    - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} they do not allow corporate influence to
      undermine the freedom of expression and journalistic independence
      enjoyed by news outlets,

    - [(iv)]{.lawlabel} they contribute to the sustainability of the
      Canadian news marketplace,

    - [(v)]{.lawlabel} they ensure a significant portion of independent
      local news businesses benefit from them, they contribute to the
      sustainability of those businesses and they encourage innovative
      business models in the Canadian news marketplace,

    - [(vi)]{.lawlabel} they involve a range of news outlets in both the
      non-profit and for-profit sectors and they were entered into with
      news businesses that reflect a diversity of business models that
      provide services to all markets and diverse populations, including
      local and regional markets in every province and territory,
      anglophone and francophone communities, and Black and other
      racialized communities,

    - [(vii)]{.lawlabel} they ensure a significant portion of Indigenous
      news outlets benefit from them and they contribute to the
      sustainability of those outlets in a way that supports the
      provision of news content by and for Indigenous peoples, and

    - [(viii)]{.lawlabel} they ensure a significant portion of official
      language minority community news outlets benefit from them and
      they contribute to the sustainability of those outlets in a way
      that supports the provision of news content by and for official
      language minority communities;

  - [(a.1)]{.lawlabel} the Commission has held public consultations in
    accordance with any conditions that its Chairperson may specify; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} any condition set out in regulations made by the
    Governor in Council.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Effect of order

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The order exempts the operator, in relation to the
  intermediary, from the application of

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} section 21 and any provision of any regulations
    made under section 85 that is in relation to section 21; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} any other provision of this Act and any provision
    of any regulations made under subsection 81(1) or section 85 that is
    specified by the Commission, in its discretion, in the order.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Conditions

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} The order may contain any conditions the Commission
  considers appropriate.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Approval of Treasury Board

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} The order is subject to the approval of the Treasury
  Board if the order exempts the operator from the application of
  section 82 or any provision of any regulations made under subsection
  81(1).

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Duration of order

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} The order remains in effect for a period of not more
  than five years and, subject to this section, may be renewed.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Interim order

- **[[12]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-12 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  Commission may, in relation to a digital news intermediary, make an
  interim order that has the same effect as an exemption order if the
  following conditions are met:

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} its operator has requested an exemption order in
    relation to the intermediary;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the operator has entered into agreements with news
    businesses that operate news outlets that produce news content
    primarily for the Canadian news marketplace;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} the Commission is unable to make the exemption
    order because it is of the opinion that, taken as a whole, the
    agreements do not satisfy the criteria set out in subparagraphs
    11(1)(a)(i) to (viii); and

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} the Commission is of the opinion that it will be
    able to change its opinion because the operator is, in good faith,
    taking measures that will permit the criteria to be satisfied within
    a reasonable period, which period must not be longer than one year.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Conditions

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The interim order must contain conditions respecting
  the measures being taken by the operator and may contain any other
  condition the Commission considers appropriate, including a condition
  requiring that public consultations be held at a time and place in
  Canada to be fixed by the Commission.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Approval of Treasury Board

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} The interim order is subject to the approval of the
  Treasury Board if the order exempts the operator from the application
  of a provision referred to in sub­section 11(4).

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Cessation of effect

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} The interim order must specify the period referred to
  in paragraph (1)(d) and ceases to have effect at the end of that
  period.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}For greater certainty

**[[13]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-13 .sectionLabel}** For greater certainty,
agreements referred to in paragraph 11(1)(a) or 12(1)(b) include
agreements that were entered into before the day on which this section
comes into force.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Review

- **[[14]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-14 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} For
  greater certainty, the Commission may review an exemption order or an
  interim order.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Repeal of exemption order

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The Commission may repeal an exemption order if

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the Commission is of the opinion that the operator
    of the digital news intermediary in question is acting in a manner
    that is inconsistent with this Act;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} a condition referred to in subsection 11(1) is no
    longer met; or

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} a condition contained in the exemption order is not
    met.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Repeal of interim order

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} The Commission may repeal an interim order if

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the Commission is of the opinion that the operator
    of the digital news intermediary in question is acting in a manner
    that is inconsistent with this Act;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} a condition referred to in paragraph 12(1)(c)
    or (d) is no longer met; or

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} a condition contained in the interim order is not
    met.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Reasons

**[[15]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-15 .sectionLabel}** The Commission must
publish on its website reasons for

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} making or not making a requested exemption order;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} deciding to make or not to make an interim order; and

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} deciding to repeal an exemption order or interim
  order.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}[Statutory Instruments
Act](/eng/acts/S-22)

**[[16]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-16 .sectionLabel}** The [Statutory
Instruments Act](/eng/acts/S-22) does not apply in respect of an
exemption order or an interim order.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Publication of orders

**[[17]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-17 .sectionLabel}** The Commission must
publish on its website each exemption order and interim order that it
makes.

## [Bargaining Process]{.HTitleText1} {#h-1412786 .Part}

### [Overview]{.HTitleText2} {#h-1412787 .Subheading}

Definition of [[party]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

**[[18]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-18 .sectionLabel}** In sections 19 to 44,
[[party]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means, as applicable, an operator, an
eligible news business or a group of eligible news businesses.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Steps in bargaining process

- **[[19]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-19 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  bargaining process consists of

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} negotiation or bargaining sessions over a period of
    90 days;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} if the parties are unable, within the negotiation
    or bargaining period, to reach an agreement, mediation sessions over
    a period of 120 days, beginning on the day after the end of the
    negotiation or bargaining period; and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} if the parties are unable, within the mediation
    period, to reach an agreement and at least one of the parties wishes
    to initiate arbitration, final offer arbitration for a period of 45
    days, beginning on the day after the end of the mediation period.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Extension

  [(1.1)]{.lawlabel} On request of both parties, the Commission may
  extend a period provided for in any of paragraphs (1)(a) to (c).

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Scope of bargaining process

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The bargaining process is limited to matters related
  to the making available, by the digital news intermediary in question,
  of news content produced by a news outlet that is identified under
  section 30 as a subject of the bargaining process and, if an
  application is made under subsection 31(1), determined by the
  Commission to be a subject of the bargaining process.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Scope of final offer arbitration

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} Any final offer arbitration under the bargaining
  process is limited to monetary disputes.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Initiation of bargaining process

**[[20]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-20 .sectionLabel}** Only an eligible news
business that is listed under subsection 29(1) or a group of eligible
news businesses that are listed under that section may initiate the
bargaining process with an operator.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Duty to bargain

**[[21]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-21 .sectionLabel}** An operator must
participate in the bargaining process with the eligible news business or
group of eligible news businesses that initiated it.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Good faith

**[[22]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-22 .sectionLabel}** Parties that are
participating in the bargaining process must do so in good faith.

### [Copyright]{.HTitleText2} {#h-1412808 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Initiation of bargaining process

**[[23]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-23 .sectionLabel}** For greater certainty, an
eligible news business or a group of eligible news businesses may
initiate the bargaining process in relation to news content in which
copyright subsists only if

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} the business or a member of the group owns the
  copyright or is otherwise authorized to bargain in relation to the
  content; or

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} the group is authorized to bargain in relation to the
  content.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Limitations and exceptions

**[[24]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-24 .sectionLabel}** For greater certainty,
limitations and exceptions to copyright under the [Copyright
Act](/eng/acts/C-42) do not limit the scope of the bargaining process.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Mediation and final offer arbitration

**[[25]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-25 .sectionLabel}** For greater certainty,
the use of news content is not to be the subject of mediation sessions
or final offer arbitration during the bargaining process if the operator
in question

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} has made payments to the eligible news business in
  question for the use of that content in accordance with a licence or
  agreement between the operator and the business; or

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} has made payments or has offered to make payments to
  the business in question for the use of that content in accordance
  with the relevant tariff approved by the Copyright Board for the use
  of that content.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Liability of operators

- **[[26]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-26 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} If
  news content is made available by a digital news intermediary and its
  operator is a party to a covered agreement in relation to the making
  available of the news content by the intermediary, the operator is not
  liable under the [Copyright Act](/eng/acts/C-42) for an infringement
  of copyright in relation to activities that are subjects of that
  agreement.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}For greater certainty

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} For greater certainty, nothing in this Act limits the
  liability of an eligible news business under the [Copyright
  Act](/eng/acts/C-42) for an infringement of copyright.

### [Eligibility]{.HTitleText2} {#h-1412824 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Eligible news businesses --- designation

- **[[27]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-27 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} At the
  request of a news business, the Commission must, by order, designate
  the business as eligible if it

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} is a [[qualified Canadian journalism
    organization]{.dfn}]{.DefinitionRef} as defined in subsection 248(1)
    of the [Income Tax Act](/eng/acts/I-3.3), or is licensed by the
    Commission under paragraph 9(1)(b) of the [Broadcasting
    Act](/eng/acts/B-9.01) as a [[campus
    station]{.dfn}]{.DefinitionRef}, [[community
    station]{.dfn}]{.DefinitionRef} or [[native
    station]{.dfn}]{.DefinitionRef} as those terms are defined in
    regulations made under that Act or other categories of licensees
    established by the Commission with a similar community mandate;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} produces news content of public interest that is
    primarily focused on matters of general interest and reports of
    current events, including coverage of democratic institutions and
    processes, and

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} regularly employs two or more journalists in
      Canada, which journalists may include journalists who own or are a
      partner in the news business and journalists who do not deal at
      arm's length with the business,

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} operates in Canada, including having content
      edited and designed in Canada,

    - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} produces news content that is not primarily
      focused on a particular topic such as industry-specific news,
      sports, recreation, arts, lifestyle or entertainment, and

    - [(iv)]{.lawlabel} is either a member of a recognized journalistic
      association and follows the code of ethics of a recognized
      journalistic association or has its own code of ethics whose
      standards of professional conduct require adherence to the
      recognized processes and principles of the journalism profession,
      including fairness, independence and rigour in reporting news and
      handling sources; or

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} operates an Indigenous news outlet in Canada and
    produces news content that includes matters of general interest,
    including coverage of matters relating to the rights of Indigenous
    peoples, including the right of self-government and treaty rights.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Revoked designation

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} Despite subsection (1), a news business must not be
  designated as eligible if it was previously designated and had its
  designation revoked under paragraph 59(1)(c).

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Ineligible news businesses

  [(3.1)]{.lawlabel} Despite subsection (1), a news business must not be
  designated as eligible if

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the news business is the subject of sanctions under
    the [United Nations Act](/eng/acts/U-2), the [Special Economic
    Measures Act](/eng/acts/S-14.5) or the [Justice for Victims of
    Corrupt Foreign Officials Act (Sergei Magnitsky
    Law)](/eng/acts/J-2.3), or is owned or controlled by an individual
    or entity that is the subject of such sanctions; or

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the news business has its headquarters in a
    [[foreign state]{.dfn}]{.DefinitionRef}, as defined in section 2 of
    the [Special Economic Measures Act](/eng/acts/S-14.5), that is the
    subject of measures under an Act referred to in paragraph (a).

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Revoked designation

  [(3.2)]{.lawlabel} If a news business described in paragraph (3.1)(a)
  or (b) was previously designated as eligible, the Commission must, by
  order, revoke the order designating the business as eligible.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}[Statutory Instruments
  Act](/eng/acts/S-22)

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} The [Statutory Instruments Act](/eng/acts/S-22) does
  not apply in respect of an order made under subsection (1).

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Provincial public broadcasters

**[[28]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-28 .sectionLabel}** The designation of a
provincial public broadcaster as an eligible news business is subject to
any other conditions specified in regulations made by the Governor in
Council.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Public list

- **[[29]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-29 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  Commission must maintain a list of eligible news businesses and
  publish that list on its website. An eligible news business is only
  included on the list if it gives its consent.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}[Statutory Instruments
  Act](/eng/acts/S-22)

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The [Statutory Instruments Act](/eng/acts/S-22) does
  not apply in respect of the list maintained under subsection (1).

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Identification of news outlets

**[[30]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-30 .sectionLabel}** When initiating the
bargaining process, an eligible news business or group of eligible news
businesses must notify the operator of the digital news intermediary in
question of the news outlets that are to be the subjects of the
bargaining process.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Application to Commission

- **[[31]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-31 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} If the
  operator is of the opinion that a news outlet identified under section
  30 by an eligible news business or group of eligible news businesses
  should not be a subject of the bargaining process, it may apply to the
  Commission for a determination of the issue.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Determination

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} A news outlet is to be a subject of the bargaining
  process if the Commission is of the opinion that the outlet is
  operated exclusively for the purpose of producing news content ---
  including local, regional and national news content --- consisting
  primarily of original news content that is

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} produced primarily for the Canadian news
    marketplace;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} focused on matters of general interest and reports
    of current events, including coverage of democratic institutions and
    processes;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} not focused on a particular topic such as
    industry-specific news, sports, recreation, arts, lifestyle or
    entertainment; and

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} not intended to promote the interests, or report on
    the activities, of an organization, an association or its members.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Special case --- Indigenous news outlet

  [(2.1)]{.lawlabel} Despite subsection (2), an Indigenous news outlet
  is to be a subject of the bargaining process if it

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} operates in Canada; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} produces news content that includes matters of
    general interest, including coverage of matters relating to the
    rights of Indigenous peoples, including the right of
    self-government.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Summary dismissal

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} If the Commission is of the opinion that an
  application under subsection (1) is frivolous, vexatious or not made
  in good faith, it may dismiss the application summarily and the news
  outlet that is otherwise the subject of the application is a subject
  of the bargaining process.

### [Final Offer Arbitration]{.HTitleText2} {#h-1412875 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Roster of qualified arbitrators

- **[[33]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-33 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  Commission must publish qualifications for arbitrators on its website
  and must maintain a roster of arbitrators who meet those
  qualifications.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Indigenous persons on roster

  [(1.1)]{.lawlabel} The Commission must ensure that the roster includes
  Indigenous persons.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Proposals

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Parties that are engaging in bargaining or mediation
  sessions may propose candidates for the roster.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Arbitration panel

- **[[34]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-34 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A
  final offer arbitration must be conducted by a panel that is composed
  of three arbitrators who

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} are selected by the parties from the roster; or

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} are appointed by the Commission from the roster, if
    the parties do not select the arbitrators within a period that the
    Commission considers reasonable.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Appointment by Commission

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The Commission must take the preferences of the
  parties into account if it appoints the arbitrators to the panel.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Status

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} An arbitration panel is not a federal board,
  commission or other tribunal for the purposes of the [Federal Courts
  Act](/eng/acts/F-7).

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Conflicts of interest

- **[[35]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-35 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} If the
  Commission is of the opinion that an arbitrator selected by the
  parties has a conflict of interest, that arbitrator is ineligible to
  be a panel member and a replacement must be

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} selected by the parties from the roster; or

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} appointed by the Commission from the roster, if the
    parties do not select the replacement within a period that the
    Commission considers reasonable.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Appointment by Commission

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The Commission must not appoint an arbitrator who has
  a conflict of interest.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Commission assistance

- **[[36]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-36 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  Commission may, at the request of an arbitration panel, provide
  administrative and technical assistance to the panel and may, on any
  terms that the Commission considers necessary, disclose to the panel
  any information, including confidential information, in the
  Commission's possession that, in the Commission's opinion, is
  necessary for a balanced and informed decision-making process, on the
  condition that the Commission ensures that the arbitration panel or
  each individual arbitrator that presides over the final offer
  arbitration does not further disclose any confidential information
  other than during the arbitration, including by imposing any further
  terms that the Commission considers necessary.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Confidentiality

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Each individual arbitrator must take all reasonably
  necessary measures to ensure that confidential information disclosed
  to them under subsection (1) is not disclosed other than during the
  arbitration.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Offence --- confidentiality

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} Every individual who contravenes subsection (2) is
  guilty of an offence and is liable on summary conviction,

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} for a first offence, to a fine of not more than
    \$5,000; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} for a second or subsequent offence, to a fine of
    not more than \$10,000.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Decision of arbitration panel

**[[37]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-37 .sectionLabel}** The arbitration panel
makes its decision by selecting the final offer made by one of the
parties.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Factors

**[[38]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-38 .sectionLabel}** An arbitration panel must
take the following factors into account in making its decision:

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} the value added, monetary and otherwise, to the news
  content in question by each party, as assessed in terms of their
  investments, expenditures and other actions in relation to that
  content;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} the benefits, monetary and otherwise, that each party
  receives from the content being made available by the digital news
  intermediary in question; and

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} the bargaining power imbalance between the news
  business and the operator of the digital news intermediary in
  question.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Dismissal of offers

- **[[39]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-39 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} An
  arbitration panel must dismiss any offer that, in its opinion,

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} allows a party to exercise undue influence over the
    amount of compensation to be paid or received;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} is not in the public interest because the offer
    would be highly likely to result in serious detriment to the
    provision of news content to persons in Canada; or

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} is inconsistent with the purposes of enhancing
    fairness in the Canadian digital news marketplace and contributing
    to its sustainability.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Effect of dismissal

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} If the arbitration panel dismisses, in accordance
  with subsection (1), the final offer made by one of the parties, it
  must accept the final offer made by the other party.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Reasons and new offers

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} If the arbitration panel dismisses, in accordance
  with subsection (1), the final offer made by each of the parties, it
  must provide written reasons to the parties and give them an
  opportunity to make a new offer.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Other submissions

**[[40]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-40 .sectionLabel}** An arbitration panel may,
in making its decision, seek oral or written submissions from the
Commission and from the Commissioner of Competition appointed under
subsection 7(1) of the [Competition Act](/eng/acts/C-34).

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Decision final

**[[41]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-41 .sectionLabel}** An arbitration panel's
decision is final.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Decision deemed to be agreement

**[[42]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-42 .sectionLabel}** An arbitration panel's
decision is deemed, for the purposes of its enforceability, to be an
agreement entered into by the parties.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Reasons

**[[43]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-43 .sectionLabel}** An arbitration panel must
provide written reasons for its decision to the parties and the
Commission.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Costs

**[[44]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-44 .sectionLabel}** The arbitration panel may
apportion the costs related to final offer arbitration between the
parties, if the parties cannot agree, within a period that the panel
considers reasonable, on how to share the costs. In doing so, the panel
must take into account each party's ability to pay, their conduct during
the arbitration and any other factor that it considers appropriate.

## [Civil Remedies]{.HTitleText1} {#h-1412935 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Right of recovery

**[[45]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-45 .sectionLabel}** For greater certainty, an
eligible news business or group of eligible news businesses may, during
the period specified in a covered agreement, collect payments due under
it and, if they are not made, recover the payments in a court of
competent jurisdiction.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Compliance order

**[[46]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-46 .sectionLabel}** For greater certainty, if
a provision of a covered agreement is not complied with, a party to the
agreement may, in addition to any other remedy available, apply to a
court of competent jurisdiction for an order directing compliance with
the provision.

## [Competition Act]{.HTitleText1} {#h-1412940 .Part}

  businesses;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} any provision of the covered agreement that is
  related to that activity; or

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} any bargaining or mediation session or any final
  offer arbitration under the bargaining process set out in sections 18
  to 44 to which an operator and a group of eligible news businesses are
  parties.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Other agreements

- **[[48]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-48
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Sections 45 and 90.1 of the
  [Competition Act](/eng/acts/C-34) also do not apply in respect of

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} any bargaining activity between an operator and a
    group of eligible news businesses that is conducted with a view to
    entering into an agreement;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} any activity, including the making of payments or
    the exchange of information, that is carried out in accordance with
    an agreement; or

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} any provision of an agreement that is related to an
    activity referred to in paragraph (b).

- Definition of [[agreement]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} In this section, [[agreement]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}
  means an agreement that

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} is not a covered agreement;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} is entered into by an operator and a group of
    eligible news businesses the members of which operate news outlets
    that produce news content primarily for the Canadian news
    marketplace; and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} is in relation to the making available of that
    content by a digital news intermediary operated by the operator.

## [Code of Conduct]{.HTitleText1} {#h-1412957 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Establishment of code

- **[[49]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-49 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  Commission must, by regulation, establish a code of conduct respecting
  bargaining in relation to news content --- including any bargaining
  and mediation sessions during the bargaining process set out in
  sections 18 to 44 --- between

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} operators of digital news intermediaries that make
    available news content that is produced primarily for the Canadian
    news marketplace by news outlets; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} eligible news businesses or groups of eligible news
    businesses.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Purpose of code

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The purpose of the code is to support fairness and
  transparency in bargaining in relation to news content.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Mandatory contents

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} The code of conduct must contain provisions

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} respecting the requirement to bargain in good faith
    that is set out in section 22;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} requiring parties to bargain in good faith even if
    they are bargaining outside of the bargaining process set out in
    sections 18 to 44;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} respecting the requirement to bargain in good faith
    that is referred to in paragraph (b); and

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} respecting the information that the parties require
    to make informed business decisions.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Discretionary contents

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} The code of conduct may, among other things,

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} prohibit the use of specified provisions in
    agreements, including agreements that are entered into as a result
    of bargaining or mediation sessions under the bargaining process set
    out in sections 18 to 44; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} set out examples of unfair behaviour that could
    arise during bargaining.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Compliance order

- **[[50]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-50 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} If an
  operator, eligible news business or group of eligible news businesses
  fails to comply with the code of conduct, the Commission may, by
  order, require the operator, business or group to take any measure the
  Commission considers necessary to remedy the non-compliance.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}[Statutory Instruments
  Act](/eng/acts/S-22)

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The [Statutory Instruments Act](/eng/acts/S-22) does
  not apply in respect of an order made under subsection (1).

## [Discrimination, Preference and Disadvantage]{.HTitleText1} {#h-1412980 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Prohibition

**[[51]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-51 .sectionLabel}** In the course of making
available news content that is produced primarily for the Canadian news
marketplace by news outlets operated by eligible news businesses, the
operator of a digital news intermediary must not act in any way that

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} unjustly discriminates against an eligible news
  business;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} gives undue or unreasonable preference to any
  individual or entity, including itself; or

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} subjects an eligible news business to an undue or
  unreasonable disadvantage.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Complaint

- **[[52]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-52 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} An
  eligible news business or group of eligible news businesses may make a
  complaint to the Commission if the business or group has reasonable
  grounds to believe that an operator has, in relation to the business
  or a member of the group, contravened section 51.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Factors to take into account

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} In determining whether an operator has contravened
  section 51, the Commission may take into account any factor it
  considers appropriate, but it must take into account whether the
  conduct in question is

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} in the normal course of business for the operator;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} retaliatory in nature; or

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} consistent with the purposes of this Act.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Dismissal of complaint

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} If the Commission is of the opinion that a complaint
  under subsection (1) is frivolous, vexatious or not made in good
  faith, it may dismiss the complaint summarily.

### [News Businesses]{.HTitleText2} {#h-1413072 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Contravention --- eligible news business

### [Administrative Monetary Penalties]{.HTitleText2} {#h-1413085 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Violation --- operators, directors, etc.

- **[[60]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-60
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Subject to any regulations made
  under paragraph 76(a), an operator or a director, officer, employee or
  agent or mandatary of an operator commits a violation if they

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} contravene a provision of this Act, a provision of
    the regulations, an order made under this Act or an undertaking that
    they entered into under section 65; or

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} make a misrepresentation of a material fact or an
    intentional omission to state a material fact to a person designated
    under section 57 or paragraph 63(a).

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Violation --- other individuals and
  entities

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} An individual or entity commits a violation if they
  contravene subsection 7(2).

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Continued violation

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} A violation that is continued on more than one day
  constitutes a separate violation in respect of each day on which it is
  continued.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Maximum amount of penalty

- **[[61]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-61
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Subject to any regulations made
  under paragraph 76(b), an individual or entity that commits a
  violation is liable to an administrative monetary penalty

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} in the case of an individual, of not more than
    \$25,000 for a first violation and of not more than \$50,000 for
    each subsequent violation; or

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} in the case of an entity, of not more than \$10
    million for a first violation and of not more than \$15 million for
    each subsequent violation.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Criteria for penalty

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The amount of the penalty is to be determined by
  taking into account

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the nature and scope of the violation;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the history of compliance with this Act, the
    regulations and orders made under this Act by the individual or
    entity that committed the violation;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} the history of the individual or entity with
    respect to any previous undertaking entered into under section 65;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} any benefit that the individual or entity obtained
    from the commission of the violation;

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} the ability of the individual or entity to pay the
    penalty;

  - [(f)]{.lawlabel} any factors established by regulations made under
    paragraph 76(c);

  - [(g)]{.lawlabel} the purpose of the penalty; and

  - [(h)]{.lawlabel} any other relevant factor.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Purpose of penalty

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} The purpose of the penalty is to promote compliance
  with this Act and not to punish.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Procedures

- **[[62]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-62
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Despite subsection 64(1), the
  Commission may impose a penalty in a decision made in the course of a
  proceeding before it under this Act, including a proceeding in respect
  of a complaint made under section 52, in which it finds that a
  violation referred to in section 60 has been committed by an
  individual or entity other than the individual or entity that entered
  into an undertaking under section 65 in connection with the same act
  or omission giving rise to the violation.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}For greater certainty

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} For greater certainty, the Commission is not to
  impose a penalty under subsection (1) on an individual or entity that
  has not been given the opportunity to be heard.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Designation

**[[63]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-63 .sectionLabel}** The Commission may

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} designate persons or classes of persons who are
  authorized to issue notices of violation or to accept an undertaking
  under section 65; and

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} establish, in respect of each violation, a short-form
  description to be used in notices of violation.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Notice of violation

- **[[64]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-64 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A
  person who is authorized to issue notices of violation may, if they
  believe on reasonable grounds that an individual or entity has
  committed a violation, other than a violation in respect of a
  contravention of section 51, issue a notice of violation and cause it
  to be served on that individual or entity.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Contents

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The notice of violation must set out

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the name of the individual or entity that is
    believed to have committed the violation;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the act or omission giving rise to the violation,
    as well as a reference to the provision that is at issue;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} the penalty that the individual or entity is liable
    to pay, as well as the time and manner in which the individual or
    entity may pay the penalty;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} a statement informing the individual or entity that
    they may pay the penalty or make representations to the Commission
    with respect to the violation and the penalty and informing them of
    the time and manner for making representations; and

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} a statement informing the individual or entity
    that, if they do not pay the penalty or make representations in
    accordance with the notice, they will be deemed to have committed
    the violation and the penalty may be imposed.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Undertaking

- **[[65]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-65 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} An
  individual or entity may enter into an undertaking at any time. The
  undertaking is valid on its acceptance by the Commission or the person
  designated to accept an undertaking.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Requirements

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} An undertaking

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} must set out every act or omission that is covered
    by the undertaking;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} must set out every provision that is at issue;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} may contain any conditions that the Commission or
    the person designated to accept the undertaking considers
    appropriate; and

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} may include a requirement to pay a specified
    amount.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}No service of notice of violation

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} If an individual or entity enters into an
  undertaking, a notice of violation must not be served on them in
  connection with any act or omission referred to in the undertaking.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Undertaking after service of notice of
  violation

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} If an individual or entity enters into an undertaking
  after a notice of violation is served on them, the proceeding that is
  commenced by the notice is ended in respect of that individual or
  entity in connection with any act or omission referred to in the
  undertaking.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Payment of penalty

- **[[66]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-66 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} If an
  individual or entity that is served with a notice of violation pays
  the penalty set out in the notice, they are deemed to have committed
  the violation and the proceedings in respect of it are ended.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Representations to Commission and
  decision

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} If an individual or entity that is served with a
  notice of violation makes representations in accordance with the
  notice, the Commission must decide, on a balance of probabilities,
  after considering any other representations that it considers
  appropriate, whether the individual or entity committed the violation.
  If the Commission decides that the individual or entity committed the
  violation, it may

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} impose the penalty set out in the notice, a lesser
    penalty or no penalty; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} suspend payment of the penalty subject to any
    conditions that the Commission considers necessary to ensure
    compliance with this Act.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Penalty

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} If an individual or entity that is served with a
  notice of violation neither pays the penalty nor makes representations
  in accordance with the notice, the individual or entity is deemed to
  have committed the violation and the Commission may impose the
  penalty.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Copy of decision

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} The Commission must cause a copy of any decision made
  under subsection (2) or (3) to be issued and served on the individual
  or entity.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Evidence

**[[67]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-67 .sectionLabel}** In a proceeding in
respect of a violation, a notice purporting to be served under
subsection 64(1) or a copy of a decision purporting to be served under
subsection 66(4) is admissible in evidence without proof of the
signature or official character of the person appearing to have signed
it.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Burden of proof

**[[68]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-68 .sectionLabel}** In a proceeding in
respect of a violation in respect of a contravention of section 51, the
burden of establishing that any discrimination is not unjust or that any
preference or disadvantage is not undue or unreasonable is on the
individual or entity that is believed to have contravened that section.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Defence

- **[[69]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-69 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} An
  individual or entity is not to be found liable for a violation, other
  than a violation in respect of a contravention of section 22, if they
  establish that they exercised due diligence to prevent its commission.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Common law principles

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Every rule and principle of the common law that makes
  any circumstance a justification or excuse in relation to a charge for
  an offence applies in respect of a violation to the extent that it is
  not inconsistent with this Act.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Directors, officers, etc.

**[[70]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-70 .sectionLabel}** A director, officer or
agent or mandatary of an entity that commits a violation is liable for
the violation if they directed, authorized, assented to, acquiesced in
or participated in the commission of the violation, whether or not the
entity is proceeded against.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Vicarious liability

**[[71]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-71 .sectionLabel}** An individual or entity
is liable for a violation that is committed by their employee acting
within the scope of their employment or their agent or mandatary acting
within the scope of their authority, whether or not the employee or
agent or mandatary is identified or proceeded against.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Limitation or prescription period

- **[[72]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-72
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Proceedings in respect of a
  violation may be instituted within, but not after, three years after
  the day on which the subject matter of the proceedings became known to
  the Commission.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Certificate

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} A document that appears to have been issued by the
  secretary to the Commission, certifying the day on which the subject
  matter of any proceedings became known to the Commission, is
  admissible in evidence without proof of the signature or official
  character of the person who appears to have signed the document and
  is, in the absence of evidence to the contrary, proof of the matter
  asserted in it.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Information made public

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Debt due to Her Majesty

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Limitation or prescription period

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Certificate of default

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Effect of registration
