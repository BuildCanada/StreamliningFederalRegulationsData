## Impact Assessment Act {#impact-assessment-act .Title-of-Act}

[S.C.]{.abbr title="Statutes of Canada"} 2019, c. 28, s. 1
:::

Assented to 2019-06-21

An Act respecting a federal process for impact assessments and the
prevention or mitigation of significant adverse effects within federal
jurisdiction

::: ReaderNote
\[Enacted by section 1 of chapter 28 of the Statutes of Canada, 2019, in
force August 28, 2019, see SI/2019-86.\]
:::
:::::

::::::::::::::::::::::::::::::::::::::::::::::::::::::::::::::::::::::::::::::::::::::::::::: section
:::: {.section .order}
###### Preamble {#preamble .MarginalNote}

Whereas Parliament is committed to having an impact assessment process
that prevents or mitigates significant adverse effects within federal
jurisdiction;

Whereas the Government of Canada is committed to implementing the United
Nations Declaration on the Rights of Indigenous Peoples;

Whereas the Government of Canada is committed to ensuring respect for
the rights of the Indigenous peoples of Canada recognized and affirmed
by section 35 of the Constitution Act, 1982;

Whereas Parliament recognizes the importance of implementing the impact
assessment process in a manner that

supports coordinated action among jurisdictions that have powers, duties
and functions in relation to the assessment of the effects of designated
projects,

fosters reconciliation and working in partnership with the Indigenous
peoples of Canada,

integrates scientific information and Indigenous knowledge,

fosters meaningful public participation,

is transparent, efficient and timely and contributes to a positive
investment climate in Canada,

encourages the use of innovative approaches and technologies to prevent
or mitigate adverse effects within federal jurisdiction, and

contributes to fostering sustainability and to the Government of
Canada's ability to meet its environmental obligations and its
commitments in respect of climate change;

Whereas Parliament is committed, in order to prevent or mitigate
significant adverse [[environmental effects]{.dfn}]{.DefinitionRef}, as
defined in section 81, to having a process for the assessment of
[[projects]{.dfn}]{.DefinitionRef}, as defined in that section, that are
to be carried out on federal lands, or those that are outside Canada and
that are to be carried out or financially supported by a federal
authority;

And whereas Parliament recognizes the importance of regional assessments
in understanding the effects of existing or future physical activities
and the importance of strategic assessments in assessing federal
policies, plans or programs that are relevant to conducting impact
assessments;

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Definitions

**[[2]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-2 .sectionLabel}** The following definitions
apply in this Act.

[[adverse effects within federal jurisdiction]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[adverse effects within federal
    jurisdiction]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means, with respect to a physical
    activity or a designated project,

    - [(a)]{.lawlabel} a non-negligible adverse change to the following
      components of the environment that are within the legislative
      authority of Parliament:

      - [(i)]{.lawlabel} [[fish]{.dfn}]{.DefinitionRef} and [[fish
        habitat]{.dfn}]{.DefinitionRef}, as defined in subsection 2(1)
        of the [Fisheries Act](/eng/acts/F-14),

      - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} [[aquatic species]{.dfn}]{.DefinitionRef}, as
        defined in subsection 2(1) of the [Species at Risk
        Act](/eng/acts/S-15.3),

      - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} [[migratory birds]{.dfn}]{.DefinitionRef}, as
        defined in subsection 2(1) of the [Migratory Birds Convention
        Act, 1994](/eng/acts/M-7.01), and

      - [(iv)]{.lawlabel} any other component of the environment that is
        set out in Schedule 3;

    - [(b)]{.lawlabel} a non-negligible adverse change to the
      environment that would occur on federal lands;

    - [(c)]{.lawlabel} a non-negligible adverse change to the marine
      environment that is caused by pollution and that would occur
      outside Canada;

    - [(d)]{.lawlabel} a non-negligible adverse change --- that is
      caused by pollution --- to [[boundary
      waters]{.dfn}]{.DefinitionRef} or [[international
      waters]{.dfn}]{.DefinitionRef}, as those terms are defined in
      subsection 2(1) of the [Canada Water Act](/eng/acts/C-11), or to
      interprovincial waters;

    - [(e)]{.lawlabel} with respect to the Indigenous peoples of Canada,
      a non-negligible adverse impact --- occurring in Canada and
      resulting from any change to the environment --- on

      - [(i)]{.lawlabel} physical and cultural heritage,

      - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} the current use of lands and resources for
        traditional purposes, or

      - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} any structure, site or thing that is of
        historical, archaeological, paleontological or architectural
        significance;

    - [(f)]{.lawlabel} a non-negligible adverse change occurring in
      Canada to the health, social or economic conditions of the
      Indigenous peoples of Canada; and

    - [(g)]{.lawlabel} a non-negligible adverse change to a health,
      social or economic matter that is within the legislative authority
      of Parliament that is set out in Schedule 3.

    In the case of a physical activity or a designated project that is
    carried out on federal lands or is a [[federal work or
    undertaking]{.dfn}]{.DefinitionRef}, as defined in subsection 3(1)

    1999](/eng/acts/C-15.31), this definition also includes the

## [Purposes]{.HTitleText1} {#h-1160170 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Purpose

- **[[6]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-6 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  purpose of this Act is to prevent or mitigate significant adverse
  effects within federal jurisdiction --- and significant direct or
  incidental adverse effects --- that may be caused by the carrying out
  of designated projects, as well as significant adverse [[environmental
  effects]{.dfn}]{.DefinitionRef}, as defined in section 81, that may be
  caused by the carrying out of [[projects]{.dfn}]{.DefinitionRef}, as
  defined in that section, by establishing processes to anticipate,
  identify and assess the potential effects of those projects in order
  to inform decision making under this or any other Act of Parliament in
  respect of those effects.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Mandate

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The Government of Canada, the Minister, the Agency
  and federal authorities, in the administration of this Act, must
  exercise their powers in a manner that fosters sustainability,
  respects the rights of the Indigenous peoples of Canada recognized and
  affirmed by section 35 of the Constitution Act, 1982, takes into
  account Indigenous knowledge, considers the cumulative effects of
  physical activities, applies the precautionary principle and promotes
  cooperation among jurisdictions and with the Indigenous peoples of
  Canada.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Application of principles to powers

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} The Government of Canada, the Minister, the Agency
  and federal authorities must, in the administration of this Act,
  exercise their powers in a manner that

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} ensures that processes referred to in
    subsection (1) are fair, predictable and efficient; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} adheres to the principles of scientific integrity,
    honesty, objectivity, thoroughness and accuracy.

## [Prohibitions]{.HTitleText1} {#h-1160193 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Proponent

- **[[7]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-7 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Subject
  to subsection (3), the proponent of a designated project must not do
  any act or thing in connection with the carrying out of the designated
  project, in whole or in part, if that act or thing may cause any
  adverse effects within federal jurisdiction.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Schedule 3

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The Governor in Council may, by order, amend Schedule
  3 to add or remove a component of the environment or a health, social
  or economic matter.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Conditions

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} The proponent of a designated project may do an act
  or thing in connection with the carrying out of the designated
  project, in whole or in part, that may cause adverse effects within
  federal jurisdiction if

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the Agency makes a decision under subsection 16(1)
    that no impact assessment of the designated project is required and
    posts that decision on the Internet site;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the proponent complies with the conditions included
    in the decision statement that is issued to the proponent under
    section 65 with respect to that designated project and is not
    expired or revoked; or

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} the Agency permits the proponent to do that act or
    thing, subject to any conditions that it establishes, for the
    purpose of providing to the Agency the information or details that
    it requires in order to prepare for a possible impact assessment of
    that designated project or for the purpose of providing to the
    Agency or a review panel the information or studies that it
    considers necessary for it to conduct the impact assessment of that
    designated project.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Federal authority

**[[8]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-8 .sectionLabel}** A federal authority must
not exercise any power or perform any duty or function conferred on it
under any Act of Parliament other than this Act that could permit a
designated project to be carried out in whole or in part and must not
provide financial assistance to any person for the purpose of enabling
that designated project to be carried out, in whole or in part, unless

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} the Agency makes a decision under subsection 16(1)
  that no impact assessment of the designated project is required and
  posts that decision on the Internet site;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} the decision statement with respect to the designated
  project that is issued to the proponent of the designated project
  under section 65 sets out that

  - [(i)]{.lawlabel} the adverse effects within federal jurisdiction ---
    and the direct or incidental adverse effects --- that are indicated
    in the report with respect to the impact assessment of that project
    are not likely to be, to some extent, significant, or

  - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} the Minister has determined under paragraph
    60(1)(b), or the Governor in Council has determined under paragraph
    62(b), that the adverse effects within federal jurisdiction --- and
    the direct or incidental adverse effects --- that are the subject of
    the determination are justified in the public interest; or

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} the exercise of the power, the performance of the
  duty or function or the provision of financial assistance is for the
  purpose of authorizing the proponent to do an act or thing referred to
  in paragraph 7(3)(c).

## [Designation of Physical Activity]{.HTitleText1} {#h-1160225 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Minister's power to designate

- **[[9]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-9 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  Minister may, on request or on the Minister's own initiative, by
  order, designate a physical activity that is not prescribed by
  regulations made under paragraph 109(b) if, in the Minister's opinion,
  the carrying out of that physical activity may cause adverse effects
  within federal jurisdiction or direct or incidental adverse effects.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Factors

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} If the Minister is of the opinion that the carrying
  out of the physical activity may cause adverse effects within federal
  jurisdiction or direct or incidental adverse effects, the Minister
  may, in deciding whether to make an order, consider

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} public concerns related to the adverse effects
    within federal jurisdiction --- or the direct or incidental adverse
    effects --- that may be caused by the carrying out of the physical
    activity;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the adverse impacts that the physical activity may
    have on the rights of the Indigenous peoples of Canada --- including
    Indigenous women --- recognized and affirmed by section 35 of the
    [Constitution Act, 1982](/eng/Const/);

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} any relevant assessment referred to in section 92,
    93 or 95;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} whether a means other than an impact assessment
    exists that would permit a jurisdiction to address the adverse
    effects within federal jurisdiction --- and the direct or incidental
    adverse effects --- that may be caused by the carrying out of the
    physical activity; and

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} any other factor that the Minister considers
    relevant.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Agency's power to require information

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} The Agency may require any person or entity to
  provide information with respect to any physical activity that can be
  designated under subsection (1).

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Minister's response --- time limit

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} The Minister must respond, with reasons, to a request
  referred to in subsection (1) within 90 days after the day on which it
  is received. The Minister must ensure that his or her response is
  posted on the Internet site.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Suspending time limit

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} The Agency may suspend the time limit for responding
  to the request until any activity that is prescribed by regulations
  made under paragraph 112(1)(c) is completed. If the Agency suspends
  the time limit, it must post on the Internet site a notice that sets
  out its reasons for doing so.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Notice posted on Internet site

  [(6)]{.lawlabel} When the Agency is of the opinion that the prescribed
  activity is completed, it must post a notice to that effect on the

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Limitation

  [(7)]{.lawlabel} The Minister must not make the designation referred
  to in subsection (1) if

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the carrying out of the physical activity has
    substantially begun; or

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} a federal authority has exercised a power or

    activity to be carried out, in whole or in part.

  the order made under subsection (1).

### [Factors To Be Considered]{.HTitleText2} {#h-1160335 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Factors --- impact assessment

- **[[22]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-22 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  impact assessment of a designated project, whether it is conducted by
  the Agency or a review panel, must take into account the following
  factors:

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the changes to the environment or to health, social
    or economic conditions and the positive and negative consequences of
    these changes that are likely to be caused by the carrying out of
    the designated project, including

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} the effects of malfunctions or accidents that may
      occur in connection with the designated project,

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} any cumulative effects that are likely to result
      from the designated project in combination with other physical
      activities that have been or will be carried out, and

    - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} the result of any interaction between those
      effects;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} mitigation measures that are technically and
    economically feasible and that would mitigate any adverse effects of
    the designated project;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} the impact that the designated project may have on
    any Indigenous group and any adverse impact that the designated
    project may have on the rights of the Indigenous peoples of Canada
    recognized and affirmed by section 35 of the [Constitution Act,
    1982](/eng/Const/);

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} the purpose of and need for the designated project;

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} alternative means of carrying out the designated
    project that are technically and economically feasible, including
    through the use of best available technologies, and the effects of
    those means;

  - [(f)]{.lawlabel} any alternatives to the designated project that are
    technically and economically feasible and are directly related to
    the designated project;

  - [(g)]{.lawlabel} Indigenous knowledge provided with respect to the
    designated project;

  - [(h)]{.lawlabel} the extent to which the designated project
    contributes to sustainability;

  - [(i)]{.lawlabel} the extent to which the effects of the designated
    project hinder or contribute to the Government of Canada's ability
    to meet its environmental obligations and its commitments in respect
    of climate change;

  - [(j)]{.lawlabel} any change to the designated project that may be
    caused by the environment;

  - [(k)]{.lawlabel} the requirements of the follow-up program in
    respect of the designated project;

  - [(l)]{.lawlabel} considerations related to Indigenous cultures
    raised with respect to the designated project;

  - [(m)]{.lawlabel} community knowledge provided with respect to the
    designated project;

  - [(n)]{.lawlabel} comments received from the public;

  - [(o)]{.lawlabel} comments from a jurisdiction that are received in
    the course of consultations conducted under section 21;

  - [(p)]{.lawlabel} any relevant assessment referred to in section 92,
    93 or 95;

  - [(q)]{.lawlabel} any assessment of the effects of the designated
    project that is conducted by or on behalf of an Indigenous governing
    body and that is provided with respect to the designated project;

  - [(r)]{.lawlabel} any study or plan that is conducted or prepared by
    a jurisdiction --- or an Indigenous governing body not referred to
    in paragraph (f) or (g) of the definition
    [[jurisdiction]{.dfn}]{.DefinitionRef} in section 2 --- that is in
    respect of a region related to the designated project and that has
    been provided with respect to the project;

  - [(s)]{.lawlabel} the intersection of sex and gender with other
    identity factors; and

  - [(t)]{.lawlabel} any other matter relevant to the impact assessment
    that the Agency requires to be taken into account.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Scope of factors

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The Agency's determination of the scope of the
  factors made under subsection 18(1.2) applies when those factors are
  taken into account under subsection (1).

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} After taking into account any comments received from

  designated project. It must also indicate, from among the effects set
  out in the report, those that are adverse effects within federal
  jurisdiction and those that are direct or incidental adverse effects,
  and specify, from among those adverse effects within federal
  jurisdiction and direct or incidental adverse effects, the ones that
  are likely to be, to some extent, significant and the extent to which
  they are significant.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Report --- Indigenous knowledge

  [(3.1)]{.lawlabel} Subject to section 119, the report must set out how
  the Agency, in determining the effects that are likely to be caused by
  the carrying out of the designated project, took into account and used
  any Indigenous knowledge provided with respect to the designated
  project.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Report --- additional content

  [(3.2)]{.lawlabel} The report must also set out a summary of any
  comments received from the public, as well as the Agency's
  recommendations with respect to any mitigation measures and follow-up
  program and the Agency's rationale and conclusions.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Copy posted on Internet site

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} A copy of the report or a summary of the report and
  an indication of how a copy of the report may be obtained must be
  posted on the Internet site on the day on which the report is
  submitted to the Minister.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Time limit established by Agency ---
  designated project

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} Before the commencement of the impact assessment, the
  Agency may establish

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} a longer time limit than the time limit referred to
    in subsection (2) to take into account circumstances that are
    specific to the designated project or to allow the Agency to
    cooperate with a jurisdiction referred to in section 21 with respect
    to the impact assessment of that project; or

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} a shorter time limit than the time limit referred
    to in subsection (2), for any reason that the Agency considers
    appropriate.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Extension of time limit by Minister

  [(6)]{.lawlabel} The Minister may extend the time limit referred to in
  subsection (2) or any time limit established under subsection (5) by
  any period --- up to a maximum of 90 days --- that is necessary to
  take into account circumstances that are specific to the designated
  project or to allow the Agency to cooperate with a jurisdiction
  referred to in section 21.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Extension of time limit by Governor in
  Council

  [(7)]{.lawlabel} The Governor in Council may, on the recommendation of
  the Minister, extend the time limit extended under subsection (6) by
  any period that is necessary to take into account circumstances that
  are specific to the designated project or to allow the Agency to
  cooperate with a jurisdiction referred to in section 21.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Posting notices on Internet site

  [(8)]{.lawlabel} The Agency must post on the Internet site a notice of
  the time limit established under subsection (5) and of any extension
  granted under this section, including the reasons for establishing
  that time limit or for granting that extension.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Suspending time limit

  [(9)]{.lawlabel} The Agency may suspend the time limit within which it
  must submit the report until any activity that is prescribed by a
  regulation made under paragraph 112(1)(c) is completed. If the Agency
  suspends the time limit, it must post on the Internet site a notice
  that sets out its reasons for doing so.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Notice posted on Internet site

  [(10)]{.lawlabel} When the Agency is of the opinion that the
  prescribed activity is completed, it must post a notice to that effect
  on the Internet site.

### [Impact Assessment by a Review Panel]{.HTitleText2} {#h-1160459 .Subheading}

#### [General Rules]{.HTitleText3} {#h-1160460 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Referral to review panel

- **[[36]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-36 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Within
  45 days after the day on which the notice of the commencement of the
  impact assessment of a designated project is posted on the Internet
  site, the Minister may, if he or she is of the opinion that it is in
  the public interest, refer the impact assessment to a review panel.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Public interest

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The Minister's determination regarding whether the
  referral of the impact assessment of the designated project to a
  review panel is in the public interest must include a consideration of
  the following factors:

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the extent to which the effects within federal
    jurisdiction or the direct or incidental effects that the carrying
    out of the designated project may cause are adverse;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} public concerns related to those effects;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} opportunities for cooperation with any jurisdiction
    that has powers, duties or functions in relation to an assessment of
    the environmental effects of the designated project or any part of
    it; and

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} any adverse impact that the designated project may
    have on the rights of the Indigenous peoples of Canada recognized
    and affirmed by section 35 of the [Constitution Act,
    1982](/eng/Const/).

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Suspending time limit

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} The Agency may suspend the time limit within which
  the Minister may refer an impact assessment to a review panel until
  any activity that is prescribed by regulations made under paragraph
  112(1)(c) is completed. If the Agency suspends the time limit, it must
  post on the Internet site a notice that sets out its reasons for doing
  so.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Notice posted on Internet site

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} When the Agency is of the opinion that the prescribed
  activity is completed, it must post a notice to that effect on the
  Internet site.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Posting notice on Internet site

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} The Agency must post on the Internet site a notice of
  any decision made by the Minister to refer the impact assessment of
  the designated project to a review panel, including the Minister's
  reasons for making that decision.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Time limit

- **[[37]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-37 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} If the
  Minister refers the impact assessment of a designated project to a
  review panel, the Agency must establish the following time limits:

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the time limit, after the day on which the notice
    referred to in subsection 19(4) with respect to the designated
    project is posted on the Internet site, within which the review
    panel must submit a report with respect to that impact assessment to
    the Minister; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the time limit, after the day on which the review
    panel submits the report, within which the Agency must post its
    recommendations under subsection 55.1(2).

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Limit of 600 days

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Subject to subsection (3), the total number of days
  for the time limits established under subsection (1) must not exceed
  600 unless the Agency is of the opinion that more time is required to
  take into account circumstances that are specific to the designated
  project or to allow the review panel to cooperate with a jurisdiction
  referred to in section 21 with respect to the impact assessment of
  that project.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Extension of time limit by Minister

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} The Minister may extend the time limit established
  under paragraph (1)(a) by any period --- up to a maximum of 90 days
  --- that is necessary to take into account circumstances that are
  specific to the designated project or to allow the review panel to
  cooperate with a jurisdiction referred to in section 21.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Extension of time limit by Governor in
  Council

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} The Governor in Council may, on the recommendation of
  the Minister, extend the time limit extended under subsection (3) by
  any period that is necessary to take into account circumstances that
  are specific to the designated project or to allow the review panel to
  cooperate with a jurisdiction referred to in section 21.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Posting notices on Internet site

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} The Agency must post the following items on the
  Internet site:

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the Agency's reasons for establishing the time
    limits under subsection (1);

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} a notice of any extension granted under subsection
    (3), including the Minister's reasons for granting that extension;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} a notice of any extension granted under subsection
    (4), including the Governor in Council's reasons for granting that

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Suspending time limit

  [(6)]{.lawlabel} The Agency may suspend the time limit within which

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Notice posted on Internet site

  [(7)]{.lawlabel} When the Agency is of the opinion that the prescribed
  activity is completed, it must post a notice to that effect on the
  Internet site.

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} ensure that the information that it uses when
    conducting the impact assessment is made available to the public;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} hold hearings in a manner that offers the public an
    opportunity to participate meaningfully, in the manner that the
    review panel considers appropriate and within the time period that
    it specifies, in the impact assessment;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} prepare a report with respect to the impact
    assessment that

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} sets out the effects that, in the opinion of the
      review panel, are likely to be caused by the carrying out of the
      designated project,

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} indicates which of the effects referred to in
      subparagraph (i) are adverse effects within federal jurisdiction
      and which are direct or incidental adverse effects and specifies,
      from among those adverse effects within federal jurisdiction and
      direct or incidental adverse effects, the ones that are likely to
      be, to some extent, significant and the extent to which they are
      significant,

      review panel, in determining the effects that are likely to be
      caused by the carrying out of the designated project, took into
      account and used any Indigenous knowledge provided with respect to
      the designated project,

    - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} sets out a summary of any comments received
      from the public, and

    - [(iv)]{.lawlabel} sets out the review panel's rationale,
      conclusions and recommendations, including conclusions and
      recommendations with respect to any mitigation measures and
      follow-up program;

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} submit the report with respect to the impact
    assessment to the Minister; and

  - [(f)]{.lawlabel} on the Minister's request, clarify any of the
    conclusions and recommendations set out in its report with respect

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Power to summon witnesses

- **[[53]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-53 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A
  review panel has the power to summon any person to appear as a witness
  before it and to order the witness to

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} give evidence, orally or in writing; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} produce any records and other things that the panel
    considers necessary for conducting its impact assessment of the
    designated project.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Enforcement powers

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} A review panel has the same power to enforce the
  attendance of witnesses and to compel them to give evidence and
  produce records and other things as is vested in a court of record.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Hearings to be public

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} A hearing by a review panel must be public unless the
  panel is satisfied after representations made by a witness that
  specific, direct and substantial harm would be caused to the witness
  or specific harm would be caused to the environment by the disclosure
  of the evidence, records or other things that the witness is ordered
  to give or produce under subsection (1).

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Non-disclosure

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} If a review panel is satisfied that the disclosure of
  evidence, records or other things would cause specific, direct and
  substantial harm to a person or Indigenous group, the evidence,
  records or things are privileged and even if their disclosure is
  authorized under subsection 119(2) must not, without the authorization
  of the person or Indigenous group, knowingly be, or be permitted to
  be, disclosed by any person who has obtained the evidence, records or
  other things under this Act.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Non-disclosure

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} If a review panel is satisfied that the disclosure of
  evidence, records or other things would cause specific harm to the
  environment, the evidence, records or things are privileged and must
  not, without the review panel's authorization, knowingly be, or be
  permitted to be, disclosed by any person who has obtained the
  evidence, records or other things under this Act.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Enforcement of summonses and orders

  [(6)]{.lawlabel} Any summons issued or order made by a review panel
  under subsection (1) must, for the purposes of enforcement, be made a
  summons or order of the Federal Court by following the usual practice
  and procedure.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Immunity

  [(7)]{.lawlabel} No action or other proceeding lies against a member
  of a review panel for or in respect of anything done or omitted to be
  done during the course of and for the purposes of the assessment by
  the review panel.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Informal proceedings

**[[54]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-54 .sectionLabel}** A review panel must, to
the extent that is consistent with the general application of the rules
of procedural fairness and natural justice, emphasize flexibility and
informality in the conduct of hearings and in particular must allow, if
appropriate, the admission of evidence that would not normally be
admissible under the rules of evidence.

  the public interest.

## [Duties of Certain Authorities in Relation to Projects]{.HTitleText1} {#h-1160814 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Definitions

**[[81]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-81 .sectionLabel}** The following definitions
apply in this section and sections 82 to 91.

[[authority]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[authority]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means

    - [(a)]{.lawlabel} a federal authority; and

    - [(b)]{.lawlabel} any other body that is set out in Schedule
      4. ([autorité]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

[[environmental effects]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[environmental effects]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means changes to the
    environment and the impact of these changes on the Indigenous
    peoples of Canada and on health, social or economic
    conditions. ([effets environnementaux]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

[[project]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[project]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means

    - [(a)]{.lawlabel} a physical activity that is carried out on
      federal lands or outside Canada in relation to a physical work and
      that is not a designated project or a physical activity designated
      by regulations made under paragraph 112(1)(a.2); and

    - [(b)]{.lawlabel} a physical activity that is designated under
      section 87 or that is part of a class of physical activities that
      is designated under that section. ([projet]{.DefinedTermLink
      lang="fr"})

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Project carried out on federal lands

**[[82]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-82 .sectionLabel}** An authority must not
carry out a project on federal lands, exercise any power or perform any
duty or function conferred on it under any Act of Parliament other than
this Act that could permit a project to be carried out, in whole or in
part, on federal lands or provide financial assistance to any person for
the purpose of enabling that project to be carried out, in whole or in
part, on federal lands, unless

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} the authority determines that the carrying out of the
  project is not likely to cause significant adverse environmental
  effects; or

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} the authority determines that the carrying out of the
  project is likely to cause significant adverse environmental effects
  and the Governor in Council decides, under subsection 90(3), that
  those effects are justified in the circumstances.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Project outside Canada

**[[83]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-83 .sectionLabel}** A federal authority must
not carry out a project outside Canada, or provide financial assistance
to any person for the purpose of enabling that project to be carried
out, in whole or in part, outside Canada, unless

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} the federal authority determines that the carrying
  out of the project is not likely to cause significant adverse
  environmental effects; or

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} the federal authority determines that the carrying
  out of the project is likely to cause significant adverse
  environmental effects and the Governor in Council decides, under
  subsection 90(3), that those effects are justified in the
  circumstances.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Factors

- **[[84]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-84 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} An
  authority's determination regarding whether the carrying out of the
  project is likely to cause significant adverse environmental effects
  must be based on a consideration of the following factors:

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} any adverse impact that the project may have on the
    rights of the Indigenous peoples of Canada recognized and affirmed
    by section 35 of the [Constitution Act, 1982](/eng/Const/);

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} Indigenous knowledge provided with respect to the
    project;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} community knowledge provided with respect to the
    project;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} comments received from the public under subsection
    86(1); and

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} the mitigation measures that are technically and
    economically feasible and that would mitigate any significant
    adverse environmental effects of the project that the authority is
    satisfied will be implemented.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Factors --- project outside Canada

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} However, if the project is to be carried out outside
  Canada, the authority's determination need not include a consideration
  of the factors set out in paragraphs (1)(a) and (b).

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Federal authority's obligation

**[[85]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-85 .sectionLabel}** Every federal authority
that is in possession of specialist or expert information or knowledge
with respect to a project must, on an authority's request and within the
period that it specifies, make that information or knowledge available
to the authority.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Notice posted on Internet site

- **[[86]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-86 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Before
  making a determination under section 82 or 83, an authority must post
  on the Internet site a notice that indicates that it intends to make
  such a determination and that invites the public to provide comments
  respecting that determination.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Notice of determination posted on
  Internet site

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} No sooner than 30 days after the day on which it
  posts the notice referred to in subsection (1), the authority must
  post on the Internet site a notice of its determination, including any
  mitigation measures that it took into account in making the
  determination.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Power to designate physical activities

**[[87]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-87 .sectionLabel}** The Minister may, by
order, designate a physical activity, or a class of physical activities,
carried out on federal lands or outside Canada that is not in relation
to a physical work and is not a designated project, but that, in the
Minister's opinion, may cause significant adverse environmental effects.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Designation of class of projects

- **[[88]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-88 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  Minister may, by order, designate a class of projects if, in the
  Minister's opinion, the carrying out of a project that is a part of
  the class will cause only insignificant adverse environmental effects.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Projects excluded

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Sections 82 and 83 do not apply to an authority in
  respect of a project that is part of a class of projects that is
  designated under subsection (1).

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Notice inviting public comments

- **[[89]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-89 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} If the
  Minister intends to designate a physical activity, or a class of
  physical activities, under section 87 or a class of projects under
  subsection 88(1), the Agency must post on the Internet site a notice
  that invites the public to provide comments respecting the designation
  within 30 days after the day on which the notice is posted.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Minister must consider public comments

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The Minister must consider any comments received from
  the public in deciding whether to make the designation.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Posting notice on Internet site

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} If the Minister designates a physical activity, or a
  class of physical activities, under section 87 or a class of projects
  under subsection 88(1), the Agency must post on the Internet site a
  notice that includes a description of the physical activity, the class
  of physical activities or the class of projects, as the case may be,
  and the Minister's reasons for making the designation.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Referral to Governor in Council

- **[[90]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-90 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} If the
  authority determines that the carrying out of a project on federal
  lands or outside Canada is likely to cause significant adverse
  environmental effects, the authority may refer to the Governor in
  Council the matter of whether those effects are justified in the
  circumstances.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Referral through Minister

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} When the determination is made by an authority other
  than a federal Minister, then the referral to the Governor in Council
  is made through the Minister responsible before Parliament for that
  authority.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Governor in Council's decision

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} When a matter has been referred to the Governor in
  Council, the Governor in Council must decide whether the significant
  adverse environmental effects are justified in the circumstances and
  must inform the authority of its decision.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Non-application --- national emergency or
emergency

**[[91]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-91 .sectionLabel}** Sections 82 and 83 do not
apply to an authority in respect of a project

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} in relation to which there are matters of national
  security;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} that is to be carried out in response to a national
  emergency for which special temporary measures are being taken under
  the [Emergencies Act](/eng/acts/E-4.5); or

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} that is to be carried out in response to an
  emergency, and the carrying out of the project without delay is in the
  interest of preventing damage to property or the environment or is in
  the interest of public health or safety.

## [Minister's Advisory Council]{.HTitleText1} {#h-1161092 .Part}

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} one person recommended by an Indigenous governing
    body or other entity that represents the interests of First Nations;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} one person recommended by an Indigenous governing
    body or other entity that represents the interests of the Inuit; and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} one person recommended by an Indigenous governing
    body or other entity that represents the interests of the Métis.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Meetings

- **[[118]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-118 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  advisory council must meet at least once a year.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}First report

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Within three months after the end of the fiscal year
  during which the first anniversary of the coming into force of this
  Act occurs, the advisory council must submit to the Minister a report
  setting out the advice it provided during the period beginning on the
  day on which this Act comes into force and ending on the day on which
  that fiscal year ends, including the advice with respect to regional
  and strategic assessments to be given priority.

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} Within three months after the end of each period of

  priority.

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} The Minister must cause a copy of the report to be

  then ensure the report is posted on the Internet site.

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} Within 90 days after the day on which he or she
  receives the report, the Minister must provide his or her comments on
  the report to the advisory council, including with respect to the
  advice the council provided about regional and strategic assessments,
  and then ensure they are posted on the Internet site.

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} The person or entity referred to in subsection (3)

## [Administration and Enforcement]{.HTitleText1} {#h-1161129 .Part}

### [Enforcement Officers and Analysts]{.HTitleText2} {#h-1161130 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Designation

- **[[120]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-120 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  President of the Agency may designate persons or classes of persons as
  enforcement officers or analysts for the purposes of the
  administration and enforcement of this Act.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Certificate

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The President of the Agency must provide every person
  designated under subsection (1) with a certificate of designation.
  That person must, if so requested, produce the certificate to the
  occupant or person in charge of a place referred to in subsection
  122(1).

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Immunity

**[[121]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-121 .sectionLabel}** No action or other
proceeding of a civil nature lies against an enforcement officer or
analyst in respect of anything that is done or omitted to be done in
good faith while exercising their powers or performing their duties or
functions under this Act.

### [Powers]{.HTitleText2} {#h-1161138 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Authority to enter

- **[[122]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-122 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} An
  enforcement officer may, for the purpose of verifying compliance or
  preventing non-compliance with this Act, including an injunction
  ordered under section 140, enter a place in which they have reasonable
  grounds to believe a designated project is or was being carried out or
  a record or anything relating to a designated project is located.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Powers on entry

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The enforcement officer may, for those purposes,

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} examine anything in the place;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} use any means of communication in the place or
    cause it to be used;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} use any computer system in the place, or cause it
    to be used, to examine data contained in or available to it;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} prepare a document, or cause one to be prepared,
    based on the data;

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} use any copying equipment in the place, or cause it
    to be used;

  - [(f)]{.lawlabel} remove anything from the place for examination or
    copying;

  - [(g)]{.lawlabel} take photographs and make recordings or sketches;

  - [(h)]{.lawlabel} direct the owner or person in charge of the place
    or a person at the place to establish their identity to the
    enforcement officer's satisfaction or to stop or start an activity;

  - [(i)]{.lawlabel} direct the owner or a person having possession,
    care or control of anything in the place not to move it, or to
    restrict its movement, for as long as, in the enforcement officer's
    opinion, is necessary;

  - [(j)]{.lawlabel} direct any person to put any machinery, vehicle or
    equipment in the place into operation or to cease operating it; and

  - [(k)]{.lawlabel} prohibit or limit access to all or part of the
    place.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Person accompanying enforcement officer

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} An enforcement officer who enters a place under
  subsection (1) may be accompanied by any other person that they
  believe is necessary to help them exercise their powers and perform
  their duties and functions under this section.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Analysts

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} An analyst who accompanies an enforcement officer may
  exercise any of the powers referred to in paragraphs (2)(a) to (g).

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Duty to assist

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} The owner or person in charge of the place and every
  person in the place must give all assistance that is reasonably
  required to enable the enforcement officer or analyst to exercise a
  power or perform a duty or function under this section and must
  provide any documents, data or information that are reasonably
  required for that purpose.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Warrant for dwelling-house

- **[[123]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-123 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} If
  the place is a dwelling-house, the enforcement officer may enter it
  without the occupant's consent only under the authority of a warrant
  issued under subsection (2).

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Authority to issue warrant

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} On [ex parte]{.otherLang lang="la"} application, a
  justice of the peace may issue a warrant authorizing an enforcement
  officer who is named in it to enter a dwelling-house, subject to any
  conditions specified in the warrant, and authorizing any analyst named
  in the warrant to accompany the enforcement officer, if the justice is
  satisfied by information on oath that

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the dwelling-house is a place referred to in
    subsection 122(1);

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} entry to the dwelling-house is necessary for any of
    the purposes of that subsection; and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} entry was refused by the occupant or there are
    reasonable grounds to believe that entry will be refused or that
    consent to entry cannot be obtained from the occupant.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Use of force

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} In executing a warrant to enter a dwelling-house, an
  enforcement officer or analyst may use force only if the use of force
  has been specifically authorized in the warrant and the enforcement
  officer or analyst is accompanied by a peace officer.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Entry on private property

- **[[124]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-124 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} For
  the purpose of gaining entry to a place referred to in subsection
  122(1), an enforcement officer and analyst may enter private property
  and pass through it, and are not liable for doing so. For greater
  certainty, no person has a right to object to that use of the property
  and no warrant is required for the entry, unless the property is a
  dwelling-house.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Person accompanying

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} A person may, at an enforcement officer or analyst's
  request, accompany them to assist them to gain entry to the place
  referred to in subsection 122(1) and is not liable for doing so.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Production of documents

- **[[125]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-125 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} An
  enforcement officer may, for the purpose of verifying compliance or
  preventing non-compliance with this Act, including an injunction
  ordered under section 140, require any person to produce at a place
  specified by the enforcement officer, within any reasonable time and
  in the manner specified, any books, records, electronic data or other
  documents that the enforcement officer believes on reasonable grounds
  contain any information relevant to the administration of this Act.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Proof of delivery

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The enforcement officer must make the request by a
  means that provides a record of delivery.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Compliance

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} Any person who is required to produce anything under
  subsection (1) must, despite any other law to the contrary, do so as
  required.

### [Notice of Non-compliance]{.HTitleText2} {#h-1161183 .Subheading}

- **[[126]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-126 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} If

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Contents of notice

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} the relevant facts surrounding the alleged

### [Orders]{.HTitleText2} {#h-1161193 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Measures required

- **[[127]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-127 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} If
  an enforcement officer believes on reasonable grounds that there is or
  is likely to be a contravention of this Act, they may, among other
  things, order a person or entity to

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} stop doing something that is or is likely to be in
    contravention of this Act or cause it to be stopped; or

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} take any measure that is necessary in order to
    comply with this Act or to mitigate the effects of non-compliance.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Contents of order

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Subject to section 128, the order must be made in
  writing and must set out

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the name of the person or entity to whom it is
    directed;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the provision of this Act or the regulations, or
    the condition established under section 64 or added or amended under
    section 68, that is alleged to have been or that is likely to be
    contravened;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} the relevant facts surrounding the alleged
    contravention;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} the measures to be taken;

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} the time or the day when each measure is to begin
    or the period during which it is to be carried out;

  - [(f)]{.lawlabel} the duration of the order;

  - [(g)]{.lawlabel} a statement that the person or entity may provide
    comments to the enforcement officer in response to the order; and

  - [(h)]{.lawlabel} a statement that a request for a review may be made
    to the President of the Agency and the time period for doing so.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Variation or cancellation of order

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} The enforcement officer may, after giving reasonable
  notice to all persons or entities named in the order, and so long as
  the President of the Agency has not received a notice requesting a
  review of the order,

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} amend or suspend a condition of the order, add a
    condition to it or delete a condition from it;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} cancel the order;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} correct a clerical error in the order; or

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} extend the order's duration.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Exigent circumstances

- **[[128]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-128 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} In
  the case of exigent circumstances, an order may be given orally on the
  condition that it is followed, within seven days, by a written order
  issued in accordance with section 127.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}For greater certainty

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} For greater certainty, [[exigent
  circumstances]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} includes circumstances in which
  the delay necessary to issue a written order that meets the
  requirements of subsection 127(2) would likely result in danger to the
  environment, human life or health or public safety.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Duty to comply with order

- **[[129]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-129 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Any
  person or entity to whom an order is given under section 127 or 128
  must comply with the order.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Measures taken by enforcement officer

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} If the person or entity does not comply with the
  order within the time specified, the enforcement officer may, on their
  own initiative and at that person or entity's expense, carry out the
  measure required.

### [Review of Orders]{.HTitleText2} {#h-1161225 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Request for review

- **[[130]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-130 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Any
  person or entity to whom an order is given under section 127 or 128
  may, by notice in writing given to the President of the Agency within
  30 days after the day on which the person or entity receives a copy of
  the order, make a request to the President for a review of the order.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Extension of period for request

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The President of the Agency may extend the period
  within which a request for a review may be made if, in the President's
  opinion, it is in the public interest to do so.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Review

**[[131]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-131 .sectionLabel}** On receipt of a request
made under subsection 130(1), the President of the Agency must designate
an individual as a review officer to review the order.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}No automatic stay on review

- **[[132]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-132
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Subject to subsection (2), the
  request for a review of an order does not suspend the operation of an
  order.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Suspension on application

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} A review officer may, on application made by a person
  or entity to whom an order is given, suspend the operation of the
  order if the review officer considers it appropriate and, in that
  case, impose on all persons or entities subject to the order
  conditions that are reasonable in the circumstances and consistent
  with the protection of the environment, human life or health or public
  safety.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Duration of suspension

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} If the operation of an order is suspended under
  subsection (2), the period for which the order is issued is suspended
  until the review is completed.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Evidence

- **[[133]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-133 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A
  review officer may order any person to give evidence in writing or
  produce any documents and things that the review officer considers
  relevant for the purpose of performing any of their functions.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Enforcement of orders

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Any order made under subsection (1) may be made an
  order of the Federal Court or of the superior court of a province and
  is enforceable in the same manner as an order of that court.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Procedure

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} An order made under subsection (1) may be made an
  order of the Federal Court or of the superior court of a province by
  following the usual practice and procedure of the court in such
  matters, or by filing a certified copy of the order with the registrar
  of the court.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Powers of review officer

**[[134]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-134 .sectionLabel}** A review officer, after
considering the order under review and giving all persons or entities
who are subject to it a reasonable opportunity to make representations,
may

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} confirm or cancel the order;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} amend or suspend a condition of the order, add a
  condition to it or delete a condition from it; or

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Duty to comply with revised order

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Any person or entity that is subject to an order
  confirmed or varied under section 134 must comply with the order.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Immunity

### [Injunctions]{.HTitleText2} {#h-1161269 .Subheading}

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} refrain from doing an act that, in the court's

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} do an act that, in the court's opinion, may prevent

### [Voluntary Reports]{.HTitleText2} {#h-1161277 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Voluntary reports

- **[[141]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-141 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} If a
  person has knowledge of the commission or reasonable likelihood of the
  commission of an offence under this Act, but is not required to report
  the matter under this Act, the person may report any information
  relating to the offence or likely offence to an enforcement officer or
  the Agency.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Request for confidentiality

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The person making the report may request that their
  identity, and any information that may reveal their identity, not be
  disclosed.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Due diligence

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} An enforcement officer and the Agency must exercise
  all due diligence to not disclose the identity of a person who makes a
  request under subsection (2) or any information that may reveal their
  identity.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Prohibition

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} A person not referred to in subsection (3), or an
  entity, must not disclose the identity of a person who makes a request
  under subsection (2) or any information that may reveal that person's
  identity unless that person authorizes the disclosure in writing.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Employee protection

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} Despite any other Act of Parliament, an employer must
  not dismiss, suspend, demote, discipline, harass or otherwise
  disadvantage an employee, or deny an employee a benefit of employment,
  with the intent to retaliate against the employee because the employee
  has

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} made a report under subsection (1);

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} acting in good faith and on the basis of reasonable
    belief, refused or stated an intention of refusing to do anything
    that is an offence under this Act; or

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} acting in good faith and on the basis of reasonable
    belief, done or stated an intention of doing anything that is
    required to be done by or under this Act.

### [Prohibitions and Offences]{.HTitleText2} {#h-1161292 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Obstruction

**[[142]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-142 .sectionLabel}** A person or entity must
not obstruct or hinder an enforcement officer or analyst who is
exercising their powers or performing their duties or functions under
this Act.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}False statements or information

**[[143]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-143 .sectionLabel}** A person or entity must
not make a false or misleading statement or provide false or misleading
information in connection with any matter under this Act to any person
who is exercising their powers or performing their duties or functions
under this Act.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Offences

- **[[144]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-144
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Every person or entity commits an
  offence that

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} contravenes section 7, subsection 122(5), 125(3),
    129(1), 135(2) or 141(4) or (5) or section 142 or 143;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} contravenes a condition established under
    subsection 64(2) or added or amended under section 68 with which the
    person or entity must comply; or

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} omits to provide to the Minister information
    required to be provided under subsection 72(1).

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Penalty --- individuals

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Every individual who commits an offence under
  subsection (1) is liable on summary conviction

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} for a first offence, to a fine of not less than
    \$5,000 and not more than \$300,000; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} for a second or subsequent offence, to a fine of
    not less than \$10,000 and not more than \$600,000.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Penalty --- small revenue corporations
  or entities

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} Every corporation or entity that commits an offence
  under subsection (1) and that the court determines under section 145
  to be a small revenue corporation or entity is liable on summary
  conviction

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} for a first offence, to a fine of not less than
    \$25,000 and not more than \$2,000,000; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} for a second or subsequent offence, to a fine of
    not less than \$50,000 and not more than \$4,000,000.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Penalty --- other corporations or
  entities

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} Every corporation or entity, other than a corporation
  or entity referred to in subsection (3), that commits an offence under
  subsection (1) is liable on summary conviction

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} for a first offence, to a fine of not less than
    \$100,000 and not more than \$4,000,000; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} for a second or subsequent offence, to a fine of
    not less than \$200,000 and not more than \$8,000,000.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Determination of small revenue
corporation or entity status

**[[145]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-145 .sectionLabel}** For the purpose of
subsection 144(3), a court may determine a corporation or entity to be a
small revenue corporation or entity if the court is satisfied that the
gross revenues of the corporation or entity for the 12 months
immediately before the day on which the subject matter of the
proceedings arose --- or, if it arose on more than one day, for the 12
months immediately before the first day on which the subject matter of
the proceedings arose --- were not more than \$5,000,000.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Continuing offences

- **[[146]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-146 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} If
  an offence of contravening section 7, subsection 129(1) or 135(2) or
  section 142, or an offence under paragraph 144(1)(b), is committed or
  continued on more than one day, it constitutes a separate offence for
  each day on which it is committed or continued.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Due diligence defence

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} A person or entity must not be found guilty of an
  offence for a contravention of section 7, subsection 129(1) or 135(2)
  or section 142, or of an offence under paragraph 144(1)(b), if they
  establish that they exercised due diligence to prevent the commission
  of the offence.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Liability of senior officers

**[[147]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-147 .sectionLabel}** If a corporation or
entity commits an offence under this Act, any [[senior
officer]{.dfn}]{.DefinitionRef}, as defined in section 2 of the
[Criminal Code](/eng/acts/C-46), of the corporation or entity who
directed, authorized, assented to, acquiesced in or participated in the
commission of the offence is a party to and guilty of the offence, and
is liable on conviction to the penalty provided for by this Act for an
individual in respect of the offence committed by the corporation or
entity, whether or not the corporation or entity has been prosecuted.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Duties of senior officers

**[[148]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-148 .sectionLabel}** Every [[senior
officer]{.dfn}]{.DefinitionRef}, as defined in section 2 of the
[Criminal Code](/eng/acts/C-46), of a corporation or entity must take
all reasonable care to ensure that the corporation or entity complies
with this Act and any order issued by an enforcement officer or review
officer.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Limitation period or prescription

**[[149]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-149 .sectionLabel}** Proceedings by way of
summary conviction in respect of an offence under this Act may be
instituted at any time within two years after the day on which the
Minister becomes aware of the acts or omissions that constitute the
alleged offence.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Admissibility of evidence

- **[[150]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-150 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} In
  proceedings for an offence under this Act, a statement, certificate,
  report or other document of the Minister, an officer or employee of
  the Agency, an enforcement officer or an analyst that is purported to
  have been signed by that person is admissible in evidence without
  proof of the signature or official character of the person appearing
  to have signed it and, in the absence of evidence to the contrary, is
  proof of the matters asserted in it.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Copies and extracts

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} In proceedings for an offence under this Act, a copy
  of or an extract from any document that is made by the Minister, an
  officer or employee of the Agency, an enforcement officer or an
  analyst that appears to have been certified under the signature of
  that person as a true copy or extract is admissible in evidence
  without proof of the signature or official character of the person
  appearing to have signed it and, in the absence of evidence to the
  contrary, has the same probative force as the original would have if
  it were proved in the ordinary way.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Presumed date of issue

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} A document referred to in this section is, in the
  absence of evidence to the contrary, presumed to have been issued on
  the date that it bears.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Notice

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} No document referred to in this section may be
  received in evidence unless the party intending to produce it has
  provided reasonable notice of that intention to the party against whom
  it is intended to be produced together with a copy of the document.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Notice to shareholders

**[[151]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-151 .sectionLabel}** If a corporation that
has shareholders is convicted of an offence under this Act, the court
must make an order directing the corporation to notify its shareholders,
in the manner and within the time directed by the court, of the facts
relating to the commission of the offence and of the details of the
punishment imposed.

## [Impact Assessment Agency of Canada]{.HTitleText1} {#h-1161347 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Agency continued

- **[[153]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-153 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  Canadian Environmental Assessment Agency is continued as the Impact
  Assessment Agency of Canada. The Impact Assessment Agency must advise
  and assist the Minister in exercising the powers and performing the
  duties and functions conferred on him or her by this Act.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Minister's responsibility

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The Minister is responsible for the Agency. The
  Minister may not, except as provided in this Act, direct the President
  of the Agency or its employees, or any review panel members, with
  respect to a report, decision, order or recommendation to be made
  under this Act.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Delegation to Agency

- **[[154]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-154 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  Minister may, subject to any terms and conditions that the Minister
  specifies, delegate to an officer or employee of the Agency any of the
  powers, duties and functions that the Minister is authorized to
  exercise or perform under this Act.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Restriction

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} However, the Minister is not authorized to delegate a
  power to make regulations or a power to delegate under subsection (1).

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Agency's objects

**[[155]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-155 .sectionLabel}** The Agency's objects
are

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} to conduct or administer impact assessments and
  administer any other requirements and procedures established by this
  Act and the regulations;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} to coordinate --- during the period that begins on
  the day on which a copy of the description of the project referred to
  in subsection 10(1) is posted on the Internet site, and that ends on
  the day on which the decision statement in respect of the project is
  issued --- consultations with Indigenous groups that may be affected
  by the carrying out of a designated project;

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} to promote harmonization in relation to the
  assessment of effects across Canada at all levels of government;

- [(d)]{.lawlabel} to promote or conduct research in matters of impact
  assessment and to encourage the development of impact assessment
  techniques and practices, including testing programs, alone or in
  cooperation with other agencies or organizations;

- [(e)]{.lawlabel} to promote impact assessment in a manner that is
  consistent with the purposes of this Act;

- [(f)]{.lawlabel} to promote, monitor and facilitate compliance with
  this Act;

- [(g)]{.lawlabel} to promote and monitor the quality of impact
  assessments conducted under this Act;

- [(h)]{.lawlabel} to develop policy related to this Act; and

- [(i)]{.lawlabel} to engage in consultation with the Indigenous peoples
  of Canada on policy issues related to this Act.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Agency's duties

- **[[156]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-156 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} In
  carrying out its objects, the Agency must

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} provide support for review panels and any
    committees established under section 92 or under an agreement or
    arrangement entered into under paragraph 93(1)(a) or (b);

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} provide, on the Minister's request, administrative
    support for any research and advisory body established under
    paragraph 114(1)(b); and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} provide information or training to facilitate the
    application of this Act.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Agency's powers

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} In carrying out its objects, the Agency may

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} undertake studies or activities or conduct research
    relating to impact assessment;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} advise persons and organizations on matters
    relating to the assessment of effects;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} issue guidelines and codes of practice;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} negotiate agreements or arrangements referred to in
    paragraphs 114(1)(c) to (f) on the Minister's behalf; and

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} establish research and advisory bodies for matters
    related to impact assessment and monitoring committees for matters
    related to the implementation of follow-up programs and adaptive
    management plans, including with respect to the interests and
    concerns of Indigenous peoples of Canada, and appoint as a member of
    any such bodies one or more persons.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Expert committee

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Appointment

former Agency.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Appropriations

Agency.
