## Restoring Mail Delivery for Canadians Act {#restoring-mail-delivery-for-canadians-act .Title-of-Act}

[S.C.]{.abbr title="Statutes of Canada"} 2011, c. 17
:::

Assented to 2011-06-26

An Act to provide for the resumption and continuation of postal services
::::

:::: section
::: Enacts
Her Majesty, by and with the advice and consent of the Senate and House
of Commons of Canada, enacts as follows:
:::

## [Short Title]{.HTitleText1} {#h-427480 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Short title

**[[1]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-1 .sectionLabel}** This Act may be cited as
the [Restoring Mail Delivery for Canadians Act](/eng/acts/R-7.2).

## [Interpretation]{.HTitleText1} {#h-427483 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Definitions

- **[[2]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-2 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  following definitions apply in this Act.

  [[arbitrator]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[arbitrator]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means the arbitrator appointed
      under section 8. ([arbitre]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

  [[collective agreement]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[collective agreement]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means the collective
      agreement between the employer and the union that expired on
      January 31, 2011. ([convention collective]{.DefinedTermLink
      lang="fr"})

  [[employee]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[employee]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means a person employed by the
      employer and bound by the collective
      agreement. ([employé]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

  [[employer]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[employer]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means the Canada Post
      Corporation. ([employeur]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

  [[Minister]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[Minister]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means the Minister of
      Labour. ([ministre]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

  [[union]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[union]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means the Canadian Union of Postal
      Workers. ([syndicat]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Words and expressions

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Unless otherwise provided, words and expressions used
  in this Act have the same meaning as in Part I of the [Canada Labour
  Code](/eng/acts/L-2).

## [Postal Services]{.HTitleText1} {#h-427495 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Resumption or continuation of postal
services

**[[3]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-3 .sectionLabel}** On the coming into force of
this Act,

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} the employer must resume without delay, or continue,
  as the case may be, postal services; and

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} every employee must, when so required, resume without
  delay, or continue, as the case may be, the duties of the employee's
  employment.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Prohibitions

**[[4]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-4 .sectionLabel}** It is prohibited for the
employer and for any officer or representative of the employer to

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} in any manner impede any employee from complying with
  paragraph 3(b); or

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} discharge or in any other manner discipline, or
  authorize or direct the discharge or discipline of, any employee by
  reason of the employee having been on strike before the coming into
  force of this Act.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Obligations

**[[5]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-5 .sectionLabel}** The union and each officer
and represent­ative of the union must

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} without delay on the coming into force of this Act,
  give notice to the employees that, by reason of that coming into
  force, postal services are to be resumed or continued, as the case may
  be, and that the employees, when so required, are to resume without
  delay, or continue, as the case may be, the duties of their
  employment;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} take all reasonable steps to ensure that employees
  comply with paragraph 3(b); and

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} refrain from any conduct that may encourage employees
  not to comply with paragraph 3(b).

## [Extension of Collective Agreement]{.HTitleText1} {#h-427509 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Extension of collective agreement

- **[[6]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-6 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The term
  of the collective agreement is extended to include the period
  beginning on February 1, 2011 and ending on the day on which a new
  collective agreement between the employer and the union comes into
  effect.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Collective agreement binding for
  extended term

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Despite anything in the collective agreement or in
  Part I of the [Canada Labour Code](/eng/acts/L-2), the collective
  agreement, as extended by subsection (1), is effective and binding on
  the parties to it for the period for which it is extended. However,
  that Part applies in respect of the collective agreement, as extended,
  as if that period were the term of the collective agreement.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Strikes and lockouts prohibited

**[[7]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-7 .sectionLabel}** During the term of the
collective agreement, as extended by subsection 6(1), it is prohibited

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} for the employer and for any officer or
  representative of the employer to declare or cause a lockout against
  the union;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} for the union and for any officer or representative
  of the union to declare or authorize a strike against the employer;
  and

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} for an employee to participate in a strike against
  the employer.

## [Final Offer Selection]{.HTitleText1} {#h-427520 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Appointment of arbitrator

**[[8]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-8 .sectionLabel}** The Minister must appoint
as arbitrator for final offer selection a person that the Minister
considers appropriate.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Powers and duties

**[[9]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-9 .sectionLabel}** The arbitrator has, with
any modifications that the circumstances require, all the powers and
duties of an arbitrator under paragraphs 60(1)(a), (a.2), (a.3) and
(a.4) and section 61 of the [Canada Labour Code](/eng/acts/L-2).

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Obligation to provide final offer

- **[[10]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-10 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Within
  the time and in the manner that the arbitrator may specify, the
  employer and the union must each submit to the arbitrator

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} a list of the matters on which the employer and the
    union were in agreement as of a date specified by the arbitrator,
    other than salaries and the term of the new collective agreement,
    and proposed contractual language that would give effect to those
    matters;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} a list of the matters remaining in dispute on that
    date, other than salaries and the term of the new collective
    agreement; and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} a final offer in respect of the matters referred to
    in paragraph (b).

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Contractual language

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The final offer must be submitted with proposed
  contractual language that can be incorporated into the new collective
  agreement.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Arbitrator's duties

- **[[11]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-11
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Subject to section 13, within 90
  days after being appointed, or within any longer period that may be
  specified by the Minister, the arbitrator must

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} determine the matters on which the employer and the
    union were in agreement as of the date specified for the purposes of
    paragraph 10(1)(a);

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} determine the matters remaining in dispute on that
    date;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} select, in order to resolve the matters remaining
    in dispute, either the final offer submitted by the employer or the
    final offer submitted by the union; and

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} make a decision in respect of the resolution of the
    matters referred to in this subsection and forward a copy of the
    decision to the Minister, the employer and the union.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Guiding principle

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} In making the selection of a final offer, the
  arbitrator is to be guided by the need for terms and conditions of
  employment that are consistent with those in comparable postal
  industries and that will provide the necessary degree of flexibility
  to ensure the short- and long-term economic viability and
  competitiveness of the Canada Post Corporation, maintain the health
  and safety of its workers and ensure the sustainability of its pension
  plan, taking into account

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} that the solvency ratio of the pension plan must
    not decline as a direct result of the new collective agreement; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} that the Canada Post Corporation must, without
    recourse to undue increases in postal rates, operate efficiently,
    improve productivity and meet acceptable standards of service.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}If no final offer submitted

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} If either the employer or the union fails to provide
  the arbitrator with a final offer in accordance with paragraph
  10(1)(c), the arbitrator must select the final offer provided by the
  other party.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Contractual language

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} The arbitrator's decision must be drafted in a manner
  that constitutes a new collective agreement between the employer and
  the union and, to the extent that it is possible, incorporate the
  contractual language that is referred to in paragraph 10(1)(a) and
  that is in the final offer selected by the arbitrator.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Proceedings prohibited

**[[12]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-12 .sectionLabel}** No order is to be made,
no process is to be entered into and no proceeding is to be taken in
court

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} to question the appointment of the arbitrator; or

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} to review, prohibit or restrain any proceeding or
  decision of the arbitrator.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}New collective agreement not precluded

- **[[13]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-13
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Subject to subsections (2) and (3),
  nothing in this Act precludes the employer and the union from entering
  into a new collective agreement at any time before the arbitrator
  makes a decision and, if they do so, the arbitrator's duties under
  this Act cease as of the day on which the new collective agreement is
  entered into.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Term

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} If the term of the new collective agreement is not
  identical to the term provided for in subsection 14(1), the term of
  the new collective agreement is deemed to be as provided for in that
  subsection.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Salaries

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} If a salary calculated in accordance with or
  determined under the new collective agreement is not identical to the
  result of the increases referred to in section 15, the new collective
  agreement is deemed to provide for the salary being increased as
  provided for in that section.

## [New Collective Agreement]{.HTitleText1} {#h-427559 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}New collective agreement

- **[[14]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-14
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Subject to section 15 and despite
  anything in Part I of the [Canada Labour Code](/eng/acts/L-2), the
  arbitrator's decision constitutes a new collective agreement between
  the employer and the union that is effective beginning on the day on
  which it is made and that binds the parties until January 31, 2015.
  However, that Part applies in respect of the new collective agreement
  as if it had been entered into under that Part.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Coming into effect of provisions

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The new collective agreement may provide that any of
  its provisions are effective and binding on a day that is before or
  after the day on which the new collective agreement becomes effective
  and binding.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Salaries

**[[15]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-15 .sectionLabel}** The new collective
agreement is deemed to provide for the following increases to salaries:

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} effective February 1, 2011, salaries in effect as of
  January 31, 2011 are increased by 1.75%;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} effective February 1, 2012, salaries in effect as of
  January 31, 2012 are increased by 1.5%;

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} effective February 1, 2013, salaries in effect as of
  January 31, 2013 are increased by 2%; and

- [(d)]{.lawlabel} effective February 1, 2014, salaries in effect as of
  January 31, 2014 are increased by 2%.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Amendments

**[[16]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-16 .sectionLabel}** Nothing in this Act is to
be construed so as to limit or restrict the rights of the parties to
agree to amend any provision of the new collective agreement, other than
its term as provided for in subsection 14(1) or the salary increases
referred to in section 15, and to give effect to the amendment.

## [Costs]{.HTitleText1} {#h-427573 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Costs

**[[17]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-17 .sectionLabel}** All costs incurred by Her
Majesty in right of Canada relating to the appointment of the arbitrator
and the performance of the arbitrator's duties under this Act are debts
due to Her Majesty in right of Canada and may be recovered as such, in
equal parts from the employer and the union, in any court of competent
jurisdiction.

## [Enforcement]{.HTitleText1} {#h-427576 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Individuals

- **[[18]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-18 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} An
  individual who contravenes any provision of this Act is guilty of an
  offence punishable on summary conviction and is liable, for each day
  or part of a day during which the offence continues, to a fine of

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} not more than \$50,000 if the individual was acting
    in the capacity of an officer or representative of the employer or
    the union when the offence was committed; or

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} not more than \$1,000 in any other case.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Employer or union

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} If the employer or the union contravenes any
  provision of this Act, it is guilty of an offence punishable on
  summary conviction and is liable, for each day or part of a day during
  which the offence continues, to a fine of not more than \$100,000.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}No imprisonment

**[[19]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-19 .sectionLabel}** Despite subsection 787(2)
of the [Criminal Code](/eng/acts/C-46), no term of imprisonment is to be
imposed in default of payment of a fine that is imposed under section
18.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Recovery of fines

**[[20]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-20 .sectionLabel}** If a person is convicted
of an offence under section 18 and the fine that is imposed is not paid
when required, the prosecutor may, by filing the conviction, enter as a
judgment the amount of the fine and costs, if any, in a superior court
of the province in which the trial was held, and the judgment is
enforceable against the person in the same manner as if it were a
judgment rendered against the person in that court in civil proceedings.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Presumption

**[[21]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-21 .sectionLabel}** For the purposes of this
Act, the union is deemed to be a person.
