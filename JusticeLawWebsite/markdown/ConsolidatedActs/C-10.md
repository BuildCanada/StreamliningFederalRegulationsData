## [Interpretation]{.HTitleText1} {#h-59879 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Definitions

- **[[2]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-2 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} In this
  Act,

  [[Board]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[Board]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means the Board of Directors of the
      Corporation; ([conseil]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

  [[Chairman]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[Chairman]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}[\[Repealed, 2006, c. 9, s.
      234\]]{.Repealed}

  [[Chairperson]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[Chairperson]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means the Chairperson of the

      conseil]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

  [[Corporation]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[Corporation]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means the Canada Post
      Corporation established by section 4; ([Société]{.DefinedTermLink
      lang="fr"})

  [[library material]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[library material]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means books, magazines,
      records, CDs, CD-ROMs, audiocassettes, videocassettes, DVDs and
      other audiovisual materials and other similar library
      materials. ([document de bibliothèque]{.DefinedTermLink
      lang="fr"})

  [[mail]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[mail]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means mailable matter from the time
      it is posted to the time it is delivered to the addressee
      thereof; ([envois]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"} ou
      [courrier]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

  [[mail bag]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[mail bag]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means any container or covering
      in which mail is transmitted, whether it contains mail or
      not; ([contenant postal]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

  [[mail contractor]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[mail contractor]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means a person who has
      entered into a contract with the Corporation for the transmission
      of mail, which contract has not expired or been
      terminated; ([entrepreneur postal]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

  [[mail conveyance]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[mail conveyance]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means any physical,
      electronic, optical or other means used to transmit
      mail; ([Version anglaise seulement]{.DefinitionOtherLangOnly})

  [[mailable matter]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[mailable matter]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means any message,
      information, funds or goods that may be transmitted by
      post; ([objets]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

  [[Minister]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[Minister]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means such member of the Queen's
      Privy Council for Canada as is designated by the Governor in
      Council as the Minister for the purposes of this
      Act; ([ministre]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

  [[post]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[post]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means to leave in a post office or
      with a person authorized by the Corporation to receive mailable
      matter; ([poster]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"} ou
      [déposer]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

  [[post office]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[post office]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} includes any place,
      receptacle, device or mail conveyance authorized by the
      Corporation for the posting, receipt, sorting, handling,
      transmission or delivery of mail; ([bureaux de
      poste]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

  [[postage]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[postage]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means the charge or surcharge
      payable for the collection, transmission and delivery by the
      Corporation of messages, information, funds or goods and for
      insurance or other special services provided by the Corporation in
      relation thereto; ([port]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

  [[postage stamp]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[postage stamp]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means any stamp, postage
      impression or postage meter impression authorized by the
      Corporation for the purpose of paying
      postage; ([timbres-poste]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

  [[postal remittance]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[postal remittance]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means any instrument
      authorized by the Corporation for the remittance of funds; ([titre
      de versement postal]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

  [[President]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[President]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means the President of the
      Corporation appointed pursuant to section
      8; ([président]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

  [[transmit]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[transmit]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means to send or convey from one
      place to another place by any physical, electronic, optical or
      other means; ([transmission]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

  [[transmit by post]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[transmit by post]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means to transmit through
      or by means of the Corporation; ([transmission
      postale]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

  [[undeliverable letter]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[undeliverable letter]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means any letter that
      for any reason cannot be delivered to the addressee thereof and
      includes any letter the delivery of which is prohibited by law or
      is refused by the addressee or on which postage due is not paid by
      the sender on demand. ([lettres non
      distribuables]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Presumption

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} For the purposes of this Act,

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} leaving mail at the place of residence or business
    of the addressee thereof,

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} depositing mail in a post office lock box or rural
    mail box or any other receptacle or device provided for the receipt
    of mail of the addressee, or

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} leaving mail with the addressee or his servant or
    agent or with any other person who may reasonably be considered to
    be authorized to receive mail by the addressee thereof,

  according to the usual manner of delivering mail to that addressee, is
  deemed to be delivery to the addressee.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Idem

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} For the purposes of this Act, a thing is deemed to be
  in the course of post from the time it is posted to the time it is
  delivered to the addressee or returned to the sender thereof.

## [PART I]{.HLabel1}[Canada Post Corporation]{.HTitleText1} {#h-59918 .Part}

### [Incorporation]{.HTitleText2} {#h-59919 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Corporation established

**[[4]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-4 .sectionLabel}** There is hereby established
a corporation to be called the Canada Post Corporation.

::: HistoricalNote
- 1980-81-82-83, c. 54, s. 4
:::

### [Objects]{.HTitleText2} {#h-59924 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Objects

- **[[5]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-5 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  objects of the Corporation are

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} to establish and operate a postal service for the
    collection, transmission and delivery of messages, information,
    funds and goods both within Canada and between Canada and places
    outside Canada;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} to manufacture and provide such products and to
    provide such services as are, in the opinion of the Corporation,
    necessary or incidental to the postal service provided by the
    Corporation; and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} to provide to or on behalf of departments and
    agencies of, and corporations owned, controlled or operated by, the
    Government of Canada or any provincial, regional or municipal
    government in Canada or to any person services that, in the opinion
    of the Corporation, are capable of being conveniently provided in
    the course of carrying out the other objects of the Corporation.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Idem

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} While maintaining basic customary postal service, the
  Corporation, in carrying out its objects, shall have regard to

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the desirability of improving and extending its
    products and services in the light of developments in the field of
    communications;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the need to conduct its operations on a
    self-sustaining financial basis while providing a standard of
    service that will meet the needs of the people of Canada and that is
    similar with respect to communities of the same size;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} the need to conduct its operations in such manner
    as will best provide for the security of mail;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} the desirability of utilizing the human resources
    of the Corporation in a manner that will both attain the objects of
    the Corporation and ensure the commitment and dedication of its
    employees to the attainment of those objects; and

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} the need to maintain a corporate identity program
    approved by the Governor in Council that reflects the role of the
    Corporation as an institution of the Government of Canada.

::: HistoricalNote
- 1980-81-82-83, c. 54, s. 5
:::

### [Board of Directors]{.HTitleText2} {#h-59940 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Board of Directors

- **[[6]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-6 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} There
  shall be a Board of Directors of the Corporation consisting of the
  Chairperson, the President and nine other directors appointed as
  provided in this Act.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Appointment of directors

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Each director, other than the Chairperson and the
  President, shall be appointed by the Minister, with the approval of
  the Governor in Council, to hold office during pleasure for a term not
  exceeding four years that will ensure, as far as possible, the
  expiration in any one year of the terms of office of not more than one
  half of the directors.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Vacancy

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} Where the office of a director becomes vacant during
  the term of the director appointed thereto, the Governor in Council
  may appoint a director for the remainder of the term.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Re-appointment

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} A director may, on the expiration of his term of
  office, be re-appointed to the Board.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Fees

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} A director, other than the Chairperson, the President
  and any director selected from among persons employed in the federal
  public administration, is entitled to receive for attendance at
  meetings of the Board and any committee thereof such fees as may be
  fixed by the Governor in Council.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Expenses

  [(6)]{.lawlabel} A director is entitled to be paid by the Corporation
  reasonable travel and living expenses incurred by him while absent
  from his ordinary place of residence in the course of his duties as a
  director.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., 1985, c. C-10, s. 6
- 2003, c. 22, s. 224(E)
- 2006, c. 9, ss. 235, 236(E)

### [Chairperson of the Board]{.HTitleText2} {#h-59956 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Appointment of Chairperson of the Board

- **[[7]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-7 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  Chairperson of the Board shall be appointed by the Governor in Council
  to hold office during pleasure for such term as the Governor in
  Council considers appropriate.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Salary

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The Chairperson shall be paid by the Corporation such
  salary or other remuneration as is fixed by the Governor in Council.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Absence or incapacity

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} If the Chairperson is absent or unable to act or if
  the office of Chairperson is vacant, the Minister may designate
  another director of the Corporation to act as the Chairperson for the
  time being, but no director so designated has authority to act as
  Chairperson for a period exceeding ninety days without the approval of
  the Governor in Council.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., 1985, c. C-10, s. 7
- R.S., 1985, c. 1 (4th Supp.), s. 44(E)

### [President of the Corporation]{.HTitleText2} {#h-59966 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Appointment of President of the

- **[[8]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-8 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The

### [Exclusive Privilege of Corporation]{.HTitleText2} {#h-60016 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Exclusive privilege

- **[[14]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-14
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Subject to section 15, the
  Corporation has the sole and exclusive privilege of collecting,
  transmitting and delivering letters to the addressee thereof within
  Canada.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Other mail

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Nothing in this Act shall be construed as requiring
  any person to transmit by post any newspaper, magazine, book,
  catalogue or goods.

::: HistoricalNote
- 1980-81-82-83, c. 54, s. 14
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Exception

- **[[15]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-15 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  exclusive privilege referred to in subsection 14(1) does not apply to

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} letters carried incidentally and delivered to the
    addressee thereof by a friend of the sender or addressee;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} commissions, affidavits, writs, processes or
    proceedings issued by a court of justice;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} letters lawfully brought into Canada and forthwith
    posted thereafter;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} letters concerning goods for delivery therewith,
    carried by a common carrier without pay, reward, advantage or profit
    for so doing;

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} letters of an urgent nature that are transmitted by
    a messenger for a fee at least equal to an amount that is three
    times the regular rate of postage payable for delivery in Canada of
    similarly addressed letters weighing fifty grams;

  - [(f)]{.lawlabel} letters of any merchant or owner of a cargo vessel
    or the cargo therein that are carried by such vessel or by any
    employee of such merchant or owner and delivered to the addressee
    thereof without pay, reward, advantage or profit for so doing;

  - [(g)]{.lawlabel} letters concerning the affairs of an organization
    that are transmitted between offices of that organization by an
    employee thereof;

  - [(h)]{.lawlabel} letters in the course of transmission by any
    electronic or optical means; and

  - [(i)]{.lawlabel} letters transmitted by any naval, army or air
    forces of any foreign country that are in Canada with the consent of
    the Government of Canada.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Collection

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Nothing in subsection (1) shall be construed as
  authorizing any person to collect or receive any letters for the
  purpose of transmitting or delivering them as described in that
  subsection.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Letters to foreign addressees

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} The exclusive privilege referred to in subsection
  14(1) does not apply to letters intended for delivery to an addressee
  outside Canada.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., 1985, c. C-10, s. 15
- 2010, c. 12, s. 1885
:::

### [Powers]{.HTitleText2} {#h-60042 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Powers of Corporation

- **[[16]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-16 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} In
  carrying out its objects and duties under this Act, the Corporation
  has the capacity, and subject to this Act, the rights, powers and
  privileges of a natural person.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Property

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Without limiting the generality of subsection (1),
  the Corporation may acquire, hold, lease, sell or dispose of any real
  or personal property.

::: HistoricalNote
- 1980-81-82-83, c. 54, s. 16
- 1984, c. 31, s. 14
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Administration of oaths

**[[17]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-17 .sectionLabel}** The Corporation may, in
any case or class of cases approved by the Governor in Council, empower
any officer of the Corporation to administer oaths and take and receive
affidavits, declarations and affirmations for any purpose relating or
incidental to the administration or enforcement of this Act or the
regulations.

::: HistoricalNote
- 1980-81-82-83, c. 54, s. 18
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Postal inspectors

**[[18]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-18 .sectionLabel}** The Corporation may
designate any employee of the Corporation as a postal inspector with the
duty to provide for the security of the officers and employees of the
Corporation, the property of the Corporation and anything in the course
of post.

::: HistoricalNote
- 1980-81-82-83, c. 54, s. 19

### [Regulations]{.HTitleText2} {#h-60058 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Regulations

- **[[19]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-19 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  Corporation may, with the approval of the Governor in Council, make
  regulations for the efficient operation of the business of the
  Corporation and for carrying the purposes and provisions of this Act
  into effect, and, without restricting the generality of the foregoing,
  may make regulations

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} prescribing, for the purposes of this Act and the
    regulations, what is a letter and what is non-mailable matter and
    undeliverable mail, other than undeliverable letters, and providing
    for the disposition of non-mailable matter, undeliverable mail and
    mail on which sufficient postage is not paid, including the
    disposition of anything found therein;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} classifying mailable matter, including the setting
    of standards for any class thereof;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} prescribing the conditions under which mailable
    matter may be transmitted by post;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} prescribing rates of postage and the terms and
    conditions and method of payment thereof;

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} providing for the reduction of rates of postage on
    mailable matter prepared in the manner prescribed by the
    regulations;

  - [(f)]{.lawlabel} providing for the refund of postage;

  - [(g)]{.lawlabel} providing for the transmission by post, free of
    postage, of

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} letters, books, tapes, records and other similar
      material for the use of the blind, and

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} mailable matter relating solely to the business
      of the Corporation and addressed to or sent by a person engaged in

  Intelligence Service Act](/eng/acts/C-23), the [Customs
  Act](/eng/acts/C-52.6) and the [Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering)
  and Terrorist Financing Act](/eng/acts/P-24.501), nothing in the
  course of post is liable to demand, seizure, detention or retention.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., 1985, c. C-10, s. 40
- R.S., 1985, c. 1 (2nd Supp.), s. 170
- 2000, c. 17, s. 86
- 2001, c. 41, s. 77
- 2005, c. 38, ss. 142, 145

### [Inspection of Mail]{.HTitleText2} {#h-60298 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Inspection of mail

- **[[41]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-41 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  Corporation may open any mail, other than a letter, if it has
  reasonable grounds to suspect that

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the conditions prescribed by regulations made under
    paragraph 19(1)(c) have not been complied with;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the manner prescribed by regulations made under
    paragraph 19(1)(e) has not been adhered to; or

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} the mail is non-mailable matter.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Idem

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The Corporation may open any undeliverable mail,
  including any undeliverable letters.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., 1985, c. C-10, s. 41

### [Customs]{.HTitleText2} {#h-60309 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Mail to be made available to a customs
officer

- **[[42]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-42 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} All
  mail arriving in Canada from a place outside Canada that contains or
  is suspected to contain anything the importation of which is
  prohibited, controlled or regulated under the [Customs
  Act](/eng/acts/C-52.6) or any other Act of Parliament shall be
  submitted to a customs officer.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Submission of exported mail to customs
  officer

  [(1.1)]{.lawlabel} On request of a customs officer, all mail leaving
  Canada for a place outside Canada that contains or is suspected to
  contain anything the exportation of which is prohibited, controlled,
  regulated or subject to reporting under the [Customs
  Act](/eng/acts/C-52.6) or any other Act of Parliament shall be
  submitted by the Corporation to the customs officer.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Mail in the course of post

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} All mail that is submitted to a customs officer under
  this section remains, for the purposes of this Act, in the course of
  post unless it is seized under the [Customs Act](/eng/acts/C-52.6) or
  seized or retained under the [Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and
  Terrorist Financing Act](/eng/acts/P-24.501).

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Notice of seizure or detention

  [(2.1)]{.lawlabel} If mail is seized or detained under the [Customs
  Act](/eng/acts/C-52.6) or seized or retained under the [Proceeds of
  Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing
  Act](/eng/acts/P-24.501), notice of the seizure, detention or
  retention shall be given in writing to the Corporation within sixty
  days after the seizure, detention or retention unless the mail has,
  before the expiry of that time, been delivered to the addressee of the
  mail or returned to the Corporation.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Mail subject to customs laws

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} A customs officer shall deal with all mail submitted
  to the officer under this section in accordance with the laws relating
  to customs and the importation of goods or currency and monetary
  instruments and, subject to those laws, shall deliver that mail to its
  addressee, on payment of any postage due on it, or shall return it to
  the Corporation.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Non-mailable matter

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} Any non-mailable matter found by a customs officer in
  any mail made available to him under this section shall be dealt with
  in accordance with the regulations.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., 1985, c. C-10, s. 42
- R.S., 1985, c. 1 (2nd Supp.), s. 171
- 2000, c. 17, s. 87
- 2001, c. 25, s. 87, c. 41, s. 78
:::

### [Prohibitory Orders]{.HTitleText2} {#h-60325 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Interim prohibitory order

- **[[43]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-43 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Where
  the Minister believes on reasonable grounds that any person

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} is, by means of mail,

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} committing or attempting to commit an offence, or

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} aiding, abetting, counselling or procuring any
      other person to commit an offence,

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} with intent to commit an offence, is using mail to
    accomplish his object, or

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} is, by means other than mail, aiding, abetting,
    counselling or procuring any other person to commit an offence by
    means of mail,

  the Minister may make an order (in this section and in sections 44 to
  47 called an "interim prohibitory order") prohibiting the delivery,
  without the consent of the Minister, of mail addressed to or posted by
  that person (in this section and in sections 44 to 47 called the
  "person affected").

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Notice

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Within ten days after the making or reinstating of an
  interim prohibitory order, the person affected shall be sent, by
  registered mail at his latest known address, notice

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} of the order and the reasons therefor;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} that he may within ten days after the date the
    notice was sent, or such longer period as the Minister may allow,
    request in writing a review of the matter by a Board of Review; and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} of the provisions of sections 46 and 59.

::: HistoricalNote
- 1980-81-82-83, c. 54, s. 41
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Board of Review

- **[[44]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-44 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Where
  the Minister receives a request referred to in paragraph 43(2)(b)
  within the period stipulated therein, he shall appoint a Board of
  Review consisting of three members to review the matter and shall
  refer the material and evidence considered by him in making the
  interim prohibitory order to the Board.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Qualification

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} At least one member of a Board of Review shall be a
  person entitled to practise law in a province.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Idem

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} No director, officer, employee or agent of the
  Corporation is eligible to be appointed or to continue as a member of
  a Board of Review.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Powers of Board

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} A Board of Review has all the powers of a
  commissioner under Part I of the [Inquiries Act](/eng/acts/I-11).

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Opening of mail

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} A Board of Review, with the consent of the person
  affected, may open and examine any mail detained pursuant to
  subsection 47(3).

::: HistoricalNote
- 1980-81-82-83, c. 54, s. 41
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Review

- **[[45]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-45 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A
  Board of Review shall review the matter referred to it and for that
  purpose shall give to the person affected and any other person who has
  an interest in the matter a reasonable opportunity, in person or by
  counsel, to appear before the Board and to make representations and
  present evidence to the Board.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Adjournment

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} A Board of Review may from time to time adjourn any
  hearing before the Board on such terms and conditions as it sees fit.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Report

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} After reviewing the matter referred to it, a Board of
  Review shall submit a report with its recommendations to the Minister,
  together with all material and evidence that was before the Board,
  and, on receipt of the report, the Minister shall reconsider the
  interim prohibitory order and either revoke it unconditionally or on
  such terms and conditions as he sees fit or declare it to be a final
  prohibitory order.

::: HistoricalNote
- 1980-81-82-83, c. 54, s. 41
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Presumption

**[[46]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-46 .sectionLabel}** Where

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} the Minister does not receive a request referred to
  in paragraph 43(2)(b) within the period stipulated therein, or

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} a person affected has made a request referred to in
  paragraph 43(2)(b) within the period stipulated therein, but, in the
  opinion of the Board, fails to attend without reasonable excuse at the
  time and place set by the Board for any hearing, including any
  adjournment thereof, in relation thereto,

the interim prohibitory order shall be deemed to be a final prohibitory
order.

::: HistoricalNote
- 1980-81-82-83, c. 54, s. 41
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Revocation of order

- **[[47]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-47 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} If the
  Minister is satisfied that a person affected will not use mail for any
  of the purposes described in subsection 43(1), he may revoke an
  interim or final prohibitory order on such terms and conditions as he
  sees fit, including the payment of any costs incurred in connection
  with the Board of Review.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Reinstatement of order

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} If the Minister is satisfied that a person affected
  has not complied with any term or condition established pursuant to
  subsection (1) or 45(3), he may reinstate the interim or final
  prohibitory order.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Effect of order

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} Subject to subsection (4), while an interim or final
  prohibitory order is in effect, the Minister may

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} detain or return to the sender any mail addressed
    to, or anything posted by, the person affected; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} declare any mail detained pursuant to paragraph (a)
    to be undeliverable mail, and any mail so declared shall be dealt
    with in accordance with the regulations.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Idem

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} While an order that is deemed to be a final
  prohibitory order pursuant to section 46 is in effect, any mail
  detained pursuant to subsection (3) is deemed to be undeliverable mail
  and shall be dealt with in accordance with the regulations.

::: HistoricalNote
- 1980-81-82-83, c. 54, s. 41
:::

### [Offences and Punishment]{.HTitleText2} {#h-60384 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Opening mail

**[[48]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-48 .sectionLabel}** Every person commits an
offence who, except where expressly authorized by or under this Act, the
[Customs Act](/eng/acts/C-52.6) or the [Proceeds of Crime (Money
Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act](/eng/acts/P-24.501), knowingly
opens, keeps, secretes, delays or detains, or permits to be opened,
kept, secreted, delayed or detained, any mail bag or mail or any
receptacle or device authorized by the Corporation for the posting of
mail.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., 1985, c. C-10, s. 48
- R.S., 1985, c. 1 (2nd Supp.), s. 172
- 2000, c. 17, s. 88
- 2001, c. 41, s. 79
:::

::: PITLink
[Previous
Version](section-48-20021231.html "Link to previous version of section 48"){.PITLink}
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Abandonment of mail

**[[49]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-49 .sectionLabel}** Every person commits an
offence who unlawfully and knowingly abandons, misdirects, obstructs,
delays or detains the progress of any mail or mail conveyance.

::: HistoricalNote
- 1980-81-82-83, c. 54, s. 43
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Delay of mail

- **[[50]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-50 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Every
  person commits an offence who, without reasonable cause, refuses to
  permit or delays permitting any mail or mail conveyance to pass on or
  use any road, ferry or other route or mode of transport access to
  which is under his control.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Reasonable cause

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} For the purposes of subsection (1), collecting toll,
  ferriage or other charge for the use of any route or mode of transport
  is deemed not to be reasonable cause.

::: HistoricalNote
- 1980-81-82-83, c. 54, s. 44
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Dangerous substances

**[[51]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-51 .sectionLabel}** Except under and in
accordance with the [Transportation of Dangerous Goods Act,
1992](/eng/acts/T-19.01), every person commits an offence who encloses
in or with any mailable matter transmitted by post, or puts into any
post office, any explosive, dangerous or destructive substance or any
thing likely to injure persons or damage property.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., 1985, c. C-10, s. 51
- 1993, c. 34, s. 17
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Removal of stamp

- **[[52]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-52 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Every
  person commits an offence who, with fraudulent intent,

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} removes from mail any postage stamp affixed
    thereon; or

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} removes from any previously used postage stamp any
    mark or cancellation that has been made thereon at any post office.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Defacing of stamp

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Every person commits an offence who, without the
  written consent of the Corporation, defaces, distorts, overprints or
  otherwise modifies any postage stamp.

::: HistoricalNote
- 1980-81-82-83, c. 54, s. 46
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Destruction of records

**[[53]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-53 .sectionLabel}** Every person commits an
offence who knowingly secretes, destroys, mutilates, obliterates,
defaces, erases or changes any record or account of any transaction
pertaining to the business of the Corporation, or refuses to produce or
deliver such record or account to any duly authorized officer of the
Corporation on demand.

::: HistoricalNote
- 1980-81-82-83, c. 54, s. 47
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Issuance of postal remittances

**[[54]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-54 .sectionLabel}** Every person commits an
offence who issues any postal remittance or authorization therefor
without receiving, or ensuring in a manner authorized by the Corporation
that the Corporation will receive, the sum payable therefor.

::: HistoricalNote
- 1980-81-82-83, c. 54, s. 48
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Evading payment of postage

**[[55]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-55 .sectionLabel}** Every person commits an
offence who, for the purpose of evading payment of postage,

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} encloses a letter or any writing intended to serve
  the purpose of a letter in mail not paid at the rate of postage for
  letters;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} uses in payment of postage any previously used
  postage stamp; or

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} affixes on mailable matter anything suggesting that

  - [(i)]{.lawlabel} the mailable matter is entitled to be transmitted
    by post free of postage or at a lower rate of postage than that
    otherwise applicable thereto, or

  - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} the postage or any part thereof has been paid or
    ought to be paid by or charged to any other person.

::: HistoricalNote
- 1980-81-82-83, c. 54, s. 49
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Violation of exclusive privilege

**[[56]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-56 .sectionLabel}** Every person who, in
violation of the exclusive privilege of the Corporation under section
14, collects, transmits or delivers to the addressee thereof, or
undertakes to collect, transmit or deliver to the addressee thereof, any
letter within Canada, or receives or has in his possession within Canada
any letter for the purpose of so transmitting or delivering it, commits
an offence in respect of each such letter.

::: HistoricalNote
- 1980-81-82-83, c. 54, s. 50
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Sale of postage stamps

**[[57]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-57 .sectionLabel}** Every person commits an
offence who, without the consent of the Corporation, engages in the
business of selling postage stamps to the public for the purpose of
payment of postage.

::: HistoricalNote
- 1980-81-82-83, c. 54, s. 51
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Use of words "post office"

- **[[58]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-58 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Every
  person commits an offence who, without the written consent of the
  Corporation, places or permits or causes to be placed or to remain on
  his premises the words "post office" or any other word or mark
  suggesting that such premises are a post office or a place for the
  receipt of letters.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Use of postal marks

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Every person commits an offence who, without the
  written consent of the Corporation, places on any thing any word or
  mark suggesting that the thing

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} has been duly authorized or approved by the
    Corporation;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} is used in the business of the Corporation; or

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} is of a kind similar or identical to any thing used
    in the business of the Corporation.

::: HistoricalNote
- 1980-81-82-83, c. 54, s. 52
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Prohibitory orders

**[[59]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-59 .sectionLabel}** Every person commits an
offence who

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} knowingly does anything prohibited by an interim or
  final prohibitory order under subsection 43(1), 45(3) or 47(2);

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} changes his name or address to avoid compliance with
  an order referred to in paragraph (a); or

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} knowingly solicits mail while an order referred to in
  paragraph (a) is in effect.

::: HistoricalNote
- 1980-81-82-83, c. 54, s. 53
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Punishment

**[[60]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-60 .sectionLabel}** Every person who
contravenes any provision of this Act or the regulations or who commits
an offence under any of sections 48 to 59

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} is guilty of an indictable offence and liable to
  imprisonment for a term not exceeding five years; or

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} is guilty of an offence punishable on summary
  conviction.

::: HistoricalNote
- 1980-81-82-83, c. 54, s. 54
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Evidence

**[[61]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-61 .sectionLabel}** In any prosecution under
this Act, evidence that any thing bears the words "Post Office", "Canada
Post Office", "Canada Mail", "Canada Post", "Canada Post Corporation" or
any similar expression is evidence that the thing was established or
authorized for use in connection with the Corporation by this Act or the
regulations.

::: HistoricalNote
- 1980-81-82-83, c. 54, s. 55
:::
