## [The Courts]{.HTitleText1} {#h-239216 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Federal Court --- Appeal Division
continued

**[[3]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-3 .sectionLabel}** The division of the Federal
Court of Canada called the Federal Court --- Appeal Division is
continued under the name "Federal Court of Appeal" in English and "Cour
d'appel fédérale" in French. It is continued as an additional court of
law, equity and admiralty in and for Canada, for the better
administration of the laws of Canada and as a superior court of record
having civil and criminal jurisdiction.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., 1985, c. F-7, s. 3
- 1993, c. 34, s. 68(F)
- 2002, c. 8, s. 16

## [The Judges]{.HTitleText1} {#h-239225 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Constitution of Federal Court of Appeal

- **[[5]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-5 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  Federal Court of Appeal consists of a chief justice called the Chief
  Justice of the Federal Court of Appeal, who is the president of the
  Federal Court of Appeal, and 14 other judges.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Supernumerary judges

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} For each office of judge of the Federal Court of
  Appeal, there is an additional office of supernumerary judge that a
  judge of the Federal Court of Appeal may elect under the [Judges
  Act](/eng/acts/J-1) to hold.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Additional office of judge

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} For the office of Chief Justice of the Federal Court
  of Appeal, there is an additional office of judge that the Chief
  Justice may elect under the [Judges Act](/eng/acts/J-1) to hold.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Judges of Federal Court

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} Every judge of the Federal Court is, by virtue of his
  or her office, a judge of the Federal Court of Appeal and has all the
  jurisdiction, power and authority of a judge of the Federal Court of
  Appeal.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Constitution of Federal Court

- **[[5.1]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-5.1 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  Federal Court consists of a chief justice called the Chief Justice of

  Federal Court and 39 other judges.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Supernumerary judges

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Appointment of judges

**[[5.2]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-5.2 .sectionLabel}** The judges of the
Federal Court of Appeal and the Federal Court are to be appointed by the
Governor in Council by letters patent under the Great Seal.

::: HistoricalNote
- 2002, c. 8, s. 16
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Who may be appointed judge

**[[5.3]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-5.3 .sectionLabel}** A person may be
appointed a judge of the Federal Court of Appeal or the Federal Court if
the person

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} is or has been a judge of a superior, county or
  district court in Canada;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} is or has been a barrister or advocate of at least 10
  years standing at the bar of any province; or

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} has, for at least 10 years,

  - [(i)]{.lawlabel} been a barrister or advocate at the bar of any
    province, and

  - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} after becoming a barrister or advocate at the bar
    of any province, exercised powers and performed duties and functions
    of a judicial nature on a full-time basis in respect of a position
    held under a law of Canada or a province.

::: HistoricalNote
- 2002, c. 8, s. 16
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Judges from Quebec

**[[5.4]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-5.4 .sectionLabel}** At least five of the
judges of the Federal Court of Appeal and at least 10 of the judges of
the Federal Court must be persons who have been judges of the Court of
Appeal or of the Superior Court of the Province of Quebec, or have been
members of the bar of that Province.

::: HistoricalNote
- 2002, c. 8, s. 16
- 2006, c. 11, s. 20

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Rank and precedence

- **[[6]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-6 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  Chief Justices of the Federal Court of Appeal and the Federal Court
  and the other judges of those courts have rank and precedence among
  themselves in the following order:

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the Chief Justice of the Federal Court of Appeal;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the Chief Justice of the Federal Court;

  - [(b.1)]{.lawlabel} the Associate Chief Justice of the Federal Court;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} the other judges of the Federal Court of Appeal,
    according to seniority determined by reference to the respective
    times when they became judges of the Federal Court of Canada or the
    Federal Court of Appeal; and

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} the other judges of the Federal Court, according to
    seniority determined by reference to the respective times when they
    became judges of the Federal Court of Canada or the Federal Court.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Absence or incapacity of Chief Justice
  --- Federal Court of Appeal

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} If the office of Chief Justice of the Federal Court
  of Appeal is vacant, or the Chief Justice is absent from Canada or is
  for any reason unable to act, the powers and duties of the Chief
  Justice shall be exercised and performed by

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the judge of the Federal Court of Appeal who has
    been designated for that purpose by the Chief Justice of that Court;
    or

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} if no judge has been designated under paragraph
    (a), or if the judge so designated is absent from Canada or is
    unable or unwilling to act, the senior judge of that court who is in
    Canada and is able and willing to act and who has not elected to
    hold office as a supernumerary judge under section 28 of the [Judges
    Act](/eng/acts/J-1).

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Absence or incapacity of Chief Justice
  --- Federal Court

  [(2.1)]{.lawlabel} If the office of Chief Justice of the Federal Court
  is vacant, or the Chief Justice is absent from Canada or is for any
  reason unable to act, the powers and duties of the Chief Justice shall
  be exercised and performed

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} by the Associate Chief Justice of the Federal
    Court;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} if the office of Associate Chief Justice is vacant,
    or the Associate Chief Justice is absent from Canada or is for any
    reason unable to act, by a judge of that court designated by the
    Chief Justice for that purpose; or

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} if no judge has been designated under paragraph
    (b), or if the judge so designated is absent from Canada or is
    unable or unwilling to act, by the senior judge of that court who is
    in Canada and is able and willing to act and who has not elected to
    hold office as a supernumerary judge under section 28 of the [Judges
    Act](/eng/acts/J-1).

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Residence of judges

- **[[7]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-7 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  judges of the Federal Court of Appeal and the Federal Court shall
  reside in the National Capital Region described in the schedule to the
  [National Capital Act](/eng/acts/N-4) or within 40 kilometres of that
  Region.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Rota of judges

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Notwithstanding subsection (1), the Rules may provide
  for a rota of judges in order to ensure continuity of judicial
  availability in any centre where the volume of work or other
  circumstances make such an arrangement expedient.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}No judge to be away for more than one
  month

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} No judge shall be required under rules made under
  subsection (2) to remain in any centre other than the National Capital
  Region for a period longer than one month, unless it becomes necessary
  to do so to complete the hearing of a cause or matter.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Tenure of office

- **[[8]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-8 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Subject
  to subsection (2), the judges of the Federal Court of Appeal and the
  Federal Court hold office during good behaviour, but are removable by
  the Governor General on address of the Senate and House of Commons.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Cessation of office

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} A judge of the Federal Court of Appeal or the Federal
  Court ceases to hold office on becoming 75 years old.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Transitional

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} A judge who holds office on March 1, 1987 may retire
  at the age of seventy years.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Oath of office for judges

- **[[9]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-9 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Before
  judges of the Federal Court of Appeal or the Federal Court begin to
  perform their duties as judges, they shall take an oath that they will
  duly and faithfully execute their powers and trusts as judges to the
  best of their skill and knowledge.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}How administered --- Federal Court of

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The oath shall be administered to the Chief Justice

  other judges of that court by the Chief Justice of that court or, in
  the absence or incapacity of the Chief Justice, by any other judge of
  that court.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}How administered --- Federal Court

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} The oath shall be administered to the Chief Justice
  of the Federal Court before the Governor General, and to the other
  judges of that court by the Chief Justice of that court or, in the
  absence or incapacity of the Chief Justice, by any other judge of that
  court.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Deputy judges of the Federal Court of
Appeal

- **[[10]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-10
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Subject to subsection (3), any judge
  of a superior, county or district court in Canada, and any person who
  has held office as a judge of a superior, county or district court in
  Canada, may, at the request of the Chief Justice of the Federal Court
  of Appeal made with the approval of the Governor in Council, act as a
  judge of the Federal Court of Appeal, and while so acting has all the
  powers of a judge of that court and shall be referred to as a deputy
  judge of that court.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Deputy judges of the Federal Court

  [(1.1)]{.lawlabel} Subject to subsection (3), any judge of a superior,
  county or district court in Canada, and any person who has held office
  as a judge of a superior, county or district court in Canada, may, at
  the request of the Chief Justice of the Federal Court made with the
  approval of the Governor in Council, act as a judge of the Federal
  Court, and while so acting has all the powers of a judge of that court
  and shall be referred to as a deputy judge of that court.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Consent required

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} No request may be made under subsection (1) or (1.1)
  to a judge of a superior, county or district court in a province
  without the consent of the chief justice or chief judge of the court
  of which he or she is a member, or of the attorney general of the
  province.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Approval of Governor in Council

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} The Governor in Council may approve the making of
  requests under subsection (1) or (1.1) in general terms or for
  particular periods or purposes, and may limit the number of persons
  who may act under this section.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Salary of deputy judge

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} A person who acts as a judge of a court under
  subsection (1) or (1.1) shall be paid a salary for the period that the
  judge acts, at the rate fixed by the [Judges Act](/eng/acts/J-1) for a
  judge of the court other than the Chief Justice and the Associate
  Chief Justice of the court, less any amount otherwise payable to him
  or her under that Act in respect of that period, and shall also be
  paid the travel allowances that a judge is entitled to be paid under
  the [Judges Act](/eng/acts/J-1).

## [Barristers, Advocates, Attorneys and Solicitors]{.HTitleText1} {#h-239328 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Barrister or advocate

- **[[11]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-11 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Every
  person who is a barrister or an advocate in a province may practise as
  a barrister or an advocate in the Federal Court of Appeal or the
  Federal Court.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Attorney or solicitor

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Every person who is an attorney or a solicitor in a
  superior court of a province may practise as an attorney or a
  solicitor in the Federal Court of Appeal or the Federal Court.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Officers of court

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} Every person who may practise as a barrister, an
  advocate, an attorney or a solicitor in the Federal Court of Appeal or
  the Federal Court is an officer of that Court.

## [Associate Judges]{.HTitleText1} {#h-239338 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Associate judges

- **[[12]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-12 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  Governor in Council may appoint as associate judges of the Federal
  Court any fit and proper persons who are barristers or advocates in a
  province and who are, in the opinion of the Governor in Council,
  necessary for the efficient performance of the work of that court
  that, under the Rules, is to be performed by them.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Number of associate judges

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The Governor in Council may, by regulation, fix the
  number of associate judges that may be appointed under subsection (1).

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Supernumerary associate judges

  [(2.1)]{.lawlabel} For each office of associate judge of the Federal
  Court, there is an additional office of supernumerary associate judge
  that an associate judge of the Federal Court may elect under the
  [Judges Act](/eng/acts/J-1) to hold.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Powers and duties

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} The powers, duties and functions of the associate
  judges shall be determined by the Rules.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Salary, allowances and annuities

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} Each associate judge shall be paid a salary, and the
  allowances and annuities, provided for under the [Judges
  Act](/eng/acts/J-1).

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Workload --- supernumerary associate
  judges

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} The Governor in Council may, by regulation, fix the
  workload of a supernumerary associate judge as a percentage of the
  workload of an associate judge.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Immunity from liability

  [(6)]{.lawlabel} An associate judge shall have the same immunity from
  liability as a judge of the Federal Court.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Term of office

  [(7)]{.lawlabel} An associate judge shall hold office during good
  behaviour but may be removed by the Governor in Council for cause.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Cessation of office

  [(8)]{.lawlabel} An associate judge, whether appointed before or after
  the coming into force of this subsection, shall cease to hold office
  on becoming 75 years old.

## [Sheriffs and Marshals]{.HTitleText1} {#h-239356 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Sheriff

- **[[13]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-13 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  Governor in Council may appoint a sheriff of the Federal Court of
  Appeal and of the Federal Court for any geographical area.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}[Ex officio]{.otherLang lang="la"}
  sheriffs

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} If no sheriff is appointed under subsection (1) for a
  court for a geographical area, the sheriff and deputy sheriffs of the
  county or other judicial division or part of the county within that
  geographical area who are appointed under provincial law are [ex
  officio]{.otherLang lang="la"} sheriff and deputy sheriffs,
  respectively, of the Federal Court of Appeal and of the Federal Court.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Deputy sheriff

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} The Rules may provide for the appointment of deputy
  sheriffs.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Sheriffs are marshals

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} Every sheriff of the Federal Court of Appeal and of
  the Federal Court is [ex officio]{.otherLang lang="la"} a marshal of
  that court and every deputy sheriff of the Federal Court of Appeal and
  of the Federal Court is [ex officio]{.otherLang lang="la"} a deputy
  marshal of that court.

## [Judicial Administrators]{.HTitleText1} {#h-239368 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Designation

- **[[14]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-14 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  Chief Justice of the Federal Court of Appeal may designate an employee
  of the Service as the Judicial Administrator of the Federal Court of
  Appeal, and the Chief Justice of the Federal Court may designate an
  employee of the Courts Administration Service as the Judicial
  Administrator of the Federal Court.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Duties --- Federal Court of Appeal

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The Judicial Administrator of the Federal Court of
  Appeal shall perform any non-judicial work that may be delegated to
  him or her by the Chief Justice of that court, in accordance with the
  instructions given by the Chief Justice, including

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the making of an order fixing the time and place of
    a hearing, or adjourning a hearing;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} arranging for the distribution of judicial business
    in the court; and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} arranging from time to time for the establishment
    of any panels of judges of the court that are necessary.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Duties --- Federal Court

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} The Judicial Administrator of the Federal Court shall
  perform any non-judicial work that may be delegated to him or her by
  the Chief Justice of that court, in accordance with the instructions
  given by the Chief Justice, including

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the making of an order fixing the time and place of
    a trial or hearing, or adjourning a trial or hearing; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} arranging for the distribution of judicial business
    in the court.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Revocation of designation

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} A designation made under subsection (1) may be
  revoked at any time and is automatically revoked when the Chief
  Justice by whom it was made ceases to hold office as Chief Justice.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Sittings of the Federal Court of Appeal

- **[[16]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-16 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Except
  as otherwise provided in this Act or any other Act of Parliament,
  every appeal and every application for leave to appeal to the Federal
  Court of Appeal, and every application for judicial review or
  reference to that court, shall be heard in that court before not fewer
  than three judges sitting together and always before an uneven number
  of judges. Otherwise, the business of the Federal Court of Appeal
  shall be dealt with by such judge or judges as the Chief Justice of
  that court may arrange.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Arrangements to be made by Chief
  Justice of the Federal Court of Appeal

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The Chief Justice of the Federal Court of Appeal
  shall designate the judges to sit from time to time and the appeals or
  matters to be heard by them.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Place of sittings

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} The place of each sitting of the Federal Court of
  Appeal shall be arranged by the Chief Justice of that court to suit,
  as nearly as may be, the convenience of the parties.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}No judge to hear appeal from own
  judgment

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} A judge shall not sit on the hearing of an appeal
  from a judgment he or she has pronounced.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Chief Justice of Federal Court of
  Appeal to preside

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} The Chief Justice of the Federal Court of Appeal,
  when present at any sittings of that court, shall preside and, in the
  absence of the Chief Justice, the senior judge of that court who is
  present shall preside.

## [Jurisdiction of Federal Court]{.HTitleText1} {#h-239408 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Relief against the Crown

- **[[17]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-17 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Except
  as otherwise provided in this Act or any other Act of Parliament, the
  Federal Court has concurrent original jurisdiction in all cases in
  which relief is claimed against the Crown.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Cases

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Without restricting the generality of subsection (1),
  the Federal Court has concurrent original jurisdiction, except as
  otherwise provided, in all cases in which

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the land, goods or money of any person is in the
    possession of the Crown;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the claim arises out of a contract entered into by
    or on behalf of the Crown;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} there is a claim against the Crown for injurious
    affection; or

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} the claim is for damages under the [Crown Liability
    and Proceedings Act](/eng/acts/C-50).

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Crown and subject: consent to
  jurisdiction

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} The Federal Court has exclusive original jurisdiction
  to hear and determine the following matters:

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the amount to be paid if the Crown and any person
    have agreed in writing that the Crown or that person shall pay an
    amount to be determined by the Federal Court, the Federal Court ---
    Trial Division or the Exchequer Court of Canada; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} any question of law, fact or mixed law and fact
    that the Crown and any person have agreed in writing shall be
    determined by the Federal Court, the Federal Court --- Trial
    Division or the Exchequer Court of Canada.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Conflicting claims against Crown

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} The Federal Court has concurrent original
  jurisdiction to hear and determine proceedings to determine disputes
  in which the Crown is or may be under an obligation and in respect of
  which there are or may be conflicting claims.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Relief in favour of Crown or against
  officer

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} The Federal Court has concurrent original
  jurisdiction

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} in proceedings of a civil nature in which the Crown
    or the Attorney General of Canada claims relief; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} in proceedings in which relief is sought against
    any person for anything done or omitted to be done in the
    performance of the duties of that person as an officer, servant or
    agent of the Crown.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Federal Court has no jurisdiction

  [(6)]{.lawlabel} If an Act of Parliament confers jurisdiction in
  respect of a matter on a court constituted or established by or under
  a law of a province, the Federal Court has no jurisdiction to
  entertain any proceeding in respect of the same matter unless the Act
  expressly confers that jurisdiction on that court.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Application for judicial review

- **[[18.1]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-18.1 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} An
  application for judicial review may be made by the Attorney General of
  Canada or by anyone directly affected by the matter in respect of
  which relief is sought.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Time limitation

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} An application for judicial review in respect of a
  decision or an order of a federal board, commission or other tribunal
  shall be made within 30 days after the time the decision or order was
  first communicated by the federal board, commission or other tribunal
  to the office of the Deputy Attorney General of Canada or to the party
  directly affected by it, or within any further time that a judge of
  the Federal Court may fix or allow before or after the end of those 30
  days.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Powers of Federal Court

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} On an application for judicial review, the Federal
  Court may

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} order a federal board, commission or other tribunal
    to do any act or thing it has unlawfully failed or refused to do or
    has unreasonably delayed in doing; or

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} declare invalid or unlawful, or quash, set aside or
    set aside and refer back for determination in accordance with such
    directions as it considers to be appropriate, prohibit or restrain,
    a decision, order, act or proceeding of a federal board, commission
    or other tribunal.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Grounds of review

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} The Federal Court may grant relief under
  subsection (3) if it is satisfied that the federal board, commission
  or other tribunal

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} acted without jurisdiction, acted beyond its
    jurisdiction or refused to exercise its jurisdiction;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} failed to observe a principle of natural justice,
    procedural fairness or other procedure that it was required by law
    to observe;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} erred in law in making a decision or an order,
    whether or not the error appears on the face of the record;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} based its decision or order on an erroneous finding
    of fact that it made in a perverse or capricious manner or without
    regard for the material before it;

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} acted, or failed to act, by reason of fraud or
    perjured evidence; or

  - [(f)]{.lawlabel} acted in any other way that was contrary to law.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Defect in form or technical
  irregularity

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} If the sole ground for relief established on an
  application for judicial review is a defect in form or a technical
  irregularity, the Federal Court may

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} refuse the relief if it finds that no substantial
    wrong or miscarriage of justice has occurred; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} in the case of a defect in form or a technical
    irregularity in a decision or an order, make an order validating the
    decision or order, to have effect from any time and on any terms
    that it considers appropriate.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Navigation and shipping

- **[[22]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-22 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  Federal Court has concurrent original jurisdiction, between subject
  and subject as well as otherwise, in all cases in which a claim for
  relief is made or a remedy is sought under or by virtue of Canadian
  maritime law or any other law of Canada relating to any matter coming
  within the class of subject of navigation and shipping, except to the
  extent that jurisdiction has been otherwise specially assigned.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Maritime jurisdiction

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Without limiting the generality of subsection (1),
  for greater certainty, the Federal Court has jurisdiction with respect
  to all of the following:

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} any claim with respect to title, possession or
    ownership of a ship or any part interest therein or with respect to
    the proceeds of sale of a ship or any part interest therein;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} any question arising between co-owners of a ship
    with respect to possession, employment or earnings of a ship;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} any claim in respect of a mortgage or hypothecation
    of, or charge on, a ship or any part interest therein or any charge
    in the nature of bottomry or respondentia for which a ship or part
    interest therein or cargo was made security;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} any claim for damage or for loss of life or
    personal injury caused by a ship either in collision or otherwise;

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} any claim for damage sustained by, or for loss of,
    a ship including, without restricting the generality of the
    foregoing, damage to or loss of the cargo or equipment of, or any
    property in or on or being loaded on or off, a ship;

  - [(f)]{.lawlabel} any claim arising out of an agreement relating to
    the carriage of goods on a ship under a through bill of lading, or
    in respect of which a through bill of lading is intended to be
    issued, for loss or damage to goods occurring at any time or place
    during transit;

  - [(g)]{.lawlabel} any claim for loss of life or personal injury
    occurring in connection with the operation of a ship including,
    without restricting the generality of the foregoing, any claim for
    loss of life or personal injury sustained in consequence of any
    defect in a ship or in her apparel or equipment, or of the wrongful
    act, neglect or default of the owners, charterers or persons in
    possession or control of a ship or of the master or crew thereof or
    of any other person for whose wrongful acts, neglects or defaults
    the owners, charterers or persons in possession or control of the
    ship are responsible, being an act, neglect or default in the
    management of the ship, in the loading, carriage or discharge of
    goods on, in or from the ship or in the embarkation, carriage or
    disembarkation of persons on, in or from the ship;

  - [(h)]{.lawlabel} any claim for loss of or damage to goods carried in
    or on a ship including, without restricting the generality of the
    foregoing, loss of or damage to passengers' baggage or personal
    effects;

  - [(i)]{.lawlabel} any claim arising out of any agreement relating to
    the carriage of goods in or on a ship or to the use or hire of a
    ship whether by charter party or otherwise;

  - [(j)]{.lawlabel} any claim for salvage including, without
    restricting the generality of the foregoing, claims for salvage of
    life, cargo, equipment or other property of, from or by an aircraft
    to the same extent and in the same manner as if the aircraft were a
    ship;

  - [(k)]{.lawlabel} any claim for towage in respect of a ship or of an
    aircraft while the aircraft is water-borne;

  - [(l)]{.lawlabel} any claim for pilotage in respect of a ship or of
    an aircraft while the aircraft is water-borne;

  - [(m)]{.lawlabel} any claim in respect of goods, materials or
    services wherever supplied to a ship for the operation or
    maintenance of the ship, including, without restricting the
    generality of the foregoing, claims in respect of stevedoring and
    lighterage;

  - [(n)]{.lawlabel} any claim arising out of a contract relating to the
    construction, repair or equipping of a ship;

  - [(o)]{.lawlabel} any claim by a master, officer or member of the
    crew of a ship for wages, money, property or other remuneration or
    benefits arising out of his or her employment;

  - [(p)]{.lawlabel} any claim by a master, charterer or agent of a ship
    or shipowner in respect of disbursements, or by a shipper in respect
    of advances, made on account of a ship;

  - [(q)]{.lawlabel} any claim in respect of general average
    contribution;

  - [(r)]{.lawlabel} any claim arising out of or in connection with a
    contract of marine insurance; and

  - [(s)]{.lawlabel} any claim for dock charges, harbour dues or canal
    tolls including, without restricting the generality of the
    foregoing, charges for the use of facilities supplied in connection
    therewith.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Jurisdiction applicable

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} For greater certainty, the jurisdiction conferred on
  the Federal Court by this section applies

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} in relation to all ships, whether Canadian or not
    and wherever the residence or domicile of the owners may be;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} in relation to all aircraft where the cause of
    action arises out of paragraphs (2)(j) to (l), whether those
    aircraft are Canadian or not and wherever the residence or domicile
    of the owners may be;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} in relation to all claims, whether arising on the
    high seas, in Canadian waters or elsewhere and whether those waters
    are naturally navigable or artificially made so, including, without
    restricting the generality of the foregoing, in the case of salvage,
    claims in respect of cargo or wreck found on the shores of those
    waters; and

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} in relation to all mortgages or hypothecations of,
    or charges by way of security on, a ship, whether registered or not,
    or whether legal or equitable, and whether created under foreign law
    or not.

## [Jurisdiction of Federal Court of Appeal]{.HTitleText1} {#h-239550 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Appeals from Federal Court

- **[[27]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-27 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} An
  appeal lies to the Federal Court of Appeal from any of the following
  decisions of the Federal Court:

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} a final judgment;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} a judgment on a question of law determined before
    trial;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} an interlocutory judgment; or

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} a determination on a reference made by a federal
    board, commission or other tribunal or the Attorney General of
    Canada.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Appeals from Tax Court of Canada,
  except from informal procedure

  [(1.1)]{.lawlabel} An appeal lies to the Federal Court of Appeal from

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} a final judgment of the Tax Court of Canada, other
    than one in respect of which section 18, 18.29, 18.3 or 18.3001 of
    the [Tax Court of Canada Act](/eng/acts/T-2) applies;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} a judgment of the Tax Court of Canada, other than
    one in respect of which section 18, 18.29, 18.3 or 18.3001 of the
    [Tax Court of Canada Act](/eng/acts/T-2) applies, on a question of
    law determined before trial; or

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} an interlocutory judgment or order of the Tax Court
    of Canada, other than one in respect of which section 18, 18.29,
    18.3 or 18.3001 of the [Tax Court of Canada Act](/eng/acts/T-2)
    applies.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Appeals from informal procedure in Tax
  Court of Canada

  [(1.2)]{.lawlabel} An appeal lies to the Federal Court of Appeal from
  a final judgment of the Tax Court of Canada in respect of which
  section 18, 18.29, 18.3 or 18.3001 of the [Tax Court of Canada
  Act](/eng/acts/T-2) applies.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Grounds for appeal

  [(1.3)]{.lawlabel} The only grounds for an appeal under subsection
  (1.2) are that the Tax Court of Canada

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} acted without jurisdiction, acted beyond its
    jurisdiction or refused to exercise its jurisdiction;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} failed to observe a principle of natural justice,
    procedural fairness or other procedure that it was required by law
    to observe;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} erred in law in making a decision or an order,
    whether or not the error appears on the face of the record;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} based its decision or order on an erroneous finding
    of fact that it made in a perverse or capricious manner or without
    regard for the material before it;

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} acted, or failed to act, by reason of fraud or
    perjured evidence; or

  - [(f)]{.lawlabel} acted in any other way that was contrary to law.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Hearing in summary way

  [(1.4)]{.lawlabel} An appeal under subsection (1.2) shall be heard and
  determined without delay and in a summary way.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Notice of appeal

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} An appeal under this section shall be brought by
  filing a notice of appeal in the Registry of the Federal Court of
  Appeal

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} in the case of an interlocutory judgment, within 10
    days after the pronouncement of the judgment or within any further
    time that a judge of the Federal Court of Appeal may fix or allow
    before or after the end of those 10 days; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} in any other case, within 30 days, not including
    any days in July and August, after the pronouncement of the judgment
    or determination appealed from or within any further time that a
    judge of the Federal Court of Appeal may fix or allow before or
    after the end of those 30 days.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Service

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} All parties directly affected by an appeal under this
  section shall be served without delay with a true copy of the notice
  of appeal, and evidence of the service shall be filed in the Registry
  of the Federal Court of Appeal.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Final judgment

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} For the purposes of this section, a final judgment
  includes a judgment that determines a substantive right except as to
  any question to be determined by a referee pursuant to the judgment.

## [Substantive Provisions]{.HTitleText1} {#h-239616 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Prejudgment interest --- cause of action
within province

- **[[36]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-36 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Except
  as otherwise provided in any other Act of Parliament, and subject to
  subsection (2), the laws relating to prejudgment interest in
  proceedings between subject and subject that are in force in a
  province apply to any proceedings in the Federal Court of Appeal or
  the Federal Court in respect of any cause of action arising in that
  province.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Prejudgment interest --- cause of
  action outside province

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} A person who is entitled to an order for the payment
  of money in respect of a cause of action arising outside a province or
  in respect of causes of action arising in more than one province is
  entitled to claim and have included in the order an award of interest
  on the payment at any rate that the Federal Court of Appeal or the
  Federal Court considers reasonable in the circumstances, calculated

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} where the order is made on a liquidated claim, from
    the date or dates the cause of action or causes of action arose to
    the date of the order; or

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} where the order is made on an unliquidated claim,
    from the date the person entitled gave notice in writing of the
    claim to the person liable therefor to the date of the order.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Interest on special damages

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} Where an order referred to in subsection (2) includes
  an amount for special damages, the interest shall be calculated under
  that subsection on the balance of special damages incurred as totalled
  at the end of each six month period following the notice in writing
  referred to in paragraph (2)(b) and at the date of the order.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Exceptions

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} Interest shall not be awarded under subsection (2)

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} on exemplary or punitive damages;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} on interest accruing under this section;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} on an award of costs in the proceeding;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} on that part of the order that represents pecuniary
    loss arising after the date of the order and that is identified by a
    finding of the Federal Court of Appeal or the Federal Court;

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} where the order is made on consent, except by
    consent of the debtor; or

  - [(f)]{.lawlabel} where interest is payable by a right other than
    under this section.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Judicial discretion

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} The Federal Court of Appeal or the Federal Court may,
  if it considers it just to do so, having regard to changes in market
  interest rates, the conduct of the proceedings or any other relevant
  consideration, disallow interest or allow interest for a period other
  than that provided for in subsection (2) in respect of the whole or
  any part of the amount on which interest is payable under this
  section.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Application

  [(6)]{.lawlabel} This section applies in respect of the payment of
  money under judgment delivered on or after the day on which this
  section comes into force, but no interest shall be awarded for a
  period before that day.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Canadian maritime law

  [(7)]{.lawlabel} This section does not apply in respect of any case in
  which a claim for relief is made or a remedy is sought under or by
  virtue of Canadian maritime law.

  paragraph 22(2)(e), (f), (g), (h), (i), (k), (m), (n), (p) or (r)
  unless, at the time of the commencement of the action, the ship,
  aircraft or other property that is the subject of the action is
  beneficially owned by the person who was the beneficial owner at the
  time when the cause of action arose.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Where action [in personam]{.otherLang
  lang="la"} may be commenced

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} No action [in personam]{.otherLang lang="la"} may be
  commenced in Canada for a collision between ships unless

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the defendant is a person who has a residence or
    place of business in Canada;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the cause of action arose in Canadian waters; or

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} the parties have agreed that the Federal Court is
    to have jurisdiction.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Exception

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} Subsection (4) does not apply to a counter-claim or
  an action for a collision, in respect of which another action has
  already been commenced in the Federal Court.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Where suit pending outside Canada

  [(6)]{.lawlabel} Where an action for a collision between ships has
  been commenced outside Canada, an action shall not be commenced in
  Canada by the same person against the same defendant on the same facts
  unless the action in the other jurisdiction has been discontinued.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Ship owned by sovereign power

  [(7)]{.lawlabel} No action [in rem]{.otherLang lang="la"} may be
  commenced in Canada against

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} any warship, coast-guard ship or police vessel;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} any ship owned or operated by Canada or a province,
    or any cargo laden thereon, where the ship is engaged on government
    service; or

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} any ship owned or operated by a sovereign power
    other than Canada, or any cargo laden thereon, with respect to any
    claim where, at the time the claim arises or the action is
    commenced, the ship is being used exclusively for non-commercial
    governmental purposes.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Arrest

  [(8)]{.lawlabel} The jurisdiction conferred on the Federal Court by
  section 22 may be exercised [in rem]{.otherLang lang="la"} against any
  ship that, at the time the action is brought, is owned by the
  beneficial owner of the ship that is the subject of the action.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Reciprocal security

  [(9)]{.lawlabel} In an action for a collision in which a ship, an
  aircraft or other property of a defendant has been arrested, or
  security has been given to answer judgment against the defendant, and
  in which the defendant has instituted a cross-action or counter-claim
  in which a ship, an aircraft or other property of the plaintiff is
  liable to arrest but cannot be arrested, the Federal Court may stay
  the proceedings in the principal action until security has been given
  to answer judgment in the cross-action or counter-claim.

## [Procedure]{.HTitleText1} {#h-239707 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Giving of judgment after judge ceases to
hold office

- **[[45]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-45 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A
  judge of the Federal Court of Appeal or the Federal Court who resigns
  or is appointed to another court or otherwise ceases to hold office
  may, at the request of the Chief Justice of that court, at any time
  within eight weeks after that event, give judgment in any cause,
  action or matter previously tried by or heard before the judge as if
  he or she had continued in office.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Taking part in giving of judgment after
  judge of Federal Court of Appeal ceases to hold office

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} If a judge of the Federal Court of Appeal who resigns
  or is appointed to another court or otherwise ceases to hold office
  has heard a cause, an action or a matter in the Federal Court of
  Appeal jointly with other judges of that court, the judge may, at the
  request of the Chief Justice of the Federal Court of Appeal, at any
  time within eight weeks after the resignation, appointment or other
  ceasing to hold office, take part in the giving of judgment by that
  court as if he or she had continued in office.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}If judge unable to take part in giving
  of judgment

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} If a person to whom subsection (2) applies or any
  other judge by whom a matter in the Federal Court of Appeal has been
  heard is unable to take part in the giving of judgment or has died,
  the remaining judges may give judgment and, for that purpose, are
  deemed to constitute the Federal Court of Appeal.

- **[[55]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-55 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The

  with respect to like process to issue out of that court.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Process against person

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} No person shall be taken into custody under process
  of execution for debt issued out of the Federal Court of Appeal or the
  Federal Court.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Process against property

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} All writs of execution or other process against
  property, whether prescribed by the Rules or authorized by subsection
  (1), shall

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} unless otherwise provided by the Rules, be
    executed, with respect to the property liable to execution and the
    mode of seizure and sale, as nearly as possible in the same manner
    as similar writs or process that are issued out of the superior
    courts of the province in which the property to be seized is
    situated are, by the law of that province, required to be executed;
    and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} bind property in the same manner as similar writs
    or process issued by the provincial superior courts, and the rights
    of purchasers under the writs or process are the same as those of
    purchasers under those similar writs or process.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Claim against property seized

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} Every claim made by a person to property seized under
  a writ of execution or other process issued out of the Federal Court
  of Appeal or the Federal Court, or to the proceeds of its sale, shall,
  unless otherwise provided by the Rules, be heard and disposed of as
  nearly as may be according to the procedure applicable to like claims
  to property seized under similar writs or process issued out of the
  courts of the provinces.

## [General]{.HTitleText1} {#h-239872 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Constitutional questions

- **[[57]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-57 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} If the
  constitutional validity, applicability or operability of an Act of
  Parliament or of the legislature of a province, or of regulations made
  under such an Act, is in question before the Federal Court of Appeal
  or the Federal Court or a federal board, commission or other tribunal,
  other than a [[court martial]{.dfn}]{.DefinitionRef} and an
  [[officer]{.dfn}]{.DefinitionRef} conducting a [[summary
  hearing]{.dfn}]{.DefinitionRef}, as defined in subsection 2(1) of the
  [National Defence Act](/eng/acts/N-5), the Act or regulation shall not
  be judged to be invalid, inapplicable or inoperable unless notice has
  been served on the Attorney General of Canada and the attorney general
  of each province in accordance with subsection (2).

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Time of notice

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The notice must be served at least 10 days before the
  day on which the constitutional question is to be argued, unless the
  Federal Court of Appeal or the Federal Court or the federal board,
  commission or other tribunal, as the case may be, orders otherwise.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Notice of appeal or application for
  judicial review

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} The Attorney General of Canada and the attorney
  general of each province are entitled to notice of any appeal or
  application for judicial review made in respect of the constitutional
  question.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Right to be heard

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} The Attorney General of Canada and the attorney
  general of each province are entitled to adduce evidence and make
  submissions to the Federal Court of Appeal or the Federal Court or the
  federal board, commission or other tribunal, as the case may be, in
  respect of the constitutional question.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Appeal

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} If the Attorney General of Canada or the attorney
  general of a province makes submissions, that attorney general is
  deemed to be a party to the proceedings for the purpose of any appeal
  in respect of the constitutional question.

- --- 2021, c. 23, s. 240

  - ###### Time limit for appeals --- summary dismissal {#time-limit-for-appeals-summary-dismissal .MarginalNote}

    - [240]{.lawlabel} [(1)]{.lawlabel} An appeal of a decision made by
      the General Division under section 53 of the former Act must be
      brought to the Appeal Division within 90 days after the day on
      which this Division comes into force. Despite subsection 56(1) of
      the new Act, no leave is necessary.

    - ###### Appeals --- summary dismissal {#appeals-summary-dismissal .MarginalNote}

      [(2)]{.lawlabel} An appeal of a decision made by the General
      Division under section 53 of the former Act that is ongoing on the
      day on which this Division comes into force, or that is referred
      to in subsection (1), is to be dealt with by the Appeal Division
      in accordance with subsections 58(1) and 59(1) of the former Act.
      Section 58.3 of the new Act does not apply in respect of the
      appeal.

    - ###### Federal Court {#federal-court-1 .MarginalNote}

      [(3)]{.lawlabel} Despite paragraph 28(1)(g.1) of the [Federal
      Courts Act](/eng/acts/F-7), the Federal Court has jurisdiction to
      hear and determine applications for judicial review of a decision
      of the Appeal Division that relates to an appeal brought under
      subsection 53(3) of the former Act or that is referred to in
      subsection (1).

    - ###### Judicial review {#judicial-review .MarginalNote}

      [(4)]{.lawlabel} If, following a judicial review referred to in
      subsection (3), the Federal Court refers the matter back to the
      Appeal Division, the matter is to be dealt with by the Appeal
      Division in accordance with subsections 58(1) and 59(1) of the
      former Act. Section 58.3 of the new Act does not apply in respect
      of the matter.

- --- 2021, c. 23, s. 241

  - ###### Applications under section 66 of former Act {#applications-under-section-66-of-former-act .MarginalNote}

    - [241]{.lawlabel} [(1)]{.lawlabel} An application under section 66
      of the former Act that is ongoing before the General Division or
      Appeal Division on the day on which this Division comes into force
      is to be dealt with in accordance with that section 66.

    - ###### Referral back to General Division {#referral-back-to-general-division .MarginalNote}

      [(2)]{.lawlabel} If, following an appeal of a decision made by the
      General Division under section 66 of the former Act, the Appeal
      Division refers the matter back to the General Division, the
      matter is to be dealt with in accordance with that section 66.

    - ###### Applications for leave to appeal --- decision under section 66 of former Act {#applications-for-leave-to-appeal-decision-under-section-66-of-former-act .MarginalNote}

      [(3)]{.lawlabel} An application for leave to appeal a decision
      made by the General Division under section 66 of the former Act is
      to be dealt with by the Appeal Division in accordance with
      subsections 58(1) and (2) of the former Act.

    - ###### Appeals --- decisions under section 66 of former Act {#appeals-decisions-under-section-66-of-former-act .MarginalNote}

      [(4)]{.lawlabel} If leave to appeal is granted, the appeal is to
      be dealt with in accordance with subsections 58(1) and 59(1) of
      the former Act and, in the case where the Appeal Division decides
      under that subsection 59(1) to give the decision that the General
      Division should have given, the appeal is also to be dealt with in
      accordance with section 66 of the former Act. Section 58.3 of the
      new Act does not apply in respect of the appeal.

    - ###### Judicial review --- leave to appeal {#judicial-review-leave-to-appeal .MarginalNote}

      [(5)]{.lawlabel} If, following a judicial review of a decision of
      the Appeal Division referred to in subsection (3), the Federal
      Court refers the matter back to the Appeal Division, the matter is
      to be dealt with by the Appeal Division in accordance with
      subsections 58(1) and (2) of the former Act.

    - ###### Judicial review --- appeals {#judicial-review-appeals .MarginalNote}

      [(6)]{.lawlabel} If, following a judicial review of a decision of
      the Appeal Division referred to in subsection (4), the Federal
      Court of Appeal refers the matter back to the Appeal Division, the
      matter is to be dealt with by the Appeal Division in accordance
      with subsections 58(1) and 59(1) of the former Act and, in the
      case where the Appeal Division decides under that subsection 59(1)
      to give the decision that the General Division should have given,
      the matter is also to be dealt with in accordance with section 66
      of the former Act. Section 58.3 of the new Act does not apply in
      respect of the matter.

      the day on which this Division comes into force, the Federal Court
