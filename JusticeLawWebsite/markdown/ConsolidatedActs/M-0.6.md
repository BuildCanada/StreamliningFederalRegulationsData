An Act respecting marine insurance

## [Short Title]{.HTitleText1} {#h-348803 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Short title

**[[1]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-1 .sectionLabel}** This Act may be cited as
the [Marine Insurance Act](/eng/acts/M-0.6).

## [Interpretation and Application]{.HTitleText1} {#h-348806 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Definitions

- **[[2]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-2 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} In this
  Act,

  [[action]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[action]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} includes a counterclaim and a
      set-off; ([action]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

  [[contract]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[contract]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means a contract of marine
      insurance as described in subsection
      6(1); ([contrat]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

  [[freight]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[freight]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} includes the profit derivable by a
      shipowner from the use of the shipowner's ship to carry the
      shipowner's goods or movables and freight payable by a third
      party, but does not include passenger
      fares; ([fret]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

  [[goods]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[goods]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means goods in the nature of
      merchandise, but does not include personal effects or provisions
      or stores for use on board a
      ship; ([marchandises]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

  [[insurable property]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[insurable property]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means any ship, goods
      or movables; ([bien assurable]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

  [[marine adventure]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[marine adventure]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means any situation where
      insurable property is exposed to maritime perils, and includes any
      situation where

      - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the earning or acquisition of any freight,
        commission, profit or other pecuniary benefit, or the security
        for any advance, loan or disbursement, is endangered by the
        exposure of insurable property to maritime perils, and

      - [(b)]{.lawlabel} any liability to a third party may be incurred
        by the owner of, or other person interested in or responsible
        for, insurable property, by reason of maritime
        perils; ([opérations maritimes]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

  [[marine policy]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[marine policy]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means the instrument
      evidencing a contract; ([police maritime]{.DefinedTermLink
      lang="fr"})

  [[maritime perils]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[maritime perils]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means the perils
      consequent on or incidental to navigation, including perils of the
      seas, fire, war perils, acts of pirates or thieves, captures,
      seizures, restraints, detainments of princes and peoples,
      jettisons, barratry and all other perils of a like kind and, in
      respect of a marine policy, any peril designated by the
      policy; ([périls de mer]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

  [[movable]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[movable]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means any movable tangible
      property, other than a ship or goods, and includes money, valuable
      securities and other documents; ([bien mobilier]{.DefinedTermLink
      lang="fr"})

  [[ship]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[ship]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} includes the hull, machinery,
      materials and outfit and the stores and provisions for the
      officers and crew and also includes fuel, oils and engine stores,
      if they are owned by the insured, and, in the case of a ship
      engaged in a special trade, the ordinary fittings required for the
      trade. ([navire]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Other terms

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The following terms have the meanings assigned by the
  provisions indicated beside them:

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} actual total loss, subsection 56(1);

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} constructive total loss, section 57;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} general average act, subsection 65(2);

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} general average contribution, subsection 65(3);

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} general average expenditure, subsection 65(2);

  - [(f)]{.lawlabel} general average loss, subsection 65(1);

  - [(g)]{.lawlabel} general average sacrifice, subsection 65(2);

  - [(h)]{.lawlabel} particular average loss, subsection 63(1);

  - [(i)]{.lawlabel} particular charges, subsection 63(2);

  - [(j)]{.lawlabel} salvage charges, subsection 64(1);

  - [(k)]{.lawlabel} time policy, subsection 29(3);

  - [(l)]{.lawlabel} unvalued policy, subsection 30(3);

  - [(m)]{.lawlabel} valued policy, subsection 30(2); and

  - [(n)]{.lawlabel} voyage policy, subsection 29(2).

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Construction of marine policies

**[[3]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-3 .sectionLabel}** Subject to this Act and
unless a contrary intention appears, the words and terms set out in the
schedule have, when used in a marine policy, the meanings assigned by
the schedule.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Rules of Canadian maritime law

**[[4]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-4 .sectionLabel}** The rules of Canadian
maritime law continue to apply in respect of contracts, except in so far
as the rules are inconsistent with this Act.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Application

**[[5]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-5 .sectionLabel}** This Act applies in respect
of contracts concluded on or after the coming into force of this Act.

## [Contract of Marine Insurance]{.HTitleText1} {#h-348844 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Contract of marine insurance

- **[[6]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-6 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A
  contract of marine insurance is a contract whereby the insurer
  undertakes to indemnify the insured, in the manner and to the extent
  agreed in the contract, against

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} losses that are incidental to a marine adventure or
    an adventure analogous to a marine adventure, including losses
    arising from a land or air peril incidental to such an adventure if
    they are provided for in the contract or by usage of the trade; or

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} losses that are incidental to the building, repair
    or launch of a ship.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Coverage

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Subject to this Act, any lawful marine adventure may
  be the subject of a contract.

## [Insurable Interest]{.HTitleText1} {#h-348852 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Insurable interest required

- **[[7]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-7 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} In order
  to recover under a contract for a loss, the insured must have an
  insurable interest in the subject-matter insured at the time of the
  loss, but need not have such an interest when the contract is
  concluded.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}"Lost or not lost" insurance

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Notwithstanding subsection (1), where the
  subject-matter is insured "lost or not lost", the insured may recover
  in respect of an insurable interest in the subject-matter acquired
  after a loss unless, at the time the contract was concluded, the
  insured was aware of the loss and the insurer was not.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Where no interest

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} An insured who has no insurable interest in the
  subject-matter insured at the time of a loss cannot acquire an
  insurable interest by any act or election after becoming aware of the
  loss.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Insurable interest - general principle

- **[[8]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-8 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Subject
  to this Act, a person who has an interest in a marine adventure has an
  insurable interest.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Interest in marine adventure

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} A person has an interest in a marine adventure if the
  person has a legal or equitable relation to the adventure, or to any
  insurable property at risk in the adventure, and may benefit from the
  safety or due arrival of insurable property, may be prejudiced by its
  loss, damage or detention or may incur liability in respect of it.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Defeasible or contingent interests

- **[[9]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-9 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A
  defeasible interest and a contingent interest are insurable interests.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Buyer of goods

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} A buyer of goods who has insured them has an
  insurable interest even though the buyer might have elected to reject
  the goods or to treat them as at the seller's risk for any reason,
  including a delay in delivering them.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Partial interest

**[[10]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-10 .sectionLabel}** A partial interest of any
nature is an insurable interest.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Master and crew's wages

**[[11]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-11 .sectionLabel}** The master and any member
of the crew of a ship have insurable interests in their own wages.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Advance freight

**[[12]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-12 .sectionLabel}** A person who advances
freight has an insurable interest, in so far as the freight is not
repayable in case of loss.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Charges of insurance

**[[13]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-13 .sectionLabel}** An insured has an
insurable interest in the charges for any insurance that the insured has
effected.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Reinsurance

**[[14]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-14 .sectionLabel}** The insurer under a
contract has an insurable interest in the risk insured and may reinsure
in respect of it, but, unless the marine policy provides otherwise, the
original insured has no right or interest in the reinsurance.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Bottomry

**[[15]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-15 .sectionLabel}** A lender of money on the
security of a ship or a ship's cargo has an insurable interest in
respect of the loan.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Quantum of mortgagor's interest

- **[[16]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-16 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A
  mortgagor of insurable property has an insurable interest in its full
  value, and the mortgagee has an insurable interest in any sum due or
  to become due under the mortgage.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Interest of mortgagee, consignee or
  other person

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} A mortgagee, consignee or other person who has an
  insurable interest in the subject-matter insured may insure on the
  person's own behalf, on behalf and for the benefit of any other
  interested person or both on the person's own behalf and on behalf and
  for the benefit of any other interested person.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Quantum of owner's interest

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} The owner of insurable property has an insurable
  interest in its full value, even where a third person has agreed, or
  is liable, to indemnify the owner in case of loss.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Assignment of interest

- **[[17]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-17 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} An
  insured who assigns or otherwise parts with an insurable interest in
  the subject-matter insured does not thereby transfer the rights of the
  insured under the contract, unless there is an express or implied
  agreement to that effect.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Exception

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Subsection (1) does not apply in respect of a
  transmission of interest by operation of law.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Gaming or wagering contracts void

- **[[18]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-18 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Every
  contract by way of gaming or wagering is void.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Presumption

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} A contract is deemed to be a contract by way of
  gaming or wagering if

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the insured has no insurable interest within the
    meaning of this Act and the contract is concluded with no
    expectation of acquiring such an interest; or

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the marine policy is made "interest or no
    interest", "without further proof of interest than the policy
    itself" or "without benefit of salvage to the insurer" or is subject
    to any other like term.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Exception

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} Paragraph (2)(b) does not apply in respect of a
  marine policy that is made "without benefit of salvage to the insurer"
  or is subject to any other like term, if there is no possibility of
  salvage.

## [Insurable Value]{.HTitleText1} {#h-348903 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Calculation of insurable value

- **[[19]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-19
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Subject to any express provision of,
  or any value specified in, the marine policy, the insurable value of
  the subject-matter insured is

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} in the case of insurance on a ship, the aggregate
    of the value of the ship at the commencement of the risk and the
    charges of insurance;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} in the case of insurance on freight, whether paid
    in advance or not, the aggregate of the gross amount of the freight
    at the risk of the insured and the charges of insurance;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} in the case of insurance on goods, the aggregate of
    the prime cost of the goods, the expenses of and incidental to
    shipping and the charges of insurance on those goods and expenses;
    and

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} in the case of insurance on any other
    subject-matter, the aggregate of the amount at the risk of the
    insured when the policy attaches and the charges of insurance.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Value of ship

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} For the purposes of paragraph (1)(a), the value of a
  ship includes money advanced for officers' and crew's wages and other
  disbursements incurred to make the ship fit for the marine adventure
  contemplated by the marine policy.

## [Disclosure and Representations]{.HTitleText1} {#h-348913 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Utmost good faith

**[[20]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-20 .sectionLabel}** A contract is based on
the utmost good faith and, if the utmost good faith is not observed by
either party, the contract may be avoided by the other party.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Disclosure by insured

- **[[21]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-21
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Subject to this section, an insured
  must disclose to the insurer, before the contract is concluded, every
  material circumstance that is known to the insured.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Disclosure by agent of insured

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Subject to this section, an agent who effects
  insurance for an insured must disclose to the insurer, before the
  contract is concluded,

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} every material circumstance that is known to the
    agent; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} every material circumstance that the insured must
    disclose, unless the insured learned of it too late to communicate
    it to the agent.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Material circumstance

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} A circumstance is material if it would influence the
  judgment of a prudent insurer in fixing the premium or determining
  whether to take the risk.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Question of fact

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} Whether any circumstance that is not disclosed is
  material or not is a question of fact.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Circumstances not disclosed

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} In the absence of any inquiry, the following
  circumstances need not be disclosed:

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} any circumstance that diminishes the risk;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} any circumstance that is known to the insurer;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} any circumstance as to which information is waived
    by the insurer; and

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} any circumstance the disclosure of which is
    superfluous by reason of any express warranty or implied warranty.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Presumptions

  [(6)]{.lawlabel} For the purposes of this section,

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} an insured is deemed to know every circumstance
    that, in the ordinary course of business, ought to be known by the
    insured;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} an agent is deemed to know every circumstance that,
    in the ordinary course of business, ought to be known by, or to have
    been communicated to, the agent; and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} an insurer is presumed to know circumstances of
    common notoriety and every circumstance that, in the ordinary course
    of an insurer's business, ought to be known by an insurer.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Effect of non-disclosure

  [(7)]{.lawlabel} If an insured or an agent of an insured fails to make
  a disclosure as required by this section, the insurer may avoid the
  contract.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Definition of "circumstance"

  [(8)]{.lawlabel} In this section, [[circumstance]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}
  includes any communication made to, or information received by, the
  insured.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Representations by insured or agent

- **[[22]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-22 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Every
  material representation made by the insured or the insured's agent to
  the insurer during the negotiations for the contract and before the
  contract is concluded must be true.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Material representation

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} A representation is material if it would influence
  the judgment of a prudent insurer in fixing the premium or determining
  whether to take the risk.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Question of fact

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} Whether any representation is material or not is a
  question of fact.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Types of representations

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} A representation may be as to a matter of fact or as
  to a matter of expectation or belief.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Fact

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} A representation as to a matter of fact is deemed to
  be true if the difference between what is represented and what is
  actually correct would not be considered material by a prudent
  insurer.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Expectation or belief

  [(6)]{.lawlabel} A representation as to a matter of expectation or
  belief is deemed to be true if it is made in good faith.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Withdrawal or correction

  [(7)]{.lawlabel} A representation may be withdrawn or corrected before
  a contract is concluded.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Effect of false representations

  [(8)]{.lawlabel} If any material representation made by the insured or
  the insured's agent to the insurer during the negotiations for the
  contract is not true and is not withdrawn or corrected before the
  contract is concluded, the insurer may avoid the contract.

## [Conclusion and Ratification of Contracts]{.HTitleText1} {#h-348959 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}When contract is deemed to be concluded

**[[23]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-23 .sectionLabel}** A contract is deemed to
be concluded when the proposal of the insured is accepted by the
insurer, whether the marine policy is then issued or not, and for the
purpose of establishing when the proposal is accepted, the slip or
covering note or other customary memorandum of the contract may be
referred to.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Ratification

**[[24]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-24 .sectionLabel}** A contract effected in
good faith by a person on behalf of another person may be ratified by
that other person even after the other person becomes aware of a loss.

## [The Marine Policy]{.HTitleText1} {#h-348964 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Marine policy required

- **[[25]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-25 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A
  contract is inadmissible in evidence, unless it is evidenced by a
  marine policy in accordance with this Act.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Issue of marine policy

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} A marine policy may be executed and issued when the
  contract is concluded or afterwards.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Contents of marine policy

**[[26]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-26 .sectionLabel}** A marine policy must
specify

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} the name of the insured or of a person who effects
  the insurance on behalf of the insured;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} the subject-matter insured;

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} the perils insured against;

- [(d)]{.lawlabel} the voyage or period, or both, covered by the
  insurance;

- [(e)]{.lawlabel} the sum insured; and

- [(f)]{.lawlabel} the name of the insurer.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Signature of insurer

- **[[27]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-27 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A
  marine policy must be signed by or on behalf of the insurer.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Exception

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Notwithstanding subsection (1), where the insurer is
  a corporation, the corporate seal is sufficient.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Subscription by two or more insurers

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} Where a marine policy is subscribed by or on behalf
  of two or more insurers, each subscription, unless the contrary is
  expressed, constitutes a distinct contract with the insured.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Specification of subject-matter

- **[[28]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-28 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A
  marine policy must specify the subject-matter insured with reasonable
  certainty, but need not specify the nature and extent of the interest
  of the insured in that subject-matter.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Specification in general terms

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} A marine policy that specifies the subject-matter
  insured in general terms shall be construed to apply to the interest
  intended by the insured to be covered.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Usage

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} Any usage regulating the specification of the
  subject-matter insured shall be taken into consideration in applying
  this section.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Voyage and time policies

- **[[29]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-29 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A
  marine policy may be a voyage policy or a time policy.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Voyage policy

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} A marine policy is a voyage policy if the contract
  insures the subject-matter "at and from", or "from", one place to
  another place or other places.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Time policy

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} A marine policy is a time policy if the contract
  insures the subject-matter for a definite period.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Combined policies

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} A marine policy may include a contract insuring the
  subject-matter as described in subsections (2) and (3).

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Valued and unvalued policies

- **[[30]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-30 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A
  marine policy may be a valued policy or an unvalued policy.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Valued policy

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} A marine policy is a valued policy if it specifies
  the agreed value of the subject-matter insured.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Unvalued policy

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} A marine policy is an unvalued policy if it does not
  specify the value of the subject-matter insured and, subject to the
  limit of the sum insured, leaves the value to be determined in
  accordance with section 19.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Value specified

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} Subject to this Act and in the absence of fraud, the
  value specified by a valued policy is, as between the insurer and the
  insured, conclusive of the insurable value of the subject-matter
  intended to be insured, regardless of whether any loss is a total loss
  or a partial loss.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Idem

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} Unless a valued policy otherwise provides, the value
  specified by the policy is not conclusive for the purpose of
  determining whether there has been a constructive total loss.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Floating policy

- **[[31]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-31 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A
  marine policy may be a floating policy, that is to say, a policy that
  describes the insurance in general terms and leaves the name of the
  ship and other particulars to be defined by subsequent declarations,
  either by endorsement on the policy or in any other customary manner.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Declarations

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Unless a floating policy otherwise provides,
  declarations must be made in the order of dispatch or shipment and
  must, in the case of goods, include all consignments within the terms
  of the policy and honestly state the value of the goods.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Rectification

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} An omission in a declaration or an erroneous
  declaration may be rectified even after loss or arrival if the
  omission or declaration was made in good faith.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Idem

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} Unless a floating policy otherwise provides, where a
  declaration of value is not made until after notice of loss or
  arrival, the policy shall be treated as an unvalued policy with
  respect to the subject-matter of that declaration.

## [Warranties]{.HTitleText1} {#h-349021 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Definition of "warranty"

- **[[32]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-32 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} In
  this section and sections 33 to 39, [[warranty]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}
  means a promissory warranty by which the insured

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} undertakes that some particular thing will or will
    not be done or that some condition will be fulfilled; or

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} affirms or negates the existence of particular
    facts.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Types of warranty

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} A warranty may be an express warranty or an implied
  warranty.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Express warranties

- **[[33]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-33 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} An
  express warranty may be in any form of words from which the intention
  to warrant may be inferred.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Inclusion in policy

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} An express warranty must be included in, or written
  on, the marine policy or be contained in a document incorporated by
  reference into the policy.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Exclusion of implied warranty

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} An express warranty does not exclude an implied
  warranty, unless they are inconsistent.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Warranty of legality

**[[34]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-34 .sectionLabel}** There is an implied
warranty in every marine policy that the marine adventure insured is
lawful and, in so far as the insured has control, will be carried out in
a lawful manner.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}No implied warranty of nationality

**[[35]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-35 .sectionLabel}** There is no implied
warranty in any marine policy as to the nationality of a ship or that
the nationality of a ship will not be changed during the risk.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Warranty of neutrality

- **[[36]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-36 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Where
  in any marine policy insurable property is expressly warranted to be
  neutral, there is an implied condition in the policy

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} that the property will have a neutral character at
    the commencement of the risk and that, in so far as the insured has
    control, that character will be preserved during the risk; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} where the property is a ship, that, in so far as
    the insured has control, the papers necessary to establish the
    neutrality of the ship will be carried on the ship and will not be
    falsified or suppressed and no simulated papers will be used.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Breach of condition

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} If any loss occurs through a breach of the implied
  condition referred to in paragraph (1)(b), the insurer may avoid the
  contract.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Warranty of seaworthiness of ship in
voyage policy

- **[[37]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-37 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} There
  is an implied warranty in every voyage policy that, at the
  commencement of the voyage, the ship will be seaworthy for the purpose
  of the particular marine adventure insured.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Warranty of fitness against perils of
  the port

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Where a voyage policy attaches while the ship is in
  port, there is an implied warranty in the policy that the ship will,
  at the commencement of the risk, be reasonably fit to encounter the
  ordinary perils of the port.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Warranty of fitness for each stage of
  voyage

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} Where a voyage policy relates to a voyage performed
  in different stages during which the ship requires different or
  further preparation or equipment, there is an implied warranty in the
  policy that, at the commencement of each stage, the ship is seaworthy
  for the purposes of that stage.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}No implied warranty of seaworthiness in
  time policy

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} There is no implied warranty in any time policy that
  the ship will be seaworthy at any stage of the marine adventure, but
  where, with the privity of the insured, the ship is sent to sea in an
  unseaworthy state, the insurer is not liable for any loss attributable
  to unseaworthiness.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}When ship deemed seaworthy

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} A ship is deemed to be seaworthy if it is reasonably
  fit in all respects to encounter the ordinary perils of the seas of
  the marine adventure insured.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}No implied warranty that goods are
seaworthy

- **[[38]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-38 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} There
  is no implied warranty in any marine policy on insurable property,
  other than a ship, that the insurable property is seaworthy.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Voyage policy on goods

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} There is an implied warranty in every voyage policy
  on insurable property, other than a ship, that, at the commencement of
  the voyage, the ship is seaworthy and reasonably fit to carry the
  insurable property to the destination contemplated by the policy.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Compliance with warranty

- **[[39]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-39
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Subject to this section, a warranty
  must be exactly complied with, whether or not it is material to the
  risk.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Effect of breach of warranty

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Subject to any express provision in the marine policy
  or any waiver by the insurer, where a warranty is not exactly complied
  with, the breach of the warranty discharges the insurer from liability
  for any loss occurring on or after the date of the breach, but does
  not affect any liability incurred by the insurer before that date.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Breach of warranty of good safety

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} A warranty that the subject-matter insured is "well"
  or "in good safety" on a particular day is not breached if the
  subject-matter is safe at any time during that day.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}When breach of warranty excused

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} A breach of a warranty is excused if, because of a
  change of circumstances, the warranty ceases to be applicable to the
  circumstances contemplated by the contract or if compliance with the
  warranty is rendered unlawful by any subsequent law.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Limit on defence to breach of warranty

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} It is no defence to a breach of a warranty that the
  breach was remedied and the warranty complied with before any loss was
  incurred.

## [The Voyage]{.HTitleText1} {#h-349074 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Implied condition as to commencement

- **[[40]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-40 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Where
  the subject-matter is insured by a voyage policy, the ship need not,
  when the contract is concluded, be at the place at and from, or from,
  which the subject-matter is insured, but there is an implied condition
  in the policy that the marine adventure will commence within a
  reasonable time and, if it is not so commenced, the insurer may avoid
  the contract.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Exception

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The implied condition may be negated by establishing
  that the delay was caused by circumstances known to the insurer before
  the contract was concluded or that the insurer waived the condition.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Change of port of departure

- **[[41]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-41 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Where
  the place of departure is specified by a marine policy and the ship
  sails from a different place, the risk does not attach.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Change of destination

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Where the destination is specified by a marine policy
  and the ship sails for a different destination, the risk does not
  attach.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Change of voyage

- **[[42]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-42 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Unless
  a marine policy otherwise provides, a change of voyage discharges the
  insurer from liability for any loss occurring on or after the time
  when the intention to change is manifested, whether or not the ship
  has in fact left the course of voyage contemplated by the policy when
  the loss occurs.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Idem

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} There is a change of voyage where, after the
  commencement of the risk, the destination of the ship is voluntarily
  changed from that contemplated by the marine policy.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Deviation from voyage

- **[[43]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-43 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A
  deviation without lawful excuse from the voyage contemplated by a
  marine policy discharges the insurer from liability for any loss
  occurring on or after the time when the deviation occurs, regardless
  of the intention to deviate and whether or not the ship returns to its
  course of voyage before the loss occurs.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Idem

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} There is a deviation from the voyage contemplated by
  a marine policy where

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the course of the voyage is specified by the policy
    and is departed from; or

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the course of the voyage is not specified by the
    policy but the usual and customary course is departed from.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Idem

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} Where a marine policy specifies the ports of
  discharge, the ship may proceed to any or all of them, but if, in the
  absence of any usage or sufficient cause, the ship does not proceed to
  them, or such of them as it goes to, in the order specified, there is
  a deviation from the voyage contemplated by the policy.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Idem

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} Where a marine policy specifies that the ports of
  discharge are within a given area and does not otherwise name them,
  the ship may proceed to any or all of them, but if, in the absence of
  any usage or sufficient cause, the ship does not proceed to them, or
  such of them as it goes to, in their geographical order, there is a
  deviation from the voyage contemplated by the policy.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Delay in voyage

**[[44]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-44 .sectionLabel}** The marine adventure
insured by a voyage policy must be carried out with reasonable dispatch
and a delay, without lawful excuse, in carrying it out discharges the
insurer from liability for any loss occurring on or after the time when
the delay becomes unreasonable.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Excuses for deviation or delay

- **[[45]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-45 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A
  deviation or delay referred to in section 43 or 44 is excused if it is

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} authorized by any special term in the marine
    policy;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} caused by circumstances beyond the control of the
    master and the master's employer;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} reasonably necessary in order to comply with an
    express warranty or an implied warranty;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} reasonably necessary for the safety of the ship or
    subject-matter insured;

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} for the purpose of saving human life or aiding a
    ship in distress where human life may be in danger;

  - [(f)]{.lawlabel} reasonably necessary for the purpose of obtaining
    medical aid for any person on board the ship; or

  - [(g)]{.lawlabel} caused by the barratrous conduct of the master or
    crew, if barratry is one of the perils insured against.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Resumption

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} When the excuse for a deviation or delay ceases, the
  voyage must be resumed with reasonable dispatch.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Transhipment

**[[46]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-46 .sectionLabel}** Where the voyage
contemplated by a marine policy is interrupted, by a peril insured
against, at an intermediate port or place in such circumstances as,
apart from the contract of affreightment, justify the master in landing
and reshipping, or transhipping, the goods or movables and sending them
to their destination, the insurer continues to be liable for a loss
occurring on or after the landing or transhipment.

## [The Premium]{.HTitleText1} {#h-349117 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Premium to be arranged

- **[[47]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-47 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A
  reasonable premium is payable if insurance is effected at a premium to
  be arranged and no arrangement is made.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Additional premium

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} A reasonable additional premium is payable if
  insurance is effected on the terms that an additional premium is to be
  arranged on the happening of a given event and that event happens but
  no arrangement is made.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Payment of premium

**[[48]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-48 .sectionLabel}** Unless otherwise agreed,
the duty of the insured or the insured's agent to pay the premium and
the duty of the insurer to issue the marine policy to the insured or the
insured's agent are concurrent conditions, and the insurer is not
required to issue the policy until the premium is paid or tendered.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Policy effected through broker

- **[[49]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-49 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Unless
  otherwise agreed, where a broker effects a marine policy on behalf of
  the insured,

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the broker is directly responsible to the insurer
    for the premium;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the broker has, as against the insured, a lien on
    the policy for the amount of the premium and the broker's charges in
    effecting the policy; and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} the insurer is directly responsible to the insured
    for any amount that may be payable in respect of losses or a
    returnable premium.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Idem

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Unless otherwise agreed, where a broker effects a
  marine policy on behalf of a person who employs the broker as a
  principal, the broker has a lien on the policy in respect of any
  balance on any insurance account that may be due to the broker from
  that person, unless, when the debt was incurred, the broker had reason
  to believe that the person was only an agent.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Acknowledgement of receipt of premium

**[[50]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-50 .sectionLabel}** Where a broker effects a
marine policy on behalf of an insured, an acknowledgement in the policy
of the receipt of the premium is, in the absence of fraud, conclusive as
between the insurer and the insured, but not as between the insurer and
the broker.

## [Assignment of Marine Policy]{.HTitleText1} {#h-349135 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Marine policy assignable

- **[[51]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-51 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A
  marine policy is assignable either before or after a loss, unless it
  expressly prohibits assignment.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Manner of assignment

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} A marine policy may be assigned by endorsement on the
  policy or in any other customary manner.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Effect of assignment

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} Where a marine policy is assigned so as to transfer
  the beneficial interest in the policy, the assignee of the policy is
  entitled to sue on it in the assignee's name and, in any such action,
  the defendant is entitled to raise any defence arising out of the
  contract that the defendant would have been entitled to raise if the
  action had been brought in the name of the person by or on behalf of
  whom the policy was effected.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Loss of interest

- **[[52]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-52 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Where
  an insured transfers or loses an interest in the subject-matter
  insured and does not, before or at the time of so doing, expressly or
  impliedly agree to assign the marine policy, no subsequent assignment
  of the marine policy is operative.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Exception

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Subsection (1) does not apply in respect of an
  assignment of a marine policy after a loss.

## [Loss and Abandonment]{.HTitleText1} {#h-349148 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Losses covered

- **[[53]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-53
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Subject to this Act and unless a
  marine policy otherwise provides, an insurer is liable only for a loss
  that is proximately caused by a peril insured against, including a
  loss that would not have occurred but for the misconduct or negligence
  of the master or crew.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Losses specifically excluded

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Without limiting the generality of subsection (1), an
  insurer is not liable for any loss attributable to the wilful
  misconduct of the insured nor, unless the marine policy otherwise
  provides, for

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} in the case of insurance on a ship or goods, any
    loss proximately caused by delay, including a delay caused by a
    peril insured against;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} ordinary wear and tear, ordinary leakage or
    breakage or inherent vice or nature of the subject-matter insured;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} any loss proximately caused by vermin; or

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} any loss or damage to machinery not proximately
    caused by maritime perils.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Total and partial losses

**[[54]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-54 .sectionLabel}** A loss may be a total
loss or a partial loss.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Types of total loss

- **[[55]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-55 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A
  total loss may be an actual total loss or a constructive total loss.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Losses covered

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Unless a marine policy otherwise provides, insurance
  against total loss includes both actual total loss and constructive
  total loss.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Actual total loss

- **[[56]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-56 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A loss
  is an actual total loss if the subject-matter insured is destroyed or
  is so damaged as to cease to be a thing of the kind insured or if the
  insured is irretrievably deprived of the subject-matter.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Idem

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Where a ship engaged in a marine adventure is missing
  and no news of the ship is received within a reasonable period, an
  actual total loss may be presumed.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Constructive total loss

- **[[57]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-57 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Unless
  a marine policy otherwise provides, a loss is a constructive total
  loss if the subject-matter insured is reasonably abandoned because the
  actual total loss of the subject-matter appears unavoidable or the
  preservation of the subject-matter from actual total loss would entail
  costs exceeding its value when the costs are incurred.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Idem

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Without limiting the generality of subsection (1), a
  loss is a constructive total loss if

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} in the case of a ship or goods, the insured is
    deprived of possession of the ship or goods by reason of a peril
    insured against and either the insured is unlikely to recover the
    ship or goods or the cost of recovery would exceed the value of the
    ship or goods when recovered;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} in the case of a ship, the ship is so damaged by a
    peril insured against that the cost of repairing it would exceed the
    value of the ship when repaired; or

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} in the case of goods, the goods are so damaged that
    the cost of repairing and forwarding them to their destination would
    exceed the value of the goods on arrival.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Cost of repair of ship

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} For the purposes of paragraph (2)(b), in estimating
  the cost of repairing a ship, no deduction may be made in respect of
  general average contributions to the repairs payable by other
  interested persons, but account is to be taken of the cost of future
  salvage operations and of any future general average contributions to
  which the ship would be liable if repaired.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Treatment

- **[[58]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-58 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} An
  insured may treat a constructive total loss as a partial loss or may
  abandon the subject-matter insured to the insurer and treat the
  constructive total loss as an actual total loss.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Notice of abandonment

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Subject to this section and section 59, an insured
  who elects to abandon the subject-matter insured to the insurer must
  give a notice of abandonment to the insurer with reasonable diligence
  after the insured receives reliable information of the loss.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Time for inquiry

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} An insured who receives doubtful information of a
  loss is entitled to a reasonable time to make inquiries before giving
  a notice of abandonment.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Manner of giving notice

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} An insured may give a notice of abandonment orally or
  in writing, or partly orally and partly in writing, and in any terms
  that indicate the insured's intention to abandon unconditionally the
  insured interest in the subject-matter to the insurer.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Failure to give notice

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} If an insured fails to give a notice of abandonment
  as required by this section, the constructive total loss may be
  treated only as a partial loss.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Notice not required

- **[[59]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-59 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} An
  insured is not required to give a notice of abandonment to the insurer
  if

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the loss is an actual total loss;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} notice is waived by the insurer; or

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} at the time the insured receives information of the
    loss, there is no possibility of benefit to the insurer if notice
    were given to the insurer.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Idem

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} An insurer who has reinsured a risk is not required
  to give a notice of abandonment to the reinsurer.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Refusal of abandonment

- **[[60]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-60 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} If an
  insured gives a notice of abandonment as required by section 58, the
  rights of the insured are not prejudiced by a refusal of the insurer
  to accept the abandonment.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Acceptance of abandonment

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} An acceptance of an abandonment may be either express
  or implied from the conduct of the insurer, but the mere silence of an
  insurer after a notice of abandonment is given does not constitute an
  acceptance.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Effect of acceptance on insured

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} On acceptance of an abandonment, the abandonment is
  irrevocable.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Effect of acceptance on insurer

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} On acceptance of an abandonment, the insurer

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} conclusively admits liability for the loss and the
    sufficiency of the notice of abandonment; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} is entitled to acquire the interest of the insured
    in whatever remains of the subject-matter insured, including all
    proprietary rights incidental thereto.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Abandonment of ship

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} On acceptance of the abandonment of a ship, the
  insurer is entitled to

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} any freight being earned at the time of, or earned
    subsequent to, the casualty causing the loss, less the costs
    incurred in earning it after the casualty; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} if the ship is carrying the shipowner's goods,
    reasonable remuneration for the carriage of the goods subsequent to
    the casualty.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Partial loss

- **[[61]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-61 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A
  partial loss is any loss that is not a total loss.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Idem

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Where insured goods reach their destination in specie
  but cannot be identified by reason of obliteration of marks or
  otherwise, the loss, if any, is a partial loss.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Recovery for partial loss

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} Unless a marine policy otherwise provides, an insured
  who brings an action for a total loss but establishes only a partial
  loss may recover for a partial loss.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Types of partial losses

**[[62]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-62 .sectionLabel}** A partial loss may be a
particular average loss, a general average loss, salvage charges or
particular charges.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Particular average loss

- **[[63]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-63 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A
  particular average loss is a loss of the subject-matter insured that
  is caused by a peril insured against and is not a general average
  loss, but does not include particular charges.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Particular charges

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Particular charges are expenses incurred by or on
  behalf of an insured for the purpose of preserving the subject-matter
  insured from a peril insured against, but do not include a general
  average loss or salvage charges.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Salvage charges

- **[[64]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-64
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Salvage charges are charges
  recoverable under maritime law by a salvor independently of any
  contract, but do not include expenses incurred for services in the
  nature of salvage rendered by the insured or the insured's agent, or
  any person hired by the insured or the insured's agent, for the
  purpose of averting a loss by a peril insured against.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Recovery of salvage charges

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Subject to any express provision in the marine
  policy, salvage charges incurred for the purpose of averting a loss by
  a peril insured against may be recovered from the insurer as a loss by
  such a peril.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Recovery of other expenses

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} The expenses referred to in subsection (1) that are
  not salvage charges may, when properly incurred, be recovered from the
  insurer as particular charges or as a general average loss, according
  to the circumstances under which they were incurred.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}General average loss

- **[[65]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-65 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A
  general average loss is a loss caused by or directly consequential on
  a general average act, and includes a general average sacrifice and a
  general average expenditure.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}General average act, sacrifice and
  expenditure

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} A general average act is any extraordinary sacrifice
  or expenditure, known as a general average sacrifice and a general
  average expenditure, respectively, that is voluntarily and reasonably
  incurred in time of peril for the purpose of preserving the property
  from peril in a common adventure.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}General average contribution

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} Subject to the conditions imposed by maritime law, a
  person who incurs a general average loss is entitled to receive from
  the other interested persons a rateable contribution, known as a
  general average contribution, in respect of the loss.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Recovery of general average expenditure
  and general average sacrifice

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} Subject to any express provision in the marine
  policy,

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} an insured who incurs a general average expenditure
    may recover from the insurer in respect of the proportion of the
    loss falling on the insured; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} an insured who incurs a general average sacrifice
    may recover from the insurer in respect of the whole loss, without
    having enforced the insured's right to contribution from other
    persons.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Recovery of general average
  contribution

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} Subject to any express provision in the marine
  policy, an insured who has paid, or is liable to pay, a general
  average contribution in respect of the subject-matter insured may
  recover the contribution from the insurer.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Condition

  [(6)]{.lawlabel} Subject to any express provision in the marine
  policy, an insurer is not liable for a general average loss or a
  general average contribution, unless the loss was incurred for the
  purpose of averting, or in connection with the avoidance of, a peril
  insured against.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Where single ownership

  [(7)]{.lawlabel} Where any ship, freight and goods, or any two of
  them, are owned by the same insured, the liability of the insurer for
  a general average loss or a general average contribution shall be
  determined as if they were owned by different persons.

## [Measure of Indemnity]{.HTitleText1} {#h-349252 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Measure of indemnity

**[[66]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-66 .sectionLabel}** The measure of indemnity
in respect of a loss under a marine policy is the amount that the
insured can recover in respect of the loss under the policy, such amount
not exceeding

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} in the case of an unvalued policy, the insurable
  value of the subject-matter insured; or

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} in the case of a valued policy, the value of the
  subject-matter insured specified by the policy.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Total loss

**[[67]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-67 .sectionLabel}** Subject to this Act and
any express provision in the policy, the measure of indemnity in respect
of a total loss of the subject-matter insured is

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} in the case of an unvalued policy, the insurable
  value of the subject-matter; and

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} in the case of a valued policy, the value of the
  subject-matter specified by the policy.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Partial loss of ship

**[[68]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-68 .sectionLabel}** Subject to any express
provision in the marine policy, the measure of indemnity in respect of a
partial loss of a ship is

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} where the ship is repaired, the reasonable cost of
  the repairs less the customary deductions, but not exceeding the sum
  insured in respect of any one casualty;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} where the ship is partially repaired, the aggregate
  of the reasonable cost of the repairs, as determined under paragraph
  (a), and the reasonable depreciation, if any, arising from the
  unrepaired damage, the aggregate not exceeding the cost, as determined
  under paragraph (a), of repairing the whole damage; and

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} where the ship is not repaired and is not sold in a
  damaged state during the risk, the reasonable depreciation arising
  from the unrepaired damage, but not exceeding the cost, as determined
  under paragraph (a), of repairing the damage.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Partial loss of freight

**[[69]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-69 .sectionLabel}** Subject to any express
provision in the policy, the measure of indemnity in respect of a
partial loss of freight is that proportion of the insurable value of the
freight, in the case of an unvalued policy, or the value of the freight
specified by the policy, in the case of a valued policy, that the part
of the freight lost by the insured bears to the whole freight at the
risk of the insured under the policy.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Partial loss of goods or movables

- **[[70]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-70
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Subject to any express provision in
  the policy, the measure of indemnity in respect of a partial loss of
  goods or movables is

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} where part of the goods or movables insured by an
    unvalued policy is totally lost, the insurable value of the part
    lost, ascertained as in the case of a total loss;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} where part of the goods or movables insured by a
    valued policy is totally lost, that proportion of the value of the
    goods or movables specified by the policy that the insurable value
    of the part lost bears to the insurable value of all the goods or
    movables, ascertained as in the case of an unvalued policy; and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} where the whole or any part of the goods or
    movables is delivered damaged at its destination, that proportion of
    the insurable value of all the goods or movables, in the case of an
    unvalued policy, or the value of all the goods or movables specified
    by the policy, in the case of a valued policy, that the difference
    between the gross value of all the goods or movables in a sound
    condition at that destination and their gross value in their damaged
    condition at that destination bears to the gross value of all the
    goods or movables in a sound condition at that destination.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Definition of "gross value"

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} For the purposes of paragraph (1)(c), [[gross
  value]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} in the case of goods or movables customarily sold
    in bond, means the bonded price of the goods or movables; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} in the case of any other goods or movables, means
    the wholesale price, or if there is no wholesale price, the
    estimated value, of the goods or movables, together with any
    freight, landing charges and duty paid in respect of them.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Gross proceeds

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} For the purposes of paragraph (1)(c), where the goods
  or movables are sold at their destination and all charges on the sale
  are paid by the sellers, their gross value in their damaged condition
  at that destination is the actual price obtained for them, which price
  is known as the gross proceeds.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Apportionment of specified value

- **[[71]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-71 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} In
  determining the measure of indemnity under a valued policy that
  specifies a single value for different types of goods,

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the value must be apportioned to those types in
    proportion to their respective insurable values, as determined under
    this Act; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the value of any part of any type of the goods is
    that proportion of the value of all the goods of that type that the
    insurable value of that part bears to the insurable value of all the
    goods of that type, as determined under this Act.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Idem

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Where the insurable value of goods cannot be
  determined for the purposes of subsection (1) because the prime cost
  of a type of goods is not ascertainable, the value specified by the
  valued policy may be apportioned to the different types of goods in
  proportion to their respective net arrived sound values.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}General average contribution

- **[[72]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-72
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Subject to any express provision in
  the marine policy, the measure of indemnity in respect of a general
  average contribution that an insured has paid or is liable to pay is

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} where the subject-matter of the contribution is
    fully insured for its contributory value, the full amount of the
    contribution; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} where the subject-matter of the contribution is not
    fully insured for its contributory value or only part of it is
    insured, that proportion of the full amount of the contribution that
    the insured value of the subject-matter bears to its contributory
    value.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Idem

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} In order to determine the measure of indemnity under
  paragraph (1)(b) in a case where a particular average loss that is to
  be deducted from the contributory value has been incurred and is
  payable by the insurer, the amount of the loss must be deducted from
  the insured value of the subject-matter.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Salvage charges

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} Where salvage charges are recoverable under a marine
  policy, the measure of indemnity in respect of the charges is to be
  determined in accordance with the principles set out in
  subsections (1) and (2).

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Third party liability

**[[73]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-73 .sectionLabel}** Subject to any express
provision in the policy, the measure of indemnity in respect of any
liability to a third party that is expressly insured against by a marine
policy is the amount paid or payable by the insured to the third party
in respect of the liability.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Other losses

**[[74]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-74 .sectionLabel}** The measure of indemnity
in respect of a loss not provided for in any of sections 67 to 73 is to
be determined, as much as possible, in accordance with those sections.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Proportional liability

**[[75]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-75 .sectionLabel}** Where a loss is
recoverable under a marine policy, the insurer, or each insurer if there
is more than one, is liable for that proportion of the measure of
indemnity in respect of the loss that the amount subscribed by the
insurer is of

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} in the case of an unvalued policy, the insurable
  value of the subject-matter; and

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} in the case of a valued policy, the value of the
  subject-matter specified by the policy.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Construction

**[[76]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-76 .sectionLabel}** Nothing in sections 66 to
75 shall be construed as affecting the provisions of this Act relating
to double insurance or prohibiting an insurer from disproving an
interest in whole or in part or from establishing that, at the time of a
loss, the whole or any part of the subject-matter insured was not at
risk under the marine policy.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Particular average warranties

- **[[77]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-77 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Where
  the subject-matter insured under a marine policy is warranted free
  from particular average, the insured cannot recover for a loss of part
  of the subject-matter, other than a loss incurred by a general average
  sacrifice, unless the contract evidenced by the policy is
  apportionable, in which case the insured may recover for a total loss
  of any apportionable part.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Idem

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Where the subject-matter insured under a marine
  policy is warranted free from particular average, either wholly or
  under a specified percentage, the insurer is nevertheless liable for
  salvage charges and, if the policy contains a sue and labour clause,
  for particular charges and other expenses properly incurred under the
  clause for the purpose of averting a loss by a peril insured against.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Addition of general to particular
  average loss

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} Unless the policy otherwise provides, where the
  subject-matter insured under a marine policy is warranted free from
  particular average under a specified percentage, a general average
  loss cannot be added to a particular average loss in order to attain
  that percentage.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Calculation of percentage

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} Where the subject-matter insured under a marine
  policy is warranted free from particular average under a specified
  percentage, for the purpose of determining whether that percentage has
  been attained, only the actual loss incurred in respect of the
  subject-matter may be considered, and no particular charges or
  expenses incurred in establishing the loss may be included.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Recovery of successive losses

- **[[78]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-78
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Subject to this Act and unless the
  marine policy otherwise provides, an insurer is liable for successive
  losses, even if the total amount of the losses exceeds the sum
  insured.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Exception

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Where, under a marine policy, a partial loss that has
  not been repaired or otherwise made good is followed by a total loss,
  the insurer is liable only for the total loss.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Liability under sue and labour clause

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} Nothing in subsections (1) and (2) shall be construed
  as affecting the liability of an insurer under a sue and labour
  clause.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Sue and labour clause

- **[[79]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-79 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Where
  a marine policy contains a sue and labour clause, the engagement
  thereby entered into is supplementary to the contract and the insured
  may recover from the insurer any expenses properly incurred under the
  clause, even if the insurer has paid for a total loss of the
  subject-matter insured or the subject-matter insured is warranted free
  from particular average, either wholly or under a specified
  percentage.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Idem

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} General average losses, general average
  contributions, salvage charges, and expenses incurred for the purpose
  of averting or diminishing a loss by a peril not insured against are
  not recoverable under a sue and labour clause.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Duty to avert or diminish loss

**[[80]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-80 .sectionLabel}** It is the duty of an
insured and an insured's agent to take such measures as are reasonable
for the purpose of averting or diminishing a loss under the marine
policy.

## [Rights of Insurer on Payment]{.HTitleText1} {#h-349329 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Subrogation where total loss

- **[[81]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-81 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} On
  payment by an insurer for a total loss of the whole of the
  subject-matter insured or, if the subject-matter insured is goods, for
  any apportionable part of the subject-matter insured, the insurer
  becomes entitled to assume the interest of the insured in the whole or
  part of the subject-matter and is subrogated to all the rights and
  remedies of the insured in respect of that whole or part from the time
  of the casualty causing the loss.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Subrogation where partial loss

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} On payment by an insurer for a partial loss of the
  subject-matter insured, the insurer acquires no title to the
  subject-matter but is subrogated to all the rights and remedies of the
  insured in respect of the subject-matter from the time of the casualty
  causing the loss to the extent that the insured is indemnified, in
  accordance with this Act, by the payment for the loss.

## [Return of Premium]{.HTitleText1} {#h-349335 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Recovery or retention

- **[[82]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-82 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A
  premium or part of a premium that is returnable to the insured may, if
  paid, be recovered by the insured from the insurer and may, if not
  paid, be retained by the insured or the insured's agent.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}When premium returnable

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} A premium or part of a premium is returnable to the
  insured in any of the circumstances described in sections 83 to 85.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Return on happening of specified event

**[[83]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-83 .sectionLabel}** Where a marine policy
contains a provision for the return of the premium or part of the
premium on the happening of a specified event, the premium or part is
returnable to the insured on the happening of that event.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Return on total failure of consideration

- **[[84]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-84 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Where
  the consideration for a premium totally fails and there is no fraud or
  illegality on the part of the insured or the insured's agent, the
  premium is returnable to the insured on the failure.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Idem

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Where any apportionable part of the consideration for
  a premium totally fails and there is no fraud or illegality on the
  part of the insured or the insured's agent, a proportionate part of
  the premium is returnable to the insured on the failure.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Particular circumstances

- **[[85]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-85
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Without limiting the generality of
  section 84, a premium or part of a premium is returnable or not
  returnable to the insured in the particular circumstances described in
  subsections (2) to (11).

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Void or avoided marine policy

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Where a marine policy is void, or is avoided by the
  insurer as of the commencement of the risk, and there is no fraud or
  illegality on the part of the insured or the insured's agent, the
  premium is returnable.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Exception

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} Where the risk is not apportionable and has once
  attached, subsection (2) does not apply and the premium is not
  returnable.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Subject-matter never imperilled

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} Where the subject-matter insured or part of the
  subject-matter insured has never been exposed to any peril insured
  against, the premium or a proportionate part of the premium, as the
  case may be, is returnable.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Exception

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} Where the subject-matter is insured "lost or not
  lost" and has arrived at its destination safely before the contract is
  concluded, subsection (4) does not apply and the premium is not
  returnable unless, at the time the contract is concluded, the insurer
  knows of the safe arrival.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}No insurable interest

  [(6)]{.lawlabel} Where an insured has no insurable interest throughout
  the period of the risk, the premium is returnable.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Exception

  [(7)]{.lawlabel} Subsection (6) does not apply in respect of a
  contract by way of gaming or wagering and the premium is not
  returnable.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Over-insurance under one policy

  [(8)]{.lawlabel} Where an insured is over-insured under an unvalued
  policy, a proportionate part of the premium is returnable.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Defeasible interest

  [(9)]{.lawlabel} Where an insured has a defeasible interest in the
  subject-matter insured that is terminated during the period of the
  risk, the premium is not returnable.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Over-insurance under several policies

  [(10)]{.lawlabel} Subject to subsections (2) to (9), where an insured
  is over-insured by double insurance, a proportionate part of the
  premiums is returnable.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Exceptions

  [(11)]{.lawlabel} Subsection (10) does not apply

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} where the double insurance is knowingly effected by
    the insured, in which case none of the premiums is returnable; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} where the policies are effected at different times
    and either the earlier policy has at any time borne the entire risk
    or a claim has been paid on the earlier policy in respect of the
    full sum insured by it, in which case the premium for the earlier
    policy is not returnable and the premium for the later policy is
    returnable.

## [Double Insurance]{.HTitleText1} {#h-349373 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Double insurance where over-insured

- **[[86]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-86 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} An
  insured is over-insured by double insurance if two or more marine
  policies are effected by or on behalf of the insured on the same
  marine adventure and interest or part thereof and the sums insured
  exceed the indemnity allowed by this Act.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Where over-insurance

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} An insured who is over-insured by double insurance

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} may claim payment from the insurers in any order,
    unless the marine policy under which the claim is made provides
    otherwise, but may not receive more than the indemnity allowed by
    this Act;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} if claiming under a valued policy, shall give
    credit, as against the value specified in the policy, for any sum
    received by the insured under any other policy without regard to the
    actual value of the subject-matter insured;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} if claiming under an unvalued policy, shall give
    credit, as against the full insurable value, for any sum received by
    the insured under any other policy; and

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} is deemed to hold any sum received in excess of the
    indemnity allowed by this Act in trust for the insurers, according
    to their right of contribution among themselves.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Right of contribution

- **[[87]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-87 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Where
  an insured is over-insured by double insurance, each insurer is
  liable, as between the insurer and the other insurers, to contribute
  rateably to the payment of a loss in proportion to the amount for
  which the insurer is liable under the insurer's contract.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Remedies for overcontribution

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} An insurer who contributes more to the payment of a
  loss than required by subsection (1) is entitled to bring an action
  against the other insurers for contribution and to such other remedies
  as a surety is entitled to for paying more than the surety's
  proportion of a debt.

## [Under-insurance]{.HTitleText1} {#h-349388 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Under-insurance

**[[88]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-88 .sectionLabel}** Where an insured is
insured for a sum that is less than the insurable value of the
subject-matter insured, in the case of an unvalued policy, or less than
the value of the subject-matter insured specified by the policy, in the
case of a valued policy, the insured is deemed to be self-insured in
respect of the uninsured difference.

## [Mutual Insurance]{.HTitleText1} {#h-349391 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Mutual insurance

- **[[89]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-89 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Mutual
  insurance is insurance whereby two or more persons mutually agree to
  insure one another against marine losses.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Application of Act

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Subject to subsections (3) and (4), this Act applies
  in respect of mutual insurance.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Premium

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} The provisions of this Act relating to premiums do
  not apply in respect of mutual insurance, but a guarantee, or such
  other arrangement as may be agreed on, may be substituted for the
  premium for mutual insurance.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Amendment by mutual insurance
  association

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} The provisions of this Act may, in the case of mutual
  insurance effected by an association, be modified by a marine policy
  issued by the association, or by the rules and regulations of the
  association, to the extent that the provisions may be modified by
  agreement of the parties to the insurance.

## [General]{.HTitleText1} {#h-349401 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Exclusion or variation of rights, duties
or liabilities

**[[90]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-90 .sectionLabel}** Any right, duty or
liability that arises under a contract by implication of law, or that is
established by this Act and may be lawfully modified by the parties to a
contract, may be negated or varied by express agreement or by usage of
the trade if the usage binds both parties to the contract.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Question of fact

**[[91]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-91 .sectionLabel}** Any question as to what
constitutes a reasonable time, a reasonable premium or reasonable
diligence for the purposes of this Act is a question of fact.

## [SCHEDULE]{.scheduleLabel}[(*Section 3*)]{.OriginatingRef}[Construction of Marine Policies]{.scheduleTitleText}  {#h-349407 .scheduleLabel}

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Definitions

  - **1** [(1)]{.lawlabel} In a marine policy,

    [[barratry]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

    :   [[barratry]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} includes every wrongful act
        wilfully committed by the master or crew of the insured ship to
        the prejudice of the owner or charterer of the
        ship; ([baraterie]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

    [[goods]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

    :   [[goods]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means goods in the nature of
        merchandise, but does not include personal effects or
        provisions, stores for use on board a ship or, in the absence of
        any usage to the contrary, deck cargo or live
        animals; ([marchandises]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

    [[pirates]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

    :   [[pirates]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} includes passengers on the
        insured ship who mutiny and persons who attack the ship from
        land; ([piraterie]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

    [[thieves]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

    :   [[thieves]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} does not include persons who
        commit a clandestine theft or passengers, officers or members of
        the crew of the insured ship who commit a
        theft. ([vol]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

  - [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Other definitions

    [(2)]{.lawlabel} In a marine policy, the words "freight" and "ship"
    have the meaning assigned by subsection 2(1) of this Act.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}References

  **2** In a marine policy, a reference

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} to "all other perils" means perils similar to the
    perils specifically mentioned in the policy;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} to "arrests, &c., of kings, princes, and people"
    includes political or executive acts, but does not include riot or
    ordinary judicial process;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} to "average unless general" means a partial loss of
    the subject-matter insured, other than a general average loss, but
    does not include particular charges; and

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} to "perils of the seas" means fortuitous accidents
    or casualties of the seas, but does not include ordinary action of
    the wind and waves.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}"Lost or not lost"

  **3** Where the subject-matter of a marine policy is insured "lost or
  not lost" and a loss occurs before the contract is concluded, the risk
  attaches unless, at the time the contract was concluded, the insured
  was aware of the loss and the insurer was not.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}"From"

  **4** Where the subject-matter of a marine policy is insured "from" a
  particular place, the risk does not attach until the voyage covered by
  the policy is commenced.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}"At and from" --- ship

  - **5** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Where a marine policy insures a ship "at and
    from" a particular place and the ship is at that place in good
    safety when the contract is concluded, the risk attaches when the
    contract is concluded.

  - [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Idem

    [(2)]{.lawlabel} Where a marine policy insures a ship "at and from"
    a particular place and the ship is not at that place when the
    contract is concluded, the risk attaches when the ship arrives there
    in good safety, and, unless the policy otherwise provides, it is
    immaterial that the ship is insured by another marine policy for a
    specified time after the arrival.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}"At and from" --- chartered freight

  - **6** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Where a marine policy insures chartered
    freight "at and from" a particular place and the ship is at that
    place in good safety when the contract is concluded, the risk
    attaches when the contract is concluded.

  - [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Idem

    [(2)]{.lawlabel} Where a marine policy insures chartered freight "at
    and from" a particular place and the ship is not at that place when
    the contract is concluded, the risk attaches when the ship arrives
    there in good safety.

  - [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}"At and from" --- other freight

    [(3)]{.lawlabel} Where a marine policy insures freight, other than
    chartered freight, "at and from" a particular place and the freight
    is payable without special conditions, the risk attaches
    proportionately as the goods are shipped, except that if the goods
    are ready for shipping and belong to the shipowner or are to be
    shipped under a contract with the shipowner, the risk attaches when
    the ship is ready to receive the goods.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}"From the loading thereof"

  **7** Where a marine policy insures goods or movables "from the
  loading thereof", the risk does not attach until they are on board the
  ship.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}"Safely landed"

  **8** Where the risk on any goods or movables continues until they are
  "safely landed", the risk ceases if they are not landed in the
  customary manner within a reasonable time after the arrival of the
  ship at the port of discharge.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}"At any port or place whatsoever"

  **9** In the absence of any licence or usage, the liberty to touch and
  stay "at any port or place whatsoever" does not authorize a change in
  the course of the ship's voyage from the port of departure to the port
  of destination.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}"Stranded"

  **10** Where a marine policy excepts a loss unless a ship is
  "stranded", the insurer is liable for any excepted loss, whether or
  not the loss is attributable to the stranding, if the risk has
  attached before the stranding and, in the case of a marine policy on
  goods, the damaged goods are on board the ship.
