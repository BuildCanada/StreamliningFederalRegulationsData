## International Transfer of Offenders Act {#international-transfer-of-offenders-act .Title-of-Act}

[S.C.]{.abbr title="Statutes of Canada"} 2004, c. 21
:::

Assented to 2004-05-14

An Act to implement treaties and administrative arrangements on the
international transfer of persons found guilty of criminal offences
::::

::::::::::::::::::::::::::: section
::: Enacts
Her Majesty, by and with the advice and consent of the Senate and House
of Commons of Canada, enacts as follows:
:::

## [Short Title]{.HTitleText1} {#h-277174 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Short title

**[[1]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-1 .sectionLabel}** This Act may be cited as
the [International Transfer of Offenders Act](/eng/acts/I-20.6).

## [Interpretation]{.HTitleText1} {#h-277177 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Definitions

**[[2]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-2 .sectionLabel}** The following definitions
apply in this Act.

[[Canadian offender]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[Canadian offender]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means a Canadian citizen
    within the meaning of the [Citizenship Act](/eng/acts/C-29) who has
    been found guilty of an offence --- and is detained, subject to
    supervision by reason of conditional release or probation or subject
    to any other form of supervision in a foreign entity --- and whose
    verdict and sentence may no longer be appealed. ([délinquant
    canadien]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

[[criminal offence]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[criminal offence]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means an offence against an
    Act of Parliament. ([infraction criminelle]{.DefinedTermLink
    lang="fr"})

[[foreign entity]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[foreign entity]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}, other than in sections 31
    and 32, means a foreign state --- or a province, state or other
    political subdivision of a foreign state, a colony, dependency,
    possession, protectorate, condominium, trust territory or any
    territory falling under the jurisdiction of a foreign state or a
    territory or other entity, including an international criminal
    tribunal --- with which Canada has entered into a treaty on the
    transfer of offenders or an administrative arrangement referred to
    in section 31 or 32. ([entité étrangère]{.DefinedTermLink
    lang="fr"})

[[foreign offender]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[foreign offender]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means a citizen or national
    of a foreign entity who has been found guilty of a criminal offence
    --- and is detained, subject to supervision by reason of conditional
    release or probation or subject to any other form of supervision in
    Canada --- and whose verdict and sentence may no longer be
    appealed. ([délinquant étranger]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

[[Minister]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[Minister]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means the Minister of Public Safety
    and Emergency Preparedness. ([ministre]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

[[penitentiary]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[penitentiary]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} has the same meaning as in
    subsection 2(1) of the [Corrections and Conditional Release
    Act](/eng/acts/C-44.6). ([pénitencier]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

[[prison]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[prison]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means a place of confinement other
    than a penitentiary. ([prison]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

[[treaty]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[treaty]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} includes an international agreement
    or convention, but does not include an administrative arrangement
    entered into under section 31 or 32. ([traité]{.DefinedTermLink
    lang="fr"})

## [Purpose and Principles]{.HTitleText1} {#h-277190 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Purpose

**[[3]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-3 .sectionLabel}** The purpose of this Act is
to enhance public safety and to contribute to the administration of
justice and the rehabilitation of offenders and their reintegration into
the community by enabling offenders to serve their sentences in the
country of which they are citizens or nationals.

::: HistoricalNote
- 2004, c. 21, s. 3
- 2012, c. 1, s. 135

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Dual criminality

- **[[4]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-4 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Subject
  to subsection (3), a transfer is not available unless the Canadian
  offender's conduct would have constituted a criminal offence if it had
  occurred in Canada at the time the Minister receives the request for a
  transfer.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Conduct determinative

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} For greater certainty, it is not relevant whether the
  conduct referred to in subsection (1) is named, defined or
  characterized by the foreign entity in the same way as it is in
  Canada.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Exception --- children

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} A transfer is available to a Canadian offender who,
  at the time the offence was committed, was a child within the meaning
  of the [Youth Criminal Justice Act](/eng/acts/Y-1.5) even if their
  conduct would not have constituted a criminal offence if it had
  occurred in Canada at that time. That offender may not be detained in
  Canada.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Effect of transfer

- **[[5]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-5 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A
  transfer may not have the effect of increasing a sentence imposed by a
  foreign entity or of invalidating a guilty verdict rendered, or a
  sentence imposed, by a foreign entity. The verdict and the sentence,
  if any, are not subject to any appeal or other form of review in
  Canada.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Evidence

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} A document supplied by a foreign entity that sets out
  a finding of guilt and a sentence, if any, and purports to be signed
  by a judicial official or a director of a place of confinement in the
  foreign entity is proof of the facts alleged, in the absence of
  evidence to the contrary and without proof of the signature or
  official character of the person appearing to have signed it.

## [Minister]{.HTitleText1} {#h-277207 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Administration of Act

- **[[6]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-6 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  Minister is responsible for the administration of this Act.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Designation by Minister

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The Minister may, in writing, designate, by name or
  position, a staff member within the meaning of subsection 2(1) of the
  [Corrections and Conditional Release Act](/eng/acts/C-44.6) to act on
  the Minister's behalf under section 8, 12, 15, 24, 30 or 37.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Request for transfer

**[[7]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-7 .sectionLabel}** A person may not be
transferred under a treaty, or an administrative arrangement entered
into under section 31 or 32, unless a request is made, in writing, to
the Minister.

## [Consent]{.HTitleText1} {#h-277215 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Consent of three parties

- **[[8]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-8 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  consent of the three parties to a transfer --- the offender, the
  foreign entity and Canada --- is required.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Withdrawal of consent

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} A foreign offender --- and, subject to the laws of
  the foreign entity, a Canadian offender --- may withdraw their consent
  at any time before the transfer takes place.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Information about treaties

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} The Minister or the relevant provincial authority, as
  the case may be, shall inform a foreign offender, and the Minister
  shall take all reasonable steps to inform a Canadian offender, of the
  substance of any treaty --- or administrative arrangement entered into
  under section 31 or 32 --- that applies to them.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Information about sentence and other
  obligations

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} The Minister

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} shall inform a Canadian offender, in writing, as to
    how their foreign sentence is to be served in Canada and, in the
    case of an offender who is required to comply with the [Sex Offender
    Information Registration Act](/eng/acts/S-8.7),

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} inform them, in writing, of that obligation and
      of sections 4 to 7.1 of that Act and sections 490.031 and 490.0311
      of the [Criminal Code](/eng/acts/C-46), and

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} on the day of the transfer at the earliest,
      deliver a copy of Form 1 of the schedule to

      - [(A)]{.lawlabel} the offender,

      - [(B)]{.lawlabel} the Attorney General of the province, or the
        minister of justice of the territory, in which the person is to
        be detained in custody, and

      - [(C)]{.lawlabel} the person in charge of the place in which the
        person is to be detained in custody; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} shall deliver to a foreign offender the information
    with which the Minister was provided by the foreign entity as to how
    their Canadian sentence is to be served.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Person authorized to consent

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} In respect of the following persons, consent is given
  by whoever is authorized to consent in accordance with the laws of the
  province where the person is detained, is released on conditions or is
  to be transferred:

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} a child or young person within the meaning of the
    [Youth Criminal Justice Act](/eng/acts/Y-1.5);

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} a person who is not able to consent and in respect
    of whom a verdict of not criminally responsible on account of mental
    disorder or of unfit to stand trial has been rendered; and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} an offender who is not able to consent.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Provincial authority

- **[[9]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-9 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} If a
  foreign offender is --- or a Canadian offender would, after their
  transfer, be --- under the authority of a province or if a Canadian
  offender is a child within the meaning of the [Youth Criminal Justice
  Act](/eng/acts/Y-1.5), the consent of the Minister and the relevant
  provincial authority is required.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Purpose and principles

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} In determining whether to consent to a transfer, the
  provincial authority shall take into account the purpose and
  principles of this Act.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Factors --- Canadian offenders

- **[[10]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-10 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} In
  determining whether to consent to the transfer of a Canadian offender,
  the Minister may consider the following factors:

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} whether, in the Minister's opinion, the offender's
    return to Canada will constitute a threat to the security of Canada;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} whether, in the Minister's opinion, the offender's
    return to Canada will endanger public safety, including

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} the safety of any person in Canada who is a
      victim, as defined in subsection 2(1) of the [Corrections and
      Conditional Release Act](/eng/acts/C-44.6), of an offence
      committed by the offender,

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} the safety of any member of the offender's
      family, in the case of an offender who has been convicted of an
      offence against a family member, or

    - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} the safety of any child, in the case of an
      offender who has been convicted of a sexual offence involving a
      child;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} whether, in the Minister's opinion, the offender is
    likely to continue to engage in criminal activity after the
    transfer;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} whether, in the Minister's opinion, the offender
    left or remained outside Canada with the intention of abandoning
    Canada as their place of permanent residence;

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} whether, in the Minister's opinion, the foreign
    entity or its prison system presents a serious threat to the
    offender's security or human rights;

  - [(f)]{.lawlabel} whether the offender has social or family ties in
    Canada;

  - [(g)]{.lawlabel} the offender's health;

  - [(h)]{.lawlabel} whether the offender has refused to participate in
    a rehabilitation or reintegration program;

  - [(i)]{.lawlabel} whether the offender has accepted responsibility
    for the offence for which they have been convicted, including by
    acknowledging the harm done to victims and to the community;

  - [(j)]{.lawlabel} the manner in which the offender will be
    supervised, after the transfer, while they are serving their
    sentence;

  - [(k)]{.lawlabel} whether the offender has cooperated, or has
    undertaken to cooperate, with a law enforcement agency; or

  - [(l)]{.lawlabel} any other factor that the Minister considers
    relevant.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Factors --- Canadian and foreign
  offenders

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} In determining whether to consent to the transfer of
  a Canadian or foreign offender, the Minister may consider the
  following factors:

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} whether, in the Minister's opinion, the offender
    will, after the transfer, commit a terrorism offence or criminal
    organization offence within the meaning of section 2 of the
    [Criminal Code](/eng/acts/C-46); and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} whether the offender was previously transferred
    under this Act or the [Transfer of Offenders Act](/eng/acts/T-15),
    chapter T-15 of the Revised Statutes of Canada, 1985.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Additional factor --- Canadian young
  persons

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} In determining whether to consent to the transfer of
  a Canadian offender who is a young person within the meaning of the
  [Youth Criminal Justice Act](/eng/acts/Y-1.5), the Minister and the
  relevant provincial authority shall consider the best interests of the
  young person.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Primary consideration --- Canadian
  children

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} In determining whether to consent to the transfer of
  a Canadian offender who is a child within the meaning of the [Youth
  Criminal Justice Act](/eng/acts/Y-1.5), the primary consideration of
  the Minister and the relevant provincial authority is to be the best
  interests of the child.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Writing

- **[[11]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-11 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A
  consent, a refusal of consent or a withdrawal of consent is to be
  given in writing.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Reasons

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} If the Minister does not consent to a transfer, the
  Minister shall give reasons.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Consent voluntary

**[[12]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-12 .sectionLabel}** The Minister shall take
all reasonable steps to determine whether an offender's consent has been
given voluntarily.

## [Continued Enforcement and Adaptation]{.HTitleText1} {#h-277279 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Continued enforcement

**[[13]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-13 .sectionLabel}** The enforcement of a
Canadian offender's sentence is to be continued in accordance with the
laws of Canada as if the offender had been convicted and their sentence
imposed by a court in Canada.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Adaptation

**[[14]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-14 .sectionLabel}** Subject to subsection
17(1) and section 18, if, at the time the Minister receives a request
for the transfer of a Canadian offender, the sentence imposed by the
foreign entity is longer than the maximum sentence provided for in
Canadian law for the equivalent offence, the Canadian offender is to
serve only the shorter sentence.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Equivalent offence

**[[15]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-15 .sectionLabel}** For the purposes of the
application of any Act of Parliament to a Canadian offender, the
Minister shall identify the criminal offence that, at the time the
Minister receives their request for a transfer, is equivalent to the
offence of which the Canadian offender was convicted.

## [Probation]{.HTitleText1} {#h-277286 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Deemed probation order

**[[16]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-16 .sectionLabel}** A foreign sentence that
consists of a period of supervision, other than by reason of conditional
release --- or a period of supervision that is, other than by reason of
a conditional release, an element of a foreign sentence of imprisonment
of less than two years --- is deemed to be a probation order under
section 731 of the [Criminal Code](/eng/acts/C-46), to a maximum of
three years, or under paragraph 42(2)(k) of the [Youth Criminal Justice
Act](/eng/acts/Y-1.5), to a maximum of two years.

## [Young Persons]{.HTitleText1} {#h-277289 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Transfer of young person --- 12 or 13
years old

- **[[17]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-17
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Subject to subsection (2), and if
  the following conditions are met, the maximum sentence to be enforced
  in Canada is the maximum youth sentence that could have been imposed
  under the [Youth Criminal Justice Act](/eng/acts/Y-1.5):

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the Canadian offender was, at the time the offence
    was committed, 12 or 13 years old; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} their sentence is longer than the maximum youth
    sentence that could have been imposed under that Act for an
    equivalent offence.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Sentence for young person convicted of
  murder --- 12 or 13 years old

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} A Canadian offender who was 12 or 13 years old at the
  time the offence was committed and whose conduct, if it had occurred
  in Canada, would have constituted first or second degree murder within
  the meaning of section 231 of the [Criminal Code](/eng/acts/C-46) is
  required to serve

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the sentence imposed by the foreign entity --- if
    less than ten years, in the case of first degree murder, or less
    than seven years, in the case of second degree murder ---
    consisting, in the same proportion as in paragraph 42(2)(q) of the
    [Youth Criminal Justice Act](/eng/acts/Y-1.5), of a committal to
    custody and a placement under conditional supervision to be served
    in the community; or

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the maximum sentence that could be imposed under
    paragraph 42(2)(q) of that Act if the sentence imposed by the
    foreign entity was ten years or more in the case of first degree
    murder or seven years or more in the case of second degree murder.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Transfer of young person --- 14 to 17
years old

**[[18]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-18 .sectionLabel}** A Canadian offender is
deemed to be serving an adult sentence within the meaning of the [Youth
Criminal Justice Act](/eng/acts/Y-1.5) if

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} the Canadian offender was, at the time the offence
  was committed, from 14 to 17 years old; and

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} their sentence is longer than the maximum youth
  sentence that could have been imposed under that Act for an equivalent
  offence.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Parole eligibility for young person
convicted of murder --- 14 to 17 years old

- **[[19]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-19 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A
  Canadian offender who was from 14 to 17 years old at the time the
  offence was committed, and who was sentenced to imprisonment for life
  for conduct that, if it had occurred in Canada, would have constituted
  first or second degree murder within the meaning of section 231 of the
  [Criminal Code](/eng/acts/C-46), is deemed to be serving an adult
  sentence within the meaning of the [Youth Criminal Justice
  Act](/eng/acts/Y-1.5). They are eligible for full parole on the day on
  which they have served the shorter of

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the period of ineligibility imposed by the foreign
    entity, and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} either

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} five years, if they were 14 or 15 years old at
      the time the offence was committed, or

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} ten years, in the case of first degree murder,
      or seven years, in the case of second degree murder, if they were
      16 or 17 years old at the time the offence was committed.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Deemed to have received adult sentence

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} A Canadian offender who was from 14 to 17 years old
  at the time the offence was committed and who received a sentence for
  a determinate period of more than ten years for conduct that, if it
  had occurred in Canada, would have constituted first degree murder
  within the meaning of section 231 of the [Criminal
  Code](/eng/acts/C-46) --- or of more than seven years for conduct
  that, if it had occurred in Canada, would have constituted second
  degree murder within the meaning of that section --- is deemed to have
  received an adult sentence within the meaning of the [Youth Criminal
  Justice Act](/eng/acts/Y-1.5).

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Deemed to have received youth sentence

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} A Canadian offender who was from 14 to 17 years old
  at the time the offence was committed and who received a sentence for
  a determinate period of ten years or less for conduct that, if it had
  occurred in Canada, would have constituted first degree murder within
  the meaning of section 231 of the [Criminal Code](/eng/acts/C-46) ---
  or of seven years or less for conduct that, if it had occurred in
  Canada, would have constituted second degree murder within the meaning
  of that section --- is deemed to have received a youth sentence within
  the meaning of the [Youth Criminal Justice Act](/eng/acts/Y-1.5).

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Placement

**[[20]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-20 .sectionLabel}** A Canadian offender who
was from 12 to 17 years old at the time the offence was committed is to
be detained

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} if the sentence imposed in the foreign entity could,
  if the offence had been committed in Canada, have been a youth
  sentence within the meaning of the [Youth Criminal Justice
  Act](/eng/acts/Y-1.5),

  - [(i)]{.lawlabel} in the case of an offender who was less than 20
    years old at the time of their transfer, in a youth custody facility
    within the meaning of that Act, and

  - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} in the case of an offender who was at least 20
    years old at the time of their transfer, in a provincial
    correctional facility for adults; and

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} if the sentence imposed in the foreign entity could,
  if the offence had been committed in Canada, have been an adult
  sentence within the meaning of that Act,

  - [(i)]{.lawlabel} in the case of an offender who was less than 18
    years old at the time of their transfer, in a youth custody facility
    within the meaning of that Act,

  - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} in the case of an offender who was at least 18
    years old at the time of their transfer, in a provincial
    correctional facility for adults if their sentence is less than two
    years, and

  - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} in the case of an offender who was at least 18
    years old at the time of their transfer, in a penitentiary if their
    sentence is at least two years.

## [Sentence Calculation]{.HTitleText1} {#h-277323 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Where committed

**[[21]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-21 .sectionLabel}** Subject to section 20, a
Canadian offender who was detained in a foreign entity is to be detained
in Canada in

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} a prison if they were sentenced to imprisonment for
  less than two years; or

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} a penitentiary if they were sentenced to imprisonment
  for two years or more.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Credit towards completion of sentence

- **[[22]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-22 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  length of a Canadian offender's sentence equals the length of the
  sentence imposed by the foreign entity minus any time that was, before
  their transfer, recognized by the foreign entity as a reduction, other
  than time spent in confinement after the sentence was imposed.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Credit for time spent in confinement

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The time that a Canadian offender spent in
  confinement, after the sentence was imposed and before their transfer,
  is subtracted from the length of the sentence determined in accordance
  with subsection (1). The resulting period constitutes the period that
  the offender is to serve on the sentence.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Eligibility for parole --- general

**[[23]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-23 .sectionLabel}** Subject to sections 19
and 24, a Canadian offender who is transferred to Canada is eligible for
full parole on the day on which they have served, commencing on the day
on which they commenced serving their sentence, the lesser of seven
years and one third of the length of the sentence as determined under
subsection 22(1).

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Eligibility for parole --- murder

- **[[24]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-24
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Subject to subsections 17(2) and
  19(1), if a Canadian offender was sentenced to imprisonment for life
  for an offence that, if it had been committed in Canada, would have
  constituted murder within the meaning of the [Criminal
  Code](/eng/acts/C-46), their full parole ineligibility period is 10
  years. If, in the Minister's opinion, the documents supplied by the
  foreign entity show that the circumstances in which the offence was
  committed were such that, if it had been committed in Canada after
  July 26, 1976, it would have been first degree murder within the
  meaning of section 231 of that Act, the full parole ineligibility
  period is

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} 15 years, if the offence was committed before the
    day on which paragraph 745.6(1)(a.1) of the [Criminal
    Code](/eng/acts/C-46) comes into force; or

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} 25 years, if the offence was committed on or after
    that day.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Multiple murders

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Subject to subsection (3), if a Canadian offender who
  was subject to a sentence of imprisonment for life for a conviction
  for murder, or an offence that, if it had been committed in Canada,
  would have constituted murder, within the meaning of the [Criminal
  Code](/eng/acts/C-46), received an additional sentence of imprisonment
  for life --- imposed by the foreign entity for a conviction for an
  offence that, if it had been committed in Canada, would have
  constituted murder within the meaning of that Act --- the full parole
  ineligibility period in respect of the additional sentence is
  established under section 745 of that Act.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Exception --- second degree murder

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} If the additional sentence referred to in
  subsection (2) is in respect of a conviction for an offence that, if
  it had been committed in Canada, would have constituted second degree
  murder within the meaning of section 231 of the [Criminal
  Code](/eng/acts/C-46) --- and if the offence was committed before all
  of the Canadian offender's convictions for murder, or for offences
  that, if they had been committed in Canada, would have constituted
  murder, within the meaning of that Act --- the full parole
  ineligibility period in respect of the additional sentence is 10
  years.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Credit for time spent in custody

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} In calculating the period of imprisonment for the
  purpose of this section, the time served by an offender includes any
  time spent in custody between the day on which they were arrested and
  taken into custody for the offence for which they were sentenced and
  the day on which the sentence was imposed.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Temporary absence and day parole ---
persons convicted of murder

**[[25]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-25 .sectionLabel}** Subject to section 746.1
of the [Criminal Code](/eng/acts/C-46),

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} a Canadian offender who is transferred to Canada ---
  and was sentenced to imprisonment for life for an offence that, if it
  had been committed in Canada, would have constituted murder within the
  meaning of that Act --- is eligible for day parole in accordance with
  the [Corrections and Conditional Release Act](/eng/acts/C-44.6) and
  for an absence without escort in accordance with the [Corrections and
  Conditional Release Act](/eng/acts/C-44.6) or the [Prisons and
  Reformatories Act](/eng/acts/P-20); and

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} their absence with escort may be authorized in
  accordance with the [Corrections and Conditional Release
  Act](/eng/acts/C-44.6) or the [Prisons and Reformatories
  Act](/eng/acts/P-20).

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Statutory release --- penitentiary

- **[[26]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-26 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} If a
  Canadian offender is detained in a penitentiary, they are entitled to
  be released on statutory release on the day on which they have served,
  commencing on the day of their transfer, two thirds of the period
  determined in accordance with subsection 22(2).

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Release --- prison

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} If a Canadian offender is detained in a prison, they
  are entitled to be released on the day on which they have served,
  commencing on the day of their transfer, the period determined in
  accordance with subsection 22(2) less the amount of any remission
  earned under the [Prisons and Reformatories Act](/eng/acts/P-20) on
  that period.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}If eligible for parole, etc., before
transfer

**[[27]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-27 .sectionLabel}** If, under the
[Corrections and Conditional Release Act](/eng/acts/C-44.6) or the
[Criminal Code](/eng/acts/C-46), the day on which a Canadian offender is
eligible for a temporary absence, day parole or full parole is before
the day of their transfer, the day of their transfer is deemed to be
their day of eligibility.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Review by Board

**[[28]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-28 .sectionLabel}** Despite sections 122 and
123 of the [Corrections and Conditional Release Act](/eng/acts/C-44.6),
the Parole Board of Canada is not required to review the case of a
Canadian offender until six months after the day of their transfer.

- 2012, c. 1, s. 160

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Application

- **[[29]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-29
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Subject to this Act, a Canadian
  offender who is transferred to Canada is subject to the [Corrections
  and Conditional Release Act](/eng/acts/C-44.6), the [Prisons and
  Reformatories Act](/eng/acts/P-20) and the [Youth Criminal Justice
  Act](/eng/acts/Y-1.5) as if they had been convicted and their sentence
  imposed by a court in Canada.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Canadian sentence

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} If, before the transfer, a Canadian offender is
  subject to a Canadian sentence of imprisonment, they are

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} eligible for full parole on the later of

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} the day established in accordance with section
      19, 23 or 24, as the case may be, and

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} the full parole eligibility date established
      under the [Corrections and Conditional Release
      Act](/eng/acts/C-44.6); and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} entitled to statutory release on the later of

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} the day established in accordance with section
      26, and

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} the statutory release date established under
      that Act.

## [Compassionate Measures]{.HTitleText1} {#h-277374 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Canadian offender

- **[[30]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-30 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A
  Canadian offender shall benefit from any compassionate measures ---
  including a cancellation of their conviction or shortening of their
  sentence --- taken by a foreign entity after the transfer.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Foreign offender

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The Minister shall take all reasonable steps to
  inform the foreign entity and the foreign offender of any
  compassionate measures taken by Canada after the transfer.

## [Administrative Arrangements]{.HTitleText1} {#h-277380 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Administrative arrangements --- offenders

**[[31]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-31 .sectionLabel}** If no treaty is in force
between Canada and a foreign entity on the transfer of offenders, the
Minister of Foreign Affairs may, with the consent of the Minister, enter
into an administrative arrangement with the foreign entity for the
transfer of an offender in accordance with this Act.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Administrative arrangements --- mentally
disordered persons

- **[[32]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-32 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} If the
  relevant provincial authority consents to the transfer, the Minister
  of Foreign Affairs may, with the consent of the Minister, enter into
  an administrative arrangement with a foreign entity for the transfer,
  in accordance with this Act, of a person in respect of whom a verdict
  of unfit to stand trial or not criminally responsible on account of
  mental disorder was rendered and may no longer be appealed.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Consent --- provincial authority

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The consent of a provincial authority to a transfer
  under this section shall take into account the purpose and principles
  of this Act. Consent to the transfer of a person in respect of whom a
  verdict of not criminally responsible on account of mental disorder
  has been rendered --- or of a citizen or national of a foreign entity
  in respect of whom a verdict of unfit to stand trial has been rendered
  --- is given by the attorney general of a province or, in the case of
  a territory, the Attorney General of Canada, on the recommendation of
  the relevant Review Board established under section 672.38 of the
  [Criminal Code](/eng/acts/C-46). Consent to the transfer of a Canadian
  citizen in respect of whom a verdict of unfit to stand trial has been
  rendered in a foreign entity is given by the relevant provincial
  authority.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Factors --- provincial authority

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} A Review Board, in deciding whether to recommend to
  the attorney general that a person be transferred --- and the relevant
  provincial authority, in deciding whether to consent to a transfer
  under subsection (2) --- shall consider the following factors:

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the best interests of the person, including their
    mental condition, the likelihood of their reintegration into society
    and their treatment and other needs; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the need to protect society from dangerous persons.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Additional factor --- unfit to stand
  trial

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} The attorney general, in deciding whether to consent
  to the transfer to a foreign entity of a person in respect of whom a
  verdict of unfit to stand trial has been rendered, shall consider
  their ability to effectively prosecute the case in the event that the
  person becomes fit to stand trial.

Definition of [[foreign entity]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

**[[33]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-33 .sectionLabel}** In sections 31 and 32,
[[foreign entity]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means a foreign state, a
province, state or other political subdivision of a foreign state, a
colony, dependency, possession, protectorate, condominium, trust
territory or any territory falling under the jurisdiction of a foreign
state or a territory or other entity, including an international
criminal tribunal.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Part XX.1 of [Criminal
Code](/eng/acts/C-46)

- **[[34]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-34
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Subject to the other provisions of
  this Act --- and, in the case of a young person, section 141 of the
  [Youth Criminal Justice Act](/eng/acts/Y-1.5) --- Part XX.1 of the
  [Criminal Code](/eng/acts/C-46) applies to a person who is transferred
  to Canada under an administrative arrangement that was entered into
  under section 32. The verdict of the foreign court is deemed to be a
  verdict of not criminally responsible on account of mental disorder
  and to have been made on the day of their transfer.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Presumption

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The person is deemed to be the subject of an order
  under paragraph 672.54(c) of the [Criminal Code](/eng/acts/C-46) and a
  warrant of committal under section 672.57 of that Act until the Review
  Board of the province to which the person is transferred makes a
  disposition under section 672.47 of that Act. The Review Board shall,
  within 45 days after the day of the person's transfer, hold a hearing
  and make a disposition.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Extension of time period

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} If the Review Board is of the opinion that there are
  exceptional circumstances that warrant it, it may take a maximum of 90
  days to hold a hearing and make a disposition.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Transportation for transfer

- **[[35]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-35 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A
  person who is discharged under paragraph 672.54(b) of the [Criminal
  Code](/eng/acts/C-46) or detained under paragraph 672.54(c) of that
  Act may --- with the consent of the attorney general of the province
  from which they are to be transported and, if applicable, the attorney
  general of the province to which they are to be transported --- be
  transported to any other place in Canada in order to expedite their
  transfer to a foreign entity.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Warrant

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} If a person is to be transported in order to expedite
  their transfer, an officer authorized by the attorney general of the
  province from which they are to be transported shall sign a warrant
  specifying the place in Canada to which they are to be transported,
  the terms of their transfer and, if applicable, the place of
  detention.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Territories

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} For the purpose of this section, in respect of a
  territory, the relevant attorney general is the Attorney General of
  Canada.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Transportation and detention

**[[36]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-36 .sectionLabel}** A warrant referred to in
subsection 35(2) is sufficient authority for

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} the person who is responsible for the custody and
  transportation of the person being transferred to convey them to the
  place in Canada to which they are to be transported and, if
  applicable, deliver them to the person in charge of the place of
  detention;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} the person in charge of the place of detention to
  detain the person being transferred; and

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} the person who is responsible for the custody and
  transportation of the person being transferred to deliver them to the
  person from the foreign entity who is responsible for the transfer.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Duration of obligation

    imprisonment provided for in Canadian law for the equivalent

## [General Provision]{.HTitleText1} {#h-277448 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Transfer to Canada not valid

- **[[37]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-37 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  foreign sentence of a person transferred to Canada under this Act is
  enforceable in Canada unless a court determines that, because the
  person is not a Canadian citizen, the transfer is not valid.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Minister to notify foreign entity and
  other ministers

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} If the court declares that the transfer of the person
  to Canada is not valid, the Minister shall notify the foreign entity,
  the minister responsible for the [Immigration and Refugee Protection
  Act](/eng/acts/I-2.5) and the minister responsible for the
  [Extradition Act](/eng/acts/E-23.01) that the transfer is not valid.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Transfer to foreign entity not valid

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} If a foreign entity declares that the transfer of a
  foreign offender is not valid, the Canadian sentence that they were
  serving before the transfer is enforceable in Canada.

respect of all requests for transfer that are pending on the day that
