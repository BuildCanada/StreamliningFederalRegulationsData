## Interpretation Act {#interpretation-act .Title-of-Act}

An Act respecting the interpretation of statutes and regulations

## [Short Title]{.HTitleText1} {#h-279141 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Short title

**[[1]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-1 .sectionLabel}** This Act may be cited as
the [Interpretation Act](/eng/acts/I-21).

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. I-23, s. 1
:::

## [Interpretation]{.HTitleText1} {#h-279146 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Definitions

- **[[2]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-2 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} In this
  Act,

  [[Act]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[Act]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means an Act of
      Parliament; ([loi]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

  [[enact]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[enact]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} includes to issue, make or
      establish; ([Version anglaise
      seulement]{.DefinitionOtherLangOnly})

  [[enactment]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[enactment]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means an Act or regulation or
      any portion of an Act or regulation; ([texte]{.DefinedTermLink
      lang="fr"})

  [[public officer]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[public officer]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} includes any person in the
      federal public administration who is authorized by or under an
      enactment to do or enforce the doing of an act or thing or to
      exercise a power, or on whom a duty is imposed by or under an
      enactment; ([fonctionnaire public]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

  [[regulation]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[regulation]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} includes an order, regulation,
      rule, rule of court, form, tariff of costs or fees, letters
      patent, commission, warrant, proclamation, by-law, resolution or
      other instrument issued, made or established

      - [(a)]{.lawlabel} in the execution of a power conferred by or
        under the authority of an Act, or

      - [(b)]{.lawlabel} by or under the authority of the Governor in
        Council; ([règlement]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

  [[repeal]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[repeal]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} includes revoke or
      cancel. ([Version anglaise seulement]{.DefinitionOtherLangOnly})

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Expired and replaced enactments

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} For the purposes of this Act, an enactment that has
  been replaced is repealed and an enactment that has expired, lapsed or
  otherwise ceased to have effect is deemed to have been repealed.

## [Application]{.HTitleText1} {#h-279162 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Application

- **[[3]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-3 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Every
  provision of this Act applies, unless a contrary intention appears, to
  every enactment, whether enacted before or after the commencement of
  this Act.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Application to this Act

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The provisions of this Act apply to the
  interpretation of this Act.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Rules of construction not excluded

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} Nothing in this Act excludes the application to an
  enactment of a rule of construction applicable to that enactment and
  not inconsistent with this Act.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. I-23, s. 3
:::

## [Enacting Clause of Acts]{.HTitleText1} {#h-279172 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Enacting clause

- **[[4]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-4 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  enacting clause of an Act may be in the following form:

  "Her Majesty, by and with the advice and consent of the Senate and
  House of Commons of Canada, enacts as follows:".

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Order of clauses

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The enacting clause of an Act shall follow the
  preamble, if any, and the various provisions within the purview or
  body of the Act shall follow in a concise and enunciative form.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. I-23, s. 4
:::

## [Operation]{.HTitleText1} {#h-279181 .Part}

### [Royal Assent]{.HTitleText2} {#h-279182 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Royal assent

- **[[5]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-5 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  Clerk of the Parliaments shall endorse on every Act, immediately after
  its title, the day, month and year when the Act was assented to in Her
  Majesty's name and the endorsement shall be a part of the Act.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Date of commencement

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} If no date of commencement is provided for in an Act,
  the date of commencement of that Act is the date of assent to the Act.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Commencement provision

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} Where an Act contains a provision that the Act or any
  portion thereof is to come into force on a day later than the date of
  assent to the Act, that provision is deemed to have come into force on
  the date of assent to the Act.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Commencement when no date fixed

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} Where an Act provides that certain provisions thereof
  are to come or are deemed to have come into force on a day other than
  the date of assent to the Act, the remaining provisions of the Act are
  deemed to have come into force on the date of assent to the Act.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. I-23, s. 5
:::

### [Day Fixed for Commencement or Repeal]{.HTitleText2} {#h-279194 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Operation when date fixed for
commencement or repeal

- **[[6]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-6 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Where an
  enactment is expressed to come into force on a particular day, it
  shall be construed as coming into force on the expiration of the
  previous day, and where an enactment is expressed to expire, lapse or
  otherwise cease to have effect on a particular day, it shall be
  construed as ceasing to have effect on the commencement of the
  following day.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}When no date fixed

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Every enactment that is not expressed to come into
  force on a particular day shall be construed as coming into force

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} in the case of an Act, on the expiration of the day
    immediately before the day the Act was assented to in Her Majesty's
    name; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} in the case of a regulation, on the expiration of
    the day immediately before the day the regulation was registered
    pursuant to section 6 of the [Statutory Instruments
    Act](/eng/acts/S-22) or, if the regulation is of a class that is
    exempted from the application of subsection 5(1) of that Act, on the
    expiration of the day immediately before the day the regulation was
    made.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Judicial notice

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} Judicial notice shall be taken of a day for the
  coming into force of an enactment that is fixed by a regulation that
  has been published in the [Canada Gazette](http://www.gazette.gc.ca/).

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., 1985, c. I-21, s. 6
- 1992, c. 1, s. 87
:::

### [Regulation Prior to Commencement]{.HTitleText2} {#h-279206 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Preliminary proceedings

**[[7]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-7 .sectionLabel}** Where an enactment is not
in force and it contains provisions conferring power to make regulations
or do any other thing, that power may, for the purpose of making the
enactment effective on its commencement, be exercised at any time before
its commencement, but a regulation so made or a thing so done has no
effect until the commencement of the enactment, except in so far as may
be necessary to make the enactment effective on its commencement.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. I-23, s. 7
:::

### [Territorial Operation]{.HTitleText2} {#h-279211 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Territorial operation

- **[[8]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-8 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Every
  enactment applies to the whole of Canada, unless a contrary intention
  is expressed in the enactment.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Amending enactment

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Where an enactment that does not apply to the whole
  of Canada is amended, no provision in the amending enactment applies
  to any part of Canada to which the amended enactment does not apply,
  unless it is provided in the amending enactment that it applies to
  that part of Canada or to the whole of Canada.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Exclusive economic zone of Canada

  [(2.1)]{.lawlabel} Every enactment that applies in respect of
  exploring or exploiting, conserving or managing natural resources,
  whether living or non-living, applies, in addition to its application
  to Canada, to the exclusive economic zone of Canada, unless a contrary
  intention is expressed in the enactment.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Continental shelf of Canada

  [(2.2)]{.lawlabel} Every enactment that applies in respect of
  exploring or exploiting natural resources that are

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} mineral or other non-living resources of the seabed
    or subsoil, or

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} living organisms belonging to sedentary species,
    that is to say, organisms that, at the harvestable stage, either are
    immobile on or under the seabed or are unable to move except in
    constant physical contact with the seabed or subsoil

  applies, in addition to its application to Canada, to the continental
  shelf of Canada, unless a contrary intention is expressed in the
  enactment.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Extra-territorial operation

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} Every Act now in force enacted prior to December 11,
  1931 that expressly or by necessary or reasonable implication was
  intended, as to the whole or any part thereof, to have
  extra-territorial operation shall be construed as if, at the date of
  its enactment, the Parliament of Canada had full power to make laws
  having extra-territorial operation as provided by the Statute of
  Westminster, 1931.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., 1985, c. I-21, s. 8
- 1996, c. 31, s. 86
:::

## [Rules of Construction]{.HTitleText1} {#h-279228 .Part}

### [Property and Civil Rights]{.HTitleText2} {#h-279229 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Duality of legal traditions and
application of provincial law

**[[8.1]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-8.1 .sectionLabel}** Both the common law and
the civil law are equally authoritative and recognized sources of the
law of property and civil rights in Canada and, unless otherwise
provided by law, if in interpreting an enactment it is necessary to
refer to a province's rules, principles or concepts forming part of the
law of property and civil rights, reference must be made to the rules,
principles and concepts in force in the province at the time the
enactment is being applied.

::: HistoricalNote
- 2001, c. 4, s. 8
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Terminology

**[[8.2]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-8.2 .sectionLabel}** Unless otherwise
provided by law, when an enactment contains both civil law and common
law terminology, or terminology that has a different meaning in the
civil law and the common law, the civil law terminology or meaning is to
be adopted in the Province of Quebec and the common law terminology or
meaning is to be adopted in the other provinces.

::: HistoricalNote
- 2001, c. 4, s. 8
:::

### [Indigenous Peoples]{.HTitleText2} {#h-1497703 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Rights of Indigenous peoples

- **[[8.3]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-8.3
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Every enactment is to be construed
  as upholding the Aboriginal and treaty rights of Indigenous peoples
  recognized and affirmed by section 35 of the [Constitution Act,
  1982](/eng/Const/), and not as abrogating or derogating from them.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Indigenous peoples

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} For the purposes of subsection (1), [[Indigenous
  peoples]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} has the meaning assigned by the
  definition [[aboriginal peoples of Canada]{.dfn}]{.DefinitionRef} in
  subsection 35(2) of the [Constitution Act, 1982](/eng/Const/).

::: HistoricalNote
- [2024, c. 30, s. 1](#1496380-1497710){.wb-lbx
  aria-controls="centred-popup" ajax-after="1496380-1497710.html"
  role="button"}
:::

### [Private Acts]{.HTitleText2} {#h-279238 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Provisions in private Acts

**[[9]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-9 .sectionLabel}** No provision in a private
Act affects the rights of any person, except as therein mentioned or
referred to.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. I-23, s. 9
:::

### [Law Always Speaking]{.HTitleText2} {#h-279243 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Law always speaking

**[[10]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-10 .sectionLabel}** The law shall be
considered as always speaking, and where a matter or thing is expressed
in the present tense, it shall be applied to the circumstances as they
arise, so that effect may be given to the enactment according to its
true spirit, intent and meaning.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. I-23, s. 10
:::

### [Imperative and Permissive Construction]{.HTitleText2} {#h-279248 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}"Shall" and "may"

**[[11]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-11 .sectionLabel}** The expression "shall" is
to be construed as imperative and the expression "may" as permissive.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. I-23, s. 28
:::

### [Enactments Remedial]{.HTitleText2} {#h-279253 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Enactments deemed remedial

**[[12]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-12 .sectionLabel}** Every enactment is deemed
remedial, and shall be given such fair, large and liberal construction
and interpretation as best ensures the attainment of its objects.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. I-23, s. 11
:::

### [Preambles and Marginal Notes]{.HTitleText2} {#h-279258 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Preamble

**[[13]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-13 .sectionLabel}** The preamble of an
enactment shall be read as a part of the enactment intended to assist in
explaining its purport and object.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. I-23, s. 12
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Marginal notes and historical references

**[[14]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-14 .sectionLabel}** Marginal notes and
references to former enactments that appear after the end of a section
or other division in an enactment form no part of the enactment, but are
inserted for convenience of reference only.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. I-23, s. 13
:::

### [Application of Interpretation Provisions]{.HTitleText2} {#h-279267 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Application of definitions and
interpretation rules

- **[[15]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-15
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Definitions or rules of
  interpretation in an enactment apply to all the provisions of the
  enactment, including the provisions that contain those definitions or
  rules of interpretation.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Interpretation sections subject to
  exceptions

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Where an enactment contains an interpretation section
  or provision, it shall be read and construed

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} as being applicable only if a contrary intention
    does not appear; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} as being applicable to all other enactments
    relating to the same subject-matter unless a contrary intention
    appears.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. I-23, s. 14
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Words in regulations

**[[16]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-16 .sectionLabel}** Where an enactment
confers power to make regulations, expressions used in the regulations
have the same respective meanings as in the enactment conferring the
power.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. I-23, s. 15
:::

### [Her Majesty]{.HTitleText2} {#h-279281 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Her Majesty not bound or affected unless
stated

**[[17]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-17 .sectionLabel}** No enactment is binding
on Her Majesty or affects Her Majesty or Her Majesty's rights or
prerogatives in any manner, except as mentioned or referred to in the
enactment.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. I-23, s. 16
:::

### [Proclamations]{.HTitleText2} {#h-279286 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Proclamation

- **[[18]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-18 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Where
  an enactment authorizes the issue of a proclamation, the proclamation
  shall be understood to be a proclamation of the Governor in Council.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Proclamation to be issued on advice

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Where the Governor General is authorized to issue a
  proclamation, the proclamation shall be understood to be a
  proclamation issued under an order of the Governor in Council, but it
  is not necessary to mention in the proclamation that it is issued
  under such an order.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Effective day of proclamations

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} A proclamation that is issued under an order of the
  Governor in Council may purport to have been issued on the day of the
  order or on any subsequent day and, if so, takes effect on that day.

- [(4)]{.lawlabel} [\[Repealed, 1992, c. 1, s. 88\]]{.Repealed}

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., 1985, c. I-21, s. 18
- 1992, c. 1, s. 88
:::

### [Oaths]{.HTitleText2} {#h-279297 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Administration of oaths

- **[[19]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-19 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Where,
  by an enactment or by a rule of the Senate or House of Commons,
  evidence under oath is authorized or required to be taken, or an oath
  is authorized or directed to be made, taken or administered, the oath
  may be administered, and a certificate of its having been made, taken
  or administered may be given by

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} any person authorized by the enactment or rule to
    take the evidence; or

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} a judge of any court, a notary public, a justice of
    the peace or a commissioner for taking affidavits, having authority
    or jurisdiction within the place where the oath is administered.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Where justice of peace empowered

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Where power is conferred on a justice of the peace to
  administer an oath or solemn affirmation or to take an affidavit or
  declaration, the power may be exercised by a notary public or a
  commissioner for taking oaths.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. I-23, s. 18
:::

### [Reports to Parliament]{.HTitleText2} {#h-279307 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Reports to Parliament

**[[20]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-20 .sectionLabel}** Where an Act requires a
report or other document to be laid before Parliament and, in compliance
with the Act, a particular report or document has been laid before
Parliament at a session thereof, nothing in the Act shall be construed
as requiring the same report or document to be laid before Parliament at
any subsequent session.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. I-23, s. 19
:::

### [Corporations]{.HTitleText2} {#h-279312 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Powers vested in corporations

- **[[21]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-21 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Words
  establishing a corporation shall be construed

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} as vesting in the corporation power to sue and be
    sued, to contract and be contracted with by its corporate name, to
    have a common seal and to alter or change it at pleasure, to have
    perpetual succession, to acquire and hold personal property for the
    purposes for which the corporation is established and to alienate
    that property at pleasure;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} in the case of a corporation having a name
    consisting of an English and a French form or a combined English and
    French form, as vesting in the corporation power to use either the
    English or the French form of its name or both forms and to show on
    its seal both the English and French forms of its name or have two
    seals, one showing the English and the other showing the French form
    of its name;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} as vesting in a majority of the members of the
    corporation the power to bind the others by their acts; and

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} as exempting from personal liability for its debts,
    obligations or acts individual members of the corporation who do not
    contravene the provisions of the enactment establishing the
    corporation.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Corporate name

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Where an enactment establishes a corporation and in
  each of the English and French versions of the enactment the name of
  the corporation is in the form only of the language of that version,
  the name of the corporation shall consist of the form of its name in
  each of the versions of the enactment.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Banking business

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} No corporation is deemed to be authorized to carry on
  the business of banking unless that power is expressly conferred on it
  by the enactment establishing the corporation.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. I-23, s. 20
:::

### [Majority and Quorum]{.HTitleText2} {#h-279326 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Majorities

- **[[22]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-22 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Where
  an enactment requires or authorizes more than two persons to do an act
  or thing, a majority of them may do it.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Quorum of board, court, commission,
  etc.

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Where an enactment establishes a board, court,
  commission or other body consisting of three or more members, in this
  section called an "association",

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} at a meeting of the association, a number of
    members of the association equal to,

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} if the number of members provided for by the
      enactment is a fixed number, at least one-half of the number of
      members, and

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} if the number of members provided for by the
      enactment is not a fixed number but is within a range having a
      maximum or minimum, at least one-half of the number of members in
      office if that number is within the range,

    constitutes a quorum;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} an act or thing done by a majority of the members
    of the association present at a meeting, if the members present
    constitute a quorum, is deemed to have been done by the association;
    and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} a vacancy in the membership of the association does
    not invalidate the constitution of the association or impair the
    right of the members in office to act, if the number of members in
    office is not less than a quorum.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. I-23, s. 21
:::

### [Appointment, Retirement and Powers of Officers]{.HTitleText2} {#h-279340 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Public officers hold office during
pleasure

- **[[23]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-23 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Every
  public officer appointed by or under the authority of an enactment or
  otherwise is deemed to have been appointed to hold office during
  pleasure only, unless it is otherwise expressed in the enactment,
  commission or instrument of appointment.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Effective day of appointments

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Where an appointment is made by instrument under the
  Great Seal, the instrument may purport to have been issued on or after
  the day its issue was authorized, and the day on which it so purports
  to have been issued is deemed to be the day on which the appointment
  takes effect.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Appointment or engagement otherwise
  than under Great Seal

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} Where there is authority in an enactment to appoint a
  person to a position or to engage the services of a person, otherwise
  than by instrument under the Great Seal, the instrument of appointment
  or engagement may be expressed to be effective on or after the day on
  which that person commenced the performance of the duties of the
  position or commenced the performance of the services, and the day on
  which it is so expressed to be effective, unless that day is more than
  sixty days before the day on which the instrument is issued, is deemed
  to be the day on which the appointment or engagement takes effect.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Remuneration

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} Where a person is appointed to an office, the
  appointing authority may fix, vary or terminate that person's
  remuneration.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Commencement of appointments or
  retirements

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} Where a person is appointed to an office effective on
  a specified day, or where the appointment of a person is terminated
  effective on a specified day, the appointment or termination is deemed
  to have been effected immediately on the expiration of the previous
  day.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. I-23, s. 22
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Implied powers respecting public officers

- **[[24]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-24 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Words
  authorizing the appointment of a public officer to hold office during
  pleasure include, in the discretion of the authority in whom the power
  of appointment is vested, the power to

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} terminate the appointment or remove or suspend the
    public officer;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} re-appoint or reinstate the public officer; and

### [Computation of Time]{.HTitleText2} {#h-279382 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Time limits and holidays

**[[26]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-26 .sectionLabel}** Where the time limited
for the doing of a thing expires or falls on a holiday, the thing may be
done on the day next following that is not a holiday.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., 1985, c. I-21, s. 26
- 1999, c. 31, s. 147(F)
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Clear days

- **[[27]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-27 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Where
  there is a reference to a number of clear days or "at least" a number
  of days between two events, in calculating that number of days the
  days on which the events happen are excluded.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Not clear days

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Where there is a reference to a number of days, not
  expressed to be clear days, between two events, in calculating that
  number of days the day on which the first event happens is excluded
  and the day on which the second event happens is included.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Beginning and ending of prescribed
  periods

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} Where a time is expressed to begin or end at, on or
  with a specified day, or to continue to or until a specified day, the
  time includes that day.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}After specified day

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} Where a time is expressed to begin after or to be
  from a specified day, the time does not include that day.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Within a time

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} Where anything is to be done within a time after,
  from, of or before a specified day, the time does not include that
  day.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. I-23, s. 25
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Calculation of a period of months after
or before a specified day

**[[28]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-28 .sectionLabel}** Where there is a
reference to a period of time consisting of a number of months after or
before a specified day, the period is calculated by

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} counting forward or backward from the specified day
  the number of months, without including the month in which that day
  falls;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} excluding the specified day; and

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} including in the last month counted under
  paragraph (a) the day that has the same calendar number as the
  specified day or, if that month has no day with that number, the last
  day of that month.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. I-23, s. 25
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Time of the day

**[[29]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-29 .sectionLabel}** Where there is a
reference to time expressed as a specified time of the day, the time is
taken to mean standard time.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. I-23, s. 25
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Time when specified age attained

**[[30]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-30 .sectionLabel}** A person is deemed not to
have attained a specified number of years of age until the commencement
of the anniversary, of the same number, of the day of that person's
birth.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. I-23, s. 25

### [Offences]{.HTitleText2} {#h-279440 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Indictable and summary conviction
offences

- **[[34]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-34 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Where
  an enactment creates an offence,

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the offence is deemed to be an indictable offence
    if the enactment provides that the offender may be prosecuted for
    the offence by indictment;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the offence is deemed to be one for which the
    offender is punishable on summary conviction if there is nothing in
    the context to indicate that the offence is an indictable offence;
    and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} if the offence is one for which the offender may be
    prosecuted by indictment or for which the offender is punishable on
    summary conviction, no person shall be considered to have been
    convicted of an indictable offence by reason only of having been
    convicted of the offence on summary conviction.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}[Criminal Code](/eng/acts/C-46) to
  apply

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} All the provisions of the [Criminal
  Code](/eng/acts/C-46) relating to indictable offences apply to
  indictable offences created by an enactment, and all the provisions of
  that Code relating to summary conviction offences apply to all other
  offences created by an enactment, except to the extent that the
  enactment otherwise provides.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Documents similarly construed

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} In a commission, proclamation, warrant or other
  document relating to criminal law or procedure in criminal matters,

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} a reference to an offence for which the offender
    may be prosecuted by indictment shall be construed as a reference to
    an indictable offence; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} a reference to any other offence shall be construed
    as a reference to an offence for which the offender is punishable on
    summary conviction.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. I-23, s. 27

### [Powers to Enter Dwelling-houses to Carry out Arrests]{.HTitleText2} {#h-279455 .Subheading}

  without prior announcement if the requirement of subsection 529.4(1)
  of the [Criminal Code](/eng/acts/C-46) is met.

      emission or reception of signs, signals, writing, images, sounds
      or intelligence of any nature by means of electromagnetic waves of
      frequencies lower than 3 000 GHz propagated in space without
      artificial guide; ([radiocommunication]{.DefinedTermLink
      lang="fr"} ou [radio]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

  [[regular force]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[regular force]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means the component of the
      Canadian Forces that is referred to in the [National Defence
      Act](/eng/acts/N-5) as the regular force; ([force
      régulière]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

  [[reserve force]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[reserve force]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means the component of the

      Act](/eng/acts/N-5) as the reserve force; ([force de
      réserve]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

  [[security]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[security]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means sufficient security, and
      [[sureties]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means sufficient sureties, and
      when those words are used one person is sufficient therefor,
      unless otherwise expressly required; ([caution]{.DefinedTermLink
      lang="fr"} ou [cautionnement]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

  [[standard time]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[standard time]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}, except as otherwise
      provided by any proclamation of the Governor in Council that may
      be issued for the purposes of this definition in relation to any
      province or territory or any part thereof, means

      - [(a)]{.lawlabel} in relation to the Province of Newfoundland and
        Labrador, Newfoundland standard time, being three hours and
        thirty minutes behind Greenwich time,

      - [(b)]{.lawlabel} in relation to the Provinces of Nova Scotia,

      - [(c)]{.lawlabel} in relation to that part of the Province of

        Island, and that part of Nunavut lying between the sixty-eighth

        standard time, being five hours behind Greenwich time,

      - [(d)]{.lawlabel} in relation to that part of the Province of
        Ontario lying west of the ninetieth meridian of west longitude,

      - [(e)]{.lawlabel} in relation to the Provinces of Saskatchewan

        Greenwich time,

      - [(f)]{.lawlabel} in relation to the Province of British
        Columbia, Pacific standard time, being eight hours behind
        Greenwich time, and

      - [(g)]{.lawlabel} in relation to Yukon, Yukon standard time,
        being nine hours behind Greenwich time; ([heure
        normale]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

  [[statutory declaration]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[statutory declaration]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means a solemn

  [[superior court]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

      - [(a.1)]{.lawlabel} in the Province of Ontario, the Court of

      - [(c)]{.lawlabel} in the Province of New Brunswick, Manitoba,
        Saskatchewan or Alberta, the Court of Appeal for the Province

      - [(d)]{.lawlabel} in the Provinces of Nova Scotia, British
        Columbia and Prince Edward Island, the Court of Appeal and the

      - [(e)]{.lawlabel} the Supreme Court of Yukon, the Supreme Court

      - [(b)]{.lawlabel} in relation to any other state, means the

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} to a [[calendar year]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means a

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} by number to a Dominical year means the period of

    year.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Affirmative and negative resolutions

- **[[39]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-39 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} In
  every Act,

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the expression [[subject to affirmative resolution
    of Parliament]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}, when used in relation to any
    regulation, means that the regulation shall be laid before
    Parliament within fifteen days after it is made or, if Parliament is
    not then sitting, on any of the first fifteen days next thereafter
    that Parliament is sitting and shall not come into force unless and
    until it is affirmed by a resolution of both Houses of Parliament
    introduced and passed in accordance with the rules of those Houses;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the expression [[subject to affirmative resolution
    of the House of Commons]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}, when used in relation
    to any regulation, means that the regulation shall be laid before
    the House of Commons within fifteen days after it is made or, if the
    House is not then sitting, on any of the first fifteen days next
    thereafter that the House is sitting and shall not come into force
    unless and until it is affirmed by a resolution of the House of
    Commons introduced and passed in accordance with the rules of that
    House;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} the expression [[subject to negative resolution of
    Parliament]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}, when used in relation to any
    regulation, means that the regulation shall be laid before
    Parliament within fifteen days after it is made or, if Parliament is
    not then sitting, on any of the first fifteen days next thereafter
    that Parliament is sitting and may be annulled by a resolution of
    both Houses of Parliament introduced and passed in accordance with
    the rules of those Houses; and

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} the expression [[subject to negative resolution of
    the House of Commons]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}, when used in relation to
    any regulation, means that the regulation shall be laid before the
    House of Commons within fifteen days after it is made or, if the
    House is not then sitting, on any of the first fifteen days next
    thereafter that the House is sitting and may be annulled by a
    resolution of the House of Commons introduced and passed in
    accordance with the rules of that House.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Effect of negative resolution

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Where a regulation is annulled by a resolution of
  Parliament or of the House of Commons, it is deemed to have been
  revoked on the day the resolution is passed and any law that was
  revoked or amended by the making of that regulation is deemed to be
  revived on the day the resolution is passed, but the validity of any
  action taken or not taken in compliance with a regulation so deemed to
  have been revoked shall not be affected by the resolution.

## [References and Citations]{.HTitleText1} {#h-279584 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Citation of enactment

- **[[40]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-40 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} In an
  enactment or document,

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} an Act may be cited by reference to its chapter
    number in the Revised Statutes, by reference to its chapter number
    in the volume of Acts for the year or regnal year in which it was
    enacted or by reference to its long title or short title, with or
    without reference to its chapter number; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} a regulation may be cited by reference to its long
    title or short title, by reference to the Act under which it was
    made or by reference to the number or designation under which it was
    registered by the Clerk of the Privy Council.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Citation includes amendment

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} A citation of or reference to an enactment is deemed
  to be a citation of or reference to the enactment as amended.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. I-23, s. 32
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Reference to two or more parts, etc.

- **[[41]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-41 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A
  reference in an enactment by number or letter to two or more parts,
  divisions, sections, subsections, paragraphs, subparagraphs, clauses,
  subclauses, schedules, appendices or forms shall be read as including
  the number or letter first mentioned and the number or letter last
  mentioned.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Reference in enactments to parts, etc.

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} A reference in an enactment to a part, division,
  section, schedule, appendix or form shall be read as a reference to a
  part, division, section, schedule, appendix or form of the enactment
  in which the reference occurs.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Reference in enactment to subsections,
  etc.

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} A reference in an enactment to a subsection,
  paragraph, subparagraph, clause or subclause shall be read as a
  reference to a subsection, paragraph, subparagraph, clause or
  subclause of the section, subsection, paragraph, subparagraph or
  clause, as the case may be, in which the reference occurs.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Reference to regulations

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} A reference in an enactment to regulations shall be
  read as a reference to regulations made under the enactment in which
  the reference occurs.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Reference to another enactment

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} A reference in an enactment by number or letter to
  any section, subsection, paragraph, subparagraph, clause, subclause or
  other division or line of another enactment shall be read as a
  reference to the section, subsection, paragraph, subparagraph, clause,
  subclause or other division or line of such other enactment as printed
  by authority of law.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. I-23, s. 33
:::

## [Repeal and Amendment]{.HTitleText1} {#h-279607 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Power of repeal or amendment reserved

- **[[42]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-42 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Every
  Act shall be so construed as to reserve to Parliament the power of
  repealing or amending it, and of revoking, restricting or modifying
  any power, privilege or advantage thereby vested in or granted to any
  person.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Amendment or repeal at same session

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} An Act may be amended or repealed by an Act passed in
  the same session of Parliament.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Amendment part of enactment

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} An amending enactment, as far as consistent with the
  tenor thereof, shall be construed as part of the enactment that it
  amends.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. I-23, s. 34
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Effect of repeal

**[[43]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-43 .sectionLabel}** Where an enactment is
repealed in whole or in part, the repeal does not

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} revive any enactment or anything not in force or
  existing at the time when the repeal takes effect,

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} affect the previous operation of the enactment so
  repealed or anything duly done or suffered thereunder,

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} affect any right, privilege, obligation or liability
  acquired, accrued, accruing or incurred under the enactment so
  repealed,

- [(d)]{.lawlabel} affect any offence committed against or contravention
  of the provisions of the enactment so repealed, or any punishment,
  penalty or forfeiture incurred under the enactment so repealed, or

- [(e)]{.lawlabel} affect any investigation, legal proceeding or remedy
  in respect of any right, privilege, obligation or liability referred
  to in paragraph (c) or in respect of any punishment, penalty or
  forfeiture referred to in paragraph (d),

and an investigation, legal proceeding or remedy as described in
paragraph (e) may be instituted, continued or enforced, and the
punishment, penalty or forfeiture may be imposed as if the enactment had
not been so repealed.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. I-23, s. 35
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Repeal and substitution

**[[44]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-44 .sectionLabel}** Where an enactment, in
this section called the "former enactment", is repealed and another
enactment, in this section called the "new enactment", is substituted
therefor,

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} every person acting under the former enactment shall
  continue to act, as if appointed under the new enactment, until
  another person is appointed in the stead of that person;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} every bond and security given by a person appointed
  under the former enactment remains in force, and all books, papers,
  forms and things made or used under the former enactment shall
  continue to be used as before the repeal in so far as they are
  consistent with the new enactment;

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} every proceeding taken under the former enactment
  shall be taken up and continued under and in conformity with the new
  enactment in so far as it may be done consistently with the new
  enactment;

- [(d)]{.lawlabel} the procedure established by the new enactment shall
  be followed as far as it can be adapted thereto

  - [(i)]{.lawlabel} in the recovery or enforcement of fines, penalties
    and forfeitures imposed under the former enactment,

  - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} in the enforcement of rights, existing or accruing
    under the former enactment, and

  - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} in a proceeding in relation to matters that have
    happened before the repeal;

- [(e)]{.lawlabel} when any punishment, penalty or forfeiture is reduced
  or mitigated by the new enactment, the punishment, penalty or
  forfeiture if imposed or adjudged after the repeal shall be reduced or
  mitigated accordingly;

- [(f)]{.lawlabel} except to the extent that the provisions of the new
  enactment are not in substance the same as those of the former
  enactment, the new enactment shall not be held to operate as new law,
  but shall be construed and have effect as a consolidation and as
  declaratory of the law as contained in the former enactment;

- [(g)]{.lawlabel} all regulations made under the repealed enactment
  remain in force and are deemed to have been made under the new
  enactment, in so far as they are not inconsistent with the new
  enactment, until they are repealed or others made in their stead; and

- [(h)]{.lawlabel} any reference in an unrepealed enactment to the
  former enactment shall, with respect to a subsequent transaction,
  matter or thing, be read and construed as a reference to the
  provisions of the new enactment relating to the same subject-matter as
  the former enactment, but where there are no provisions in the new
  enactment relating to the same subject-matter, the former enactment
  shall be read as unrepealed in so far as is necessary to maintain or
  give effect to the unrepealed enactment.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. I-23, s. 36
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Repeal does not imply enactment was in
force

- **[[45]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-45 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  repeal of an enactment in whole or in part shall not be deemed to be
  or to involve a declaration that the enactment was previously in force
  or was considered by Parliament or other body or person by whom the
  enactment was enacted to have been previously in force.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Amendment does not imply change in law

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The amendment of an enactment shall not be deemed to
  be or to involve a declaration that the law under that enactment was
  or was considered by Parliament or other body or person by whom the
  enactment was enacted to have been different from the law as it is
  under the enactment as amended.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Repeal does not declare previous law

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} The repeal or amendment of an enactment in whole or
  in part shall not be deemed to be or to involve any declaration as to
  the previous state of the law.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Judicial construction not adopted

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} A re-enactment, revision, consolidation or amendment
  of an enactment shall not be deemed to be or to involve an adoption of
  the construction that has by judicial decision or otherwise been
  placed on the language used in the enactment or on similar language.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. I-23, s. 37
:::

## [Demise of Crown]{.HTitleText1} {#h-279653 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Effect of demise

- **[[46]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-46 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Where
  there is a demise of the Crown,

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the demise does not affect the holding of any
    office under the Crown in right of Canada; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} it is not necessary by reason of the demise that
    the holder of any such office again be appointed thereto or, having
    taken an oath of office or allegiance before the demise, again take
    that oath.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Continuation of proceedings

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} No writ, action or other process or proceeding, civil
  or criminal, in or issuing out of any court established by an Act is,
  by reason of a demise of the Crown, determined, abated, discontinued
  or affected, but every such writ, action, process or proceeding
  remains in full force and may be enforced, carried on or otherwise
  proceeded with or completed as though there had been no such demise.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. I-23, s. 38
