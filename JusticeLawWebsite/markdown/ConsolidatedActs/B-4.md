[R.S.C.]{.abbr title="Revised Statutes of Canada"}, 1985, c. B-4
:::

## [Short Title]{.HTitleText1} {#h-29040 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Short title

**[[1]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-1 .sectionLabel}** This Act may be cited as
the [Bills of Exchange Act](/eng/acts/B-4).

## [PART I]{.HLabel1}[General]{.HTitleText1} {#h-29063 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Thing done in good faith

**[[3]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-3 .sectionLabel}** A thing is deemed to be
done in good faith, within the meaning of this Act, where it is in fact
done honestly, whether it is done negligently or not.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 3
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Signature

**[[4]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-4 .sectionLabel}** Where, by this Act, any
instrument or writing is required to be signed by any person, it is not
necessary that he should sign it with his own hand, but it is sufficient
if his signature is written thereon by some other person by or under his
authority.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 4
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}What required of corporation

**[[5]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-5 .sectionLabel}** In the case of a
corporation, where, by this Act, any instrument or writing is required
to be signed, it is sufficient if the instrument or writing is duly
sealed with the corporate seal, but nothing in this section shall be
construed as requiring the bill or note of a corporation to be under
seal.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 5
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Computation of time

- **[[6]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-6 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Where,
  by this Act, the time limited for doing any act or thing is less than
  three days, in reckoning time, non-business days are excluded.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Saturdays

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} In all matters relating to bills or notes,

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} if the time for doing any act or thing expires or
    falls on a Saturday, that time is deemed to expire or fall, as the
    case may be, on the next following business day;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} a bill or note payable on demand cannot be duly
    presented for acceptance or payment on a Saturday; and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} failure to do any act or thing on a Saturday does
    not give rise to any rights.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Cheques

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} Notwithstanding any other provision of this Act, a
  cheque may be presented and paid on a Saturday or a non-juridical day
  if the drawee is open for business at the time of the presentment and
  the presentment in all other respects is in accordance with this Act,
  and the non-acceptance or non-payment of a cheque so presented gives
  rise to the same rights as though it had been presented on a business
  day other than a Saturday.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Where bank not open for business

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} In all matters relating to bills or notes,
  notwithstanding any other provision of this Act, if a branch of a bank
  carrying on business is not open for business on a business day

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the time for doing any act or thing at the branch,
    if the time expires or falls on that day, is deemed to expire or
    fall, as the case may be, on the next following business day on
    which the branch is open for business;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} a bill or note payable on demand cannot be duly
    presented for acceptance or payment at the branch on that day; and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} failure to do any act or thing by reason of the
    branch not being open for business on that day does not give rise to
    any rights.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 6
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Crossing dividend warrants

**[[7]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-7 .sectionLabel}** The provisions of this Act
relating to crossed cheques apply to a warrant for payment of dividend.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 7
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}[Bank Act](/eng/acts/B-1.01) not affected

**[[8]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-8 .sectionLabel}** Nothing in this Act affects
the provisions of the [Bank Act](/eng/acts/B-1.01).

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 8
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Common law of England

**[[9]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-9 .sectionLabel}** The rules of the common law
of England, including the law merchant, save in so far as they are
inconsistent with the express provisions of this Act, apply to bills,
notes and cheques.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 10
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Protest evidence

**[[10]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-10 .sectionLabel}** A protest of any bill or
note within Canada, and any copy thereof as copied by the notary or
justice of the peace, is, in any action, evidence of presentation and
dishonour, and also of service of notice of the presentation and
dishonour as stated in the protest or copy.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 11
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Copy of protest, evidence

**[[11]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-11 .sectionLabel}** Where a bill or note,
presented for acceptance, or payable outside Canada, is protested for
non-acceptance or non-payment, a notarial copy of the protest and of the
notice of dishonour, and a notarial certificate of the service of the
notice, shall be received in all courts as evidence of the protest,
notice and service.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 12
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Officer of bank not to act as notary

**[[12]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-12 .sectionLabel}** No clerk, teller or agent
of any bank shall act as a notary in the protesting of any bill or note
payable at the bank or at any of the branches of the bank in which he is
employed.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 13
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Purchase of patent right

- **[[13]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-13 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Every
  bill or note the consideration of which consists, in whole or in part,
  of the purchase money of a patent right, or of a partial interest,
  limited geographically or otherwise, in a patent right, shall have
  written or printed prominently and legibly across the face thereof,
  before it is issued, the words "Given for a patent right".

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Absence of necessary words

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} If the words "Given for a patent right" are not
  written or printed on any instrument in the manner prescribed in
  subsection (1), the instrument and any renewal thereof is void, except
  in the hands of a holder in due course without notice of the
  consideration.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 14
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Transferee to take with equities

**[[14]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-14 .sectionLabel}** The endorsee or other
transferee of any instrument referred to in section 13 having the words
"Given for a patent right" printed or written thereon takes the
instrument subject to any defence or set-off in respect of the whole or
any part thereof that would have existed between the original parties.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 15
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Offence and punishment

**[[15]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-15 .sectionLabel}** Every person who issues,
sells or transfers, by endorsement or delivery, any instrument referred
to in section 13 not having the words "Given for a patent right" printed
or written across the face thereof in the manner prescribed by that
section, knowing the consideration of that instrument to have consisted,
in whole or in part, of the purchase money of a patent right, or of a
partial interest, limited geographically or otherwise, in a patent
right, is guilty of an indictable offence and liable to imprisonment for
any term not exceeding one year, or to such fine, not exceeding two
hundred dollars, as the court thinks fit.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 16
:::

## [PART II]{.HLabel1}[Bills of Exchange]{.HTitleText1} {#h-29132 .Part}

### [Form and Interpretation of Bill]{.HTitleText2} {#h-29133 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Bill of exchange

- **[[16]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-16 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A bill
  of exchange is an unconditional order in writing, addressed by one
  person to another, signed by the person giving it, requiring the
  person to whom it is addressed to pay, on demand or at a fixed or
  determinable future time, a sum certain in money to or to the order of
  a specified person or to bearer.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Non-compliance with requisites

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} An instrument that does not comply with the
  requirements of subsection (1), or that orders any act to be done in
  addition to the payment of money, is not, except as hereinafter
  provided, a bill.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Unconditional order

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} An order to pay out of a particular fund is not
  unconditional within the meaning of this section, except that an
  unqualified order to pay, coupled with

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} an indication of a particular fund out of which the
    drawee is to reimburse himself or a particular account to be debited
    with the amount, or

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} a statement of the transaction that gives rise to
    the bill,

  is unconditional.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 17
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Instrument payable on contingency

- **[[17]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-17 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} An
  instrument expressed to be payable on a contingency is not a bill and
  the happening of the event does not cure the defect.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Addressed to two or more drawees

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} A bill may be addressed to two or more drawees,
  whether they are partners or not, but an order addressed to two
  drawees in the alternative, or to two or more drawees in succession,
  is not a bill.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 18
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Payee, drawer or drawee

- **[[18]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-18 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A bill
  may be drawn payable to, or to the order of, the drawer, or it may be
  drawn payable to, or to the order of, the drawee.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Two or more payees

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} A bill may be made payable to two or more payees
  jointly, or it may be made payable in the alternative to one of two,
  or one or some of several payees.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Holder of office payee

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} A bill may be made payable to the holder of an office
  for the time being.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 19
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Drawee to be named

**[[19]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-19 .sectionLabel}** The drawee must be named
or otherwise indicated in a bill with reasonable certainty.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 20
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Transfer words

- **[[20]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-20 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} When a
  bill contains words prohibiting transfer, or indicating an intention
  that it should not be transferable, it is valid as between the parties
  thereto, but it is not negotiable.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Negotiable bill

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} A negotiable bill may be payable either to order or
  to bearer.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}When payable to bearer

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} A bill is payable to bearer that is expressed to be
  so payable, or on which the only or last endorsement is an endorsement
  in blank.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Certainty of payee

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} Where a bill is not payable to bearer, the payee must
  be named or otherwise indicated therein with reasonable certainty.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Fictitious payee

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} Where the payee is a fictitious or non-existing
  person, the bill may be treated as payable to bearer.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 21
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Bill payable to order

- **[[21]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-21 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A bill
  is payable to order that is expressed to be so payable, or that is
  expressed to be payable to a particular person, and does not contain
  words prohibiting transfer or indicating an intention that it should
  not be transferable.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}When payable to person or order

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Where a bill, either originally or by endorsement, is
  expressed to be payable to the order of a specified person, and not to
  him or his order, it is nevertheless payable to him or his order at
  his option.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 22
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}When payable on demand

- **[[22]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-22 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A bill
  is payable on demand

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} that is expressed to be payable on demand or on
    presentation; or

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} in which no time for payment is expressed.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Endorsed when overdue

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Where a bill is accepted or endorsed when it is
  overdue, it shall, with respect to the acceptor who so accepts it, or
  any endorser who so endorses it, be deemed a bill payable on demand.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 23
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Determinable future time

**[[23]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-23 .sectionLabel}** A bill is payable at a
determinable future time, within the meaning of this Act, that is
expressed to be payable

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} at sight or at a fixed period after date or sight; or

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} on or at a fixed period after the occurrence of a
  specified event that is certain to happen, though the time of
  happening is uncertain.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 24
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Inland bill

- **[[24]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-24 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} An
  inland bill is a bill that is, or on the face of it purports to be,

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} both drawn and payable within Canada; or

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} drawn within Canada on a person resident in Canada.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Foreign bill

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Any other bill is a foreign bill.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Presumption

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} Unless the contrary appears on the face of a bill,
  the holder may treat it as an inland bill.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 25
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Bill or note

**[[25]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-25 .sectionLabel}** Where in a bill drawer
and drawee are the same person, or where the drawee is a fictitious
person or a person not having capacity to contract, the holder may treat
the instrument, at his option, either as a bill or as a note.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 26
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Valid bill

**[[26]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-26 .sectionLabel}** A bill is not invalid by
reason only that it

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} is not dated;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} does not specify the value given, or that any value
  has been given therefor;

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} does not specify the place where it is drawn or the
  place where it is payable; or

- [(d)]{.lawlabel} is antedated or post-dated, or bears date on a Sunday
  or other non-juridical day.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 27
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Sum certain

- **[[27]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-27 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  sum payable by a bill is a sum certain within the meaning of this Act,
  although it is required to be paid

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} with interest;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} by stated instalments;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} by stated instalments, with a provision that on
    default in payment of any instalment the whole shall become due; or

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} according to an indicated rate of exchange or a
    rate of exchange to be ascertained as directed by the bill.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Figures and words

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Where the sum payable by a bill is expressed in words
  and also in figures and there is a discrepancy between the two, the
  sum denoted by the words is the amount payable.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}With interest

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} Where a bill is expressed to be payable with
  interest, unless the instrument otherwise provides, interest runs from
  the date of the bill and, if the bill is undated, from the issue
  thereof.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 28
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}True date presumption

**[[28]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-28 .sectionLabel}** Where a bill or an
acceptance, or any endorsement on a bill, is dated, the date shall,
unless the contrary is proved, be deemed to be the true date of the
drawing, acceptance or endorsement, as the case may be.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 29
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Undated bill payable after date

**[[29]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-29 .sectionLabel}** Where a bill expressed to
be payable at a fixed period after date is issued undated, or where the
acceptance of a bill payable at sight or at a fixed period after sight
is undated, any holder may insert therein the true date of issue or
acceptance, and the bill shall be payable accordingly, but where the
holder in good faith and by mistake inserts a wrong date, or in every
other case where a wrong date is inserted, if the bill subsequently
comes into the hands of a holder in due course, the bill is not voided
thereby, but operates and is payable as if the date so inserted had been
the true date.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 30
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Perfecting bill

**[[30]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-30 .sectionLabel}** Where a simple signature
on a blank paper is delivered by the signer in order that it may be
converted into a bill, it operates, in the absence of evidence to the
contrary, as an authority to fill it up as a complete bill for any
amount, using the signature for that of the drawer or acceptor, or an
endorser, and, in like manner, when a bill is wanting in any material
particular, the person in possession of it has, in the absence of
evidence to the contrary, the authority to fill up the omission in any
way he thinks fit.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 31
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}When completed

- **[[31]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-31 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} In
  order that any instrument referred to in section 30 when completed may
  be enforceable against any person who became a party thereto prior to
  its completion, it must be filled up within a reasonable time and
  strictly in accordance with the authority given, but where any such
  instrument, after completion, is negotiated to a holder in due course,
  it is valid and effectual for all purposes in his hands, and he may
  enforce it as if it had been filled up within a reasonable time and
  strictly in accordance with the authority given.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Reasonable time

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Reasonable time within the meaning of this section is
  a question of fact.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 32
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Referee in case of need

- **[[32]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-32 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  drawer of a bill and any endorser may insert therein the name of a
  person, who shall be called the referee in case of need, to whom the
  holder may resort in case of need, that is to say, in case the bill is
  dishonoured by non-acceptance or non-payment.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Option

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The holder may, at his option, resort to the referee
  in case of need or not, as he thinks fit.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 33
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Stipulations

**[[33]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-33 .sectionLabel}** The drawer of a bill, and
any endorser, may insert therein an express stipulation

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} negativing or limiting his own liability to the
  holder; or

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} waiving, with respect to himself, some or all of the
  holder's duties.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 34
:::

### [Acceptance]{.HTitleText2} {#h-29269 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Acceptance

- **[[34]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-34 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  acceptance of a bill is the signification by the drawee of his assent
  to the order of the drawer.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Drawee's name wrong

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Where in a bill the drawee is wrongly designated or
  his name is misspelt, he may accept the bill as therein described,
  adding, if he thinks fit, his proper signature or he may accept by his
  proper signature.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 35
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Acceptance

- **[[35]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-35 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} An
  acceptance is invalid unless it complies with the following
  conditions:

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} it must be written on the bill and be signed by the
    drawee; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} it must not express that the drawee will perform
    his promise by any other means than the payment of money.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Mere signature

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The mere signature of the drawee written on the bill
  without additional words is a sufficient acceptance.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 36
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Acceptance

- **[[36]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-36 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A bill
  may be accepted

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} before it has been signed by the drawer or while
    otherwise incomplete; or

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} when it is overdue or after it has been dishonoured
    by a previous refusal to accept, or by non-payment.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Acceptance after dishonour

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} When a bill payable at sight or after sight is
  dishonoured by non-acceptance and the drawee subsequently accepts it,
  the holder, in the absence of any different agreement, is entitled to
  have the bill accepted as of the date of first presentment to the
  drawee for acceptance.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 37
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Kinds

- **[[37]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-37 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} An
  acceptance is either general or qualified.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}General

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} A general acceptance assents without qualification to
  the order of the drawer.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Qualified

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} A qualified acceptance in express terms varies the
  effect of the bill as drawn and, in particular, an acceptance is
  qualified that is

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} conditional, that is to say, that makes payment by
    the acceptor dependent on the fulfilment of a condition therein
    stated;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} partial, that is to say, an acceptance to pay part
    only of the amount for which the bill is drawn;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} qualified as to time; or

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} the acceptance of one or more of the drawees, but
    not of all.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Specified place

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} An acceptance to pay at a particular specified place
  is not on that account conditional or qualified.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 38
:::

### [Delivery]{.HTitleText2} {#h-29310 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}When acceptance complete

**[[38]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-38 .sectionLabel}** Every contract on a bill,
whether it is the drawer's, the acceptor's or an endorser's, is
incomplete and revocable until delivery of the instrument in order to
give effect thereto, but where an acceptance is written on a bill and
the drawee gives notice to, or according to the directions of, the
person entitled to the bill that he has accepted it, the acceptance then
becomes complete and irrevocable.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 39
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Requisites

- **[[39]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-39 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} As
  between immediate parties and as regards a remote party, other than a
  holder in due course, the delivery of a bill

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} in order to be effectual must be made either by or
    under the authority of the party drawing, accepting or endorsing, as
    the case may be; or

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} may be shown to have been conditional or for a
    special purpose only, and not for the purpose of transferring the
    property in the bill.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Presumption

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Where the bill is in the hands of a holder in due
  course, a valid delivery of the bill by all parties prior to him, so
  as to make them liable to him, is conclusively presumed.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 40
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Parting with possession

**[[40]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-40 .sectionLabel}** Where a bill is no longer
in the possession of a party who has signed it as drawer, acceptor or
endorser, a valid and unconditional delivery by him is presumed until
the contrary is proved.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 41
:::

### [Computation of Time, Non-juridical Days and Days of Grace]{.HTitleText2} {#h-29328 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Computation of time

**[[41]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-41 .sectionLabel}** Where a bill is not
payable on demand, three days, called days of grace, are, in every case,
where the bill itself does not otherwise provide, added to the time of
payment as fixed by the bill, and the bill is due and payable on the
last day of grace, but whenever the last day of grace falls on a legal
holiday or non-juridical day in the province where any such bill is
payable, the day next following, not being a legal holiday or
non-juridical day in that province, is the last day of grace.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 42
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Non-juridical days

**[[42]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-42 .sectionLabel}** In all matters relating
to bills of exchange, the following and no other days shall be observed
as legal holidays or non-juridical days:

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} in all the provinces,

  - [(i)]{.lawlabel} Sundays, New Year's Day, Good Friday, Victoria Day,
    Canada Day, Labour Day, National Day for Truth and Reconciliation,
    which is observed on September 30, Remembrance Day and Christmas
    Day,

  - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} the birthday (or the day fixed by proclamation for
    the celebration of the birthday) of the reigning Sovereign,

  - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} any day appointed by proclamation to be observed
    as a public holiday, or as a day of general prayer or mourning or
    day of public rejoicing or thanksgiving, throughout Canada, and

  - [(iv)]{.lawlabel} the day next following New Year's Day, Christmas
    Day and the birthday of the reigning Sovereign (if no other day is
    fixed by proclamation for the celebration of the birthday) when
    those days respectively fall on a Sunday;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} in any province, any day appointed by proclamation of
  the lieutenant governor of the province to be observed as a public
  holiday, or for a fast or thanksgiving within the province, and any
  day that is a non-juridical day by virtue of an Act of the legislature
  of the province; and

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} in any city, town, municipality or other organized
  district, any day appointed to be observed as a civic holiday by
  resolution of the council, or other authority charged with the
  administration of the civic or municipal affairs of the city, town,
  municipality or district.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., 1985, c. B-4, s. 42

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Time of payment

at sight, or at a fixed period after date, after sight or after the

::: HistoricalNote

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Sight bill

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Due date

- **[[45]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-45 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Every

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Definition of "month"

### [Capacity and Authority of Parties]{.HTitleText2} {#h-29359 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Capacity of parties

- **[[46]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-46
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Capacity to incur liability as a
  party to a bill is coextensive with capacity to contract.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Corporations

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Nothing in this section enables a corporation to make
  itself liable as drawer, acceptor or endorser of a bill, unless it may
  do so under the law in force relating to that corporation.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 47
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Effect of disability on holder

**[[47]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-47 .sectionLabel}** Where a bill is drawn or
endorsed by any infant, minor or corporation having no capacity or power
to incur liability on a bill, the drawing or endorsement entitles the
holder to receive payment of the bill and to enforce it against any
other party thereto.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 48
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Forgery

- **[[48]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-48
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Subject to this Act, where a
  signature on a bill is forged, or placed thereon without the authority
  of the person whose signature it purports to be, the forged or
  unauthorized signature is wholly inoperative, and no right to retain
  the bill or to give a discharge therefor or to enforce payment thereof
  against any party thereto can be acquired through or under that
  signature, unless the party against whom it is sought to retain or
  enforce payment of the bill is precluded from setting up the forgery
  or want of authority.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Ratification

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Nothing in this section affects the ratification of
  an unauthorized signature not amounting to a forgery.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Recovery of amount paid on forged
  cheque

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} Where a cheque payable to order is paid by the drawee
  on a forged endorsement out of the funds of the drawer, or is so paid
  and charged to his account, the drawer has no right of action against
  the drawee for the recovery of the amount so paid, nor any defence to
  any claim made by the drawee for the amount so paid, as the case may
  be, unless he gives notice in writing of the forgery to the drawee
  within one year after he has acquired notice of the forgery.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Default of notice

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} In case of failure by the drawer to give notice of
  the forgery within the period referred to in subsection (3), the
  cheque shall be held to have been paid in due course with respect to
  every other party thereto or named therein, who has not previously
  instituted proceedings for the protection of his rights.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 49
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Recovery of amount paid on forged
endorsement

- **[[49]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-49 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Where
  a bill bearing a forged or an unauthorized endorsement is paid in good
  faith and in the ordinary course of business by or on behalf of the
  drawee or acceptor, the person by whom or on whose behalf the payment
  is made has the right to recover the amount paid from the person to
  whom it was paid or from any endorser who has endorsed the bill
  subsequent to the forged or unauthorized endorsement if notice of the
  endorsement being a forged or an unauthorized endorsement is given to
  each such subsequent endorser within the time and in the manner
  mentioned in this section.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Rights against prior endorsers

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Any person or endorser from whom an amount has been
  recovered under subsection (1) has the like right of recovery against
  any prior endorser subsequent to the forged or unauthorized
  endorsement.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Notice of forgery

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} The notice referred to in subsection (1) shall be
  given within a reasonable time after the person seeking to recover the
  amount has acquired notice that the endorsement is forged or
  unauthorized, and may be given in the same manner, and if sent by post
  may be addressed in the same way as notice of protest or dishonour of
  a bill may be given or addressed under this Act.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 50
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Procuration signatures

**[[50]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-50 .sectionLabel}** A signature by
procuration operates as notice that the agent has but a limited
authority to sign, and the principal is bound by such signature only if
the agent in so signing was acting within the actual limits of his
authority.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 51
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Signing in representative capacity

- **[[51]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-51 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Where
  a person signs a bill as drawer, endorser or acceptor and adds words
  to his signature indicating that he has signed for or on behalf of a
  principal, or in a representative character, he is not personally
  liable thereon, but the mere addition to his signature of words
  describing him as an agent, or as filling a representative character,
  does not exempt him from personal liability.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Rule for determining capacity

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} In determining whether a signature on a bill is that
  of the principal or that of the agent by whose hand it is written, the
  construction most favourable to the validity of the instrument shall
  be adopted.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 52
:::

### [Consideration]{.HTitleText2} {#h-29402 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Valuable consideration

- **[[52]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-52
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Valuable consideration for a bill
  may be constituted by

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} any consideration sufficient to support a simple
    contract; or

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} an antecedent debt or liability.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Form of bill

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} An antecedent debt or liability is deemed valuable
  consideration, whether the bill is payable on demand or at a future
  time.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 53
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Holder for value

- **[[53]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-53 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Where
  value has, at any time, been given for a bill, the holder is deemed to
  be a holder for value as regards the acceptor and all parties to the
  bill who became parties prior to that time.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}In case of lien

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Where the holder of a bill has a lien on it, arising
  either from contract or by implication of law, he is deemed to be a
  holder for value to the extent of the sum for which he has a lien.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 54
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Accommodation bill

- **[[54]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-54 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} An
  accommodation party to a bill is a person who has signed a bill as
  drawer, acceptor or endorser, without receiving value therefor, and
  for the purpose of lending his name to some other person.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Liability of party

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} An accommodation party is liable on a bill to a
  holder for value, and it is immaterial whether, when that holder took
  the bill, he knew that party to be an accommodation party or not.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 55
:::

### [Holder in Due Course]{.HTitleText2} {#h-29426 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Holder in due course

- **[[55]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-55 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A
  holder in due course is a holder who has taken a bill, complete and
  regular on the face of it, under the following conditions, namely,

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} that he became the holder of it before it was
    overdue and without notice that it had been previously dishonoured,
    if such was the fact; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} that he took the bill in good faith and for value,
    and that at the time the bill was negotiated to him he had no notice
    of any defect in the title of the person who negotiated it.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Title defective

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} In particular, the title of a person who negotiates a
  bill is defective within the meaning of this Act when he obtained the
  bill, or the acceptance thereof, by fraud, duress or force and fear,
  or other unlawful means, or for an illegal consideration, or when he
  negotiates it in breach of faith, or under such circumstances as
  amount to a fraud.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 56
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Right of subsequent holder

**[[56]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-56 .sectionLabel}** A holder, whether for
value or not, who derives his title to a bill through a holder in due
course, and who is not himself a party to any fraud or illegality
affecting it, has all the rights of that holder in due course as regards
the acceptor and all parties to the bill prior to that holder.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 57
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Presumption of value

- **[[57]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-57 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Every
  party whose signature appears on a bill is, in the absence of evidence
  to the contrary, deemed to have become a party thereto for value.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Presumed holder in due course

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Every holder of a bill is, in the absence of evidence
  to the contrary, deemed to be a holder in due course, but if, in an
  action on a bill, it is admitted or proved that the acceptance, issue
  or subsequent negotiation of the bill is affected with fraud, duress
  or force and fear, or illegality, the burden of proof that he is the
  holder in due course is on him, unless and until he proves that,
  subsequent to the alleged fraud or illegality, value has in good faith
  been given for the bill by some other holder in due course.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 58
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Usurious consideration

**[[58]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-58 .sectionLabel}** No bill, although given
for a usurious consideration or on a usurious contract, is void in the
hands of a holder, unless the holder had at the time of its transfer to
him actual knowledge that it was originally given for a usurious
consideration or on a usurious contract.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 59
:::

### [Negotiation]{.HTitleText2} {#h-29451 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}By transfer

- **[[59]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-59 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A bill
  is negotiated when it is transferred from one person to another in
  such a manner as to constitute the transferee the holder of the bill.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}By delivery

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} A bill payable to bearer is negotiated by delivery.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}By endorsement

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} A bill payable to order is negotiated by the
  endorsement of the holder.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 60
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Without endorsement

- **[[60]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-60 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Where
  the holder of a bill payable to his order transfers it for value
  without endorsing it, the transfer gives the transferee such title as
  the transferor had in the bill, and the transferee in addition
  acquires the right to have the endorsement of the transferor.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Representative capacity

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Where any person is under obligation to endorse a
  bill in a representative capacity, he may endorse the bill in such
  terms as to negative personal liability.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 61
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Endorsing

- **[[61]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-61 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} An
  endorsement in order to operate as a negotiation must be

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} written on the bill itself and be signed by the
    endorser; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} an endorsement of the entire bill.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Allonge

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} An endorsement written on an allonge, or on a copy of
  a bill issued or negotiated in a country where copies are recognized,
  is deemed to be written on the bill itself.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Partial endorsement

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} A partial endorsement, that is to say, an endorsement
  that purports to transfer to the endorsee a part only of the amount
  payable, or that purports to transfer the bill to two or more
  endorsees severally, does not operate as a negotiation of the bill.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 62
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Signature sufficient

- **[[62]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-62 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  simple signature of the endorser on a bill, without additional words,
  is a sufficient endorsement.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Two or more payees

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Where a bill is payable to the order of two or more
  payees or endorsees who are not partners, all must endorse, unless the
  one endorsing has authority to endorse for the others.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 63
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Misspelling payee's name

**[[63]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-63 .sectionLabel}** Where, in a bill payable
to order, the payee or endorsee is wrongly designated or his name is
misspelt, he may endorse the bill as therein described, adding his
proper signature, or he may endorse by his proper signature.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 64
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Presumption as to order of endorsement

**[[64]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-64 .sectionLabel}** Where there are two or
more endorsements on a bill, each endorsement is deemed to have been
made in the order in which it appears on the bill, until the contrary is
proved.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 65
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Disregarding condition

**[[65]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-65 .sectionLabel}** Where a bill purports to
be endorsed conditionally, the condition may be disregarded by the
payer, and payment to the endorsee is valid, whether the condition has
been fulfilled or not.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 66
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Endorsement

- **[[66]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-66 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} An
  endorsement may be made in blank or special.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}In blank

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} An endorsement in blank specifies no endorsee, and a
  bill so endorsed becomes payable to bearer.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Special

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} A special endorsement specifies the person to whom,
  or to whose order, the bill is to be payable.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Application of Act

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} The provisions of this Act relating to a payee apply,
  with such modifications as the circumstances require, to an endorsee
  under a special endorsement.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Conversion of blank endorsement

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} Where a bill has been endorsed in blank, any holder
  may convert the blank endorsement into a special endorsement by
  writing above the endorser's signature a direction to pay the bill to
  or to the order of himself or some other person.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 67
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Restrictive endorsement

- **[[67]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-67 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} An
  endorsement may contain terms making it restrictive.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Idem

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} An endorsement is restrictive that prohibits the
  further negotiation of the bill, or that expresses that it is a mere
  authority to deal with the bill as thereby directed, and not a
  transfer of the ownership thereof, as, for example, if a bill is
  endorsed "Pay \... only", or "Pay \... for the account of \...", or
  "Pay \..., or order, for collection".

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Rights of endorsee

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} A restrictive endorsement gives the endorsee the
  right to receive payment of the bill and to sue any party thereto that
  his endorser could have sued, but gives him no power to transfer his
  rights as endorsee unless it expressly authorizes him to do so.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}If further transfer is authorized

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} Where a restrictive endorsement authorizes further
  transfer, all subsequent endorsees take the bill with the same rights
  and subject to the same liabilities as the first endorsee under the
  restrictive endorsement.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 68
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}When negotiability ceases

**[[68]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-68 .sectionLabel}** Where a bill is
negotiable in its origin, it continues to be negotiable until it has
been

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} restrictively endorsed; or

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} discharged by payment or otherwise.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 69
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Overdue bill

- **[[69]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-69 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Where
  an overdue bill is negotiated, it can be negotiated only subject to
  any defect of title affecting it at its maturity, and thenceforward no
  person who takes it can acquire or give a better title than the person
  from whom he took it had.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}When demand bill overdue

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} A bill payable on demand is deemed to be overdue,
  within the meaning and for the purposes of this section, when it
  appears on the face of it to have been in circulation for an
  unreasonable length of time.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Time

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} What is an unreasonable length of time for the
  purpose of subsection (2) is a question of fact.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 70
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Presumption

**[[70]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-70 .sectionLabel}** Except where an
endorsement bears date after the maturity of the bill, every negotiation
is, in the absence of evidence to the contrary, deemed to have been
effected before the bill was overdue.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 71
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Taking bill with notice of dishonour

**[[71]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-71 .sectionLabel}** Where a bill that is not
overdue has been dishonoured, any person who takes it with notice of the
dishonour takes it subject to any defect of title attaching thereto at
the time of dishonour, but nothing in this section affects the rights of
a holder in due course.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 72
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Reissue of bill

**[[72]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-72 .sectionLabel}** Where a bill is
negotiated back to the drawer, to a prior endorser or to the acceptor,
that party may, subject to this Act, reissue and further negotiate the
bill, but he is not entitled to enforce the payment of the bill against
any intervening party to whom he was previously liable.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 73
:::

### [Rights and Powers of Holder]{.HTitleText2} {#h-29549 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Rights and powers of holder

**[[73]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-73 .sectionLabel}** The rights and powers of
the holder of a bill are as follows:

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} he may sue on the bill in his own name;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} where he is a holder in due course, he holds the bill
  free from any defect of title of prior parties, as well as from mere
  personal defences available to prior parties among themselves, and may
  enforce payment against all parties liable on the bill;

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} where his title is defective, if he negotiates the
  bill to a holder in due course, that holder obtains a good and
  complete title to the bill; and

- [(d)]{.lawlabel} where his title is defective, if he obtains payment
  of the bill, the person who pays him in due course gets a valid
  discharge for the bill.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 74
:::

### [Presentment for Acceptance]{.HTitleText2} {#h-29558 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}When presentment for acceptance necessary

- **[[74]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-74 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Where
  a bill is payable at sight or after sight, presentment for acceptance
  is necessary in order to fix the maturity of the instrument.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Express stipulation

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Where a bill expressly stipulates that it shall be
  presented for acceptance, or where a bill is drawn payable elsewhere
  than at the residence or place of business of the drawee, it must be
  presented for acceptance before it can be presented for payment.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Other cases

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} In no other case is presentment for acceptance
  necessary in order to render liable any party to the bill.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 75
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Presentment excused

**[[75]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-75 .sectionLabel}** Where the holder of a
bill, drawn payable elsewhere than at the place of business or residence
of the drawee, has not time, with the exercise of reasonable diligence,
to present the bill for acceptance before presenting it for payment on
the day that it falls due, the delay caused by presenting the bill for
acceptance before presenting it for payment is excused and does not
discharge the drawer and endorsers.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 76
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Sight bill

- **[[76]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-76
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Subject to this Act, when a bill
  payable at sight or after sight is negotiated, the holder must either
  present it for acceptance or negotiate it within a reasonable time.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}If not presented

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} If the holder does not comply with the requirement of
  subsection (1), the drawer and all endorsers prior to that holder are
  discharged.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Reasonable time

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} In determining what is a reasonable time within the
  meaning of this section, regard shall be had to the nature of the
  bill, the usage of trade with respect to similar bills and the facts
  of the particular case.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 77
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Rules for presenting for acceptance

**[[77]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-77 .sectionLabel}** A bill is duly presented
for acceptance that is presented in accordance with the following rules:

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} the presentment must be made by or on behalf of the
  holder to the drawee or to a person authorized to accept or refuse
  acceptance on his behalf, at a reasonable hour on a business day and
  before the bill is overdue;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} where a bill is addressed to two or more drawees who
  are not partners, presentment must be made to all of them, unless one
  has authority to accept for all, in which case presentment may be made
  to him only;

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} where the drawee is dead, presentment may be made to
  his personal representative; and

- [(d)]{.lawlabel} where authorized by agreement or usage, a presentment
  through the post office is sufficient.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 78
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Excuses

- **[[78]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-78
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Presentment in accordance with the
  rules set out in section 77 is excused, and a bill may be treated as
  dishonoured by non-acceptance where

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the drawee is dead, or is a fictitious person or a
    person not having capacity to contract by bill;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} after the exercise of reasonable diligence, the
    presentment cannot be effected; or

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} although the presentment has been irregular,
    acceptance has been refused on some other ground.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}No excuse

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The fact that the holder has reason to believe that
  the bill, on presentment, will be dishonoured does not excuse
  presentment.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 79
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Time for acceptance

- **[[79]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-79 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  drawee may accept a bill on the day of its due presentment to him for
  acceptance or at any time within two days thereafter.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Dishonour

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} When a bill is duly presented for acceptance and is
  not accepted within the time mentioned in subsection (1), the person
  presenting it must treat it as dishonoured by non-acceptance.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Loss of rights

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} If the person does not treat the bill as dishonoured,
  the holder loses his right of recourse against the drawer and
  endorsers.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Date of acceptance

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} In the case of a bill payable at sight or after
  sight, the acceptor may date his acceptance thereon as of any of the
  days mentioned in subsection (1) but not later than the day of his
  actual acceptance of the bill.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Refusing acceptance

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} If the acceptance is not dated as described in
  subsection (4), the holder may refuse to take the acceptance and may
  treat the bill as dishonoured by non-acceptance.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 80
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Dishonour by non-acceptance

**[[80]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-80 .sectionLabel}** A bill is dishonoured by
non-acceptance when

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} it is duly presented for acceptance and such an
  acceptance as is prescribed by this Act is refused or cannot be
  obtained; or

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} presentment for acceptance is excused and the bill is
  not accepted.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 81
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Recourse

**[[81]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-81 .sectionLabel}** Subject to this Act, when
a bill is dishonoured by non-acceptance, an immediate right of recourse
against the drawer and endorsers accrues to the holder, and no
presentment for payment is necessary.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 82
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Qualified acceptance

- **[[82]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-82 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  holder of a bill may refuse to take a qualified acceptance and, if he
  does not obtain an unqualified acceptance, may treat the bill as
  dishonoured by non-acceptance.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Presumption of assent

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} When the drawer or endorser of a bill receives notice
  of a qualified acceptance and does not within a reasonable time
  express his dissent to the holder, he shall be deemed to have assented
  thereto.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 83
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Qualified acceptance without authority

- **[[83]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-83 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Where
  a qualified acceptance is taken and the drawer or endorser has not
  expressedly or impliedly authorized the holder to take a qualified
  acceptance, or does not subsequently assent thereto, the drawer or
  endorser is discharged from his liability on the bill.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Partial acceptance

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} This section does not apply to a partial acceptance
  of which due notice has been given.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 84
:::

### [Presentment for Payment]{.HTitleText2} {#h-29636 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Necessity for presentment

- **[[84]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-84
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Subject to this Act, a bill must be
  duly presented for payment.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}If not presented

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} If a bill is not duly presented for payment, the
  drawer and endorsers are discharged.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Manner of presentment

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} Where the holder of a bill presents it for payment,
  he shall exhibit the bill to the person from whom he demands payment.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 85
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Time for presentment

- **[[85]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-85 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A bill
  is duly presented for payment that is presented when the bill is

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} not payable on demand, on the day it falls due; or

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} payable on demand, within a reasonable time after
    its issue, in order to render the drawer liable, and within a
    reasonable time after its endorsement, in order to render the
    endorser liable.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Reasonable time

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} In determining what is a reasonable time within the
  meaning of this section, regard shall be had to the nature of the
  bill, the usage of trade with respect to similar bills and the facts
  of the particular case.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 86
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}By and to whom

- **[[86]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-86
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Presentment of a bill must be made
  by the holder or by a person authorized to receive payment on his
  behalf, at the proper place as defined in section 87, and either to
  the person designated by the bill as payer or to his representative or
  a person authorized to pay or to refuse payment on his behalf, if with
  the exercise of reasonable diligence such person can there be found.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Two acceptors

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} When a bill is drawn on or accepted by two or more
  persons who are not partners and no place of payment is specified,
  presentment must be made to all of them.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Personal representation

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} When the drawee or acceptor of a bill is dead and no
  place of payment is specified, presentment of the bill must be made to
  a personal representative if there is one and with the exercise of
  reasonable diligence he can be found.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 87
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Proper place for presentment

**[[87]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-87 .sectionLabel}** A bill is presented at
the proper place

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} where a place of payment is specified in the bill or
  acceptance and the bill is there presented;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} where no place of payment is specified, but the
  address of the drawee or acceptor is given in the bill, and the bill
  is there presented;

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} where no place of payment is specified and no address
  given, and the bill is presented at the drawee's or acceptor's place
  of business, if known, and if not, at his ordinary residence, if
  known; or

- [(d)]{.lawlabel} in any other case, if presented to the drawee or
  acceptor wherever he can be found, or if presented at his latest known
  place of business or residence.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 88
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Sufficient presentment

**[[88]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-88 .sectionLabel}** Where a bill is presented
at the proper place as defined in section 87 and after the exercise of
reasonable diligence no person authorized to pay or refuse payment can
there be found, no further presentment to the drawee or acceptor is
required.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 89
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Presentment at post office

- **[[89]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-89 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Where
  the place of payment specified in the bill or acceptance is any city,
  town or village and no place therein is specified, and the bill is
  presented at the drawee's or acceptor's known place of business or
  known ordinary residence therein, and if there is no such place of
  business or residence, the bill is presented at the post office or
  principal post office in such city, town or village, such presentment
  is sufficient.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Through post office

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Where authorized by agreement or usage, a presentment
  through the post office is sufficient.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 90
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Delay in presentment

- **[[90]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-90 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Delay
  in making presentment of a bill for payment is excused where the delay
  is caused by circumstances beyond the control of the holder and not
  imputable to his default, misconduct or negligence.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Diligence

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Where the cause of delay ceases to operate,
  presentment must be made with reasonable diligence.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 91
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}When presentment is dispensed with

- **[[91]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-91
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Presentment of a bill for payment is
  dispensed with

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} where, after the exercise of reasonable diligence,
    presentment, as required by this Act, cannot be effected;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} where the drawee is a fictitious person;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} with respect to the drawer, where the drawee or
    acceptor is not bound, as between himself and the drawer, to accept
    or pay the bill, and the drawer has no reason to believe that the
    bill would be paid if presented; or

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} with respect to an endorser, where the bill was
    accepted or made for the accommodation of that endorser, and he has
    no reason to expect that the bill would be paid if presented;

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} by waiver of presentment, express or implied.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Not dispensed with

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The fact that the holder has reason to believe that
  the bill will, on presentment, be dishonoured does not dispense with
  the necessity for presentment.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 92
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}When no place specified

- **[[92]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-92 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} When
  no place of payment is specified in a bill or acceptance, presentment
  for payment is not necessary in order to render the acceptor liable.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}If place specified

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} When a place of payment is specified in a bill or
  acceptance, the acceptor, in the absence of an express stipulation to
  that effect, is not discharged by the omission to present the bill for
  payment on the day that it matures, but if any suit or action is
  instituted thereon before presentation, the costs thereof shall be in
  the discretion of the court.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Delivery on payment

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} When a bill is paid, the holder shall forthwith
  deliver it to the party paying it.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 93
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Time for presentment

- **[[93]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-93 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Where
  the address of the acceptor for honour of a bill is in the same place
  where the bill is protested for non-payment, the bill must be
  presented to him not later than the day following its maturity.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Parties in different places

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Where the address of the acceptor for honour is in a
  place other than the place where a bill is protested for non-payment,
  the bill must be forwarded not later than the day following its
  maturity for presentment to him.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Excuses for delay

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} Delay in presentment or non-presentment is excused by
  any circumstance that would, in case of acceptance by a drawee, excuse
  delay in presentment for payment or non-presentment for payment.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 94
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Dishonour by non-payment

- **[[94]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-94 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A bill
  is dishonoured by non-payment when

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} it is duly presented for payment and payment is
    refused or cannot be obtained; or

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} presentment is excused and the bill is overdue and
    unpaid.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Recourse

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Subject to this Act, when a bill is dishonoured by
  non-payment, an immediate right of recourse against the drawer,
  acceptor and endorsers accrues to the holder.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 95
:::

### [Notice of Dishonour]{.HTitleText2} {#h-29729 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Notice of dishonour

- **[[95]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-95
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Subject to this Act, when a bill has
  been dishonoured by non-acceptance or by non-payment, notice of
  dishonour must be given to the drawer and each endorser, and any
  drawer or endorser to whom the notice is not given is discharged.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Subsequent holder

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Where a bill is dishonoured by non-acceptance and
  notice of dishonour is not given, the rights of a holder in due course
  subsequent to the omission are not prejudiced by the omission.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Notice of subsequent dishonour

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} Where a bill is dishonoured by non-acceptance and due
  notice of dishonour is given, it is not necessary to give notice of a
  subsequent dishonour by non-payment, unless the bill is accepted in
  the meantime.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Notice to acceptor

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} In order to render the acceptor of a bill liable, it
  is not necessary that notice of dishonour be given to him.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 96
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Conditions for validity of notice

**[[96]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-96 .sectionLabel}** Notice of dishonour in
order to be valid and effectual must be given

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} not later than the juridical or business day next
  following the dishonouring of the bill;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} by or on behalf of the holder, or by or on behalf of
  an endorser, who at the time of giving notice is himself liable on the
  bill;

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} in the case of the death, if known to the party
  giving notice, of the drawer or endorser, to a personal representative
  if there is one and with the exercise of reasonable diligence he can
  be found; and

- [(d)]{.lawlabel} in case of two or more drawers or endorsers who are
  not partners, to each of them, unless one of them has authority to
  receive notice for the others.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 97
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}How notice given

- **[[97]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-97 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Notice
  of dishonour may be given

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} as soon as the bill is dishonoured;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} to the party to whom notice is required to be given
    or to his agent in that behalf;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} by an agent either in his own name or in the name
    of any party entitled to give notice, whether that party is his
    principal or not; or

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} in writing or by personal communication and in any
    terms that identify the bill and intimate that the bill has been
    dishonoured by non-acceptance or non-payment.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Misdescription

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} A misdescription of the bill does not vitiate the
  notice unless the party to whom the notice is given is in fact misled
  thereby.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 98
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Form of notice

- **[[98]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-98 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} In
  point of form, the return of a dishonoured bill to the drawer or
  endorser is a sufficient notice of dishonour, and a written notice
  need not be signed.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Verbal supplement

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} An insufficient written notice may be supplemented
  and validated by verbal communication.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 99
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Notice by agent

- **[[99]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-99 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Where
  a bill when dishonoured is in the hands of an agent, he may himself
  give notice to the parties liable on the bill, or he may give notice
  to his principal, in which case the principal on receipt of the notice
  has the same time for giving notice as if the agent had been an
  independent holder.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Time for notice

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Where the agent gives notice to his principal, he
  must do so within the same time as if he were an independent holder.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 100
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Notice to antecedent parties

**[[100]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-100 .sectionLabel}** Where a party to a bill
receives due notice of dishonour, he has, after the receipt of the
notice, the same period of time for giving notice to antecedent parties
that a holder has after dishonour.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 101
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Benefit of notice

**[[101]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-101 .sectionLabel}** A notice of dishonour
enures for the benefit

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} of all subsequent holders and of all prior endorsers
  who have a right of recourse against the party to whom the notice is
  given, where given on behalf of the holder; and

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} of the holder and all endorsers subsequent to the
  party to whom the notice is given, where given, by or on behalf of an
  endorser entitled under this Part to give notice.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 102
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}How notice addressed

- **[[102]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-102
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Notwithstanding anything in this
  Act, notice of dishonour of any bill payable in Canada is sufficiently
  given if it is addressed in due time to any party to the bill entitled
  to the notice, at his customary address or place of residence or at
  the place at which the bill is dated, unless any such party has, under
  his signature, designated another place, in which case the notice
  shall be sufficiently given if addressed to him in due time at that
  other place.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Sufficiency of notice

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} A notice referred to in subsection (1) shall be
  sufficient, although the place of residence of such party is other
  than either of the places mentioned in that subsection, and shall be
  deemed to have been duly served and given for all purposes if it is
  deposited in any post office, with the postage paid thereon, at any
  time during the day on which presentment has been made or on the next
  following juridical or business day.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Death of party

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} The notice referred to in subsection (1) is not
  invalid by reason only of the fact that the party to whom it is
  addressed is dead.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 103
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Miscarriage in post service

**[[103]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-103 .sectionLabel}** Where a notice of
dishonour is duly addressed and posted, as provided in section 102, the
sender is deemed to have given due notice of dishonour, notwithstanding
any miscarriage by the post office.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 104
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Excuse for delay

- **[[104]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-104
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Delay in giving notice of dishonour
  is excused where the delay is caused by circumstances beyond the
  control of the party giving notice and not imputable to his default,
  misconduct or negligence.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Diligence

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Where the cause of delay in giving notice of
  dishonour ceases to operate, the notice must be given with reasonable
  diligence.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 105
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Notice dispensed with

- **[[105]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-105
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Notice of dishonour is dispensed
  with

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} when, after the exercise of reasonable diligence,
    notice as required by this Act cannot be given to or does not reach
    the drawer or endorser sought to be charged; or

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} by waiver, express or implied.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Time of waiver

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Notice of dishonour may be waived before the time of
  giving notice has arrived or after the omission to give due notice.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 106
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Dispensing with notice re drawer

**[[106]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-106 .sectionLabel}** Notice of dishonour is
dispensed with as regards the drawer where

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} the drawer and drawee are the same person;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} the drawee is a fictitious person or a person not
  having capacity to contract;

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} the drawer is the person to whom the bill is
  presented for payment;

- [(d)]{.lawlabel} the drawee or acceptor is, as between himself and the
  drawer, under no obligation to accept or pay the bill; or

- [(e)]{.lawlabel} the drawer has countermanded payment.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 107
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Dispensing with notice re endorser

**[[107]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-107 .sectionLabel}** Notice of dishonour is
dispensed with as regards the endorser where

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} the drawee is a fictitious person or a person not
  having capacity to contract, and the endorser was aware of the fact at
  the time he endorsed the bill;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} the endorser is the person to whom the bill is
  presented for payment; or

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} the bill was accepted or made for his accommodation.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 108
:::

### [Protest]{.HTitleText2} {#h-29829 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Necessity of protest

**[[108]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-108 .sectionLabel}** In order to render the
acceptor of a bill liable, it is not necessary to protest it.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 109
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Protest dispensed with

**[[109]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-109 .sectionLabel}** Protest is dispensed
with by any circumstances that would dispense with notice of dishonour.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 110
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Delay excused

- **[[110]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-110
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Delay in noting or protesting is
  excused where the delay is caused by circumstances beyond the control
  of the holder and not imputable to his default, misconduct or
  negligence.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Diligence

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Where the cause of delay in noting or protesting
  ceases to operate, the bill must be noted or protested with reasonable
  diligence.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 111
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Foreign bill, non-acceptance

- **[[111]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-111
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Where a foreign bill appearing on
  the face of it to be such has been dishonoured by non-acceptance, it
  must be duly protested for non-acceptance.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Non-payment

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Where a foreign bill that has not been previously
  dishonoured by non-acceptance is dishonoured by non-payment, it must
  be duly protested for non-payment.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Balance

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} Where a foreign bill has been accepted only as to
  part, it must be protested as to the balance.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Discharge

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} Where a foreign bill is not protested as required by
  this section, the drawer and endorsers are discharged.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 112
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Protest of inland bill

**[[112]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-112 .sectionLabel}** Where an inland bill
has been dishonoured, it may, if the holder thinks fit, be noted and
protested for non-acceptance or non-payment, as the case may be, but it
is not necessary to note or protest an inland bill in order to have
recourse against the drawer or endorsers.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 113
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Protest unnecessary

**[[113]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-113 .sectionLabel}** Where a bill does not
on the face of it appear to be a foreign bill, protest thereof in case
of dishonour is unnecessary.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 114
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Subsequent protest for non-payment

**[[114]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-114 .sectionLabel}** A bill that has been
protested for non-acceptance, or a bill of which protest for
non-acceptance has been waived, may be subsequently protested for
non-payment.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 115
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Protest for better security

**[[115]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-115 .sectionLabel}** Where the acceptor of a
bill suspends payment before it matures, the holder may cause the bill
to be protested for better security against the drawer and endorsers.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 116
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Acceptance for honour

- **[[116]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-116
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Where a dishonoured bill has been
  accepted for honour under protest or contains a reference in case of
  need, it must be protested for non-payment before it is presented for
  payment to the acceptor for honour, or referee in case of need.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Protest for non-payment

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} When a bill is dishonoured by the acceptor for
  honour, it must be protested for non-payment by him.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 117
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Noting equivalent to protest

**[[117]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-117 .sectionLabel}** For the purposes of
this Act, where a bill is required to be protested within a specified
time or before some further proceeding is taken, it is sufficient that
the bill has been noted for protest before the expiration of the
specified time or the taking of the proceeding.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 118
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Protest on day of dishonour

- **[[118]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-118
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Subject to this Act, when a bill is
  protested, the protest must be made or noted on the day of its
  dishonour.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Extending protest

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} When a bill has been duly noted, the formal protest
  may be extended thereafter at any time as of the date of the noting.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 119
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Protest on copy or particulars

**[[119]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-119 .sectionLabel}** Where a bill is lost or
destroyed, or is wrongly or accidentally detained from the person
entitled to hold it, or is accidentally retained in a place other than
where payable, protest may be made on a copy or written particulars
thereof.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 120
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Place of protest

- **[[120]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-120 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A
  bill must be protested at the place where it is dishonoured, or at
  some other place in Canada situated within five miles of the place of
  presentment and dishonour of the bill.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Where bill returned by post

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} When a bill is presented through the post office and
  returned by post dishonoured, it may be protested at the place to
  which it is returned, not later than on the day of its return or the
  next juridical day.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Time of protest

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} Every protest for dishonour, either for
  non-acceptance or non-payment, may be made on the day of the
  dishonour, and in case of non-acceptance at any time after
  non-acceptance, and in case of non-payment at any time after three
  o'clock in the afternoon, local time.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 121
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Contents of protest

**[[121]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-121 .sectionLabel}** A protest must contain
a copy of the bill, or the original bill may be annexed thereto, must be
signed by the notary making it and must specify

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} the person at whose request the bill is protested;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} the place and date of protest;

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} the cause or reason for protest; and

- [(d)]{.lawlabel} the demand made and the answer given, if any, or the
  fact that the drawee or acceptor could not be found.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 122
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}When notary not accessible

**[[122]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-122 .sectionLabel}** Where a dishonoured
bill is authorized or required to be protested and the services of a
notary cannot be obtained at the place where the bill is dishonoured,
any justice of the peace resident in the place may present and protest
the bill and give all necessary notices and has all the necessary powers
of a notary with respect thereto.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 123
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Expense

- **[[123]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-123 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  expense of noting and protesting any bill and the postages thereby
  incurred shall be allowed and paid to the holder in addition to any
  interest thereon.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Notaries' fees

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Notaries may charge the fees in each province allowed
  them.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 124
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Forms

- **[[124]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-124 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  Forms in the schedule may be used in noting or protesting any bill and
  in giving notice thereof.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Annexing copy or original of bill

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} A copy of the bill and endorsement may be included in
  the Forms, or the original bill may be annexed and the necessary
  changes in that behalf made in the Forms.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 125
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}How notice of protest given

**[[125]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-125 .sectionLabel}** Notice of the protest
of any bill payable in Canada is sufficiently given and is sufficient
and deemed to have been duly given and served, if given during the day
on which protest has been made or on the next following juridical or
business day, to the same parties and in the same manner and addressed
in the same way as is provided by this Part for notice of dishonour.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 126
:::

### [Liabilities of Parties]{.HTitleText2} {#h-29933 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Equitable assignment

**[[126]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-126 .sectionLabel}** A bill, of itself, does
not operate as an assignment of funds in the hands of the drawee
available for the payment thereof, and the drawee of a bill who does not
accept as required by this Act is not liable on the instrument.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 127
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Engagement by acceptance

**[[127]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-127 .sectionLabel}** The acceptor of a bill
by accepting it engages that he will pay it according to the tenor of
his acceptance.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 128
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Estoppel

**[[128]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-128 .sectionLabel}** The acceptor of a bill
by accepting it is precluded from denying to a holder in due course

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} the existence of the drawer, the genuineness of his
  signature and his capacity and authority to draw the bill;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} in the case of a bill payable to drawer's order, the
  then capacity of the drawer to endorse, but not the genuineness or
  validity of his endorsement; or

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} in the case of a bill payable to the order of a third
  person, the existence of the payee and his then capacity to endorse,
  but not the genuineness or validity of his endorsement.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 129
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Drawer

**[[129]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-129 .sectionLabel}** The drawer of a bill by
drawing it

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} engages that on due presentment it shall be accepted
  and paid according to its tenor, and that if it is dishonoured he will
  compensate the holder or any endorser who is compelled to pay it, if
  the requisite proceedings on dishonour are duly taken; and

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} is precluded from denying to a holder in due course
  the existence of the payee and his then capacity to endorse.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 130
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Liability by signature

**[[130]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-130 .sectionLabel}** No person is liable as
drawer, endorser or acceptor of a bill who has not signed it as such,
but when a person signs a bill otherwise than as a drawer or acceptor,
he thereby incurs the liabilities of an endorser to a holder in due
course and is subject to all the provisions of this Act respecting
endorsers.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 131
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Trade-name or assumed name

- **[[131]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-131
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Where a person signs a bill in a
  trade-name or assumed name, he is liable thereon as if he had signed
  it in his own name.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Firm name

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The signature of the name of a firm is equivalent to
  the signature, by the person so signing, of the names of all persons
  liable as partners in that firm.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 132
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Endorser

**[[132]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-132 .sectionLabel}** The endorser of a bill
by endorsing it, subject to the effect of any express stipulation
authorized by this Act,

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} engages that on due presentment it shall be accepted
  and paid according to its tenor, and that if it is dishonoured he will
  compensate the holder or a subsequent endorser who is compelled to pay
  it, if the requisite proceedings on dishonour are duly taken;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} is precluded from denying to a holder in due course
  the genuineness and regularity in all respects of the drawer's
  signature and all previous endorsements; and

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} is precluded from denying to his immediate or a
  subsequent endorsee that the bill was, at the time of his endorsement,
  a valid and subsisting bill, and that he had then a good title
  thereto.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 133
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Measure of damages

**[[133]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-133 .sectionLabel}** Where a bill is
dishonoured, the measure of damages, which shall be deemed to be
liquidated damages, are

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} the amount of the bill;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} interest thereon from the time of presentment for
  payment, if the bill is payable on demand, and from the maturity of
  the bill in any other case; and

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} the expenses of noting and protesting.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 134
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Recovery of damages

**[[134]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-134 .sectionLabel}** In the case of a bill
that has been dishonoured, the holder may recover from any party liable
on the bill, the drawer who has been compelled to pay the bill may
recover from the acceptor, and an endorser who has been compelled to pay
the bill may recover from the acceptor or from the drawer, or from a
prior endorser, the damages prescribed in section 133.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 135
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Re-exchange and interest

**[[135]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-135 .sectionLabel}** In the case of a bill
that has been dishonoured abroad, in addition to the damages prescribed
in section 133, the holder may recover from the drawer or any endorser,
and the drawer or an endorser who has been compelled to pay the bill may
recover from any party liable to him, the amount of the re-exchange with
interest thereon until the time of payment.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 136
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Transferor by delivery

- **[[136]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-136
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Where the holder of a bill payable
  to bearer negotiates it by delivery without endorsing it, he is called
  a "transferor by delivery".

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Liability of transferor

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} A transferor by delivery is not liable on the
  instrument.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 137
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Warranty by transferor

**[[137]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-137 .sectionLabel}** A transferor by
delivery who negotiates a bill thereby warrants to his immediate
transferee, being a holder for value, that

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} the bill is what it purports to be;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} he has a right to transfer it; and

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} at the time of transfer, he is not aware of any fact
  that renders it valueless.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 138
:::

### [Discharge of Bill]{.HTitleText2} {#h-30002 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Payment

- **[[138]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-138 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A
  bill is discharged by payment in due course by or on behalf of the
  drawee or acceptor.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Payment in due course

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Payment in due course means payment made at or after
  the maturity of the bill to the holder thereof in good faith and
  without notice that his title to the bill is defective.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Accommodation bill

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} Where an accommodation bill is paid in due course by
  the party accommodated, the bill is discharged.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 139
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Payment by drawer or endorser

**[[139]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-139 .sectionLabel}** Subject to the
provisions of section 138 with respect to an accommodation bill, when a
bill is paid by the drawer or endorser, it is not discharged, but,

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} where a bill payable to, or to the order of, a third
  party is paid by the drawer, the drawer may enforce payment thereof
  against the acceptor, but may not reissue the bill; and

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} where a bill is paid by an endorser, or where a bill
  payable to drawer's order is paid by the drawer, the party paying it
  is remitted to his former rights as regards the acceptor or antecedent
  parties, and he may, if he thinks fit, strike out his own and
  subsequent endorsements and again negotiate the bill.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 140
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Acceptor holding at maturity

**[[140]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-140 .sectionLabel}** When the acceptor of a
bill is or becomes the holder of it, at or after its maturity, in his
own right, the bill is discharged.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 141
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Renouncing rights

- **[[141]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-141 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} When
  the holder of a bill, at or after its maturity, absolutely and
  unconditionally renounces his rights against the acceptor, the bill is
  discharged.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Against one party

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The liabilities of any party to a bill may in like
  manner be renounced by the holder before, at or after its maturity.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}In writing

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} A renunciation must be in writing, unless the bill is
  delivered to the acceptor.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Holder in due course

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} Nothing in this section affects the rights of a
  holder in due course without notice of renunciation.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 142
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Cancellation of bill

- **[[142]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-142
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Where a bill is intentionally
  cancelled by the holder or his agent and the cancellation is apparent
  thereon, the bill is discharged.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Of any signature

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} In like manner, any party liable on a bill may be
  discharged by the intentional cancellation of his signature by the
  holder or his agent.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Discharge of endorser

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} In any case described in subsection (2), any endorser
  who would have had a right of recourse against the party whose
  signature is cancelled is also discharged.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 143
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Unintentional cancellation

**[[143]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-143 .sectionLabel}** A cancellation made
unintentionally, or under a mistake, or without the authority of the
holder, is inoperative, but where a bill or any signature thereon
appears to have been cancelled, the burden of proof lies on the party
who alleges that the cancellation was made unintentionally, or under a
mistake, or without authority.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 144
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Alteration of bill

- **[[144]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-144
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Subject to subsection (2), where a
  bill or an acceptance is materially altered without the assent of all
  parties liable on the bill, the bill is voided, except as against a
  party who has himself made, authorized or assented to the alteration
  and subsequent endorsers.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Right of holder in due course

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Where a bill has been materially altered, but the
  alteration is not apparent, and the bill is in the hands of a holder
  in due course, the holder may avail himself of the bill as if it had
  not been altered and may enforce payment of it according to its
  original tenor.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 145
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Material alteration

**[[145]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-145 .sectionLabel}** In particular, any
alteration

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} of the date,

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} of the sum payable,

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} of the time of payment,

- [(d)]{.lawlabel} of the place of payment, or

- [(e)]{.lawlabel} by the addition of a place of payment without the
  acceptor's assent where a bill has been accepted generally,

is a material alteration.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 146
:::

### [Acceptance and Payment for Honour]{.HTitleText2} {#h-30063 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Acceptance for honour under protest

**[[146]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-146 .sectionLabel}** Where a bill of
exchange has been protested for dishonour by non-acceptance, or
protested for better security, and is not overdue, any person, not being
a party already liable thereon, may, with the consent of the holder,
intervene and accept the bill under protest for the honour of any party
liable thereon or for the honour of the person for whose account the
bill is drawn.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 147
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}In part

**[[147]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-147 .sectionLabel}** A bill may be accepted
for honour for part only of the sum for which it is drawn.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 148
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Deemed to be for honour of drawer

**[[148]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-148 .sectionLabel}** Where an acceptance for
honour does not expressly state for whose honour it is made, it is
deemed to be an acceptance for the honour of the drawer.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 149
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Maturity of after-sight bill

**[[149]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-149 .sectionLabel}** Where a bill payable
after sight is accepted for honour, its maturity is calculated from the
date of protesting for non-acceptance and not from the date of the
acceptance for honour.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 150
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Requirements

**[[150]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-150 .sectionLabel}** An acceptance for
honour under protest, in order to be valid, must be

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} written on the bill, and indicate that it is an
  acceptance for honour; and

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} signed by the acceptor for honour.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 151
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Liability of acceptor for honour

- **[[151]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-151 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  acceptor for honour of a bill by accepting it engages that he will, on
  due presentment, pay the bill according to the tenor of his
  acceptance, if it is not paid by the drawee, if it has been duly
  presented for payment and protested for non-payment and if he receives
  notice of those facts.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Liability to holder and others

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The acceptor for honour is liable to the holder and
  to all parties to the bill subsequent to the party for whose honour he
  has accepted.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 152
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Payment for honour under protest

- **[[152]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-152
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Where a bill has been protested for
  non-payment, any person may intervene and pay it under protest for the
  honour of any party liable thereon or for the honour of the person for
  whose account the bill is drawn.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}If more than one offer

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Where two or more persons offer to pay a bill for the
  honour of different parties, the person whose payment will discharge
  most parties to the bill has the preference.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Refusal to receive payment

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} Where the holder of a bill refuses to receive payment
  under protest, he loses his right of recourse against any party who
  would have been discharged by that payment.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Entitled to bill

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} The payer for honour, on paying to the holder the
  amount of the bill and the notarial expenses incidental to its
  dishonour, is entitled to receive both the bill itself and the
  protest.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Liability for refusing

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} Where the holder does not on demand in a case
  described in subsection (4) deliver up the bill and protest, he is
  liable to the payer for honour in damages.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 153
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Attestation of payment for honour

- **[[153]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-153
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Payment for honour under protest, in
  order to operate as such and not as a mere voluntary payment, must be
  attested by a notarial act of honour, which may be appended to the
  protest or form an extension of it.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Declaration

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The notarial act of honour must be founded on a
  declaration made by the payer for honour, or his agent in that behalf,
  declaring his intention to pay the bill for honour, and for whose
  honour he pays.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 154
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Discharge and subrogation

**[[154]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-154 .sectionLabel}** Where a bill has been
paid for honour, all parties subsequent to the party for whose honour it
is paid are discharged, but the payer for honour is subrogated for and
succeeds to both the rights and duties of the holder with respect to the
party for whose honour he pays, and all parties liable to that party.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 155
:::

### [Bill in a Set]{.HTitleText2} {#h-30129 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Bills in set

- **[[157]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-157
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Where a bill is drawn in a set, each
  part of the set being numbered, and containing a reference to the
  other parts, the whole of the parts constitute one bill.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Acceptance

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The acceptance may be written on any part, but it
  must be written on one part only.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 158
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Endorsing more than one part

- **[[158]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-158
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Where the holder of a set endorses
  two or more parts to different persons, he is liable on every such
  part, and every endorser subsequent to him is liable on the part he
  has himself endorsed as if the parts were separate bills.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Negotiation to different holders

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Where two or more parts of a set are negotiated to
  different holders in due course, the holder whose title first accrues
  is, as between such holders, deemed the true owner of the bill, but
  nothing in this subsection affects the rights of a person who in due
  course accepts or pays the part first presented to him.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Accepting more than one part

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} Where the drawee accepts more than one part and such
  accepted parts get into the hands of different holders in due course,
  he is liable on every such part as if it were a separate bill.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Payments without delivery

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} When the acceptor of a bill drawn in a set pays it
  without requiring the part bearing his acceptance to be delivered up
  to him, and that part at maturity is outstanding in the hands of a
  holder in due course, he is liable to the holder thereof.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Discharge

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} Subject to this section, where any one part of a bill
  drawn in a set is discharged by payment or otherwise, the whole bill
  is discharged.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 159
:::

### [Conflict of Laws]{.HTitleText2} {#h-30150 .Subheading}

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Conforming to the law of Canada

::: HistoricalNote

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Currency

::: HistoricalNote

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Due date

**[[163]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-163 .sectionLabel}** Where a bill is drawn

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 164
:::

### [Official Images and Electronic Presentment]{.HTitleText2} {#h-30176 .Subheading}

though it were the eligible bill.

::: HistoricalNote
- 2007, c. 6, s. 398
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Electronic presentment

- **[[163.3]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-163.3
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Despite anything in this Act, a bank

  electronically in accordance with by-laws, rules or standards made

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Discharge by payment

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The eligible bill and its official image are

- 2007, c. 6, s. 398
:::

- **[[163.4]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-163.4
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} In the absence of evidence to the

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Admissibility

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} An official image of an eligible bill is admissible

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} the destruction does not affect any person's rights,

  have been materially altered or intentionally cancelled.

::: HistoricalNote
- 2007, c. 6, s. 398

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Damages

## [PART III]{.HLabel1}[Cheques on a Bank]{.HTitleText1} {#h-30218 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Definition of "bank"

**[[164]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-164 .sectionLabel}** In this Part,
[[bank]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} includes every member of the Canadian
Payments Association established under the [Canadian Payments
Act](/eng/acts/C-21) and every local cooperative credit society, as
defined in that Act, that is a member of a central, as defined in that
Act, that is a member of the Canadian Payments Association.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., 1985, c. B-4, s. 164
- 2001, c. 9, s. 586
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Cheque

- **[[165]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-165 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A
  cheque is a bill drawn on a bank, payable on demand.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Provisions as to bills apply

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Except as otherwise provided in this Part, the
  provisions of this Act applicable to a bill payable on demand apply to
  a cheque.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Cheque for deposit to account

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} Where a cheque is delivered to a bank for deposit to
  the credit of a person and the bank credits him with the amount of the
  cheque, the bank acquires all the rights and powers of a holder in due
  course of the cheque.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 165
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Presentment for payment

- **[[166]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-166
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Subject to this Act,

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} where a cheque is not presented for payment within
    a reasonable time of its issue and the drawer or the person on whose
    account it is drawn had the right at the time of presentment, as
    between him and the bank, to have the cheque paid, and suffers
    actual damage through the delay, he is discharged to the extent of
    the damage, that is to say, to the extent to which the drawer or
    person is a creditor of the bank to a larger amount than he would
    have been had the cheque been paid; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the holder of the cheque, with respect to which the
    drawer or person is discharged, shall be a creditor, in lieu of the
    drawer or person, of the bank to the extent of the discharge, and
    entitled to recover the amount from it.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Reasonable time

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} In determining what is a reasonable time, within this
  section, regard shall be had to the nature of the instrument, the
  usage of trade and of banks and the facts of the particular case.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 166
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Authority to pay

**[[167]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-167 .sectionLabel}** The duty and authority
of a bank to pay a cheque drawn on it by its customer are determined by

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} countermand of payment; or

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} notice of the customer's death.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 167
:::

### [Crossed Cheques]{.HTitleText2} {#h-30247 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Crossed generally

- **[[168]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-168
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Where a cheque bears across its face
  an addition of

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the word "bank" between two parallel transverse
    lines, either with or without the words "not negotiable", or

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} two parallel transverse lines simply, either with
    or without the words "not negotiable",

  that addition constitutes a crossing, and the cheque is crossed
  generally.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Crossed specially

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Where a cheque bears across its face an addition of
  the name of a bank, either with or without the words "not negotiable",
  that addition constitutes a crossing, and the cheque is crossed
  specially and to that bank.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 168
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}By drawer

- **[[169]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-169 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A
  cheque may be crossed generally or specially by the drawer.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}By holder

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Where a cheque is uncrossed, the holder may cross it
  generally or specially.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Varying

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} Where a cheque is crossed generally, the holder may
  cross it specially.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Words may be added

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} Where a cheque is crossed generally or specially, the
  holder may add the words "not negotiable".

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}By bank for collection

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} Where a cheque is crossed specially, the bank to
  which it is crossed may again cross it specially to another bank for
  collection.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Changing crossing

  [(6)]{.lawlabel} Where an uncrossed cheque, or a cheque crossed
  generally, is sent to a bank for collection, it may cross it specially
  to itself.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Uncrossing

  [(7)]{.lawlabel} A crossed cheque may be reopened or uncrossed by the
  drawer writing between the transverse lines the words "pay cash", and
  initialling the same.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 169
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Material part

- **[[170]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-170 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A
  crossing authorized by this Act is a material part of the cheque.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Altering crossing

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} It is not lawful for any person to obliterate or,
  except as authorized by this Act, to add to or alter the crossing.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 170
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Crossed to more than one bank

**[[171]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-171 .sectionLabel}** Where a cheque is
crossed specially to more than one bank, except when crossed to another
bank as agent for collection, the bank on which it is drawn shall refuse
payment thereof.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 171
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Liability for improper payment

- **[[172]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-172
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Subject to subsection (2), where the
  bank on which a cheque crossed as described in section 171 is drawn
  nevertheless pays the cheque, or pays a cheque crossed generally
  otherwise than to a bank, or, if crossed specially, otherwise than to
  the bank to which it is crossed or to the bank acting as its agent for
  collection, it is liable to the true owner of the cheque for any loss
  he sustains owing to the cheque having been so paid.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Payment in good faith and without
  negligence

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Where a cheque is presented for payment that does not
  at the time of presentment appear to be crossed, or to have had a
  crossing that has been obliterated, or to have been added to or
  altered otherwise than as authorized by this Act, the bank paying the
  cheque in good faith and without negligence shall not be responsible
  or incur any liability, nor shall the payment be questioned by reason
  of the cheque having been crossed, or of the crossing having been
  obliterated or having been added to or altered otherwise than as
  authorized by this Act, and of payment having been made otherwise than
  to a bank or to the bank to which the cheque is or was crossed, or to
  the bank acting as its agent for collection, as the case may be.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 172
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Protection in such case

**[[173]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-173 .sectionLabel}** Where the bank on which
a crossed cheque is drawn in good faith and without negligence pays it,
if crossed generally, to a bank, or, if crossed specially, to the bank
to which it is crossed or to a bank acting as its agent for collection,
the bank paying the cheque and, if the cheque has come into the hands of
the payee, the drawer shall respectively be entitled to the same rights
and be placed in the same position as if payment of the cheque had been
made to the true owner thereof.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 173
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}"Not negotiable" cross

**[[174]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-174 .sectionLabel}** Where a person takes a
crossed cheque that bears on it the words "not negotiable", he does not
have and is not capable of giving a better title to the cheque than the
person from whom he took it had.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 174
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Customer without title

**[[175]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-175 .sectionLabel}** Where a bank, in good
faith and without negligence, receives for a customer payment of a
cheque crossed generally or specially to itself and the customer has no
title or a defective title thereto, the bank does not incur any
liability to the true owner of the cheque by reason only of having
received that payment.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 175
:::

## [PART IV]{.HLabel1}[Promissory Notes]{.HTitleText1} {#h-30305 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Definition

- **[[176]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-176 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A
  promissory note is an unconditional promise in writing made by one
  person to another person, signed by the maker, engaging to pay, on
  demand or at a fixed or determinable future time, a sum certain in
  money to, or to the order of, a specified person or to bearer.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Endorsed by maker

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} An instrument in the form of a note payable to the
  maker's order is not a note within the meaning of this section, unless
  it is endorsed by the maker.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Pledge of collateral security

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} A note is not invalid by reason only that it contains
  also a pledge of collateral security with authority to sell or dispose
  thereof.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 176
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Inland note

- **[[177]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-177 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A
  note that is, or on the face of it purports to be, both made and
  payable within Canada is an inland note.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Foreign note

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Any other note is a foreign note.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 177
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Delivery

**[[178]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-178 .sectionLabel}** A note is inchoate and
incomplete until delivery thereof to the payee or bearer.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 178
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Joint and several liability

- **[[179]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-179 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A
  note may be made by two or more makers, and they may be liable thereon
  jointly, or jointly and severally, according to its tenor.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Individual promise

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Where a note bears the words "I promise to pay" and
  is signed by two or more persons, it is deemed to be their joint and
  several note.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 179
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Demand note presentment

- **[[180]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-180
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Where a note payable on demand has
  been endorsed, it must be presented for payment within a reasonable
  time of the endorsement.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Reasonable time

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} In determining what is a reasonable time, regard
  shall be had to the nature of the instrument, the usage of trade and
  the facts of the particular case.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 180
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Endorser discharged

**[[181]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-181 .sectionLabel}** Where a note payable on
demand that has been endorsed is not presented for payment within a
reasonable time, the endorser is discharged but, if it has, with the
assent of the endorser, been delivered as a collateral or continuing
security, it need not be presented for payment so long as it is held as
such security.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 181
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Not deemed overdue

**[[182]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-182 .sectionLabel}** Where a note payable on
demand is negotiated, it is not deemed to be overdue, for the purpose of
affecting the holder with defects of title of which he had no notice, by
reason that it appears that a reasonable time for presenting it for
payment has elapsed since its issue.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 182
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Presentment at particular place

- **[[183]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-183
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Where a note is, in the body of it,
  made payable at a particular place, it must be presented for payment
  at that place.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Liability of maker

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} In the case described in subsection (1), the maker is
  not discharged by the omission to present the note for payment on the
  day that it matures, but if any suit or action is instituted thereon
  against him before presentation, the costs thereof are in the
  discretion of the court.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Note payable generally

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} When no place of payment is specified in the body of
  the note, presentment for payment is not necessary in order to render
  the maker liable.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 183
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Liability of endorser

- **[[184]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-184
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Presentment for payment is necessary
  in order to render the endorser of a note liable.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Presentment at particular place

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Where a note is, in the body of it, made payable at a
  particular place, presentment at that place is necessary in order to
  render an endorser liable.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Presentment elsewhere

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} When a place of payment is indicated by way of
  memorandum only, presentment at that place is sufficient to render the
  endorser liable, but a presentment to the maker elsewhere, if
  sufficient in other respects, shall also suffice.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 184
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Effect of being maker

**[[185]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-185 .sectionLabel}** The maker of a note, by
making it,

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} engages that he will pay it according to its tenor;
  and

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} is precluded from denying to a holder in due course
  the existence of the payee and his then capacity to endorse.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 185
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Application of Act to notes

- **[[186]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-186
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Subject to this Part, and except as
  provided by this section, the provisions of this Act relating to bills
  apply, with such modifications as the circumstances require, to notes.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Terms corresponding

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} In the application of the provisions of this Act
  relating to bills, the maker of a note shall be deemed to correspond
  with the acceptor of a bill, and the first endorser of a note shall be
  deemed to correspond with the drawer of an accepted bill payable to
  drawer's order.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Provisions inapplicable

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} The provisions of this Act with respect to bills and
  relating to

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} presentment for acceptance,

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} acceptance,

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} acceptance under protest, and

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} bills in a set,

  do not apply to notes.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 186
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Protest of foreign notes

**[[187]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-187 .sectionLabel}** Where a foreign note is
dishonoured, protest thereof is unnecessary, except for the preservation
of the liabilities of endorsers.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. B-5, s. 187

## [PART V]{.HLabel1}[Consumer Bills and Notes]{.HTitleText1} {#h-30390 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Definitions

**[[188]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-188 .sectionLabel}** In this Part,

[[consumer purchase]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[consumer purchase]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means a purchase, other
    than a cash purchase, of goods or services or an agreement to
    purchase goods or services

    - [(a)]{.lawlabel} by an individual other than for resale or for use
      in the course of his business, profession or calling, and

    - [(b)]{.lawlabel} from a person who is engaged in the business of
      selling or providing those goods or services; ([achat de
      consommation]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

[[goods]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[goods]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means any article that is or may be
    the subject of trade or commerce, but does not include land or any
    interest therein; ([marchandises]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

[[purchaser]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[purchaser]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means the individual by whom a
    consumer purchase is made; ([acheteur]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

[[seller]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[seller]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means the person from whom a consumer
    purchase is made; ([vendeur]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

[[services]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[services]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} includes repairs and
    improvements. ([services]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. 4(1st Supp.), s. 1
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Consumer bill

- **[[189]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-189 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A
  consumer bill is a bill of exchange issued in respect of a consumer
  purchase and on which the purchaser or any person signing to
  accommodate the purchaser is liable as a party, but does not include

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} a cheque that is dated the date of its issue or
    prior thereto, or at the time it is issued is post-dated not more
    than thirty days; or

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} a bill of exchange that

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} would be a cheque within the meaning of section
      165 but for the fact that the party on which it is drawn is a
      financial institution, other than a bank, that as part of its
      business accepts money on deposit from members of the public and
      honours any such bill directed to be paid out of any such deposit
      to the extent of the amount of the deposit, and

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} is dated the date of its issue or prior thereto,
      or at the time it is issued is post-dated not more than thirty
      days.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Consumer note

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} A consumer note is a promissory note

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} issued in respect of a consumer purchase; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} on which the purchaser or any one signing to
    accommodate him is liable as a party.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Presumption as to issue

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} Without limiting or restricting the circumstances in
  which, for the purposes of this Part, a bill of exchange or a
  promissory note shall be considered to be issued in respect of a
  consumer purchase, a bill of exchange or a promissory note shall be
  conclusively presumed to be so issued if

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the consideration for its issue was the lending or
    advancing of money or other valuable security by a person other than
    the seller, in order to enable the purchaser to make the consumer
    purchase; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the seller and the person who lent or advanced the
    money or other valuable security were, at the time the bill or note
    was issued, not dealing with each other at arm's length within the
    meaning of the [Income Tax Act](/eng/acts/I-3.3).

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Application of Act to consumer bills
  and notes

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} Except as otherwise provided in this Part, the
  provisions of this Act applicable to bills of exchange and cheques
  apply, with such modifications as the circumstances require, to
  consumer bills, and those applicable to promissory notes apply to
  consumer notes, with such modifications as the circumstances require.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. 4(1st Supp.), s. 1
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Consumer bill or note to be marked

- **[[190]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-190
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Every consumer bill or consumer note
  shall be prominently and legibly marked on its face with the words
  "Consumer Purchase" before or at the time when the instrument is
  signed by the purchaser or by any person signing to accommodate the
  purchaser.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Effect where not marked

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} A consumer bill or consumer note that is not marked
  as required by this section is void, except in the hands of a holder
  in due course without notice that the bill or note is a consumer bill
  or consumer note or except as against a drawee without that notice.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. 4(1st Supp.), s. 1
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Rights of holder of consumer bill or note

**[[191]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-191 .sectionLabel}** Notwithstanding any
agreement to the contrary, the right of a holder of a consumer bill or
consumer note that is marked as required by section 190 to have the
whole or any part thereof paid by the purchaser or any party signing to
accommodate the purchaser is subject to any defence or right of set-off,
other than counter-claim, that the purchaser would have had in an action
by the seller on the consumer bill or consumer note.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. 4(1st Supp.), s. 1
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Obtaining signature to unmarked
instrument

- **[[192]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-192
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Every person who, knowing that an
  instrument, other than an instrument described in paragraph 189(1)(a)
  or (b), has been, is being or is to be issued in respect of a consumer
  purchase, obtains the signature of the purchaser or of any person
  signing to accommodate the purchaser to that instrument without its
  being or having been marked as required by section 190 is guilty of

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} an offence and liable on summary conviction to a
    fine not exceeding one thousand dollars; or

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} an indictable offence and liable to a fine not
    exceeding five thousand dollars.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Transfer of unmarked consumer bill or
  note

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Every person who, knowing that a consumer bill or
  consumer note not marked as required by section 190 is a consumer bill
  or consumer note, transfers it is, unless he is the purchaser or any
  person signing to accommodate the purchaser, guilty of

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} an offence and liable on summary conviction to a
    fine not exceeding one thousand dollars; or

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} an indictable offence and liable to a fine not
    exceeding five thousand dollars.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., c. 4(1st Supp.), s. 1
:::
