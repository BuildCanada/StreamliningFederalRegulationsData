## Accessible Canada Act {#accessible-canada-act .Title-of-Act}

[S.C.]{.abbr title="Statutes of Canada"} 2019, c. 10
:::

Assented to 2019-06-21

An Act to ensure a barrier-free Canada
::::

::::::::::: section
::: {.section .order}
###### Preamble {#preamble .MarginalNote}

Whereas the Canadian Charter of Rights and Freedoms guarantees the right
to the equal protection and equal benefit of the law without
discrimination and, in particular, discrimination on the basis of
disability;

Whereas the [Canadian Human Rights Act](/eng/acts/H-6) recognizes that
all individuals should have an opportunity equal with other individuals
to make for themselves the lives that they are able and wish to have and
to have their needs accommodated without discrimination and, in
particular, discrimination on the basis of disability;

Whereas a proactive and systemic approach for identifying, removing and
preventing barriers to accessibility without delay complements the
rights of persons with disabilities under the [Canadian Human Rights
Act](/eng/acts/H-6);

Whereas Canada is a State Party to the United Nations Convention on the
Rights of Persons with Disabilities and Canada has agreed to take
appropriate measures respecting accessibility and to develop and monitor
minimum accessibility standards;

Whereas barriers to accessibility can impact all persons in Canada, in
particular those with disabilities and their families, and can prevent
persons with disabilities from achieving their full and equal
participation in society;

And whereas Parliament considers that it is essential to ensure the
economic, social and civic participation of all persons in Canada,
regardless of their disabilities, and to allow them to fully exercise
their rights and responsibilities in a barrier-free Canada;
:::

::: Enacts
Now, therefore, Her Majesty, by and with the advice and consent of the
Senate and House of Commons of Canada, enacts as follows:
:::

## [Short Title]{.HTitleText1} {#h-1153392 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Short title

**[[1]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-1 .sectionLabel}** This Act may be cited as
the [Accessible Canada Act](/eng/acts/A-0.6).

## [Interpretation]{.HTitleText1} {#h-1153395 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Definitions

**[[2]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-2 .sectionLabel}** The following definitions
apply in this Act.

[[Accessibility Commissioner]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[Accessibility Commissioner]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means the member
    of the Canadian Human Rights Commission that is appointed under
    subsection 26(1) of the [Canadian Human Rights Act](/eng/acts/H-6)
    and that is referred to in that Act as the "Accessibility
    Commissioner". ([commissaire à l'accessibilité]{.DefinedTermLink
    lang="fr"})

[[barrier]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[barrier]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means anything --- including
    anything physical, architectural, technological or attitudinal,
    anything that is based on information or communications or anything
    that is the result of a policy or a practice --- that hinders the
    full and equal participation in society of persons with an
    impairment, including a physical, mental, intellectual, cognitive,
    learning, communication or sensory impairment or a functional
    limitation. ([obstacle]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

[[broadcasting undertaking]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[broadcasting undertaking]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} has the same
    meaning as in subsection 2(1) of the [Broadcasting
    Act](/eng/acts/B-9.01). ([entreprise de
    radiodiffusion]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

[[Canadian carrier]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[Canadian carrier]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} has the same meaning as in
    subsection 2(1) of the [Telecommunications
    Act](/eng/acts/T-3.4). ([entreprise canadienne]{.DefinedTermLink
    lang="fr"})

[[disability]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[disability]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means any impairment, including a
    physical, mental, intellectual, cognitive, learning, communication
    or sensory impairment --- or a functional limitation --- whether
    permanent, temporary or episodic in nature, or evident or not, that,
    in interaction with a barrier, hinders a person's full and equal
    participation in society. ([handicap]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

[[Minister]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[Minister]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means the member of the Queen's
    Privy Council for Canada designated under section
    4. ([ministre]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

[[personal information]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[personal information]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} has the same meaning as
    in section 3 of the [Privacy Act](/eng/acts/P-21). ([renseignements
    personnels]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

[[regulated entity]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[regulated entity]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means an entity or person
    referred to in subsection 7(1). ([entité
    réglementée]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

[[Standards Organization]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[Standards Organization]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means the Canadian
    Accessibility Standards Development Organization established under
    subsection 17(1). ([organisation de normalisation]{.DefinedTermLink
    lang="fr"})

[[telecommunications service provider]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[telecommunications service provider]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} has the
    same meaning as in subsection 2(1) of the [Telecommunications
    Act](/eng/acts/T-3.4). ([fournisseur de services de
    télécommunication]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

## [Purpose of Act]{.HTitleText1} {#h-1153414 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Purpose

**[[5]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-5 .sectionLabel}** The purpose of this Act is
to benefit all persons, especially persons with disabilities, through
the realization, within the purview of matters coming within the
legislative authority of Parliament, of a Canada without barriers, on or
before January 1, 2040, particularly by the identification and removal
of barriers, and the prevention of new barriers, in the following areas:

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} employment;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} the built environment;

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} information and communication technologies;

- [(c.1)]{.lawlabel} communication, other than information and
  communication technologies;

- [(d)]{.lawlabel} the procurement of goods, services and facilities;

- [(e)]{.lawlabel} the design and delivery of programs and services;

- [(f)]{.lawlabel} transportation; and

- [(g)]{.lawlabel} areas designated under regulations made under
  paragraph 117(1)(b).

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Clarification

- **[[5.1]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-5.1 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  area of communication referred to in paragraph 5(c.1)

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} includes the use of American Sign Language, Quebec
    Sign Language and Indigenous sign languages; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} does not include
    [[broadcasting]{.dfn}]{.DefinitionRef} as defined in subsection 2(1)
    of the [Broadcasting Act](/eng/acts/B-9.01) or
    [[telecommunications]{.dfn}]{.DefinitionRef} as defined in
    subsection 2(1) of the [Telecommunications Act](/eng/acts/T-3.4).

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Recognition of sign languages

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} American Sign Language, Quebec Sign Language and
  Indigenous sign languages are recognized as the primary languages for
  communication by deaf persons in Canada.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Interpretation

**[[5.2]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-5.2 .sectionLabel}** Nothing in this Act,
including its purpose of the realization of a Canada without barriers,
should be construed as requiring or authorizing any delay in the removal
of barriers or the implementation of measures to prevent new barriers as
soon as is reasonably possible.

## [Principles]{.HTitleText1} {#h-1153434 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Principles

**[[6]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-6 .sectionLabel}** This Act is to be carried
out in recognition of, and in accordance with, the following principles:

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} all persons must be treated with dignity regardless
  of their disabilities;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} all persons must have the same opportunity to make
  for themselves the lives that they are able and wish to have
  regardless of their disabilities;

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} all persons must have barrier-free access to full and
  equal participation in society, regardless of their disabilities;

- [(d)]{.lawlabel} all persons must have meaningful options and be free
  to make their own choices, with support if they desire, regardless of
  their disabilities;

- [(e)]{.lawlabel} laws, policies, programs, services and structures
  must take into account the disabilities of persons, the different ways
  that persons interact with their environments and the multiple and
  intersecting forms of marginalization and discrimination faced by
  persons;

- [(f)]{.lawlabel} persons with disabilities must be involved in the
  development and design of laws, policies, programs, services and
  structures; and

- [(g)]{.lawlabel} the development and revision of accessibility
  standards and the making of regulations must be done with the
  objective of achieving the highest level of accessibility for persons
  with disabilities.

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} each entity named or set out in any of Schedules I
    to V to the [Financial Administration Act](/eng/acts/F-11);

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} each [[Crown corporation]{.dfn}]{.DefinitionRef},
    as defined in subsection 83(1) of the [Financial Administration
    Act](/eng/acts/F-11) that is not referred to in Schedule III to that
    Act;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} every portion of the federal public administration
    that is designated under subsection (3);

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} the Canadian Forces;

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} any person, partnership or unincorporated
    organization that operates a work or carries on an undertaking or
    business that is within the legislative authority of Parliament,
    other than a work, undertaking or business of a local or private
    nature in Yukon, the Northwest Territories or Nunavut; and

  - [(f)]{.lawlabel} any entity or person --- including a trustee,
    executor, administrator, liquidator of the succession, guardian,
    curator or tutor --- that acts in the name of, or for the benefit
    of, any entity or person in the operation of a work or carrying on
    of an undertaking or business that is within the legislative
    authority of Parliament, other than a work, undertaking or business
    of a local or private nature in Yukon, the Northwest Territories or
    Nunavut.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Parliamentary entities

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} This Act also applies, to the extent provided for in
  Part 9, to the entities referred to in the definition of
  [[parliamentary entity]{.dfn}]{.DefinitionRef} in section 134.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Designation

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} For the purposes of paragraph (1)(c), the Governor in
  Council may, by order, designate any portion of the federal public
  administration that is not named or set out in any of Schedules I to V
  to the [Financial Administration Act](/eng/acts/F-11).

**[[8]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-8 .sectionLabel}** Nothing in this Act applies

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Canadian Forces

**[[9]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-9 .sectionLabel}** Nothing in this Act is to

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Royal Canadian Mounted Police

**[[10]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-10 .sectionLabel}** Nothing in this Act is to

## [PART 1]{.HLabel1}[Minister's Powers, Duties and Functions]{.HTitleText1} {#h-1153464 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Mandate

- **[[11]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-11 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  Minister's mandate is the realization of a Canada without barriers on
  or before January 1, 2040.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Powers

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} In carrying out his or her mandate, the Minister may,
  among other things,

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} provide information, advice and assistance in
    relation to matters relating to accessibility; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} promote, support and conduct research into the
    identification and removal of barriers and the prevention of new
    barriers.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Minister's powers, duties and functions

**[[12]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-12 .sectionLabel}** The Minister's powers,
duties and functions extend to and include all matters relating to
accessibility over which Parliament has jurisdiction and that are not by
law assigned to any other Minister or to any department, board or agency
of the Government of Canada.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Policies, programs and projects

**[[13]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-13 .sectionLabel}** The Minister may
initiate, recommend, implement and promote policies, programs and
projects in relation to matters relating to accessibility.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Grants and contributions

**[[14]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-14 .sectionLabel}** The Minister may make
grants and contributions in support of the Minister's programs and
projects in relation to matters relating to accessibility.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Information

**[[15]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-15 .sectionLabel}** Subject to the
[Statistics Act](/eng/acts/S-19), the Minister may collect, analyse,
interpret, publish and distribute information in relation to matters
relating to accessibility.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Coordination with provincial and
territorial authorities

**[[16]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-16 .sectionLabel}** The Minister must make
every reasonable effort to collaborate with provincial or territorial
authorities with a view to coordinating efforts in relation to matters
relating to accessibility.

## [PART 2]{.HLabel1}[Canadian Accessibility Standards Development Organization]{.HTitleText1} {#h-1153482 .Part}

### [Establishment]{.HTitleText2} {#h-1153483 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Canadian Accessibility Standards
Development Organization

- **[[17]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-17 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A
  corporation is established to be known as the Canadian Accessibility
  Standards Development Organization.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Agent of Her Majesty

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The Standards Organization is an agent of Her Majesty
  in right of Canada.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Head office

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} The head office of the Standards Organization is to
  be at a place in Canada that is designated by the Governor in Council.

### [Mandate]{.HTitleText2} {#h-1153491 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Mandate

**[[18]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-18 .sectionLabel}** The Standards
Organization's mandate is to contribute to the realization of a Canada
without barriers, on or before January 1, 2040, through, among other
things,

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} the development and revision of accessibility
  standards;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} the recommendation of accessibility standards to the
  Minister;

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} the provision of information, products and services
  in relation to the accessibility standards that it has developed or
  revised;

- [(d)]{.lawlabel} the promotion, support and conduct of research into
  the identification and removal of barriers and the prevention of new
  barriers; and

- [(e)]{.lawlabel} the dissemination of information, including
  information about best practices, in relation to the identification
  and removal of barriers and the prevention of new barriers.

### [Powers]{.HTitleText2} {#h-1153499 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Powers

**[[19]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-19 .sectionLabel}** The Standards
Organization, in carrying out its mandate, may

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} enter into contracts, agreements or other
  arrangements with any person or entity, including any government, in
  the name of Her Majesty in right of Canada or in its own name;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} make grants and contributions;

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} establish and register its own marks under the
  [Trademarks Act](/eng/acts/T-13) and authorize and regulate their use
  subject to that Act;

- [(d)]{.lawlabel} license, sell or otherwise make available any patent,
  copyright, industrial design, trademark or other similar property
  right that it holds, controls or administers;

- [(e)]{.lawlabel} charge a fee for any accessibility standard that it
  develops or revises and any information, product or service that it
  provides under this Act;

- [(f)]{.lawlabel} spend any money that it receives through its
  activities, in the fiscal year in which the money is received or in
  the subsequent fiscal year;

- [(g)]{.lawlabel} acquire any money, securities or other personal or
  movable property by gift or bequest and expend, administer or dispose
  of the property subject to the terms, if any, on which the gift or
  bequest was made; and

- [(h)]{.lawlabel} undertake any other activities that it considers
  conducive to the furtherance of its mandate and the exercise of its
  powers.

### [Board of Directors]{.HTitleText2} {#h-1153518 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Establishment and composition

**[[22]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-22 .sectionLabel}** The Standards
Organization is to have a board of directors consisting of not more than
11 directors, including a Chair and a Vice-Chair.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Appointment and tenure

- **[[23]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-23 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  directors are to be appointed by the Governor in Council to hold
  office on a part-time basis and during pleasure for a term of not more
  than four years that will ensure, to the extent possible, the end in
  any one year of the terms of office of not more than one half of the
  directors.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Appointment considerations

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The appointment of directors is to be made having
  regard to the following considerations:

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} that at all times, as far as possible, the majority
    of the directors are persons with disabilities;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the importance of having directors that are
    representative of the diversity of Canadian society; and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} the importance of having directors that are
    representative of the diversity of disabilities faced by Canadians.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Persons not eligible for appointment

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} A person is not eligible to be appointed or to
  continue as a director if the person

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} is not a Canadian citizen or a [[permanent
    resident]{.dfn}]{.DefinitionRef} within the meaning of subsection
    2(1) of the [Immigration and Refugee Protection
    Act](/eng/acts/I-2.5);

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} is a member of the Senate or House of Commons or a
    member of a provincial or territorial legislature; or

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} is employed on a full-time basis in the federal
    public administration or the public service of a province or
    territory.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Reappointment

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} A director is eligible for reappointment in the same
  or another capacity.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Remuneration and expenses

**[[24]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-24 .sectionLabel}** A director is to be paid
the remuneration that is fixed by the Governor in Council and is
entitled to be paid reasonable travel and living expenses incurred while
absent from their ordinary place of residence in the course of
performing their duties under this Act.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Benefits

**[[25]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-25 .sectionLabel}** A director is deemed to
be an employee for the purposes of the [Government Employees
Compensation Act](/eng/acts/G-5) and employed in the federal public
administration for the purposes of regulations made under section 9 of
the [Aeronautics Act](/eng/acts/A-2).

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Role of board of directors

**[[26]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-26 .sectionLabel}** The board of directors is
responsible for

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} setting the strategic direction for the Standards
  Organization;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} supervising and managing the Standards Organization's
  activities and affairs; and

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} advising the Chief Executive Officer on matters
  relating to the Standards Organization's mandate.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}By-laws

- **[[27]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-27 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  board of directors may make by-laws respecting the carrying out of its
  activities and the conduct of its affairs.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Copy to Minister

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The board of directors must send a copy of every
  by-law to the Minister.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Advisory and other committees

**[[28]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-28 .sectionLabel}** The board of directors
may, in accordance with the by-laws, appoint advisory or other
committees.

### [Chair]{.HTitleText2} {#h-1153552 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Role of Chair

- **[[29]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-29 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  Chair presides over meetings of the board of directors and performs
  any other duties that are assigned by the board.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Absence or incapacity of Chair

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} In the event of the absence or incapacity of the
  Chair, or a vacancy in that office, the Vice-Chair acts as Chair.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Absence or incapacity of Chair and
  Vice-Chair

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} In the event of the absence or incapacity of the
  Chair and the Vice-Chair or a vacancy in both those offices, the
  Minister may authorize another director to act as Chair, but no
  director so authorized has authority to act for a term of more than 90
  days without the Governor in Council's approval.

### [Chief Executive Officer]{.HTitleText2} {#h-1153560 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Appointment

- **[[30]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-30 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  Chief Executive Officer of the Standards Organization is to be
  appointed by the Governor in Council to hold office on a full-time
  basis during pleasure for a term of up to five years.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Reappointment

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The Chief Executive Officer is eligible for
  reappointment.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Remuneration and expenses

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} The Chief Executive Officer is to be paid the
  remuneration that is fixed by the Governor in Council and is entitled
  to be paid reasonable travel and living expenses incurred while absent
  from his or her ordinary place of work in the course of performing his
  or her duties under this Act.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Benefits

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} The Chief Executive Officer is deemed to be employed
  in the public service for the purposes of the [Public Service
  Superannuation Act](/eng/acts/P-36), an employee for the purposes of
  the [Government Employees Compensation Act](/eng/acts/G-5) and
  employed in the federal public administration for the purposes of
  regulations made under section 9 of the [Aeronautics
  Act](/eng/acts/A-2).

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Role of Chief Executive Officer

- **[[31]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-31 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  Chief Executive Officer is responsible for the Standards
  Organization's day-to-day operations.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Rank of deputy head

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The Chief Executive Officer has the rank and the
  powers of a deputy head of a department.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Absence or incapacity of Chief
  Executive Officer

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} In the event of the absence or incapacity of the
  Chief Executive Officer, or a vacancy in that office, the Minister may
  authorize any person to act as Chief Executive Officer, but no person
  so authorized has authority to act for a term of more than 90 days
  without the Governor in Council's approval.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Committees

- **[[32]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-32 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  Chief Executive Officer may establish committees to assist in the
  development and revision of accessibility standards.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Public notice

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} As soon as feasible after establishing a committee,

  preparation of the plan.

  made under that subsection that is indicated in the request.

  prescribed by regulations made under subsection 45(1) and must
  indicate the format prescribed by regulations made under that

## [PART 5]{.HLabel1}[Administration and Enforcement]{.HTitleText1} {#h-1154074 .Part}

### [Inspections]{.HTitleText2} {#h-1154075 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Power to enter

- **[[73]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-73
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Subject to subsection (7), the
  Accessibility Commissioner may, for a purpose related to verifying
  compliance or preventing non-compliance with any of sections 47 to 49,
  56 to 58, 65 to 67 and 69 to 71 or any provision of regulations made
  under subsection 117(1), enter any place, including a conveyance, in
  which he or she has reasonable grounds to believe there is any record,
  report, electronic data or other document, or any information or
  thing, relevant to that purpose.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Other powers

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The Accessibility Commissioner may, for that purpose,

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} open and examine any receptacle or package found in
    the place;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} examine anything found in the place;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} examine any record, report, electronic data or
    other document that is found in the place and make copies of it or
    take extracts from it;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} use or cause to be used any computer system at the
    place to examine any electronic data referred to in paragraph (c);

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} reproduce any document from any electronic data
    referred to in paragraph (c), or cause it to be reproduced, in the
    form of a printout or other output;

  - [(f)]{.lawlabel} take the record, report or other document referred
    to in paragraph (c) or the printout or other output referred to in
    paragraph (e) for examination or copying;

  - [(g)]{.lawlabel} use or cause to be used any copying equipment at
    the place to make copies of any document;

  - [(h)]{.lawlabel} take photographs and make recordings and sketches;

  - [(i)]{.lawlabel} order the owner or person having possession of any
    thing to which any provision of regulations made under subsection
    117(1) applies that is found in that place to move it or, for any
    time that may be necessary, not to move it or to restrict its
    movement;

  - [(j)]{.lawlabel} order the owner or person having possession of any
    conveyance that is found in the place to stop the conveyance, to
    move it or, for any time that may be necessary, not to move it or to
    restrict its movement;

  - [(k)]{.lawlabel} order any person in that place to establish their
    identity to the Accessibility Commissioner's satisfaction, or to the
    satisfaction of the Accessibility Commissioner's delegate, as the
    case may be; and

  - [(l)]{.lawlabel} order a person in that place to start any activity
    that is necessary for the purpose of the inspection or to stop any
    activity that impedes the inspection.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Means of telecommunication

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} For the purposes of subsection (1), the Accessibility
  Commissioner is considered to have entered a place when accessing it
  remotely by a means of telecommunication.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Limitation --- place not accessible to
  the public

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} The Accessibility Commissioner who, by a means of
  telecommunication, accesses remotely a place that is not accessible to
  the public must do so with the knowledge of the owner or person in
  charge of the place and must be remotely in the place for no longer
  than the period necessary for the purpose referred to in subsection
  (1).

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Accompanying individual

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} The Accessibility Commissioner may be accompanied by
  any other individual the Accessibility Commissioner believes is
  necessary to help him or her exercise his or her powers or perform his
  or her duties or functions under this section.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Entering private property

  [(6)]{.lawlabel} The Accessibility Commissioner and any individual
  accompanying him or her may enter and pass through private property,
  other than a dwelling-house on that property, in order to gain entry
  to a place referred to in subsection (1). For greater certainty, they
  are not liable for doing so.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Consent required to enter
  dwelling-house

  [(7)]{.lawlabel} In the case of a dwelling-house, the Accessibility
  Commissioner may enter it only with the consent of an occupant.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Assistance

  [(8)]{.lawlabel} The owner or other person in charge of a place
  entered by the Accessibility Commissioner or his or her delegate under
  subsection (1) and every individual found in the place must give the
  Accessibility Commissioner or the delegate, as the case may be, all
  reasonable assistance in the individual's power and provide the
  Accessibility Commissioner or delegate with any information that the
  Accessibility Commissioner or delegate may reasonably require.

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} include a provision for the deposit of reasonable

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} provide for the reduction, in whole or in part, of

  cause the regulated entity or person to be served with a notice of

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the proceedings commenced in respect of the

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} any security given under the compliance agreement

  must cause the regulated entity or person to be served with a notice

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} that instead of being liable to pay the amount of

    within the time and in the manner set out in the notice of default,
    and without taking account of the limit set out in subsection 91(2),
    an amount that is twice the amount of that penalty;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} that the security, if any, given under the

    Canada.

  (4)(a), the regulated entity or person may not deduct from the amount

  the notice of default within the time and in the manner specified in
  the notice of default.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Effect of service of notice of default

  [(6)]{.lawlabel} If served with a notice of default under paragraph

  [(7)]{.lawlabel} If the regulated entity or person pays the amount set

  the violation are ended.

  penalty set out in the notice of violation.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Effect of payment

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} If the regulated entity or person pays the amount set
  out in the notice of violation within the time and in the manner
  specified in the notice of violation,

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} they are deemed to have committed the violation in
    respect of which the amount is paid;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the Accessibility Commissioner must accept the
    amount in complete satisfaction of the penalty in respect of the
    violation; and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} the proceedings commenced in respect of the
    violation are ended.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Deeming

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} If the regulated entity or person does not pay the
  amount set out in the notice of violation within the time and in the
  manner specified in the notice of violation, they are deemed to have
  committed the violation identified in the notice of violation.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Review --- with respect to facts

- **[[84]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-84 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} On
  completion of a review requested under subsection 80(1), or under
  paragraph 81(2)(b) with respect to the acts or omissions that
  constitute the violation, the Accessibility Commissioner must
  determine, on a balance of probabilities, whether the regulated entity
  or person that requested the review committed the violation.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Violation not committed --- effect

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} If the Accessibility Commissioner determines under
  subsection (1) that the regulated entity or person did not commit the
  violation, the proceedings commenced in respect of it are ended.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Violation committed --- penalty

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} If the Accessibility Commissioner determines that the
  regulated entity or person committed the violation, and a penalty was
  set out in the notice of violation, the Accessibility Commissioner
  must determine whether the amount of the penalty was fixed in
  accordance with regulations made under subsection 91(1) and

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} if the Accessibility Commissioner determines that
    it was correctly fixed, he or she must confirm the amount of the
    penalty; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} if the Accessibility Commissioner determines that
    it was not correctly fixed, he or she must correct the amount.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Notice of decision

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} The Accessibility Commissioner must cause the
  regulated entity or person to be served with a notice that sets out
  the Accessibility Commissioner's decision under this section and the
  reasons for it and, if the amount of the penalty was confirmed or
  corrected by the Accessibility Commissioner, the time and manner in
  which that amount is to be paid.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Payment

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} The regulated entity or person is liable to pay,
  within the time and in the manner specified in the notice, the amount
  of the penalty confirmed or corrected by the Accessibility
  Commissioner.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Effect of payment

  [(6)]{.lawlabel} If a regulated entity or person pays the amount
  referred to in subsection (5), the Accessibility Commissioner must
  accept the amount in complete satisfaction of the penalty in respect
  of the violation and the proceedings commenced in respect of the
  violation are ended.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Certain defences not available

- **[[85]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-85 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A
  regulated entity or person named in a notice of violation does not
  have a defence by reason that they

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} exercised due diligence to prevent the violation;
    or

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} reasonably and honestly believed in the existence
    of facts that, if true, would exonerate them.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Common law principles

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Every rule and principle of the common law that
  renders any circumstance a justification or excuse if the act or
  omission to which the violation relates could have been the subject of
  a charge for an offence under this Act but for section 127 applies in
  respect of a violation to the extent that it is not inconsistent with
  this Act.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Party to violation

**[[86]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-86 .sectionLabel}** If a regulated entity
commits a violation, any of the following persons that directed,
authorized, assented to, acquiesced in or participated in the commission
of the violation is a party to and liable for the violation whether or
not the regulated entity is proceeded against under this Act:

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} an officer, director, agent or mandatary of the
  regulated entity;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} a senior official of the regulated entity; or

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} any other person authorized to exercise managerial or
  supervisory functions on behalf of the regulated entity.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Employees or agents or mandataries

**[[87]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-87 .sectionLabel}** A regulated entity is
liable for a violation that is committed by any of their employees or
agents or mandataries acting in the course of their employment or the
scope of their authority as agent or mandatary, whether or not the
employee or agent or mandatary that actually committed the violation is
identified.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Continuing violation

**[[88]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-88 .sectionLabel}** A violation that is
committed or continued on more than one day constitutes a separate
violation in respect of each day on which it is committed or continued.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Evidence

**[[89]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-89 .sectionLabel}** In any proceeding in
respect of a violation, a notice of violation purporting to be issued
under this Act is admissible in evidence without proof of the signature
or official character of the individual purporting to have signed the
notice of violation.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Limitation period or prescription

**[[90]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-90 .sectionLabel}** No proceedings in respect
of a violation may be commenced after the expiry of two years after the
day on which the subject matter of the proceedings arose.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Regulations

- **[[91]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-91 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  Governor in Council may make regulations

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} classifying each violation as a minor violation, a
    serious violation or a very serious violation;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} fixing a penalty, or a range of penalties, in
    respect of each violation;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} establishing criteria to be considered in
    determining the amount of the penalty if a range of penalties is
    established;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} respecting the determination of a lesser amount for
    the purposes of subparagraph 79(1)(b)(iii) and the time and manner
    in which it is to be paid;

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} respecting the circumstances under which, the
    criteria by which and the manner in which a penalty for a violation
    may be reduced under the terms of a compliance agreement entered
    into under subsection 82(1);

  - [(f)]{.lawlabel} respecting the circumstances under which reviews
    under section 84 are to be oral or in writing; and

  - [(g)]{.lawlabel} specifying information for the purposes of section
    93.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Paragraph (1)(b)

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The maximum penalty in respect of a violation that
  may be fixed under regulations made under paragraph (1)(b) is
  \$250,000.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Powers regarding notices of violation

**[[92]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-92 .sectionLabel}** The Accessibility
Commissioner may establish the form of notices of violation and
establish, in respect of each violation, a short-form description to be
used in notices of violation.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Publication

**[[93]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-93 .sectionLabel}** The Accessibility
Commissioner may make public

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} the name of a regulated entity or person that is
  determined under section 84, or that is deemed by this Act, to have
  committed a violation;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} the nature of the violation;

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} the amount of the penalty imposed, if any; and

- [(d)]{.lawlabel} any other information specified in regulations made
  under subsection 91(1).

## [PART 6]{.HLabel1}[Remedies]{.HTitleText1} {#h-1154257 .Part}

### [Filing of Complaint]{.HTitleText2} {#h-1154258 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Right to file complaint

- **[[94]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-94 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Any
  individual that has suffered physical or psychological harm, property
  damage or economic loss as the result of --- or that has otherwise
  been adversely affected by --- a contravention by a regulated entity
  of any provision of regulations made under subsection 117(1) may file
  with the Accessibility Commissioner a complaint that is in a form
  acceptable to the Accessibility Commissioner.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Exception --- [Federal Public Sector
  Labour Relations Act](/eng/acts/P-33.3) --- employee

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} An [[employee]{.dfn}]{.DefinitionRef}, as defined in
  subsection 2(1) of the [Federal Public Sector Labour Relations
  Act](/eng/acts/P-33.3), is not entitled to file a complaint in respect
  of a contravention of any provision of regulations made under
  subsection 117(1) if he or she is entitled to present an individual
  grievance in respect of that contravention under section 208 of that
  Act, determined without taking into account subsection 208(2) of that
  Act and the definition of [[employee]{.dfn}]{.DefinitionRef} in
  subsection 206(1) of that Act.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Exception --- [Federal Public Sector
  Labour Relations Act](/eng/acts/P-33.3) --- RCMP member

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} An [[employee]{.dfn}]{.DefinitionRef}, as defined in
  subsection 2(1) of the [Federal Public Sector Labour Relations
  Act](/eng/acts/P-33.3), that is an RCMP member is not entitled to file
  a complaint in respect of a contravention of any provision of
  regulations made under subsection 117(1) if he or she is entitled to
  present an individual grievance in respect of that contravention under
  section 238.24 of that Act, determined without taking into account
  subsection 208(2) of that Act.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Exception --- [Public Service
  Employment Act](/eng/acts/P-33.01)

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} An individual is not entitled to file a complaint in
  respect of a contravention of any provision of regulations made under
  subsection 117(1) if he or she is entitled to make a complaint in
  respect of that contravention under section 65 or 77 of the [Public
  Service Employment Act](/eng/acts/P-33.01).

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Notice

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} The Accessibility Commissioner must cause a written
  notice of a complaint to be served on the regulated entity against
  which the complaint was made.

### [Investigation]{.HTitleText2} {#h-1154270 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Power to conduct investigation

**[[95]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-95 .sectionLabel}** The Accessibility
Commissioner may conduct an investigation into a complaint filed under
subsection 94(1) unless it appears to him or her that

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} the complainant ought to exhaust grievance or review
  procedures otherwise reasonably available;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} the complaint is one that could more appropriately be
  dealt with, initially or completely, according to a procedure provided
  for under an Act of Parliament other than this Act;

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} the complaint is beyond the jurisdiction of the
  Accessibility Commissioner;

- [(d)]{.lawlabel} the complaint is trivial, frivolous, vexatious or
  made in bad faith; or

- [(e)]{.lawlabel} the complaint is based on acts or omissions the
  complainant became aware of more than one year, or any longer period
  of time that the Accessibility Commissioner considers appropriate in
  the circumstances, before the filing of the complaint.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Notice

- **[[96]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-96 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  Accessibility Commissioner must cause a written notice advising of
  whether or not he or she has decided to investigate a complaint to be
  served on the complainant and the regulated entity against which the
  complaint was made.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Time and manner for application for
  review

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} If the Accessibility Commissioner's decision is that
  the complaint will not be investigated, the notice must specify the
  time within which and the manner in which an application may be made
  for a review of the decision.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Joint investigation

**[[97]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-97 .sectionLabel}** If the Accessibility
Commissioner is of the opinion that two or more complaints involve
substantially the same issues of fact, he or she may conduct a joint
investigation into the complaints.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Powers of Accessibility Commissioner

**[[98]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-98 .sectionLabel}** In the conduct of an
investigation of a complaint, the Accessibility Commissioner may

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} summon and enforce the appearance of persons before
  the Accessibility Commissioner and compel them to give oral or written
  evidence on oath and to produce any records and things that the
  Accessibility Commissioner considers necessary to investigate the
  complaint, in the same manner and to the same extent as a superior
  court of record;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} administer oaths;

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} receive and accept any evidence and other
  information, whether on oath, by affidavit or otherwise, that the
  Accessibility Commissioner sees fit, whether or not it is or would be
  admissible in a court of law;

- [(d)]{.lawlabel} enter any place --- including a conveyance --- other
  than a dwelling-house;

- [(e)]{.lawlabel} converse in private with any person in any place
  entered under paragraph (d) and otherwise carry out in that place any
  inquiries that the Accessibility Commissioner sees fit; and

- [(f)]{.lawlabel} exercise any of the powers referred to in any of
  paragraphs 73(2)(a) to (l).

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Dispute resolution mechanisms

**[[99]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-99 .sectionLabel}** The Accessibility
Commissioner may attempt to resolve complaints by means of a dispute
resolution mechanism.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Discontinuance of investigation

- **[[100]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-100 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  Accessibility Commissioner may discontinue the investigation of a
  complaint if he or she is of the opinion that

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} there is insufficient evidence to pursue the
    investigation;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} any of the circumstances mentioned in paragraphs
    95(a) to (e) applies; or

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} the matter has been resolved --- by means of a
    dispute resolution mechanism or otherwise --- by the complainant and
    the regulated entity.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Notice

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The Accessibility Commissioner must cause the
  complainant and the regulated entity to be served with written notice
  of the discontinuance of the investigation that sets out the reasons
  for the discontinuance and that specifies the time within which and
  the manner in which an application may be made for a review of the
  decision to discontinue the investigation.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Complaint dismissed

- **[[101]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-101 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} At
  the conclusion of an investigation, the Accessibility Commissioner
  must dismiss the complaint if he or she finds that the complaint is
  not substantiated.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Notice

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The Accessibility Commissioner must cause the
  complainant and the regulated entity to be served with a written
  notice of the dismissal of the complaint that sets out the reasons for
  the dismissal and that specifies the time within which and the manner
  in which an application may be made for an appeal of the decision to
  dismiss the complaint.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Complaint substantiated

- **[[102]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-102 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} If,
  at the conclusion of an investigation, the Accessibility Commissioner
  finds that the complaint is substantiated, he or she may order the
  regulated entity to do one or more of the following:

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} take the appropriate corrective measures specified
    in the order;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} make available to the complainant, on the first
    reasonable occasion, the rights, opportunities or privileges that
    were denied to the complainant as a result of the contravention to
    which the complaint relates;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} pay compensation to the complainant for any or all
    of the wages that they were deprived of and for any or all of the
    expenses incurred by the complainant as a result of the
    contravention;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} pay compensation to the complainant for any or all
    additional costs of obtaining alternative goods, services,
    facilities or accommodation, and for any or all of the expenses
    incurred by the complainant, as a result of the contravention;

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} pay compensation to the complainant in an amount
    that is not more than the amount referred to in subsection (2) for
    any pain and suffering that the complainant experienced as a result
    of the contravention;

  - [(f)]{.lawlabel} pay to the complainant an amount that is not more
    than the amount referred to in subsection (2), if the Accessibility
    Commissioner determines that the contravention is the result of a
    wilful or reckless practice.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Amount

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The amount, for the purposes of each of paragraphs
  (1)(e) and (f), is

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} for the calendar year during which subsection (1)
    comes into force, \$20,000; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} for each subsequent calendar year, the amount that
    is equal to the product obtained by multiplying

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} the amount determined under this subsection for
      the preceding calendar year

    by

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} the ratio that the Consumer Price Index for the
      preceding calendar year bears to the Consumer Price Index for the
      calendar year before that preceding calendar year.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Definition of [[Consumer Price
  Index]{.dfn}]{.DefinitionRef}

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} In subsection (2), [[Consumer Price
  Index]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}, for a calendar year, means the average of
  the Consumer Price Index for Canada, as published by Statistics Canada
  under the authority of the [Statistics Act](/eng/acts/S-19), for each
  month in the calendar year.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Amount to be published

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} The Accessibility Commissioner must, as soon as it is
  determined, publish the amount that is the amount for the purposes of
  paragraphs (1)(e) and (f) for each calendar year after the year during
  which subsection (1) comes into force.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Interest

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} An order to pay compensation under paragraph (1)(c)
  or (d) may include an award of interest at a rate and for a period
  that the Accessibility Commissioner considers appropriate.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Copy

  [(6)]{.lawlabel} The Accessibility Commissioner must cause the
  complainant and the regulated entity to be served with a copy of the
  order made under subsection (1) and a notice that specifies the time
  within which and the manner in which an application may be made for an
  appeal of the order.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Review by Accessibility Commissioner

- **[[103]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-103 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  Accessibility Commissioner may, on application made within the time
  and in the manner specified in the notice served under section 96 or
  100, as the case may be, review a decision under section 95 not to
  investigate a complaint or a decision under section 100 to discontinue
  an investigation.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Representations

  [(1.1)]{.lawlabel} The complainant must be given the opportunity to
  make representations to the officer or employee conducting the review
  in a manner that is accessible to the complainant.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Powers

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} After concluding the review, the Accessibility
  Commissioner must

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} confirm the decision not to investigate the
    complaint;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} investigate the complaint;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} confirm the decision to discontinue the
    investigation; or

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} continue the investigation.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Notice

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} The Accessibility Commissioner must cause the
  complainant and the regulated entity to be served with a written
  notice that sets out the Accessibility Commissioner's decision under
  subsection (2) and the reasons for it.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Decision final

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} Every decision made by the Accessibility Commissioner
  under any of paragraphs (2)(a) to (d) is final and is not to be
  questioned or reviewed in any court.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Appeal

- **[[104]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-104 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A
  complainant or regulated entity that is affected by a decision made
  under section 101 or an order made under subsection 102(1) may appeal
  the decision or order to the Canadian Human Rights Tribunal, in
  writing, within 30 days after the day on which a copy of the order or
  notice of the decision is served or any longer period --- that is not
  more than 60 days after that day --- that the Canadian Human Rights
  Tribunal considers appropriate in the circumstances.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Nature of appeal

  [(1.1)]{.lawlabel} The appeal lies on any ground of appeal that
  involves a question of law or fact alone, or a question of mixed law
  and fact, including a principle of natural justice.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Grounds of appeal

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The request for appeal must contain a statement of
  the grounds of appeal and set out the evidence that supports those
  grounds.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Assignment of member

- **[[105]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-105 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} On
  receipt of an application for an appeal, the Chairperson of the
  Canadian Human Rights Tribunal must assign a member of the Tribunal to
  hear the appeal, but the Chairperson may assign a panel of three
  members of the Tribunal to hear the appeal if he or she considers that
  the complexity of the matters under appeal requires three members.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Chair of panel

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} If a panel of three members is assigned, the
  Chairperson must designate one of them to be its chair, but the
  Chairperson is to be the chair if he or she is a member of the panel.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Decision

- **[[106]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-106 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  member or panel of members of the Canadian Human Rights Tribunal
  assigned to hear the appeal may, by order, confirm, vary, give the
  decision that the Accessibility Commissioner should have given or
  rescind the decision or order to which the appeal relates or refer the
  complaint back to the Accessibility Commissioner for reconsideration
  in accordance with any direction the Canadian Human Rights Tribunal
  may give.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Nature of appeal

  [(1.1)]{.lawlabel} An appeal shall be on the merits based on the
  record of the proceedings before the Accessibility Commissioner, but
  the member or panel of members of the Canadian Human Rights Tribunal
  shall allow arguments and, if he, she or it considers it necessary for
  the purposes of the appeal, shall hear evidence not previously
  available.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Panel's decision

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} A decision made by a majority of the members of the
  panel is the decision of the panel or, if no decision is supported by
  the majority, the decision of the panel's chair is the decision of the
  panel.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Copy

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} A copy of the order made by the member or panel of
  members of the Canadian Human Rights Tribunal must be provided to the
  Accessibility Commissioner and the parties to the appeal.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Decision final

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} Every decision made under subsection (1) is final and
  is not to be questioned or reviewed in any court.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Report of activities

**[[107]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-107 .sectionLabel}** The Canadian Human
Rights Tribunal must include in its annual report referred to in
subsection 61(3) of the [Canadian Human Rights Act](/eng/acts/H-6) a
report of its activities under this Act during the year.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Regulations

**[[108]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-108 .sectionLabel}** The Governor in Council
may make regulations

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} prescribing the procedures to be followed by the
  Accessibility Commissioner when conducting an investigation; and

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} governing the manner in which complaints are to be
  investigated by the Accessibility Commissioner.

### [General]{.HTitleText2} {#h-1154376 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Duty to act informally and expeditiously

**[[109]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-109 .sectionLabel}** The Accessibility
Commissioner must deal with complaints filed under subsection 94(1) and
applications made under subsection 103(1) as informally and
expeditiously as the circumstances and considerations of fairness and
natural justice permit.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Disclosure of personal information

**[[110]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-110 .sectionLabel}** For the purpose of the
administration of Part III of the [Canadian Human Rights
Act](/eng/acts/H-6), the Accessibility Commissioner may disclose to any
officer or employee of the Canadian Human Rights Commission any personal
information that is contained in a complaint filed with the
Accessibility Commissioner.

## [PART 7]{.HLabel1}[Chief Accessibility Officer]{.HTitleText1} {#h-1154381 .Part}

### [Duties and Functions]{.HTitleText2} {#h-1154398 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Advice

**[[113]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-113 .sectionLabel}** The Chief Accessibility
Officer may --- or, if requested to do so by the Minister, must ---
provide information or advice to the Minister in respect of systemic or
emerging accessibility issues.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Special report

- **[[114]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-114 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  Chief Accessibility Officer may --- or, if requested to do so by the

  paragraph (4)(a).

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Entering into compliance agreement

  [(7)]{.lawlabel} After considering a request to enter into a
  compliance agreement, the Accessibility Commissioner may enter into a
  compliance agreement with the parliamentary entity or specified person
  on any terms that the Accessibility Commissioner considers
  appropriate, other than any terms that would provide for a deposit of
  security or for a penalty.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Notice

  [(8)]{.lawlabel} If the Accessibility Commissioner is satisfied that a
  parliamentary entity or specified person that has entered into a
  compliance agreement has complied with it, he or she must cause the
  parliamentary entity or specified person to be served with a notice of
  compliance. If he or she is satisfied that the parliamentary entity or
  specified person has not complied with the agreement, he or she must
  cause them to be served with a notice of default.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Limitation period or prescription

  [(9)]{.lawlabel} No notice of contravention is to be issued after the
  expiry of two years after the day on which the subject matter of the
  contravention arose.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Application --- sections 85 and 87

  [(10)]{.lawlabel} Sections 85 and 87 apply with respect to
  contraventions referred to in this section, with any reference to a
  regulated entity to be read as a reference to a parliamentary entity,
  any reference to a person to be read as a reference to a specified
  person and any reference to a violation to be read as a reference to a
  contravention.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Definition of [[specified
  person]{.dfn}]{.DefinitionRef}

  [(11)]{.lawlabel} In this section, [[specified
  person]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means a person performing duties and
  functions in the course of the activities or business of a
  parliamentary entity.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Application of Part 6

- **[[141]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-141
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Subject to subsections (2) and (3),
  sections 94 to 104, subsection 106(1) and sections 108 to 110 apply
  with respect to a parliamentary entity as if it were a regulated
  entity.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Exception

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} An individual is not entitled to file a complaint
  under subsection 94(1) in respect of a contravention by a
  parliamentary entity of any provision of regulations made under
  subsection 117(1) if the individual is entitled to present a grievance
  under section 62 of the [Parliamentary Employment and Staff Relations
  Act](/eng/acts/P-1.3) in respect of the contravention.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Appeal

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} An appeal with respect to a parliamentary entity
  under subsection 104(1) is to be made to the Federal Public Sector
  Labour Relations and Employment Board instead of to the Canadian Human
  Rights Tribunal and, with respect to such an appeal, a reference to
  the member or panel of members of the Tribunal in subsection 106(1) is
  to be read as a reference to a panel of the Board.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}For greater certainty

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} For greater certainty, section 35 of the [Federal
  Public Sector Labour Relations and Employment Board
  Act](/eng/acts/P-33.35) does not apply with respect to an order of the
  Federal Public Sector Labour Relations and Employment Board made with
  respect to an appeal under subsection 104(1).

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Application of Part 8 --- regulations

- **[[142]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-142
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Regulations made under any of
  paragraphs 117(1)(a) to (l) --- and under paragraph 117(1)(m) with
  respect to sections 69 to 71 --- apply with respect to a parliamentary
  entity as if it were a regulated entity, but only to the extent that
  the regulations apply generally to regulated entities that are
  departments named in Schedule I to the [Financial Administration
  Act](/eng/acts/F-11).

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Exemption

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} On application by a parliamentary entity and after
  consulting with the Accessibility Commissioner, the Speaker of the
  Senate or the Speaker of the House of Commons --- or, in the case of
  the Library of Parliament, the Parliamentary Protective Service and
  the office of the Parliamentary Budget Officer, both Speakers acting
  jointly --- may, in writing and on any terms that they consider
  necessary, exempt the parliamentary entity from the application of any
  provision of the regulations referred to in subsection (1) if the
  Speaker or Speakers are satisfied that the parliamentary entity will
  take or has taken measures that will result in an equivalent or
  greater level of accessibility for persons with disabilities.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Amendment or revocation

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} The Speaker or Speakers who gave an exemption under
  subsection (2) must consult with the Accessibility Commissioner before
  amending it but need not do so before revoking it.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}[Statutory Instruments
  Act](/eng/acts/S-22)

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} For greater certainty, an exemption, amendment or
  revocation referred to in this section is not a statutory instrument
  for the purposes of the [Statutory Instruments Act](/eng/acts/S-22).

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Sections 122 to 125, 127 and 131

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} Sections 122 to 125, 127 and 131 apply with respect
  to a parliamentary entity.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Sections 126 and 132

  [(6)]{.lawlabel} Sections 126 and 132 apply with respect to a
  parliamentary entity as if it were a regulated entity.

### [Notification of Speakers]{.HTitleText2} {#h-1154626 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Notification --- entrance into place

- **[[143]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-143 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  Accessibility Commissioner must notify the Speaker of the Senate or
  the Speaker of the House of Commons, or both, of his or her intention
  to enter, under section 73 or paragraph 98(d), a place that is under
  the authority of a parliamentary entity.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Other notifications

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The Accessibility Commissioner must notify the
  Speaker of the Senate or the Speaker of the House of Commons, or both,
  as soon as possible after he or she

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} makes an order with respect to a parliamentary
    entity under section 74;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} makes a compliance order with respect to a
    parliamentary entity under section 75;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} makes a decision under subsection 76(4) in respect
    of a compliance order referred to in paragraph (b);

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} begins to conduct an investigation of a
    parliamentary entity under section 95;

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} makes an order under subsection 102(1) with respect
    to a parliamentary entity;

  - [(f)]{.lawlabel} issues a notice of contravention under subsection
    140(1) or (2);

  - [(g)]{.lawlabel} makes a decision under subsection 140(5); or

  - [(h)]{.lawlabel} causes a notice of compliance or default to be
    served under subsection 140(8).

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Notice or order --- complaint

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} Whenever the Accessibility Commissioner causes a
  parliamentary entity to be served with a notice under subsection
  94(5), 96(1), 100(2), 101(2) or 103(3), the Accessibility Commissioner
  must provide a copy to the Speaker of the Senate or the Speaker of the
  House of Commons, or both.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Notification --- appeal under subsection
104(1)

- **[[144]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-144 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  Federal Public Sector Labour Relations and Employment Board must
  notify the Speaker of the Senate or the Speaker of the House of
  Commons, or both, as soon as possible after the Board receives a
  request for appeal of a decision or order with respect to a
  parliamentary entity under subsection 104(1).

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Power of Speakers

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} If the Speaker of the Senate or the Speaker of the
  House of Commons is notified that an appeal has been brought,

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the Board must, at the Speaker's request, provide
    to the Speaker a copy of any document that is filed with the Board
    in the appeal and that is necessary to enable the Speaker to present
    evidence and make representations under paragraph (b); and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the Speaker may present evidence and make
    representations to the Board in the appeal.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Non-compliance with compliance order

- **[[145]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-145 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  Accessibility Commissioner must provide to the Speaker of the Senate
  or the Speaker of the House of Commons, or both, a compliance order
  that is made under section 75 or amended under subsection 76(4) with
  respect to a parliamentary entity, if the order is not complied with.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Order under subsection 102(1)

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The Accessibility Commissioner must provide an order
  made under subsection 102(1) with respect to a parliamentary entity to
  the Speaker of the Senate or the Speaker of the House of Commons, or
  both, if the order is not complied with.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Order under subsection 106(1)

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} The Federal Public Sector Labour Relations and
  Employment Board must, at the request of the Accessibility
  Commissioner or the complainant, provide an order made under
  subsection 106(1) with respect to a parliamentary entity to the
  Speaker of the Senate or the Speaker of the House of Commons, or both,
  if the order is not complied with.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Tabling by Speaker

**[[146]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-146 .sectionLabel}** The Speaker of the
Senate or the Speaker of the House of Commons, or both, must, within a
reasonable time after receiving it, table every notice of default
received under paragraph 143(2)(h) and every order received under
section 145 in the House over which the Speaker presides.
