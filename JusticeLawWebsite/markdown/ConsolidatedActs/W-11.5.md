## Workers Mourning Day Act {#workers-mourning-day-act .Title-of-Act}

[S.C.]{.abbr title="Statutes of Canada"} 1991, c. 15
:::

Assented to 1991-02-01

An Act respecting a Day of Mourning for Persons Killed or Injured in the
Workplace
::::

::::: section
::: {.section .order}
WHEREAS it is desirable that Canadians should designate a day of
mourning to remember workers killed, disabled or injured in the
workplace and workers afflicted with industrial disease;

AND WHEREAS Canadians seek earnestly to set an example of their
commitment to the issue of health and safety in the workplace;
:::

::: Enacts
NOW, THEREFORE, Her Majesty, by and with the advice and consent of the
Senate and House of Commons of Canada, enacts as follows:
:::

## [Short Title]{.HTitleText1} {#h-465174 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Short title

**[[1]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-1 .sectionLabel}** This Act may be cited as
the [Workers Mourning Day Act](/eng/acts/W-11.5).

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Day of Mourning

- **[[2]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-2
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Throughout Canada, in each and every
  year, the 28th day of April shall be known under the name of \"Day of
  Mourning for Persons Killed or Injured in the Workplace\".

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Not a legal holiday

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} For greater certainty, the Day of Mourning for
  Persons Killed or Injured in the Workplace is not a legal holiday or a
  non-juridical day and shall not be required to be kept or observed as
  such.
