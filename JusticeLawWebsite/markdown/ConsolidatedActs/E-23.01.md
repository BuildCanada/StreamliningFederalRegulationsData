## [Short Title]{.HTitleText1} {#h-212737 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Short title

**[[1]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-1 .sectionLabel}** This Act may be cited as
the [Extradition Act](/eng/acts/E-23.01).

## [PART 1]{.HLabel1}[Interpretation]{.HTitleText1} {#h-212740 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Definitions

**[[2]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-2 .sectionLabel}** The definitions in this
section apply in this Act.

[[Attorney General]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[Attorney General]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means the Attorney General
    of Canada. ([procureur général]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

[[court]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[court]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means

    - [(a)]{.lawlabel} in Ontario, the Ontario Court (General Division);

    - [(b)]{.lawlabel} in Quebec, the Superior Court;

    - [(c)]{.lawlabel} in New Brunswick, Manitoba, Alberta and
      Saskatchewan, the Court of Queen's Bench;

    - [(d)]{.lawlabel} in Nova Scotia, British Columbia, Yukon and the
      Northwest Territories, the Supreme Court, and in Nunavut, the
      Nunavut Court of Justice; and

    - [(e)]{.lawlabel} in Prince Edward Island and Newfoundland, the
      Trial Division of the Supreme Court. ([tribunal]{.DefinedTermLink
      lang="fr"})

[[court of appeal]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[court of appeal]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means

    - [(a)]{.lawlabel} in the Province of Prince Edward Island, the
      Appeal Division of the Supreme Court; and

    - [(b)]{.lawlabel} in all other provinces, the Court of
      Appeal. ([cour d'appel]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

[[extradition agreement]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[extradition agreement]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means an agreement
    that is in force, to which Canada is a party and that contains a
    provision respecting the extradition of persons, other than a
    specific agreement. ([accord]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

[[extradition partner]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[extradition partner]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means a State or entity
    with which Canada is party to an extradition agreement, with which
    Canada has entered into a specific agreement or whose name appears
    in the schedule. ([partenaire]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

[[International Criminal Court]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[International Criminal Court]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means the
    International Criminal Court as defined in subsection 2(1) of the
    [Crimes Against Humanity and War Crimes
    Act](/eng/acts/C-45.9). ([Cour pénale
    internationale]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

[[judge]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[judge]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means a judge of the
    court. ([juge]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

[[justice]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[justice]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} has the same meaning as in section 2
    of the [Criminal Code](/eng/acts/C-46). ([juge de
    paix]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

[[Minister]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[Minister]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means the Minister of
    Justice. ([ministre]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

[[specific agreement]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[specific agreement]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means an agreement
    referred to in section 10 that is in force. ([accord
    spécifique]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

[[State or entity]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[State or entity]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means

    - [(a)]{.lawlabel} a State other than Canada;

    - [(b)]{.lawlabel} a province, state or other political subdivision
      of a State other than Canada;

    - [(c)]{.lawlabel} a colony, dependency, possession, protectorate,
      condominium, trust territory or any territory falling under the
      jurisdiction of a State other than Canada;

    - [(d)]{.lawlabel} an international criminal court or tribunal; or

    - [(e)]{.lawlabel} a territory. ([État ou entité]{.DefinedTermLink
      lang="fr"})

## [PART 2]{.HLabel1}[Extradition from Canada]{.HTitleText1} {#h-212768 .Part}

### [Extraditable Conduct]{.HTitleText2} {#h-212769 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}General principle

- **[[3]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-3 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A person
  may be extradited from Canada in accordance with this Act and a
  relevant extradition agreement on the request of an extradition
  partner for the purpose of prosecuting the person or imposing a
  sentence on --- or enforcing a sentence imposed on --- the person if

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} subject to a relevant extradition agreement, the
    offence in respect of which the extradition is requested is
    punishable by the extradition partner, by imprisoning or otherwise
    depriving the person of their liberty for a maximum term of two
    years or more, or by a more severe punishment; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the conduct of the person, had it occurred in
    Canada, would have constituted an offence that is punishable in
    Canada,

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} in the case of a request based on a specific
      agreement, by imprisonment for a maximum term of five years or
      more, or by a more severe punishment, and

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} in any other case, by imprisonment for a maximum
      term of two years or more, or by a more severe punishment, subject
      to a relevant extradition agreement.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Conduct determinative

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} For greater certainty, it is not relevant whether the
  conduct referred to in subsection (1) is named, defined or
  characterized by the extradition partner in the same way as it is in
  Canada.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Extradition of a person who has been
  sentenced

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} Subject to a relevant extradition agreement, the
  extradition of a person who has been sentenced to imprisonment or
  another deprivation of liberty may only be granted if the portion of
  the term remaining is at least six months long or a more severe
  punishment remains to be carried out.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Further proceedings

**[[4]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-4 .sectionLabel}** For greater certainty, the
discharge of a person under this Act or an Act repealed by section 129
or 130 does not preclude further proceedings, whether or not they are
based on the same conduct, with a view to extraditing the person under
this Act unless the judge is of the opinion that those further
proceedings would be an abuse of process.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Jurisdiction

**[[5]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-5 .sectionLabel}** A person may be extradited

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} whether or not the conduct on which the extradition
  partner bases its request occurred in the territory over which it has
  jurisdiction; and

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} whether or not Canada could exercise jurisdiction in
  similar circumstances.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Retrospectivity

**[[6]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-6 .sectionLabel}** Subject to a relevant
extradition agreement, extradition may be granted under this Act whether
the conduct or conviction in respect of which the extradition is
requested occurred before or after this Act or the relevant extradition
agreement or specific agreement came into force.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}No immunity

**[[6.1]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-6.1 .sectionLabel}** Despite any other Act
or law, no person who is the subject of a request for surrender by the
International Criminal Court or by any international criminal tribunal
that is established by resolution of the Security Council of the United
Nations and whose name appears in the schedule, may claim immunity under
common law or by statute from arrest or extradition under this Act.

- 2000, c. 24, s. 48
:::

### [Functions of the Minister]{.HTitleText2} {#h-212793 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Functions of the Minister

**[[7]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-7 .sectionLabel}** The Minister is responsible
for the implementation of extradition agreements, the administration of
this Act and dealing with requests for extradition made under them.

### [Publication of Extradition Agreements]{.HTitleText2} {#h-212796 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Publication in [Canada
Gazette](http://www.gazette.gc.ca/)

- **[[8]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-8 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Unless
  the extradition agreement has been published under subsection (2), an
  extradition agreement --- or the provisions respecting extradition
  contained in a multilateral extradition agreement --- must be
  published in the [Canada Gazette](http://www.gazette.gc.ca/) no later
  than 60 days after it comes into force.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Publication in *Canada Treaty Series*

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} An extradition agreement --- or the provisions
  respecting extradition contained in a multilateral extradition
  agreement --- may be published in the *Canada Treaty Series* and, if
  so published, the publication must be no later than 60 days after it
  comes into force.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Judicial notice

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} Agreements and provisions published in the [Canada
  Gazette](http://www.gazette.gc.ca/) or the *Canada Treaty Series* are
  to be judicially noticed.

### [Designated States and Entities]{.HTitleText2} {#h-212804 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Designated extradition partners

- **[[9]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-9 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  names of members of the Commonwealth or other States or entities that
  appear in the schedule are designated as extradition partners.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Amendments to the schedule

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The Minister of Foreign Affairs, with the agreement
  of the Minister, may, by order, add to or delete from the schedule the
  names of members of the Commonwealth or other States or entities.

### [Specific Agreements]{.HTitleText2} {#h-212810 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Specific agreements

- **[[10]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-10 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  Minister of Foreign Affairs may, with the agreement of the Minister,
  enter into a specific agreement with a State or entity for the purpose
  of giving effect to a request for extradition in a particular case.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Inconsistency

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} For greater certainty, if there is an inconsistency
  between this Act and a specific agreement, this Act prevails to the
  extent of the inconsistency.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Evidence

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} A certificate issued by or under the authority of the
  Minister of Foreign Affairs to which is attached a copy of a specific
  agreement entered into by Canada and a State or entity is conclusive
  evidence of the agreement and its contents without proof of the
  signature or official character of the person appearing to have signed
  the certificate or agreement.

### [Minister's Power to Receive Requests]{.HTitleText2} {#h-212818 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Request to go to Minister

- **[[11]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-11 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A
  request by an extradition partner for the provisional arrest or
  extradition of a person shall be made to the Minister.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Provisional arrest request to go to
  Minister

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} A request by an extradition partner for the
  provisional arrest of a person may also be made to the Minister
  through Interpol.

### [Warrant for Provisional Arrest]{.HTitleText2} {#h-212824 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Minister's approval of request for
provisional arrest

**[[12]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-12 .sectionLabel}** The Minister may, after
receiving a request by an extradition partner for the provisional arrest
of a person, authorize the Attorney General to apply for a provisional
arrest warrant, if the Minister is satisfied that

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} the offence in respect of which the provisional
  arrest is requested is punishable in accordance with paragraph
  3(1)(a); and

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} the extradition partner will make a request for the
  extradition of the person.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Provisional arrest warrant

- **[[13]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-13 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A
  judge may, on [ex parte]{.otherLang lang="la"} application of the
  Attorney General, issue a warrant for the provisional arrest of a
  person, if satisfied that there are reasonable grounds to believe that

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} it is necessary in the public interest to arrest
    the person, including to prevent the person from escaping or
    committing an offence;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the person is ordinarily resident in Canada, is in
    Canada or is on the way to Canada; and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} a warrant for the person's arrest or an order of a
    similar nature has been issued or the person has been convicted.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Contents of the warrant

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} A provisional arrest warrant must

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} name or describe the person to be arrested;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} set out briefly the offence in respect of which the
    provisional arrest was requested; and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} order that the person be arrested without delay and
    brought before the judge who issued the warrant or before another
    judge in Canada.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Execution throughout Canada

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} A provisional arrest warrant may be executed anywhere
  in Canada without being endorsed.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Discharge if no proceedings

- **[[14]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-14 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A
  person who has been provisionally arrested, whether detained or
  released on judicial interim release, must be discharged

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} when the Minister notifies the court that an
    authority to proceed will not be issued under section 15;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} if the provisional arrest was made pursuant to a
    request made under an extradition agreement that contains a period
    within which a request for extradition must be made and the
    supporting documents provided,

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} when the period has expired and the extradition
      partner has not made the request or provided the documents, or

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} when the request for extradition has been made
      and the documents provided within the period but the Minister has
      not issued an authority to proceed before the expiry of 30 days
      after the expiry of that period; or

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} if the provisional arrest was not made pursuant to
    a request made under an extradition agreement or was made pursuant
    to an extradition agreement that does not contain a period within
    which a request for extradition must be made and the supporting
    documents provided,

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} when 60 days have expired after the provisional
      arrest and the extradition partner has not made the request or
      provided the documents, or

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} when the request for extradition has been made
      and the documents provided within 60 days but the Minister has not
      issued an authority to proceed before the expiry of 30 additional
      days.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Extension

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} On application of the Attorney General, a judge

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} may extend a period referred to in subsection (1);
    or

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} shall, in the case of a person arrested on the
    request of the International Criminal Court, extend a period
    referred to in subsection (1) for the period specified by the
    Attorney General, not to exceed 30 days.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Release of person

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} In extending a period under subsection (2), the judge
  may also grant the person judicial interim release or vary the
  conditions of their judicial interim release.

- 1999, c. 18, s. 14
- 2000, c. 24, s. 49

### [Authority to Proceed]{.HTitleText2} {#h-212860 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Minister's power to issue

- **[[15]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-15 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  Minister may, after receiving a request for extradition and being
  satisfied that the conditions set out in paragraph 3(1)(a) and
  subsection 3(3) are met in respect of one or more offences mentioned
  in the request, issue an authority to proceed that authorizes the
  Attorney General to seek, on behalf of the extradition partner, an
  order of a court for the committal of the person under section 29.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Competing requests

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} If requests from two or more extradition partners are
  received by the Minister for the extradition of a person, the Minister
  shall determine the order in which the requests will be authorized to
  proceed.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Contents of authority to proceed

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} The authority to proceed must contain

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the name or description of the person whose
    extradition is sought;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the name of the extradition partner; and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} the name of the offence or offences under Canadian
    law that correspond to the alleged conduct of the person or the
    conduct in respect of which the person was convicted, as long as one
    of the offences would be punishable in accordance with paragraph
    3(1)(b).

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Copy of authority to proceed

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} A copy of an authority to proceed produced by a means
  of telecommunication that produces a writing has the same probative
  force as the original for the purposes of this Part.

### [Arrest or Summons Following Authority to Proceed]{.HTitleText2} {#h-212873 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Warrant of arrest or summons

- **[[16]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-16 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  Attorney General may, after the Minister issues an authority to
  proceed, apply [ex parte]{.otherLang lang="la"} to a judge in the
  province in which the Attorney General believes the person is or to
  which the person is on their way, or was last known to be, for the
  issuance of a summons to the person or a warrant for the arrest of the
  person.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}When provisionally arrested

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} If the person has been arrested pursuant to a
  provisional arrest warrant issued under section 13, the Attorney
  General need not apply for a summons or warrant under subsection (1).

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Issuance of summons or warrant of
  arrest

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} The judge to whom an application is made shall issue
  a summons to the person, or a warrant for the arrest of the person, in
  accordance with subsection 507(4) of the [Criminal
  Code](/eng/acts/C-46), with any modifications that the circumstances
  require.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Execution throughout Canada

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} A warrant that is issued under this section may be
  executed, and a summons issued under this section may be served,
  anywhere in Canada without being endorsed.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Date of hearing --- summons

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} A summons that is issued under this section must

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} set a date for the appearance of the person before
    a judge that is not later than 15 days after its issuance; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} require the person to appear at a time and place
    stated in it for the purposes of the [Identification of Criminals
    Act](/eng/acts/I-1).

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Effect of appearance

  [(6)]{.lawlabel} A person appearing as required by subsection (5) is
  considered, for the purposes only of the [Identification of Criminals
  Act](/eng/acts/I-1), to be in lawful custody charged with an
  indictable offence.

### [Appearance]{.HTitleText2} {#h-212889 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Appearance

- **[[17]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-17 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A
  person who is arrested under section 13 or 16 is to be brought before
  a judge or a justice within twenty-four hours after the person is
  arrested, but if no judge or no justice is available during this time,
  the person shall be brought before a judge or a justice as soon as
  possible.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Appearance before justice

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The justice before whom a person is brought under
  subsection (1) shall order that the person be detained in custody and
  brought before a judge.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Decision of judge

- **[[18]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-18 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  judge before whom a person is brought following arrest under section
  13 or 16 shall

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} if the person has been arrested on the request of
    the International Criminal Court, order the detention in custody of
    the person unless

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} the person shows cause, in accordance with
      subsection 522(2) of the [Criminal Code](/eng/acts/C-46), that
      their detention in custody is not justified, and

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} the judge is satisfied that, given the gravity
      of the alleged offence, there are urgent and exceptional
      circumstances that justify release --- with or without conditions
      --- and that the person will appear as required; or

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} in any other case, order the release, with or
    without conditions, or detention in custody of the person.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Mandatory adjournment

  [(1.1)]{.lawlabel} An application for judicial interim release in
  respect of a person referred to in paragraph (1)(a) shall, at the
  request of the Attorney General, be adjourned to await receipt of the
  recommendations of the Pre-Trial Chamber of the International Criminal
  Court. If the recommendations are not received within six days, the
  judge may proceed to hear the application.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Recommendations of Pre-Trial Chamber

  [(1.2)]{.lawlabel} If the Pre-Trial Chamber of the International
  Criminal Court submits recommendations, the judge shall consider them
  before rendering a decision.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Review by court of appeal

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} A decision respecting judicial interim release may be
  reviewed by a judge of the court of appeal and that judge may

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} confirm the decision;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} vary the decision; or

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} substitute any other decision that, in the judge's
    opinion, should have been made.

::: HistoricalNote
- 1999, c. 18, s. 18
- 2000, c. 24, s. 50

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}[Criminal Code](/eng/acts/C-46)

**[[19]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-19 .sectionLabel}** Part XVI of the [Criminal
Code](/eng/acts/C-46) applies, with any modifications that the
circumstances require, in respect of a person arrested under section 13
or 16 or to whom a summons has been issued under section 16.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Section 679 of the [Criminal
Code](/eng/acts/C-46)

**[[20]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-20 .sectionLabel}** Section 679 of the
[Criminal Code](/eng/acts/C-46) applies, with any modifications that the
circumstances require, to the judicial interim release of a person
pending

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} a determination of an appeal from an order of
  committal made under section 29;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} the Minister's decision under section 40 respecting
  the surrender of the person; or

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} a determination of a judicial review of the
  Minister's decision under section 40 to order the surrender of the
  person.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Date of hearing --- provisional arrest

- **[[21]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-21 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} If a
  person has been provisionally arrested, the judge before whom the
  person is brought shall

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} order the person to appear before the court from
    time to time during the period referred to in paragraph 14(1)(b) or
    (c); and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} set a date for the extradition hearing if the
    Minister has issued an authority to proceed.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Date of hearing after authority to
  proceed issued

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} If a person has been arrested or is a person to whom
  a summons has been issued under section 16, the judge before whom the
  person is brought shall set a date for the extradition hearing.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Hearing

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} The judge shall set an early date for the extradition
  hearing, whether that date is in or out of the prescribed sessions of
  the court.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Application for transfer

- **[[22]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-22 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} On
  application of the Attorney General or the person arrested or to whom
  a summons has been issued under section 16, the judge shall, if
  satisfied that the interests of justice so require, order that the
  proceedings be transferred to another place in Canada and that the
  person appear before a judge in that place, and

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} if the person is detained, that the person be
    conveyed by a peace officer to the place; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} if the person is not detained or has been released
    on judicial interim release, that the person be summoned to appear
    at the place.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Execution throughout Canada

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} A summons issued under paragraph (1)(b) may be served
  anywhere in Canada without being endorsed.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Order respecting expenses

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} If the order under subsection (1) was made on the
  application of the Attorney General, the judge may order that the
  Attorney General pay the person's reasonable travel expenses incurred
  further to the order.

### [Substitution and Amendment of Authority to Proceed]{.HTitleText2} {#h-212938 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Substitution of authority to proceed

- **[[23]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-23 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  Minister may substitute another authority to proceed at any time
  before the extradition hearing begins. All documents issued and orders
  made by the court apply in respect of the new authority to proceed,
  unless the court, on application of the person or the Attorney
  General, orders otherwise.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}New date for hearing

  [(1.1)]{.lawlabel} Where the Minister substitutes another authority to
  proceed under subsection (1) and the person applies for another date
  to be set for the beginning of the extradition hearing in order to
  give the person an opportunity to examine the new authority, the judge
  may set another date for the hearing.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Amendment of authority to proceed

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The judge may, on application of the Attorney
  General, amend the authority to proceed after the hearing has begun in
  accordance with the evidence that is produced during the hearing.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Withdrawal of the authority to proceed

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} The Minister may at any time withdraw the authority
  to proceed and, if the Minister does so, the court shall discharge the
  person and set aside any order made respecting their judicial interim
  release or detention.

### [Extradition Hearing]{.HTitleText2} {#h-212948 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Extradition hearing

- **[[24]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-24 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  judge shall, on receipt of an authority to proceed from the Attorney
  General, hold an extradition hearing.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Application of Part XVIII of the
  [Criminal Code](/eng/acts/C-46)

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} For the purposes of the hearing, the judge has,
  subject to this Act, the powers of a justice under Part XVIII of the
  [Criminal Code](/eng/acts/C-46), with any modifications that the
  circumstances require.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Competence

**[[25]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-25 .sectionLabel}** For the purposes of the
[Constitution Act, 1982](/eng/Const/), a judge has, with respect to the
functions that the judge is required to perform in applying this Act,
the same competence that that judge possesses by virtue of being a
superior court judge.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Order restricting publication of evidence

**[[26]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-26 .sectionLabel}** Before beginning a
hearing in respect of a judicial interim release or an extradition
hearing, a judge may, on application by the person or the Attorney
General and on being satisfied that the publication or broadcasting of
the evidence would constitute a risk to the holding of a fair trial by
the extradition partner, make an order directing that the evidence taken
not be published or broadcast before the time that the person is
discharged or, if surrendered, the trial by the extradition partner has
concluded.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Exclusion of person from hearing

**[[27]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-27 .sectionLabel}** The presiding judge may
make an order excluding any person from the court for all or part of an
extradition hearing or hearing in respect of a judicial interim release
if the judge is of the opinion that it is in the interest of public
morals, the maintenance of order or the proper administration of justice
to exclude the person.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Power to compel witnesses

**[[28]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-28 .sectionLabel}** A judge who presides over
an extradition hearing or a hearing in respect of a judicial interim
release may compel a witness to attend the hearing and sections 698 to
708 of the [Criminal Code](/eng/acts/C-46) apply, with any modifications
that the circumstances require.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Order of committal

- **[[29]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-29 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A
  judge shall order the committal of the person into custody to await
  surrender if

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} in the case of a person sought for prosecution,
    there is evidence admissible under this Act of conduct that, had it
    occurred in Canada, would justify committal for trial in Canada on
    the offence set out in the authority to proceed and the judge is
    satisfied that the person is the person sought by the extradition
    partner; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} in the case of a person sought for the imposition
    or enforcement of a sentence, the judge is satisfied that the
    conviction was in respect of conduct that corresponds to the offence
    set out in the authority to proceed and that the person is the
    person who was convicted.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Order of committal

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The order of committal must contain

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the name of the person;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the offence set out in the authority to proceed for
    which the committal is ordered;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} the place at which the person is to be held in
    custody; and

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} the name of the extradition partner.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Discharge of person

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} A judge shall order the person discharged if the
  judge does not order their committal under subsection (1).

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Relevant date

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} The date of the authority to proceed is the relevant
  date for the purposes of subsection (1).

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Extradition when person not present at
  conviction

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} Subject to a relevant extradition agreement, if a
  person has been tried and convicted without the person being present,
  the judge shall apply paragraph (1)(a).

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Authority to keep person in custody

- **[[30]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-30 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  order of committal constitutes the authority to keep the person in
  custody, subject to an order of judicial interim release.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Duration of order

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The order of committal remains in force until the
  person is surrendered or discharged or until a new hearing is ordered
  under paragraph 54(a).

### [Rules of Evidence]{.HTitleText2} {#h-212984 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Definition of
[[document]{.dfn}]{.DefinitionRef}

**[[31]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-31 .sectionLabel}** For the purposes of
sections 32 to 38, [[document]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means data recorded
in any form, and includes photographs and copies of documents.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Evidence

- **[[32]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-32
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Subject to subsection (2), evidence
  that would otherwise be admissible under Canadian law shall be
  admitted as evidence at an extradition hearing. The following shall
  also be admitted as evidence, even if it would not otherwise be
  admissible under Canadian law:

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the contents of the documents contained in the
    record of the case certified under subsection 33(3);

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the contents of the documents that are submitted in
    conformity with the terms of an extradition agreement; and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} evidence adduced by the person sought for
    extradition that is relevant to the tests set out in subsection
    29(1) if the judge considers it reliable.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Exception --- Canadian evidence

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Evidence gathered in Canada must satisfy the rules of
  evidence under Canadian law in order to be admitted.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Record of the case

- **[[33]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-33 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  record of the case must include

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} in the case of a person sought for the purpose of
    prosecution, a document summarizing the evidence available to the
    extradition partner for use in the prosecution; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} in the case of a person sought for the imposition
    or enforcement of a sentence,

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} a copy of the document that records the
      conviction of the person, and

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} a document describing the conduct for which the
      person was convicted.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Other documents --- record of the case

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} A record of the case may include other relevant
  documents, including documents respecting the identification of the
  person sought for extradition.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Certification of record of the case

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} A record of the case may not be admitted unless

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} in the case of a person sought for the purpose of
    prosecution, a judicial or prosecuting authority of the extradition
    partner certifies that the evidence summarized or contained in the
    record of the case is available for trial and

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} is sufficient under the law of the extradition
      partner to justify prosecution, or

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} was gathered according to the law of the
      extradition partner; or

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} in the case of a person sought for the imposition
    or enforcement of a sentence, a judicial, prosecuting or
    correctional authority of the extradition partner certifies that the
    documents in the record of the case are accurate.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Authentication not required

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} No authentication of documents is required unless a
  relevant extradition agreement provides otherwise.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Record of the case and supplements

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} For the purposes of this section, a record of the
  case includes any supplement added to it.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Oath or solemn affirmation

**[[34]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-34 .sectionLabel}** A document is admissible
whether or not it is solemnly affirmed or under oath.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}No proof of signature

**[[35]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-35 .sectionLabel}** A document purporting to
have been signed by a judicial, prosecuting or correctional authority,
or a public officer, of the extradition partner shall be admitted
without proof of the signature or official character of the person
appearing to have signed it.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Translated documents

**[[36]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-36 .sectionLabel}** A translation of a
document into one of Canada's official languages shall be admitted
without any further formality.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Evidence of identity

**[[37]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-37 .sectionLabel}** The following are
evidence that the person before the court is the person referred to in
the order of arrest, the document that records the conviction or any
other document that is presented to support the request:

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} the fact that the name of the person before the court
  is similar to the name that is in the documents submitted by the
  extradition partner; and

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} the fact that the physical characteristics of the
  person before the court are similar to those evidenced in a
  photograph, fingerprint or other description of the person.

### [Judge's Report]{.HTitleText2} {#h-213024 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Report of the judge

- **[[38]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-38 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A
  judge who issues an order of committal of a person to await surrender
  shall transmit to the Minister the following documents:

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} a copy of the order;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} a copy of the evidence adduced at the hearing that
    has not already been transmitted to the Minister; and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} any report that the judge thinks fit.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Right to appeal

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} When the judge orders the committal of a person, the
  judge shall inform the person that they will not be surrendered until
  after the expiry of 30 days and that the person has a right to appeal
  the order and to apply for judicial interim release.

### [Property]{.HTitleText2} {#h-213033 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Property seized

- **[[39]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-39
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Subject to a relevant extradition
  agreement, a judge who makes an order of committal may order that any
  thing that was seized when the person was arrested and that may be
  used in the prosecution of the person for the offence for which the
  extradition was requested be transferred to the extradition partner at
  the time the person is surrendered.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Conditions of order

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The judge may include in the order any conditions
  that the judge considers desirable, including conditions

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} respecting the preservation and return to Canada of
    a thing; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} respecting the protection of the interests of third
    parties.

### [Powers of Minister]{.HTitleText2} {#h-213041 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Surrender

- **[[40]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-40 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  Minister may, within a period of 90 days after the date of a person's
  committal to await surrender, personally order that the person be
  surrendered to the extradition partner.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}When refugee claim

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Before making an order under subsection (1) with
  respect to a person who has made a claim for refugee protection under
  the [Immigration and Refugee Protection Act](/eng/acts/I-2.5), the
  Minister shall consult with the minister responsible for that Act.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Powers of the Minister

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} The Minister may seek any assurances that the
  Minister considers appropriate from the extradition partner, or may
  subject the surrender to any conditions that the Minister considers
  appropriate, including a condition that the person not be prosecuted,
  nor that a sentence be imposed on or enforced against the person, in
  respect of any offence or conduct other than that referred to in the
  order of surrender.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}No surrender

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} If the Minister subjects surrender of a person to
  assurances or conditions, the order of surrender shall not be executed
  until the Minister is satisfied that the assurances are given or the
  conditions agreed to by the extradition partner.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Extension of time

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} If the person has made submissions to the Minister
  under section 43 and the Minister is of the opinion that further time
  is needed to act on those submissions, the Minister may extend the
  period referred to in subsection (1) as follows:

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} if the person is the subject of a request for
    surrender by the International Criminal Court, and an issue has been
    raised as to the admissibility of the case or the jurisdiction of
    that Court, for a period ending not more than 45 days after the
    Court's ruling on the issue; or

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} in any other case, for one additional period that
    does not exceed 60 days.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Notice of extension of time

  [(6)]{.lawlabel} If an appeal has been filed under section 50 and the
  Minister has extended the period referred to in subsection (1), the
  Minister shall file with the court of appeal a notice of extension of
  time before the expiry of that period.

- 2001, c. 27, s. 250

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}When appeal pending

- **[[41]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-41 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  Minister may postpone the making of the order of surrender if

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} an appeal has been filed under section 50;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the Minister files a notice of postponement with
    the court of appeal before the expiry of the period referred to in
    subsection 40(1); and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} the order is made not later than 45 days after the

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}No further deferral of appeal

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} When the Minister has filed a notice of postponement
  with the court of appeal under paragraph (1)(b), that court may not
  defer the hearing of the appeal under subsection 51(2).

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Amendments

**[[42]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-42 .sectionLabel}** The Minister may amend a
surrender order at any time before its execution.

### [Submissions]{.HTitleText2} {#h-213069 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Submissions

- **[[43]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-43 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  person may, at any time before the expiry of 30 days after the date of
  the committal, make submissions to the Minister in respect of any
  ground that would be relevant to the Minister in making a decision in
  respect of the surrender of the person.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Late acceptance of submissions

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The Minister may accept submissions even after the
  expiry of those 30 days in circumstances that the Minister considers
  appropriate.

### [Reasons for Refusal]{.HTitleText2} {#h-213075 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}When order not to be made

- **[[44]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-44 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  Minister shall refuse to make a surrender order if the Minister is
  satisfied that

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the surrender would be unjust or oppressive having
    regard to all the relevant circumstances; or

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the request for extradition is made for the purpose
    of prosecuting or punishing the person by reason of their race,
    religion, nationality, ethnic origin, language, colour, political
    opinion, sex, sexual orientation, age, mental or physical disability
    or status or that the person's position may be prejudiced for any of
    those reasons.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}When Minister may refuse to make order

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The Minister may refuse to make a surrender order if
  the Minister is satisfied that the conduct in respect of which the
  request for extradition is made is punishable by death under the laws
  that apply to the extradition partner.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Refusal in extradition agreement

- **[[45]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-45 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  reasons for the refusal of surrender contained in a relevant
  extradition agreement, other than a multilateral extradition
  agreement, or the absence of reasons for refusal in such an agreement,
  prevail over sections 46 and 47.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Exception --- multilateral extradition
  agreement

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The reasons for the refusal of surrender contained in
  a relevant multilateral extradition agreement prevail over sections 46
  and 47 only to the extent of any inconsistency between either of those
  sections and those provisions.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}When order not to be made

- **[[46]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-46 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  Minister shall refuse to make a surrender order if the Minister is
  satisfied that

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the prosecution of a person is barred by
    prescription or limitation under the law that applies to the
    extradition partner;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the conduct in respect of which extradition is
    sought is a military offence that is not also an offence under
    criminal law; or

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} the conduct in respect of which extradition is
    sought is a political offence or an offence of a political
    character.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Restriction

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} For the purpose of subparagraph (1)(c), conduct that
  constitutes an offence mentioned in a multilateral extradition
  agreement for which Canada, as a party, is obliged to extradite the
  person or submit the matter to its appropriate authority for
  prosecution does not constitute a political offence or an offence of a
  political character. The following conduct also does not constitute a
  political offence or an offence of a political character:

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} murder or manslaughter;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} inflicting serious bodily harm;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} sexual assault;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} kidnapping, abduction, hostage-taking or extortion;

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} using explosives, incendiaries, devices or
    substances in circumstances in which human life is likely to be
    endangered or serious bodily harm or substantial property damage is
    likely to be caused; and

  - [(f)]{.lawlabel} an attempt or conspiracy to engage in, counselling,
    aiding or abetting another person to engage in, or being an
    accessory after the fact in relation to, the conduct referred to in
    any of paragraphs (a) to (e).

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}When Minister may refuse to make order

**[[47]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-47 .sectionLabel}** The Minister may refuse
to make a surrender order if the Minister is satisfied that

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} the person would be entitled, if that person were
  tried in Canada, to be discharged under the laws of Canada because of
  a previous acquittal or conviction;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} the person was convicted in their absence and could
  not, on surrender, have the case reviewed;

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} the person was less than eighteen years old at the
  time of the offence and the law that applies to them in the territory
  over which the extradition partner has jurisdiction is not consistent
  with the fundamental principles governing the [Youth Criminal Justice
  Act](/eng/acts/Y-1.5);

- [(d)]{.lawlabel} the conduct in respect of which the request for
  extradition is made is the subject of criminal proceedings in Canada
  against the person; or

- [(e)]{.lawlabel} none of the conduct on which the extradition partner
  bases its request occurred in the territory over which the extradition
  partner has jurisdiction.

**[[47.1]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-47.1 .sectionLabel}** The grounds for

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}When refugee claim

### [Appeal]{.HTitleText2} {#h-213122 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Appeal

**[[49]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-49 .sectionLabel}** A person may appeal
against an order of committal --- or the Attorney General, on behalf of
the extradition partner, may appeal the discharge of the person or a
stay of proceedings --- to the court of appeal of the province in which
the order of committal, the order discharging the person or the order
staying the proceedings was made,

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} on a ground of appeal that involves a question of law
  alone;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} on a ground of appeal that involves a question of
  fact or a question of mixed law and fact, with leave of the court of
  appeal or a judge of the court of appeal; or

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} on a ground of appeal not set out in paragraph (a)
  or (b) that appears to the court of appeal to be a sufficient ground
  of appeal, with leave of the court of appeal.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Notice of appeal

- **[[50]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-50 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} An
  appellant who proposes to appeal to a court of appeal or to obtain the
  leave of that court to appeal must give notice of appeal or notice of
  the application for leave to appeal not later than 30 days after the
  decision of the judge with respect to the committal or discharge of
  the person, or the stay of proceedings, as the case may be, in any
  manner that may be directed by the rules of court.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Extension of time

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The court of appeal or a judge of the court of appeal
  may, either before or after the expiry of the 30 days referred to in
  subsection (1), extend the time within which notice of appeal or
  notice of an application for leave to appeal may be given.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Hearing of appeal

- **[[51]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-51 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} An
  appeal under this Act shall be scheduled for hearing by the court of
  appeal at an early date whether that date is in or out of the
  prescribed sessions of that court.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Deferral of appeal

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The hearing of an appeal against an order of
  committal may be deferred by the court of appeal until the Minister
  makes a decision in respect of the surrender of the person under
  section 40.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Provisions of the [Criminal
Code](/eng/acts/C-46) to apply

- **[[52]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-52
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Sections 677, 678.1, 682 to 685 and
  688 of the [Criminal Code](/eng/acts/C-46) apply, with any
  modifications that the circumstances require, to appeals under this
  Act.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Rules

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Unless inconsistent with the provisions of this Act,
  rules made by the court of appeal under section 482 of the [Criminal
  Code](/eng/acts/C-46) in relation to appeals to that court under that
  Act apply, with any modifications that the circumstances require, to
  appeals under this Act.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Powers of the court of appeal

**[[53]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-53 .sectionLabel}** On the hearing of an
appeal against an order of committal of a person, the court of appeal
may

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} allow the appeal, in respect of any offence in
  respect of which the person has been committed, if it is of the
  opinion

  - [(i)]{.lawlabel} that the order of committal should be set aside on
    the ground that it is unreasonable or cannot be supported by the
    evidence,

  - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} that the order of committal should be set aside on
    the ground of a wrong decision on a question of law, or

  - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} that, on any ground, there was a miscarriage of
    justice; or

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} dismiss the appeal

  - [(i)]{.lawlabel} if it does not allow the appeal on any ground
    referred to in paragraph (a), or

  - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} even though the court of appeal is of the opinion
    that on the ground referred to in subparagraph (a)(ii) the appeal
    may be decided in favour of the appellant, if it is of the opinion
    that no substantial wrong or miscarriage of justice has occurred and
    the order of committal should be upheld.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Effect of allowing appeal

**[[54]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-54 .sectionLabel}** If the court of appeal
allows an appeal under paragraph 53(a), it shall

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} set aside the order of committal and

  - [(i)]{.lawlabel} discharge the person, or

  - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} order a new extradition hearing; or

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} amend the order of committal to exclude an offence in
  respect of which the court is of the opinion that the person has not
  been properly committed on a ground referred to in subparagraph
  53(a)(i), (ii) or (iii).

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Powers

- **[[55]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-55 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} On the
  hearing of an appeal against the discharge of a person or against a
  stay of proceedings, the court of appeal may

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} allow the appeal and set aside the order of
    discharge or stay, if it is of the opinion

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} that the order of discharge should be set aside
      on the ground that it is unreasonable or cannot be supported by
      the evidence,

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} that the order of discharge or the stay of
      proceedings should be set aside on the ground of a wrong decision
      on a question of law, or

    - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} that, on any ground, there was a miscarriage of
      justice; or

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} dismiss the appeal.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Order for new extradition hearing or
  committal

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The court of appeal may, if it sets aside a stay of
  proceedings, order a new extradition hearing. The court of appeal may,
  if it sets aside an order of discharge, order a new extradition
  hearing or order the committal of the person.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Deferral of Supreme Court appeal

- **[[56]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-56 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  Supreme Court may defer, until the Minister makes a decision with
  respect to the surrender of the person under section 40, the hearing
  of an application for leave to appeal, or the hearing of an appeal,
  from a decision of the court of appeal on an appeal taken under
  section 49, or on any other appeal in respect of a matter arising
  under this Act.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Deferral of Supreme Court appeal

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The Supreme Court may also, if an application for
  judicial review is made under section 57 or otherwise, defer the
  hearing until the court of appeal makes its determination on the
  application.

### [Judicial Review of Minister's Order]{.HTitleText2} {#h-213173 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Review of order

- **[[57]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-57
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Despite the [Federal Courts
  Act](/eng/acts/F-7), the court of appeal of the province in which the
  committal of the person was ordered has exclusive original
  jurisdiction to hear and determine applications for judicial review
  under this Act, made in respect of the decision of the Minister under
  section 40.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Application

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} An application for judicial review may be made by the
  person.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Time limitation

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} An application for judicial review shall be made, in
  accordance with the rules of court of the court of appeal, within 30
  days after the time the decision referred to in subsection (1) was
  first communicated by the Minister to the person, or within any
  further time that the court of appeal, either before or after the
  expiry of those 30 days, may fix or allow.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Section 679 of the [Criminal
  Code](/eng/acts/C-46)

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} Section 679 of the [Criminal Code](/eng/acts/C-46)
  applies, with any modifications that the circumstances require, to an
  application for judicial review.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Hearing of application

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} An application for judicial review shall be scheduled
  for hearing by the court of appeal at an early date whether that date
  is in or out of the prescribed sessions of that court.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Powers of court of appeal

  [(6)]{.lawlabel} On an application for judicial review, the court of
  appeal may

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} order the Minister to do any act or thing that the
    Minister has unlawfully failed or refused to do or has unreasonably
    delayed in doing; or

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} declare invalid or unlawful, quash, set aside, set
    aside and refer back for determination in accordance with any
    directions that it considers appropriate, prohibit or restrain the
    decision of the Minister referred to in subsection (1).

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Grounds of review

  [(7)]{.lawlabel} The court of appeal may grant relief under this
  section on any of the grounds on which the Federal Court may grant
  relief under subsection 18.1(4) of the [Federal Courts
  Act](/eng/acts/F-7).

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Defect in form or technical
  irregularity

  [(8)]{.lawlabel} If the sole ground for relief established in an
  application for judicial review is a defect in form or a technical
  irregularity, the court of appeal may

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} refuse the relief if it finds that no substantial
    wrong or miscarriage of justice has occurred; or

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} in the case of a defect in form or a technical
    irregularity in the decision, make an order validating the order, to
    have effect from the time and on the terms that it considers
    appropriate.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}One hearing by court of appeal

  [(9)]{.lawlabel} If an appeal under section 49 or any other appeal in
  respect of a matter arising under this Act is pending, the court of
  appeal may join the hearing of that appeal with the hearing of an
  application for judicial review.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Provincial rules of judicial review
  apply

  [(10)]{.lawlabel} Unless inconsistent with the provisions of this Act,
  all laws, including rules, respecting judicial review in force in the
  province of the court of appeal apply, with any modifications that the
  circumstances require, to applications under this section.

### [Order of Surrender]{.HTitleText2} {#h-213201 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Contents of the surrender order

**[[58]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-58 .sectionLabel}** An order of surrender
must

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} contain the name of the person who is to be
  surrendered;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} describe the offence in respect of which the
  extradition is requested, the offence for which the committal was
  ordered or the conduct for which the person is to be surrendered;

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} state the extradition partner to which the person is
  to be conveyed;

- [(d)]{.lawlabel} direct the person who has custody of the person to be
  surrendered to deliver them into the custody of the person or a member
  of the class of persons referred to in paragraph (e);

- [(e)]{.lawlabel} designate the person or class of persons authorized
  for the purposes of section 60;

- [(f)]{.lawlabel} set out any assurances or conditions to which the
  surrender is subject;

- [(g)]{.lawlabel} fix, in the case of postponement of surrender under
  section 64, the period of time at or before the expiry of which the
  person is to be surrendered; and

- [(h)]{.lawlabel} fix, in the case of a temporary surrender under
  section 66,

  - [(i)]{.lawlabel} the period of time at or before the expiry of which
    the person to be surrendered must be returned to Canada, and

  - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} the period of time at or before the expiry of
    which final surrender shall take place.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Surrender for other offences

**[[59]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-59 .sectionLabel}** Subject to a relevant
extradition agreement, the Minister may, if the request for extradition
is based on more than one offence, order the surrender of a person for
all the offences even if not all of them fulfil the requirements set out
in section 3, if

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} the person is being surrendered for at least one
  offence that fulfils the requirements set out in section 3; and

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} all the offences relate to conduct that, had it
  occurred in Canada, would have constituted offences that are
  punishable under the laws of Canada.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Power to convey

**[[60]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-60 .sectionLabel}** On the execution of a
surrender order, the person or persons designated under paragraph 58(e)
shall have the authority to receive, hold in custody and convey the
person into the territory over which the extradition partner has
jurisdiction.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Escape

- **[[61]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-61 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} If the
  person escapes while in custody, the law that applies with respect to
  a person who is accused or convicted of a crime against the laws of
  Canada and who escapes applies with respect to the person.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Arrest

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} If the person escapes while in custody, the person or
  member of the class of persons having custody of the person has the
  power to arrest them in fresh pursuit.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Delay before surrender

- **[[62]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-62 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} No
  person may be surrendered

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} until a period of 30 days has expired after the
    date of the committal for surrender; or

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} if an appeal or a judicial review in respect of a
    matter arising under this Act, or any appeal from an appeal or
    judicial review, is pending, until after the date of the final
    decision of the court on the appeal or judicial review.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Waiver of period of time

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The person may waive the period referred to in
  paragraph (1)(a) if they do so in writing.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Place of surrender

**[[63]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-63 .sectionLabel}** A surrender may take
place at any place within or outside Canada that is agreed to by Canada
and the extradition partner.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Postponement of surrender

- **[[64]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-64 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Unless
  the Minister orders otherwise, a surrender order made in respect of a
  person accused of an offence within Canadian jurisdiction or who is
  serving a sentence in Canada after a conviction for an offence, other
  than an offence with respect to the conduct to which the order relates
  does not take effect until the person has been discharged, whether by
  acquittal, by expiry of the sentence or otherwise.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Offence before or after surrender

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} For greater certainty, the person need not have been
  accused of the offence within Canadian jurisdiction before the
  surrender order was made.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Return to Canada

**[[65]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-65 .sectionLabel}** If a person returns to
Canada after surrender before the expiry of a sentence that they were
serving in Canada at the time of surrender, the remaining part of the
sentence must be served.

### [Temporary Surrender]{.HTitleText2} {#h-213241 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Temporary surrender

- **[[66]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-66 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  Minister may order the temporary surrender to an extradition partner
  of a person who is ordered committed under section 29 while serving a
  term of imprisonment in Canada so that the extradition partner may
  prosecute the person or to ensure the person's presence in respect of
  appeal proceedings that affect the person, on condition that the
  extradition partner give the assurances referred to in subsections (3)
  and (4).

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Time limits

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} An order of temporary surrender is subject to the
  time limits set out in subsections 40(1) and (5) and paragraph
  41(1)(c).

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Assurances

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} The Minister may not order temporary surrender under
  subsection (1) unless the extradition partner gives an assurance that
  the person will remain in custody while temporarily surrendered to the
  extradition partner and

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} in the case of temporary surrender for a trial,
    that the person will be returned within 30 days after the completion
    of the trial, unless a relevant extradition agreement provides for
    another time limit; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} in the case of temporary surrender for an appeal,
    that the person will be returned within 30 days after the completion
    of the proceedings for which the presence of the person was
    required, unless a relevant extradition agreement provides for
    another time limit.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Time limit

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} The Minister may require the extradition partner to
  give an assurance that the person will be returned no later than a
  specified date or that the person will be returned on request of the
  Minister.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Assurances in extradition agreements

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} Any assurance referred to in subsections (3) and (4)
  that is included in a relevant extradition agreement need not be
  repeated as a specific assurance.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Final surrender after temporary
  surrender

  [(6)]{.lawlabel} A person shall, subject to subsection (7), be
  surrendered to the extradition partner without a further request for
  extradition after the person

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} has been temporarily surrendered;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} has been convicted by the extradition partner and
    had a term of imprisonment imposed on them;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} has been returned to Canada under subsection (4);
    and

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} has finished serving the portion of the sentence
    that they were serving in custody in Canada at the time of the
    temporary surrender, unless the Minister orders that they be
    surrendered earlier.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}No final surrender if circumstances
  warrant

  [(7)]{.lawlabel} The Minister may, in circumstances that the Minister
  considers appropriate, revoke the surrender order and order the
  discharge of the person.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Notice

  [(8)]{.lawlabel} The authority who has custody of the person to be
  surrendered under subsection (6) shall give the Minister reasonable
  notice of the time when the portion of the person's sentence to be
  served in custody is to expire.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Final surrender when Canadian sentence
  expires

  [(9)]{.lawlabel} When the sentence that the person is serving in
  Canada expires during the period during which the person is
  temporarily surrendered to an extradition partner, the surrender is
  considered to be a final surrender.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Waiver of return

  [(10)]{.lawlabel} The Minister may, after consultation with the
  Minister of Public Safety and Emergency Preparedness or the
  appropriate provincial minister responsible for corrections, waive the
  return of the person by the extradition partner.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Final surrender despite subsection 3(3)

  [(11)]{.lawlabel} A person may be surrendered under subsection (6)
  even if the term of imprisonment imposed by the extradition partner,
  or the portion of the term remaining to be served, is less than that
  required by subsection 3(3).

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Order for surrender

**[[67]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-67 .sectionLabel}** An order of surrender

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} remains eligible for remission in accordance with the

  sentence in Canada.

### [Remedy]{.HTitleText2} {#h-213279 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Remedy in case of delay

**[[69]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-69 .sectionLabel}** A judge of the superior
court of the province in which the person is detained who has the power
to grant a writ of [habeas corpus]{.otherLang lang="la"}, may, on
application made by or on behalf of the person, and on proof that
reasonable notice of the intention to make the application has been
given to the Minister, order the person to be discharged out of custody
unless sufficient cause is shown against the discharge if

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} the Minister has not made an order of surrender under
  section 40

  - [(i)]{.lawlabel} before the expiry of the period referred to in
    subsection 40(1) and any additional period referred to in subsection
    40(5), or

  - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} if a notice of postponement has been filed under
    paragraph 41(1)(b), before the expiry of 45 days after the date of
    the decision of the court of appeal referred to in paragraph
    41(1)(c); or

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} the person is not surrendered and conveyed to the
  extradition partner

  - [(i)]{.lawlabel} within 45 days after the order of surrender is made
    by the Minister under section 40, or

  - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} if an appeal or judicial review in respect of any
    matter arising under this Act, or an appeal from such an appeal or
    judicial review, is pending, within 45 days after the final decision
    of the court is made,

  over and above, in any case referred to in subparagraph (i) or (ii),
  the time required to convey the person to the extradition partner.

### [Consent]{.HTitleText2} {#h-213289 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Consent to surrender

### [Transit]{.HTitleText2} {#h-213334 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Transit

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Sections to apply

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Variation of authorization

  authorization or who fails to comply with some other condition of the
  authorization is, for the purposes of the [Immigration and Refugee
  Protection Act](/eng/acts/I-2.5), deemed to be a person who entered

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Specialty if person is in Canada

**[[80]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-80 .sectionLabel}** Subject to a relevant
extradition agreement, a person who has been extradited to Canada by a
requested State or entity shall not, unless the person has voluntarily
left Canada after surrender or has had a reasonable opportunity of
leaving Canada,

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} be detained or prosecuted, or have a sentence imposed
  or executed, or a disposition made or executed under the [Young
  Offenders Act](/eng/acts/Y-1), chapter Y-1 of the Revised Statutes of
  Canada, 1985, in Canada in respect of an offence that is alleged to
  have been committed, or was committed, before surrender other than

  - [(i)]{.lawlabel} the offence in respect of which the person was
    surrendered or an included offence,

  - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} another offence in respect of which the requested
    State or entity consents to the person being detained or prosecuted,
    or

  - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} another offence in respect of which the person
    consents to being detained or prosecuted; or

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} be detained in Canada for the purpose of being
  surrendered to another State or entity for prosecution or for
  imposition or execution of a sentence in respect of an offence that is
  alleged to have been committed, or was committed, before surrender to
  Canada, unless the requested State or entity consents.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Conveyance of surrendered person

- **[[81]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-81 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A
  person who is surrendered to Canada by a requested State or entity may
  be brought into Canada by an agent of the requested State or entity if
  the Minister so authorizes and be delivered to an appropriate
  authority to be dealt with according to law.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Power to convey

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} On the execution of a surrender order, the authorized
  agent of the requested State or entity shall have the authority to
  hold the person in custody in Canada until delivery under subsection
  (1).

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Escape

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} If the person escapes while in custody, the law that
  applies with respect to a person who is accused or convicted of a
  crime against the laws of Canada and who escapes applies with respect
  to the person.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Arrest

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} If the person escapes, the authorized agent of the
  requested State or entity has the power to arrest them in fresh
  pursuit.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Order of detention for temporary
surrender

- **[[82]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-82
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Subject to subsection (2), a judge
  shall, on application of the competent authority made at any time
  before the temporary surrender, order the detention in custody of a
  person who is serving a term of imprisonment or has otherwise lawfully
  been deprived of their liberty in a requested State or entity and
  whose temporary surrender Canada has requested for the purpose of
  prosecution or appeal.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Time limit

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The order must contain a provision that the person
  will not be detained in custody after

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} a date specified in the order;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} in the case of surrender for a trial, 45 days after
    the completion of the trial; or

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} in the case of surrender for an appeal, 30 days
    after the completion of the proceedings for which the presence of
    the person was required.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Order of detention to prevail

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} An order made under subsection (1) prevails over an
  order made by a Canadian court, a judge of a Canadian court, a
  Canadian justice of the peace or any other person who has power in
  Canada to compel the appearance of a person, in respect of anything
  that occurred before the person is transferred to Canada.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Variation of detention order

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} The judge who made the detention order or another
  judge may vary its terms and conditions and, in particular, may extend
  the duration of the detention.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Return

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} Subject to subsection (6), the person shall be
  returned to the requested State or entity on completion of the
  proceedings in Canada for which the person was temporarily surrendered
  or on the expiry of the period set out in the order, whichever is
  sooner.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Return if right of appeal

  [(6)]{.lawlabel} The person shall not be returned to the requested
  State or entity

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} if the person has been convicted in Canada, before
    30 days after the conviction, unless the person or the competent
    authority declares that there will be no appeal; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} if the person has been acquitted, before 30 days
    after the acquittal, unless the competent authority declares that
    there will be no appeal.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Return for appeal

  [(7)]{.lawlabel} The court of appeal may, on application, recommend
  that the Minister request another temporary surrender of a person who
  has been returned to the requested State or entity after trial, if the
  court of appeal is satisfied that the interests of justice require
  their presence for the appeal.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Commencement of sentence

- **[[83]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-83
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Subject to subsection (3), the
  sentence or disposition of a person who has been temporarily
  surrendered and who has been convicted and sentenced, or found guilty
  and sentenced, in Canada, or in respect of whom a disposition has been
  made under the [Young Offenders Act](/eng/acts/Y-1), chapter Y-1 of
  the Revised Statutes of Canada, 1985, does not commence until their
  final extradition to Canada.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Warrant of committal

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The warrant of committal issued under the [Criminal
  Code](/eng/acts/C-46) in respect of the person must state that the
  person is to be committed to custody to serve the sentence or
  disposition immediately on their final extradition to Canada.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}If concurrent sentences ordered

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} The sentencing judge may order that the person's
  sentence, or the disposition under the [Young Offenders
  Act](/eng/acts/Y-1), chapter Y-1 of the Revised Statutes of Canada,
  1985, be executed concurrently with the sentence they are serving in
  the requested State or entity, in which case the warrant of committal
  or order of disposition shall state that the person is to be committed
  to custody under subsection (2) only for any portion of the sentence
  or disposition remaining at the time of their final extradition to
  Canada.

- 1999, c. 18, s. 83
- 2002, c. 1, s. 194

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Cases pending --- former [Extradition

repealed, if the hearing in respect of the extradition had already begun

Act repealed by section 130 of this Act applies to a matter respecting
the return under that Act of a person as though it had not been
repealed, if the hearing before the provincial court judge in respect of
the return had already begun on the day on which this Act comes into
force.
