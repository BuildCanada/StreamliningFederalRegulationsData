## Preclearance Act, 2016 {#preclearance-act-2016 .Title-of-Act}

[S.C.]{.abbr title="Statutes of Canada"} 2017, c. 27
:::

Assented to 2017-12-12

An Act respecting the preclearance of persons and goods in Canada and
the United States
::::

:::::::::: section
::: {.section .order}
###### Preamble {#preamble .MarginalNote}

Whereas the Agreement on Land, Rail, Marine, and Air Transport
Preclearance between the Government of Canada and the Government of the
United States of America (the "Agreement") was done at Washington on
March 16, 2015;

Whereas the Agreement builds on the Agreement on Air Transport
Preclearance between the Government of Canada and the Government of the
United States of America, done at Toronto on January 18, 2001;

Whereas the Agreement imposes reciprocal obligations on both countries
to facilitate travel and trade while enhancing the security of both
countries;

Whereas, under the Agreement, United States preclearance officers may be
authorized to conduct preclearance in Canada of travellers and goods
bound for the United States and, as part of preclearance, to exercise
powers, and perform duties and functions, under the laws of the United
States on the admission of travellers and goods into the United States;

Whereas the exercise of any power and performance of any duty or
function under United States law in Canada is subject to Canadian law,
including the Canadian Charter of Rights and Freedoms, the [Canadian
Bill of Rights](/eng/acts/C-12.3) and the [Canadian Human Rights
Act](/eng/acts/H-6);

And whereas, under the Agreement, Canadian border services officers and
other Canadian public officers may be authorized to conduct preclearance
in the United States of travellers and goods bound for Canada and, as
part of preclearance, to exercise powers and perform duties and
functions under Canadian law concerning the admission of travellers and
goods into Canada;
:::

::: Enacts
Now, therefore, Her Majesty, by and with the advice and consent of the
Senate and House of Commons of Canada, enacts as follows:
:::

## [Short Title]{.HTitleText1} {#h-396237 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Short title

**[[1]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-1 .sectionLabel}** This Act may be cited as
the [Preclearance Act, 2016](/eng/acts/P-19.32).

## [Interpretation and Application]{.HTitleText1} {#h-396240 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Definitions

- **[[2]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-2 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  following definitions apply in this Act.

  [[Agreement]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[Agreement]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means the Agreement on Land,
      Rail, Marine, and Air Transport Preclearance between the
      Government of Canada and the Government of United States of
      America, done at Washington on March 16,
      2015. ([Accord]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

  [[goods]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[goods]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} includes

      - [(a)]{.lawlabel} currency and monetary instruments; and

      - [(b)]{.lawlabel} for greater certainty, animals and plants and
        their products, conveyances and any document in any
        form. ([biens]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

  [[Minister]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[Minister]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means, in relation to section 6,
      7 or 8, subsection 11(2) or 34(2), section 41 or 45 or subsection
      59(1), a Minister designated under subsection 4(2) for the purpose
      of that provision or, in the absence of such a designation, the
      Minister who is responsible for this
      Act. ([ministre]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}For greater certainty

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} For greater certainty, travellers or goods en route
  towards the United States or Canada on their way to another country
  are, for the purposes of this Act, bound for the United States or
  Canada, as the case may be.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Definition of [[Act of
  Parliament]{.dfn}]{.DefinitionRef}

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} For greater certainty, in this Act [[Act of
  Parliament]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means an Act of the Parliament of
  Canada.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Binding on Her Majesty

**[[3]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-3 .sectionLabel}** This Act is binding on Her
Majesty in right of Canada or a province.

### [Designation of Preclearance Areas and Preclearance Perimeters]{.HTitleText2} {#h-396277 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Preclearance area

- **[[6]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-6 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  Minister may designate an area in a location set out in the schedule
  as a preclearance area. The Minister may provide that the designated
  area is a preclearance area only during specified periods and in
  specified circumstances.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Other preclearance areas

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} If the Minister has designated a preclearance area
  under subsection (1) and a conveyance --- that is intended to be used
  to transport travellers or goods that are undergoing
  preclearance --- is stationed in preparation for departure to the
  United States such that all or part of the conveyance is outside that
  preclearance area, the following are also preclearance areas during
  the period in which the conveyance is so stationed:

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the conveyance;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} a corridor between the preclearance area designated
    under subsection (1) and the conveyance; and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} any other conveyance that is used to transport
    travellers or goods between the preclearance area designated under
    subsection (1) and the conveyance.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Preclearance perimeter

**[[7]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-7 .sectionLabel}** The Minister may designate
as a preclearance perimeter an area that is adjacent to where a
conveyance referred to in paragraph 6(2)(a) will be stationed in
preparation for departure to the United States, but the designated area
is only a preclearance perimeter during the period in which such a
conveyance is so stationed.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Modify or cancel

**[[8]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-8 .sectionLabel}** The Minister may modify or
cancel anything that he or she has done under subsection 6(1) or section
7.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Canadian law

**[[9]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-9 .sectionLabel}** For greater certainty,
Canadian law applies, and may be administered and enforced, in
preclearance areas and preclearance perimeters.

### [Powers, Duties and Functions in Preclearance Area and Preclearance Perimeter]{.HTitleText2} {#h-396292 .Subheading}

#### [Preclearance]{.HTitleText3} {#h-396293 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Preclearance powers, duties and functions

- **[[10]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-10
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Subject to subsection (2), a
  preclearance officer may, in a preclearance area or preclearance
  perimeter, exercise the powers and perform the duties and functions
  conferred on them under the laws of the United States on importation
  of goods, immigration, agriculture and public health and safety, in
  order to determine whether a traveller or goods bound for the United
  States is or are admissible into that country and, if applicable, to
  permit them to enter that country.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Limitation

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} A preclearance officer is not permitted to exercise
  any powers of questioning or interrogation, examination, search,
  seizure, forfeiture, detention or arrest that are conferred under the
  laws of the United States.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Compliance with Canadian law

- **[[11]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-11 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A
  preclearance officer must exercise their powers and perform their
  duties and functions under this Act in accordance with Canadian law,
  including the Canadian Charter of Rights and Freedoms, the [Canadian
  Bill of Rights](/eng/acts/C-12.3) and the [Canadian Human Rights
  Act](/eng/acts/H-6).

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Training --- preclearance officer

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The Minister must, in accordance with paragraph 2 of
  Article IX of the Agreement, provide every preclearance officer with
  training on the Canadian law that applies to the exercise of the
  preclearance officer's powers and the performance of their duties and
  functions under this Act.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Duties, taxes and fees

- **[[12]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-12 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} For
  greater certainty, in the course of preclearing travellers and goods,
  a preclearance officer may collect duties, taxes and fees in
  accordance with the laws of the United States on importation of goods,
  immigration, agriculture and public health and safety.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Penalties or sanctions

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} For greater certainty, in the course of preclearing
  travellers and goods, a preclearance officer may impose administrative
  monetary penalties or other civil sanctions in accordance with those
  laws.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Exception --- Canadian prosecution

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} However, no such penalty or other sanction is to be
  imposed if a prosecution for an offence under an Act of Parliament is
  instituted in Canada with respect to the act or omission that would
  give rise to it. If such a prosecution is instituted after a penalty
  or sanction has been imposed, the penalty or sanction is a nullity and
  any payment in respect of it must be reimbursed.

#### [Other Powers and Justification]{.HTitleText3} {#h-396311 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Frisk search for dangerous thing

- **[[13]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-13 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A
  preclearance officer may, in a preclearance area or preclearance
  perimeter, conduct a frisk search of a person if the officer has
  reasonable grounds to suspect that the person has on their person
  anything that would present a danger to human life or safety.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Power to detain things

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The preclearance officer may detain anything found
  during the search that would present a danger to human life or safety.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Detention --- offence

- **[[14]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-14 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} If a
  preclearance officer has reasonable grounds to believe that a person
  has committed an offence under an Act of Parliament, the officer may,
  in a preclearance area or preclearance perimeter,

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} detain the person; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} detain any goods they have on their person and any
    goods under their control, if the goods may be evidence of the
    offence.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Delivery

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The preclearance officer must, as soon as feasible,
  deliver the person and any detained goods into the custody of a police
  officer or border services officer.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Detention --- public health

- **[[15]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-15 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} If a
  preclearance officer has reasonable grounds to believe that a
  traveller bound for the United States poses a risk of significant harm
  to public health, the officer may, in a preclearance area or
  preclearance perimeter, detain the traveller.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Delivery

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The preclearance officer must, as soon as feasible,
  deliver the traveller into the custody of a police officer, a border
  services officer or a person designated as a quarantine officer under
  subsection 5(2) of the [Quarantine Act](/eng/acts/Q-1.1).

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Justification

- **[[16]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-16 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A
  preclearance officer is, if they act on reasonable grounds, justified
  in doing what they are required or authorized to do under this Act and
  in using as much force as is necessary for that purpose.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Limitation

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} However, a preclearance officer is not justified in
  using force that is intended or is likely to cause death or grievous
  bodily harm unless the officer has reasonable grounds to believe that
  it is necessary for self-preservation or the preservation of anyone
  under the officer's protection from death or grievous bodily harm.

### [Preclearance Area]{.HTitleText2} {#h-396334 .Subheading}

#### [Access]{.HTitleText3} {#h-396335 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Limited access

**[[17]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-17 .sectionLabel}** Only the following
persons may enter a preclearance area:

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} travellers bound for the United States;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} preclearance officers;

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} police officers and border services officers, for the
  purposes of exercising powers and performing duties and functions
  under this Part;

- [(d)]{.lawlabel} persons who are authorized by the Minister under
  section 45 or by regulation; and

- [(e)]{.lawlabel} subject to any regulations, persons who are
  authorized by the operator of a facility.

#### [Obligations]{.HTitleText3} {#h-396343 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Traveller's obligations

- **[[18]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-18 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A
  traveller bound for the United States who is in a preclearance area
  must

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} have in their possession identification that is
    issued by the federal government, a provincial or local government
    or a foreign government and that, except in the circumstances that
    may be prescribed by regulation, contains their photograph; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} on entry, immediately report to a preclearance
    officer.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Obligations subject to withdrawal

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Unless they withdraw from preclearance under section
  29, a traveller bound for the United States who is in a preclearance
  area must

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} answer truthfully any question that is asked by a
    preclearance officer in accordance with this Act;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} as directed by a preclearance officer, present any
    goods in the traveller's possession, open or unpack the goods, and
    unload a conveyance for which they are responsible or open any part
    of it;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} comply with any other direction given to the
    traveller in accordance with this Act by a preclearance officer,
    police officer or border services officer; and

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} comply with any other requirement that is
    prescribed by regulation.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Obligations of other persons

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} A person, other than a traveller bound for the United
  States, who is in a preclearance area must comply with any requirement
  that is prescribed by regulation.

#### [Powers, Duties and Functions]{.HTitleText3} {#h-396357 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Only in preclearance area

**[[19]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-19 .sectionLabel}** A preclearance officer
may exercise the powers, and perform the duties and functions, set out
in sections 20 to 26 only in a preclearance area.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}General powers

- **[[20]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-20 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A
  preclearance officer may, for the purpose of conducting preclearance,

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} question a traveller bound for the United States;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} collect information from a traveller bound for the
    United States;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} examine, search and detain goods bound for the
    United States, including by taking samples of the goods in
    reasonable amounts;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} direct a person to report to a preclearance
    officer, identify themselves, and state their reason for being in a
    preclearance area; and

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} direct a traveller, or a person not authorized to
    be in a preclearance area, to leave a preclearance area.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Biometric information

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} In the course of conducting preclearance, a
  preclearance officer may, for the purpose of verifying the identity of
  a traveller bound for the United States, collect biometric information
  from the traveller, other than from the traveller's bodily substances.
  However, the officer is not permitted to collect biometric information
  unless notification that travellers may withdraw from preclearance is
  provided in the preclearance area, through signage or other means of
  communication.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Frisk search --- concealed goods

**[[21]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-21 .sectionLabel}** A preclearance officer
may, for the purpose of conducting preclearance, conduct a frisk search
of a traveller bound for the United States if the officer has reasonable
grounds to suspect that the traveller has on their person concealed
goods.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Strip search

- **[[22]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-22 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A
  preclearance officer may, for the purpose of a strip search, detain a
  traveller bound for the United States if the officer has reasonable
  grounds to suspect that

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the traveller has on their person concealed goods
    or anything that would present a danger to human life or safety; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the search is necessary for the purpose of
    conducting preclearance.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Request --- border services officer

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} On detaining a traveller for the purpose of a strip
  search, the preclearance officer must immediately request that a
  border services officer conduct the search and advise of the grounds
  on which the traveller was detained.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Strip search --- border services
  officer

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} A border services officer may conduct the strip
  search if they have reasonable grounds to suspect that

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the traveller has on their person concealed goods
    or anything that would present a danger to human life or safety; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the strip search is necessary for the purpose of
    conducting preclearance.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Strip search --- preclearance officer

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} A preclearance officer may conduct the strip search,
  in a manner consistent with section 11, if they have reasonable
  grounds to suspect that the conditions under paragraphs (1)(a) and (b)
  are still met and if

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} a border services officer declines to conduct it;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the Canada Border Services Agency advises the
    preclearance officer that no border services officer is able to
    conduct the search within a reasonable time; or

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} the Agency and the preclearance officer agree that
    a border services officer is to conduct the search within a
    specified period, but no border services officer arrives within that
    period.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Observation by preclearance officer

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} A strip search conducted under subsection (3) may be
  observed by a preclearance officer who is of the same sex as the
  traveller or, if no such preclearance officer is available, by any
  suitable person of the same sex as the traveller that a preclearance
  officer may authorize.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Monitored bowel movement

- **[[23]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-23 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A
  preclearance officer may, for the purpose of a monitored bowel
  movement, detain a traveller bound for the United States if the
  officer has reasonable grounds to suspect that

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the traveller is concealing goods inside their
    body;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the monitoring would permit the finding or
    retrieval of the goods; and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} the monitoring is necessary for the purpose of
    conducting preclearance.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Delivery to border services officer

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} On detaining a traveller for the purpose of a
  monitored bowel movement, the preclearance officer must deliver the
  traveller into the custody of a border services officer as soon as
  feasible and advise that officer of the grounds on which the traveller
  was detained.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Monitoring by border services officer

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} A border services officer may conduct a monitored
  bowel movement if they have reasonable grounds to suspect that

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the traveller is concealing goods inside their
    body;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the monitoring would permit the finding or
    retrieval of the goods; and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} the monitoring is necessary for the purpose of
    conducting preclearance.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}X-ray or body cavity search

- **[[24]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-24 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A
  preclearance officer or border services officer may request that a
  traveller bound for the United States undergo an x-ray search or a
  body cavity search if the officer has reasonable grounds to suspect
  that

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the traveller is concealing goods inside their
    body;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the x-ray search would permit the finding or
    identification of the goods or the body cavity search would permit
    the finding or retrieval of the goods; and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} the search is necessary for the purpose of
    conducting preclearance.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Traveller's consent

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Despite paragraph 18(2)(c), no x-ray search or body
  cavity search may be conducted unless the traveller consents.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Transfer to medical facility

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} A police officer or border services officer may
  transfer the traveller to a medical facility for the search. A
  preclearance officer is not permitted to do so.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Person conducting search

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} Only an x-ray technician may, with the consent of a
  physician, conduct an x-ray search, and only a physician may conduct a
  body cavity search.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Danger to traveller's health or safety

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} Regardless of whether the traveller consents to an
  x-ray search or body cavity search,

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} if a preclearance officer has reasonable grounds to
    suspect that there is a clear and substantial danger to the
    traveller's health or safety, the officer may deliver the traveller
    to a police officer or border services officer so that the police
    officer or border services officer may transfer the traveller to a
    medical facility; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} if a border services officer has reasonable grounds
    to suspect that there is such a danger, the officer may transfer the
    traveller to a medical facility.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Right to be taken before senior officer

- **[[25]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-25 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A
  preclearance officer or border services officer must, before
  conducting a search of a traveller under any of sections 21 to 23,
  inform the traveller of their right to be taken before the officer's
  senior officer and, if the traveller requests it, must take the
  traveller before the senior officer.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Senior officer's agreement

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} If the traveller is brought before a senior officer,
  the search is permitted only if that officer agrees that the
  preclearance officer or border services officer, as the case may be,
  is authorized under the applicable section to conduct the search.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Search by officer of same sex

- **[[26]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-26
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Neither a preclearance officer nor a
  border services officer is permitted to conduct a search under any of
  sections 21 to 23 of a person of the opposite sex. If no such officer
  of the same sex as the person is available, the officer may authorize
  any suitable person of the same sex to conduct the search.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Appointed observer

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} A preclearance officer or border services officer may
  authorize a person of the same sex as the traveller being searched to
  observe a search under any of sections 21 to 23 that is conducted by
  the officer.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Situation referred to in certain
provisions

**[[26.1]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-26.1 .sectionLabel}** Regardless of any
applicable recourse, a traveller may, in a prescribed manner, inform the
Canadian senior officials of the Preclearance Consultative Group
established under the Agreement of any situation referred to in sections
22, 23 and 24, subsection 31(2) and section 32 of this Act.

### [Preclearance Perimeter]{.HTitleText2} {#h-396431 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Traveller's obligations

- **[[27]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-27 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A
  traveller bound for the United States who is in a preclearance
  perimeter must comply with

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} any direction given to the traveller in accordance
    with this Act by a preclearance officer, police officer or border
    services officer; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} any requirement that is prescribed by regulation.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Obligations of other persons

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} A person, other than a traveller bound for the United
  States, who is in a preclearance perimeter must

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} as directed by a preclearance officer, identify
    themselves and state their reason for being in the preclearance
    perimeter; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} comply with any requirement that is prescribed by
    regulation.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Powers of preclearance officer

**[[28]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-28 .sectionLabel}** In a preclearance
perimeter, a preclearance officer may, for the purpose of conducting
preclearance or of maintaining the security of or control over the
border between Canada and the United States,

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} direct that a person identify themselves and state
  their reason for being in the preclearance perimeter;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} question a traveller bound for the United States;

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} direct a traveller bound for the United States to go
  to a preclearance area;

- [(d)]{.lawlabel} examine the exterior of a conveyance referred to in
  paragraph 6(2)(a);

- [(e)]{.lawlabel} examine, search and detain goods --- including by
  taking samples of the goods in reasonable amounts --- that are bound
  for the United States and that are to be loaded onto such a
  conveyance; and

- [(f)]{.lawlabel} return goods bound for the United States to a
  preclearance area, or direct a person with control of the goods to do
  so.

### [Withdrawal]{.HTitleText2} {#h-396449 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Traveller may withdraw from preclearance

**[[29]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-29 .sectionLabel}** Unless they are detained
under this Act, every traveller bound for the United States may withdraw
from preclearance and, subject to section 30, may leave a preclearance
area or preclearance perimeter without departing for the United States.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Traveller's obligations

**[[30]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-30 .sectionLabel}** A traveller who withdraws
from preclearance must

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} answer truthfully any question asked by a
  preclearance officer under paragraph 31(2)(b) for the purpose of
  identifying the traveller or of determining their reason for
  withdrawing; and

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} comply with any other direction given by a
  preclearance officer in accordance with subsection 31(2).

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}No preclearance on withdrawal

- **[[31]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-31 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A
  preclearance officer is, after a traveller has indicated that he or
  she is withdrawing from preclearance, permitted to exercise only the
  powers, and perform only the duties and functions, under this section
  and sections 13 to 15 and 32 with respect to the traveller.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Powers on withdrawal

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} A preclearance officer may, for the purpose of
  maintaining the security of or control over the border between Canada
  and the United States, exercise the following powers in a preclearance
  area or preclearance perimeter with respect to a traveller who is
  withdrawing from preclearance:

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} direct the traveller to identify themselves and to
    produce identification that contains their photograph and that is
    issued by the federal government, a provincial or local government
    or a foreign government;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} question the traveller for the purposes of
    identifying them or determining their reason for withdrawing;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} record and retain information obtained from the
    traveller under paragraph (a) or (b), including by making a copy of
    the traveller's identification;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} take and retain a photograph of the traveller, if
    the traveller has not produced identification that contains their
    photograph and allows their identity to be verified;

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} visually examine a conveyance used by the traveller
    and, if the conveyance transports goods on a commercial basis, open
    its cargo compartments to visually examine the contents;

  - [(f)]{.lawlabel} examine, using means or devices that are minimally
    intrusive, a conveyance used by the traveller, without opening or
    entering it, if the preclearance officer has reasonable grounds to
    suspect that the traveller could compromise the security of or
    control over the border.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Limitation

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} A preclearance officer may exercise the powers set
  out in subsection (2) only to the extent that doing so would not
  unreasonably delay the traveller's withdrawal.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Powers --- suspected offence

- **[[32]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-32 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} If a
  preclearance officer has reasonable grounds to suspect that a
  traveller who is withdrawing from preclearance has committed an
  offence under an Act of Parliament, the officer may, for the purpose
  of maintaining the security of or control over the border, exercise
  any of the following powers in a preclearance area or preclearance
  perimeter:

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} direct the traveller to identify themselves and to
    produce identification that contains their photograph and that is
    issued by the federal government, a provincial or local government
    or a foreign government;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} take and retain a photograph of the traveller, if
    the traveller has not produced identification that contains their
    photograph and allows their identity to be verified;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} question the traveller;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} collect information from the traveller;

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} examine, search and detain goods in the traveller's
    possession or control, including by taking samples of the goods in
    reasonable amounts;

  - [(f)]{.lawlabel} conduct a frisk search of the traveller, if the
    officer also has reasonable grounds to suspect that the traveller
    has on their person concealed goods; and

  - [(g)]{.lawlabel} detain the traveller for the purpose of a strip
    search, if the officer also has reasonable grounds to suspect that

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} the traveller has on their person concealed goods
      or anything that would present a danger to human life or safety,
      and

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} the search is necessary for the purpose of
      maintaining the security of or control over the border.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Strip search

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} A strip search referred to in paragraph (1)(g) may be
  conducted only in accordance with subsections 22(2) to (5) and
  sections 25 and 26, with the reference to "paragraphs (1)(a) and (b)"
  in subsection 22(4) to be read as a reference to "subparagraphs
  32(1)(g)(i) and (ii)".

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Detention of traveller

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} The exercise of any of the powers set out in
  subsection (1) with respect to a traveller constitutes a detention of
  the traveller.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Limitation re information

- **[[33]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-33 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} No
  person is permitted to disclose or use information obtained from a
  traveller after their withdrawal from preclearance except for the
  purpose of maintaining the security of or control over the border
  between Canada and the United States or as otherwise authorized by
  law.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Limitation re biometric information

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} No person is permitted to

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} collect any biometric information in respect of a
    traveller after the traveller has indicated that they are
    withdrawing from preclearance;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} use a photograph of the traveller obtained under
    paragraph 31(2)(d) or 32(1)(b) to produce biometric information; or

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} disclose such a photograph for the purpose of
    producing biometric information.

### [Seizure and Forfeiture]{.HTitleText2} {#h-396493 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Preclearance officer

- **[[34]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-34
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Subject to subsection (2), in a
  preclearance area or preclearance perimeter, a preclearance officer
  may seize, including as forfeit, or accept if they are abandoned,
  goods that are detained by a preclearance officer under paragraph
  20(1)(c) or 28(e) to the extent, and in a manner, that is consistent
  with the laws of the United States on importation of goods,
  immigration, agriculture and public health and safety.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Transfer to Canadian officer

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} If the Minister has provided notice that the
  importation into Canada, exportation from Canada, possession or
  handling of goods of the type that are detained contravenes Canadian
  law or if the preclearance officer suspects that the detained goods
  constitute evidence of an offence under Canadian law, the preclearance
  officer must transfer the detained goods to a person who is prescribed
  by regulation, if the goods are prescribed by regulation, or, if the
  goods are not so prescribed, to a police officer or border services
  officer.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Return of goods to preclearance officer

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} If a person or officer to whom goods are transferred
  under subsection (2) subsequently returns the goods to a preclearance
  officer, the preclearance officer may seize them, including as
  forfeit, or accept them if they have been abandoned.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Return or disposal of goods

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} Subject to any regulations, a preclearance officer
  may, to the extent and in a manner that is consistent with the laws
  referred to in subsection (1), return any goods that they have seized
  or dispose of any goods that they have seized or accepted.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Power to detain

### [Offences and Punishment]{.HTitleText2} {#h-396521 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}False or deceptive statements

- **[[37]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-37 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Every
  person who makes an oral or written statement to a preclearance
  officer, police officer or border services officer, with respect to
  the preclearance of a person or any goods, that the person knows to be
  false or deceptive or to contain information that the person knows to
  be false or deceptive is guilty of an offence punishable on summary
  conviction and is liable to a maximum fine of \$5,000.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}No imprisonment

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Despite subsection 787(2) of the [Criminal
  Code](/eng/acts/C-46), a term of imprisonment is not to be imposed for
  default of payment of a fine imposed under subsection (1).

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Obstruction of officer

**[[38]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-38 .sectionLabel}** Every person who resists
or wilfully obstructs a preclearance officer, police officer or border
services officer in the exercise of the officer's powers or the
performance of their duties and functions under this Part, or a person
lawfully acting in aid of such an officer,

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} is guilty of an indictable offence and liable to
  imprisonment for a term of not more than two years; or

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} is guilty of an offence punishable on summary
  conviction.

### [Civil Liability and Immunity]{.HTitleText2} {#h-396531 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Claim against the United States

- **[[39]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-39 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} An
  action or other proceeding of a civil nature, in which the United
  States is not immune under the [State Immunity Act](/eng/acts/S-18)
  from the jurisdiction of a court in Canada, may be brought against the
  United States in respect of anything that is, or is purported to be,
  done or omitted by a preclearance officer in the exercise of their
  powers or the performance of their duties and functions under this
  Part.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Preclearance officer's immunity

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} No action or other proceeding of a civil nature may
  be brought against a preclearance officer in respect of anything that
  is done or omitted in the exercise of their powers or the performance
  of their duties and functions under this Part.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}For greater certainty

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} For greater certainty, a preclearance officer is not
  a servant of the Crown for the purposes of the [Crown Liability and
  Proceedings Act](/eng/acts/C-50).

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Decision not reviewable

**[[40]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-40 .sectionLabel}** No decision of a
preclearance officer to refuse to conduct preclearance, or to refuse the
admission of persons or goods into the United States in accordance with
the laws of the United States, is subject to judicial review in Canada.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Reciprocity

**[[41]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-41 .sectionLabel}** The Governor in Council
may, on the recommendation of the Minister, by order, restrict any
immunity or privileges under section 39 or 40 with respect to an action
or other proceeding of a civil nature if, in the opinion of the Governor
in Council, the immunity or privileges exceed those accorded to Canada
by the United States.

### [Limitation on Requests for Extradition or Provisional Arrest]{.HTitleText2} {#h-396543 .Subheading}

## [PART 2]{.HLabel1}[Preclearance by Canada in the United States]{.HTitleText1} {#h-396561 .Part}

### [Definitions]{.HTitleText2} {#h-396562 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Definitions

**[[46]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-46 .sectionLabel}** The following definitions
apply in this Part.

[[border services officer]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[border services officer]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means a person who
    is assigned to conduct preclearance in the United States and is

    - [(a)]{.lawlabel} employed by the Canada Border Services Agency in
      the administration or enforcement of the [Customs
      Act](/eng/acts/C-52.6), the [Customs Tariff](/eng/acts/C-54.011)
      or the [Special Import Measures Act](/eng/acts/S-15);

    - [(b)]{.lawlabel} designated under subsection 9(2) of the [Canada
      Border Services Agency Act](/eng/acts/C-1.4); or

    - [(c)]{.lawlabel} designated or authorized under section 6 of the
      [Immigration and Refugee Protection Act](/eng/acts/I-2.5). ([agent
      des services frontaliers]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

[[other public officer]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[other public officer]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means a person, other
    than a border services officer, who is designated or authorized to
    exercise powers or perform duties or functions under preclearance
    legislation described in paragraph (a) of the definition of that
    expression in this section, and who is assigned to conduct
    preclearance in the United States. ([autre
    fonctionnaire]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

[[preclearance]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[preclearance]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means the exercise of powers
    and the performance of duties and functions by a border services
    officer or other public officer under sections 47 to 51 and
    regulations made under paragraph
    57(1)(a). ([précontrôle]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

[[preclearance area]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[preclearance area]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means an area in the
    United States that is

    - [(a)]{.lawlabel} designated by the Government of the United States
      as a preclearance area under the Agreement; and

    - [(b)]{.lawlabel} designated as a customs office under section 5 of
      the [Customs Act](/eng/acts/C-52.6). ([zone de
      précontrôle]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

[[preclearance legislation]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[preclearance legislation]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means

    - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the provisions --- of an Act of Parliament other
      than this Act, or of regulations made under such an Act --- that
      apply in respect of the entry of persons or the importation of
      goods into Canada, including provisions relating to customs,
      agriculture or public health and safety; and

    - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the provisions --- of an Act of a provincial
      legislature, or of regulations made under such an Act --- that
      authorize the collection by a border services officer of taxes,
      fees, mark-ups or other amounts.

    It does not include any provision that creates an offence or any
    provision of the [Immigration and Refugee Protection
    Act](/eng/acts/I-2.5) or its regulations. ([législation relative au
    précontrôle]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

[[preclearance perimeter]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[preclearance perimeter]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means an area in the
    United States that is

    - [(a)]{.lawlabel} designated by the Government of the United States
      as a preclearance perimeter under the Agreement; and

    - [(b)]{.lawlabel} designated as a customs office under section 5 of
      the [Customs Act](/eng/acts/C-52.6). ([périmètre de
      précontrôle]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

### [Preclearance]{.HTitleText2} {#h-396581 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Application of preclearance legislation

- **[[47]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-47
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Subject to the regulations,
  preclearance legislation applies to a traveller and goods bound for
  Canada in a preclearance area or preclearance perimeter as if the
  traveller had entered, and the goods had been imported into, Canada.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Powers, duties and functions

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Subject to section 49 and the regulations, a border
  services officer or other public officer may, in a preclearance area
  or preclearance perimeter, exercise the powers and perform the duties
  and functions that are conferred on the officer under preclearance
  legislation with respect to travellers and goods bound for Canada, as
  if the officer were in Canada.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Refusal

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} A border services officer or other public officer
  may, in a preclearance area or preclearance perimeter, refuse to
  permit travellers to proceed, or goods to be taken, to Canada through
  the preclearance area or preclearance perimeter if the entry of the
  traveller, or the importation of the goods, into Canada does not
  comply with preclearance legislation.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}For greater certainty

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} For greater certainty, a border services officer or
  other public officer in a preclearance area or preclearance perimeter
  may, to the extent they are permitted to do so under the powers,
  duties and functions that are conferred on them under preclearance
  legislation,

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} subject to subsection 49(4), impose an
    administrative monetary penalty or other civil sanction in
    accordance with preclearance legislation; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} collect any amounts --- including duties, taxes and
    fees --- owing under preclearance legislation.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Sections 20 and 21 of [Customs
  Act](/eng/acts/C-52.6)

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} For the purposes of sections 20 and 21 of the
  [Customs Act](/eng/acts/C-52.6), [[goods]{.dfn}]{.DefinitionRef}, as
  defined in section 2 of that Act, are to be treated as if they have
  been transported entirely within Canada if they are transported from a
  preclearance area or preclearance perimeter to a place in Canada over
  territory or waters outside Canada.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Application of [Immigration and Refugee
Protection Act](/eng/acts/I-2.5)

- **[[48]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-48 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} For
  greater certainty, a traveller in a preclearance area or preclearance
  perimeter who is seeking to enter Canada is, for the purposes of the
  [Immigration and Refugee Protection Act](/eng/acts/I-2.5), outside
  Canada.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}No claim for refugee protection

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} No claim for refugee protection under section 99 of
  the [Immigration and Refugee Protection Act](/eng/acts/I-2.5) may be
  made in a preclearance area or preclearance perimeter.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Powers, duties and functions

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} Subject to section 49 and the regulations, a border
  services officer may, in a preclearance area or preclearance
  perimeter, exercise the powers and perform the duties and functions
  specified in their designation or authorization under section 6 of the
  [Immigration and Refugee Protection Act](/eng/acts/I-2.5) with respect
  to travellers bound for Canada.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Preparation of
  report --- inadmissibility

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} A border services officer may prepare a report
  setting out the relevant facts, and transmit it to the Minister of
  Public Safety and Emergency Preparedness, if the officer is of the
  opinion that a foreign national or permanent resident in a
  preclearance area or preclearance perimeter is inadmissible on any
  ground of inadmissibility that is set out in the [Immigration and
  Refugee Protection Act](/eng/acts/I-2.5) and that is prescribed by
  regulations made under subsection (7).

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Refusal to permit entrance

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} If the Minister of Public Safety and Emergency
  Preparedness is of the opinion that the report is well-founded, he or
  she may refuse to permit the foreign national --- or, despite
  subsections 19(2) and 27(1) of the [Immigration and Refugee Protection
  Act](/eng/acts/I-2.5), the permanent resident --- from entering Canada
  through the preclearance area or preclearance perimeter.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Delegation of powers

  [(6)]{.lawlabel} Anything that may be done by the Minister of Public
  Safety and Emergency Preparedness under subsection (5) may be done by
  a border services officer who is so authorized in writing by that
  Minister, without proof of the authenticity of the authorization.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Regulations

  [(7)]{.lawlabel} The Governor in Council may make regulations
  prescribing grounds of inadmissibility that are set out in the
  [Immigration and Refugee Protection Act](/eng/acts/I-2.5) for the
  purpose of subsection (4). The regulations may distinguish between
  foreign nationals and permanent residents and between classes of
  foreign nationals.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Definitions

  [(8)]{.lawlabel} In this section, [[foreign
  national]{.dfn}]{.DefinitionRef} and [[permanent
  resident]{.dfn}]{.DefinitionRef} have the same meanings as in
  subsection 2(1) of the [Immigration and Refugee Protection
  Act](/eng/acts/I-2.5).

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Limitation

- **[[49]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-49 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A
  border services officer or other public officer is not permitted, in a
  preclearance area or preclearance perimeter, to exercise any powers of
  questioning or interrogation, examination, search, seizure,
  forfeiture, detention or arrest, except to the extent that such powers
  are conferred on the officer by the laws of the United States.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Exception

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Subsection (1) does not apply to an examination
  within the meaning of the [Immigration and Refugee Protection
  Act](/eng/acts/I-2.5).

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Restriction

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} The authority that may designate or authorize a
  border services officer or other public officer to exercise powers, or
  perform duties or functions, under preclearance legislation or the
  [Immigration and Refugee Protection Act](/eng/acts/I-2.5) may restrict
  or exclude the exercise of a power, or the performance of a duty or
  function, in a preclearance area or preclearance perimeter by the
  officer or by a class of border service officers or other public
  officers.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}No penalty or sanction --- United
  States prosecution

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} No administrative monetary penalty or other civil
  sanction may be imposed with respect to an act or omission committed
  by a person in a preclearance area or preclearance perimeter if a
  prosecution for an offence is instituted in the United States with
  respect to the act or omission that would give rise to it. If such a
  prosecution is instituted after a penalty or sanction has been
  imposed, the penalty or sanction is a nullity and any payment in
  respect of it must be reimbursed.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Proceeds of crime --- reporting

**[[50]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-50 .sectionLabel}** If a border services
officer transfers, to a United States federal, state, tribal or local
law enforcement agent, currency or a monetary instrument that was
retained in a preclearance area or preclearance perimeter as a result of
the application of the [Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and
Terrorist Financing Act](/eng/acts/P-24.501), the officer must, without
delay, report the circumstances of the transfer to the President of the
Canada Border Services Agency and to the Financial Transactions and
Reports Analysis Centre of Canada. The officer's report must be provided
in the form and manner that are specified by the Centre.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Detained goods

**[[51]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-51 .sectionLabel}** If goods are detained
under the laws of the United States by a border services officer or
other public officer in the course of preclearance, a border services
officer or other public officer may, to the extent permitted by the laws
of the United States, deal with those goods as if they were detained
under the applicable preclearance legislation.

### [Detention and Delivery under Laws of the United States]{.HTitleText2} {#h-396625 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}For greater certainty

- **[[52]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-52 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} For
  greater certainty, section 19 and subsection 27(1) of the [Immigration
  and Refugee Protection Act](/eng/acts/I-2.5) do not operate so as to
  prevent a border services officer from, under the laws of the United
  States, detaining a person in a preclearance area or preclearance
  perimeter and delivering them into the custody of a United States
  federal, state, tribal or local law enforcement agent.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Information

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} If a person or goods are detained under the laws of
  the United States in a preclearance area or preclearance perimeter by
  a border services officer and delivered into the custody of a United
  States federal, state, tribal or local law enforcement agent, a border
  services officer may disclose to the agent any
  information --- including [[personal
  information]{.dfn}]{.DefinitionRef}, as defined in section 3 of the
  [Privacy Act](/eng/acts/P-21) --- that relates to the circumstances of
  that detention and delivery.

### [Exemption from Compliance on Entry into Canada]{.HTitleText2} {#h-396631 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Exempt from further compliance

- **[[53]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-53 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} If a
  traveller bound for Canada has fully met, in a preclearance area or
  preclearance perimeter, an obligation under preclearance legislation
  or under the [Immigration and Refugee Protection Act](/eng/acts/I-2.5)
  that applies on entry into Canada, the traveller is exempt from again
  complying with that obligation once they enter Canada, unless a border
  services officer or other public officer requires the traveller to do
  so.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Obligation under regulations

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} If a traveller bound for Canada has fully met, in a
  preclearance area or preclearance perimeter, an obligation under the
  regulations that is an adaptation of an equivalent obligation under
  preclearance legislation or under the [Immigration and Refugee
  Protection Act](/eng/acts/I-2.5) that applies on entry into Canada,
  the traveller is exempt from complying with that equivalent obligation
  once they enter Canada, unless a border services officer or other
  public officer requires the traveller to do so.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Officers

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} In this section, a reference to a "border services
  officer or other public officer" includes a reference to a person who
  is employed, designated or authorized as set out in the definition
  [[border services officer]{.dfn}]{.DefinitionRef} or [[other public
  officer]{.dfn}]{.DefinitionRef} in section 46 but who has not been
  assigned to conduct preclearance in the United States.

### [Withdrawal]{.HTitleText2} {#h-396639 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Traveller may withdraw from preclearance

- **[[54]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-54
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Despite any preclearance legislation
  and the [Immigration and Refugee Protection Act](/eng/acts/I-2.5),
  every traveller bound for Canada, unless they are detained, may
  withdraw from preclearance and may leave a preclearance area or
  preclearance perimeter without departing for Canada.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}For greater certainty

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} For greater certainty, despite any obligation that
  would otherwise apply under section 47, under the [Immigration and
  Refugee Protection Act](/eng/acts/I-2.5) or under the regulations, a
  traveller who withdraws from preclearance is not required to answer
  any question asked of them for the purpose of conducting preclearance.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Traveller's obligations

**[[55]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-55 .sectionLabel}** Subject to the laws of
the United States, a traveller who withdraws from preclearance must

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} answer truthfully any question asked by a border
  services officer for the purpose of identifying the traveller or of
  determining their reason for withdrawing; and

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} comply with any other direction made by a border
  services officer that such an officer is authorized to make under the
  laws of the United States when a traveller withdraws.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Powers, duties and functions on
withdrawal

**[[56]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-56 .sectionLabel}** After a traveller has
indicated that he or she is withdrawing from preclearance, a border
services officer or other public officer is not permitted to exercise
any powers, or perform any duties or functions, other than those
authorized under the laws of the United States when a traveller
withdraws.

### [Regulations]{.HTitleText2} {#h-396651 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Governor in Council

- **[[57]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-57 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  Governor in Council may make regulations

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} adapting, for the purpose of applying it in a
    preclearance area or preclearance perimeter,

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} any preclearance legislation described in
      paragraph (a) of the definition of that expression in section 46,
      or

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} any provision of the [Immigration and Refugee
      Protection Act](/eng/acts/I-2.5) or of regulations made under that
      Act; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} restricting or excluding the application of any
    such preclearance legislation or provision in a preclearance area or
    preclearance perimeter.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Different treatment

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The regulations made under subsection (1) may
  distinguish between individual preclearance areas or individual
  preclearance perimeters and between classes of such areas or
  perimeters.

### [Offences --- Acts or Omissions in Preclearance Area or Preclearance Perimeter]{.HTitleText2} {#h-396661 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Deemed commission in Canada

- **[[58]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-58 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A
  person is deemed to have committed an act or omission in Canada if the
  act or omission is committed in a preclearance area or preclearance
  perimeter and, had it been committed in Canada, would constitute

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} an offence, under any Act of Parliament other than
    the [Immigration and Refugee Protection Act](/eng/acts/I-2.5), that
    relates to the entry of persons or importation of goods into Canada;
    or

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} a conspiracy to commit, an attempt to commit, being
    an accessory after the fact in relation to, or any counselling in
    relation to, such an offence.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Element of offence --- entry or
  importation

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} For the purpose of determining whether an act or
  omission would constitute an offence referred to in paragraph (1)(a)
  or (b),

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} if an element of the offence is the entry of a
    person into Canada, a person who is a traveller bound for Canada and
    who enters a preclearance area or preclearance perimeter is
    considered to have entered Canada; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} if an element of the offence is the importation of
    goods into Canada, goods that are bound for Canada and that are
    brought into a preclearance area or preclearance perimeter are
    considered to have been imported into Canada.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Jurisdiction

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} If a person is alleged to have committed an act or
  omission that is deemed to have been committed in Canada under
  subsection (1), proceedings for an offence in respect of that act or
  omission may be commenced in any territorial division in Canada. The
  person may be tried and punished for that offence as if the offence
  had been committed in that territorial division.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Previously tried in the United States

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} If a person is alleged to have committed an act or
  omission that is deemed to have been committed in Canada under
  subsection (1) and they have been tried and dealt with in the United
  States for an offence in respect of the act or omission so that, if
  they had been tried and dealt with in Canada, they would be able to
  plead [autrefois acquit]{.otherLang lang="la"}, [autrefois
  convict]{.otherLang lang="la"} or pardon, they are deemed to have been
  so tried and dealt with in Canada.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}For greater certainty

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} For greater certainty, sections 135 and 136 of the
  [Immigration and Refugee Protection Act](/eng/acts/I-2.5) apply with
  respect to an act or omission committed in a preclearance area or
  preclearance perimeter.

### [Jurisdiction --- Offence by Canadian Officer]{.HTitleText2} {#h-396677 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Primary criminal jurisdiction

- **[[59]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-59 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  Attorney General of Canada is responsible for advising the Minister
  with respect to the exercise or waiver of primary criminal
  jurisdiction under Article X of the Agreement in relation to an act or
  omission committed in the United States by a border services officer
  or other public officer in the exercise of their powers, and
  performance of their duties and functions, under this Part.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Additional factors

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The Attorney General of Canada may, in providing
  advice with respect to the waiver of primary criminal jurisdiction
  under paragraphs 15 and 16 of Article X of the Agreement, consider the
  following factors in addition to those set out in that paragraph 16:

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the severity of the sentence a border services
    officer or other public officer accused of an offence is likely, if
    convicted, to receive in Canada as compared to the United States;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the impact with respect to evidence if proceedings
    are held in Canada as compared to the United States;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} the impact on witnesses if proceedings are held in
    Canada as compared to the United States;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} the jurisdiction that has the greater interest in
    prosecuting the border services officer or other public officer for
    the act or omission in question; and

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} any other factor that the Attorney General
    considers relevant.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Exclusive authority

**[[60]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-60 .sectionLabel}** The Attorney General of
Canada has exclusive authority to commence and conduct a prosecution or
other criminal proceedings with respect to an act or omission committed
in the United States by a border services officer or other public
officer in the exercise of their powers, and performance of their duties
and functions, under this Part, and for that purpose the Attorney
General of Canada may exercise any of the powers, or perform any of the
duties and functions, assigned under the [Criminal Code](/eng/acts/C-46)
to an Attorney General.
