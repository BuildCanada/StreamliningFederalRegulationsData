## Export and Import of Rough Diamonds Act {#export-and-import-of-rough-diamonds-act .Title-of-Act}

[S.C.]{.abbr title="Statutes of Canada"} 2002, c. 25
:::

Assented to 2002-12-12

An Act providing for controls on the export, import or transit across
Canada of rough diamonds and for a certification scheme for the export
of rough diamonds in order to meet Canada's obligations under the
Kimberley Process
::::

:::::::::::::::::::::: section
::: {.section .order}
###### Preamble {#preamble .MarginalNote}

WHEREAS the Kimberley Process establishes minimum requirements for an
international scheme of certification for rough diamonds with a view to
breaking the link between armed conflict and the trade in rough
diamonds;

AND WHEREAS Canada is a participant in the Kimberley Process;
:::

::: Enacts
NOW, THEREFORE, Her Majesty, by and with the advice and consent of the
Senate and House of Commons of Canada, enacts as follows:
:::

## [Short Title]{.HTitleText1} {#h-202530 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Short title

**[[1]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-1 .sectionLabel}** This Act may be cited as
the [Export and Import of Rough Diamonds Act](/eng/acts/E-18.5).

## [Interpretation]{.HTitleText1} {#h-202533 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Definitions

**[[2]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-2 .sectionLabel}** The following definitions
apply in this Act.

[[Canadian Certificate]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[Canadian Certificate]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means a Kimberley
    Process Certificate issued by the Minister under paragraph
    9(1)(a). ([certificat canadien]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

[[in transit]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[in transit]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}, in respect of rough diamonds,
    means from a place outside Canada in transit through Canada to a
    place outside Canada. ([en transit]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

[[Kimberley Process]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[Kimberley Process]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means the international
    understanding among participants that was recognized by Resolution
    55/56 adopted by the General Assembly of the United Nations on
    December 1, 2000, as that understanding is amended from time to
    time. ([Processus de Kimberley]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

[[Kimberley Process Certificate]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[Kimberley Process Certificate]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means a
    document, issued by a participant, that certifies that rough
    diamonds for export or import or in transit have been handled in a
    manner that meets the minimum requirements of the Kimberley
    Process. ([certificat du Processus de Kimberley]{.DefinedTermLink
    lang="fr"})

[[Minister]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[Minister]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means the Minister of Natural
    Resources. ([ministre]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

[[participant]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[participant]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means a state, international
    organization of states or dependent territory of a state, or a
    customs territory, named in the
    schedule. ([participant]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

[[resident of Canada]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[resident of Canada]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means an individual who
    ordinarily resides in Canada or a corporation that has its head
    office, or operates a branch office, in Canada. ([résident du
    Canada]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

[[rough diamond]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[rough diamond]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means a diamond that is
    unsorted, unworked or simply sawn, cleaved or bruted, and that falls
    under subheading 7102.10, 7102.21 or 7102.31 in the List of Tariff
    Provisions set out in the schedule to the Customs Tariff, but does
    not include diamonds that are of a class prescribed by
    regulation. ([diamant brut]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

## [Ministerial Powers]{.HTitleText1} {#h-202546 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Amendment of schedule

**[[3]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-3 .sectionLabel}** The Minister may, by order,
amend the schedule by adding the name of a state, international
organization of states or dependent territory of a state, or a customs
territory, that participates in the Kimberley Process or by deleting the
name of an entity that ceases to participate in that Process.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Disclosure

**[[4]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-4 .sectionLabel}** For the purpose of
administering this Act, the Minister may disclose any information
received in an application for a Canadian Certificate or gathered in the
course of an inspection under this Act if the Minister considers the
disclosure to be in the public interest, taking into account the
competitive position of the individual, corporation, partnership, trust,
organization or association of persons affected by the disclosure.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Statistics

**[[5]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-5 .sectionLabel}** The Minister may collect,
compile and use statistics respecting Canadian Certificates and
Kimberley Process Certificates accompanying imports into Canada for
analysis, study or exchange with other participants. The Minister may
also publish those statistics in any form and manner that the Minister
considers appropriate.

## [Exporting Rough Diamonds]{.HTitleText1} {#h-202562 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Requirements for exporting rough diamonds

- **[[8]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-8 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Every
  person who exports rough diamonds must ensure that, on export, they
  are in a container that meets the requirements of the regulations and
  are accompanied by a Canadian Certificate.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Rough diamonds to be parcelled
  separately

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Every person who exports rough diamonds must ensure
  that, on export, the rough diamonds in the container are not parcelled
  with diamonds excluded from the definition [[rough
  diamond]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} or with anything else.

- 2002, c. 25, s. 8
- 2005, c. 51, s. 3

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Issuance of Canadian Certificate

- **[[9]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-9 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} On
  receiving an application for a Canadian Certificate from a resident of
  Canada for the export of rough diamonds, the Minister must

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} if the application meets the requirements of the
    regulations and of subsection (2), issue a Canadian Certificate;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} if the application does not meet the requirements
    of the regulations, send a notice to the applicant containing
    written reasons for the deficiency; or

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} if the application does not meet the criteria in
    subsection (2), reject the application and send to the applicant
    written reasons for the rejection.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Criteria

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Before issuing a Canadian Certificate, the Minister
  must be satisfied that

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the export is to a participant;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the information contained in the application is
    accurate;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} the rough diamonds in respect of which the
    application is made originated in Canada, were extracted from
    mineral concentrates in Canada, were imported from a participant or
    were in Canada at the time of the coming into force of this section;
    and

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} the fees established by the regulations for the
    issuance of the certificate have been paid.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Notice of deficiency

**[[10]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-10 .sectionLabel}** If an applicant who
receives a notice under paragraph 9(1)(b) does not remedy the deficiency
within such time as the Minister considers reasonable, the Minister may
reject the application.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Replacement certificate

**[[11]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-11 .sectionLabel}** If any information
appearing on a Canadian Certificate is inaccurate or has changed, the
Minister may, on application by the holder of the certificate made in
accordance with the regulations, issue a replacement certificate.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Invalidation of Canadian Certificate

**[[12]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-12 .sectionLabel}** If the Minister
determines that information provided by an applicant in order to obtain
a Canadian Certificate, or information appearing on the certificate, is
inaccurate or has changed, the Minister may invalidate the certificate.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Reporting

- **[[13]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-13 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Every
  person who exports rough diamonds must report the export to the
  Minister in accordance with the regulations.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Points of exit

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Every person who exports rough diamonds must do so at
  a point of exit designated under the regulations, if any.

## [Importing Rough Diamonds]{.HTitleText1} {#h-202593 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Requirements for importing rough diamonds

- **[[14]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-14 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Every
  person who imports rough diamonds must ensure that, on import, they
  are in a container that meets the requirements of the regulations and
  are accompanied by a Kimberley Process Certificate that

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} was issued by a participant;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} has not been invalidated by the participant; and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} contains accurate information.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Rough diamonds to be parcelled
  separately

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Every person who imports rough diamonds must ensure
  that, on import, the rough diamonds in the container are not parcelled
  with diamonds excluded from the definition [[rough
  diamond]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} or with anything else.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Exception

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Point of entry

## [In-transit Rough Diamonds]{.HTitleText1} {#h-202616 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Seizure of in-transit rough diamonds

- **[[17]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-17 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} An
  investigator may seize in-transit rough diamonds if they are not
  accompanied by a Kimberley Process Certificate or are in a container
  that has been opened.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Return of in-transit rough diamonds

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The Minister may order in-transit rough diamonds
  accompanied by a Kimberley Process Certificate to be returned to the
  participant who issued the certificate if they arrive in Canada in a
  container that has been opened.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}No seizure in case of return

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} If the Minister orders their return, the rough
  diamonds may not be seized.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Interpretation

**[[18]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-18 .sectionLabel}** For the purposes of this
Act, in-transit rough diamonds are deemed not to be imported or
exported.

## [Inspections]{.HTitleText1} {#h-202626 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Powers

- **[[19]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-19 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} For
  the purpose of administering this Act, an inspector may

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} enter and inspect any place or conveyance, other
    than a dwelling-place, where the inspector has reason to believe
    that there are rough diamonds to which this Act applies or any
    record, book of account or other document or data relevant to the
    administration of this Act;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} open or cause to be opened any package or container
    that the inspector has reason to believe contains anything referred
    to in paragraph (a);

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} require any person to present anything referred to
    in paragraph (a) for inspection in the manner and under any
    conditions that the inspector considers necessary to carry out the
    inspection;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} require any person to present any document or thing
    that may serve to establish the identity of the person or the origin
    of the rough diamonds;

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} examine rough diamonds or any other things related
    to rough diamonds;

  - [(f)]{.lawlabel} examine any record, book of account or other
    document or data that the inspector has reason to believe contains
    information that is relevant to the administration of this Act and
    make copies of any of them; and

  - [(g)]{.lawlabel} take measurements of rough diamonds and conduct
    tests or analyses that do not affect their value.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Operation of computer and copying
  equipment

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} In exercising any of the powers referred to in
  subsection (1), an inspector may

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} use any computer or data processing system to
    examine any data contained in or available to the computer or
    system;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} reproduce any record from the data, in the form of
    a printout or other intelligible output, and remove the printout or
    other output for examination or copying; and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} use any equipment at the place to make copies of
    any data or any record, book of account or other document.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Stopping a conveyance

**[[20]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-20 .sectionLabel}** An inspector may stop a
conveyance or direct that it be moved to a place where an inspection may
be carried out if the inspector has reason to believe that it contains
rough diamonds to which this Act applies or any record, book of account
or other document or data that is relevant to the administration of this
Act.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Production of designation document

**[[21]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-21 .sectionLabel}** When exercising their
powers under this Act, inspectors must, on request, show their
designation document.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Duty to assist

**[[22]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-22 .sectionLabel}** The owner or person in
charge of a place or conveyance that is entered by an inspector and
every person found there must give the inspector all reasonable
assistance to enable the inspector to exercise his or her powers and
carry out his or her duties and provide the inspector with any
information relevant to the administration of this Act that the
inspector requires.

## [Disposition of Things Seized]{.HTitleText1} {#h-202653 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Notice of reason for seizure

**[[25]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-25 .sectionLabel}** An investigator or a
peace officer who seizes rough diamonds or other things must, as soon as
is practicable, advise their owner, or the person having the possession,
care or control of them at the time of the seizure, of the reason for
the seizure and that an application may be made for their return under
subsection 27(4).

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Storage or removal

**[[26]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-26 .sectionLabel}** The investigator or peace
officer may store the rough diamonds or other things seized at the place
where they were seized or remove them to a secure place for storage.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Duration of detention

- **[[27]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-27
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Subject to subsection (3), rough
  diamonds or other things seized may not be detained after

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} an investigator determines that they meet the
    requirements of this Act; or

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the expiry of a period of 180 days after the day of
    their seizure.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Return of rough diamonds if no
  proceedings

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} If no prosecution under this Act has been instituted
  on the expiry of the 180-day period, the rough diamonds or other
  things seized must be returned to their owner or the person having the
  possession, care or control of them at the time of their seizure.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Exception

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} If a prosecution under this Act is instituted, the
  rough diamonds and other things seized may be detained until the
  proceedings are concluded.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Application for return

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} If a prosecution under this Act is instituted and
  rough diamonds or other things have been seized but not forfeited,
  their owner or the person having the possession, care or control of
  them at the time of their seizure may apply to the court before which
  the proceedings are being held for an order that the rough diamonds or
  other things be returned.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Order

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} After hearing the application, the court may order
  the rough diamonds or other things seized to be returned if the court
  is satisfied that sufficient evidence exists or may reasonably be
  obtained without further detaining them.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Return of rough diamonds on acquittal

  [(6)]{.lawlabel} If the accused is acquitted, the court may order that
  the rough diamonds or other things seized be returned to their owner
  or the person having the possession, care or control of them at the
  time of their seizure.

## [Forfeiture]{.HTitleText1} {#h-202673 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Forfeiture of rough diamonds and other
things

**[[28]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-28 .sectionLabel}** If a person is convicted
of an offence under this Act, the convicting court may, on its own
motion or at the request of any party to the proceedings, in addition to
any punishment imposed, order the forfeiture to Her Majesty in right of
Canada of rough diamonds or other things seized, by means of or in
relation to which the offence was committed.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Consensual forfeiture

**[[29]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-29 .sectionLabel}** If the owner of rough
diamonds or other things seized consents at any time to their
forfeiture, they are forfeited to Her Majesty in right of Canada.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Forfeiture certificate

**[[30]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-30 .sectionLabel}** If rough diamonds or
other things are forfeited under this Act, the Minister shall provide
their former owner or the person having the possession, care or control
of them at the time they were seized with a forfeiture certificate.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Disposition of rough diamonds forfeited

**[[31]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-31 .sectionLabel}** Rough diamonds or other
things forfeited under this Act must be disposed of in the manner
prescribed by the regulations.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Return of things seized if no forfeiture

- **[[32]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-32 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} If
  rough diamonds or other things seized are not forfeited under this
  Act, they must be returned to their owner or the person having the
  possession, care or control of them at the time of their seizure.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Exception

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} If the owner of rough diamonds or other things seized
  or the person having the possession, care or control of them at the
  time of their seizure is convicted of an offence under this Act and a
  fine is imposed, the rough diamonds or other things may be retained
  until the fine is paid.

## [Liability for Costs]{.HTitleText1} {#h-202687 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Liability for costs

- **[[33]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-33
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Persons convicted of an offence
  under this Act are jointly and severally, or solidarily, liable for
  all the costs relating to the seizure, detention or forfeiture of
  rough diamonds or other things incurred by Her Majesty in right of
  Canada in excess of the net proceeds of disposition, if any.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Debt due to Her Majesty

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The costs referred to in subsection (1) are debts due
  to Her Majesty, and proceedings to recover them may be instituted in a
  court of competent jurisdiction at any time within five years after
  the time they were incurred.

## [Regulations]{.HTitleText1} {#h-202693 .Part}

- [(g)]{.lawlabel} respecting the disposition of rough diamonds or other

## [Offences and Punishment]{.HTitleText1} {#h-202710 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}False or misleading information, and
misrepresentation

**[[36]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-36 .sectionLabel}** No person shall wilfully
furnish any false or misleading information or knowingly make any
misrepresentation in an application for a Canadian Certificate or in
connection with any subsequent use of the certificate or the export or
disposition of the rough diamonds to which it relates.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Forging or altering a Canadian
Certificate

**[[37]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-37 .sectionLabel}** No person shall forge a
Canadian Certificate or erase or alter information appearing on one.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Transfer, sale, etc., of a Canadian
Certificate

**[[38]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-38 .sectionLabel}** No person shall transfer,
assign, give, barter or sell a Canadian Certificate if the person knows
or ought to know that it will be used for the export of rough diamonds
other than those in respect of which it was issued.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Failing to maintain or falsifying records

**[[39]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-39 .sectionLabel}** No person shall, in order
to avoid compliance with this Act,

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} destroy, alter, mutilate, secrete or dispose of
  records, books of account or other documents or data that they are
  required to keep under the regulations;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} make false or deceptive entries in those records,
  books, documents or data; or

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} omit a material particular from those records, books,
  documents or data.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Obstruction

**[[40]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-40 .sectionLabel}** No person shall interfere
with an inspector who is doing, or hinder or prevent an inspector from
doing, anything that the inspector is authorized to do under this Act.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Summary conviction offence

- **[[40.1]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-40.1
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Every person who contravenes
  subsection 13(1) or (2) or 16(1) or (2) or section 22 or 40 is guilty
  of an offence punishable on summary conviction.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Limitation period

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} A prosecution under subsection (1) may be instituted
  not later than three years after the time when the subject-matter of
  the complaint arose.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Punishment

- **[[41]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-41
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Subject to subsection (2), every
  person who contravenes section 8 or 14 or any of sections 36 to 39 is
  guilty of

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} an indictable offence and liable to a fine in an
    amount that is in the discretion of the court or to imprisonment for
    a term not exceeding 10 years, or to both; or

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} an offence punishable on summary conviction and
    liable to a fine not exceeding \$25,000 or to imprisonment for a
    term not exceeding 12 months, or to both.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Defence

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} No person may be convicted of an offence for
  contravening section 14 in relation to rough diamonds that the
  Minister has ordered returned under subsection 15(1).

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Limitation period

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} A prosecution under paragraph (1)(b) may be
  instituted not later than three years after the time when the
  subject-matter of the complaint arose.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Factors to be considered when imposing
  sentence

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} If an offender is convicted or discharged, under
  section 730 of the [Criminal Code](/eng/acts/C-46), of an offence
  under this Act, the court sentencing or discharging the offender
  shall, in addition to considering any other relevant factors, consider
  the nature and value of the exported or imported rough diamonds that
  are the subject-matter of the offence.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Additional fine

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} If a person has been convicted of an offence under
  this Act and the court is satisfied that monetary benefits accrued to
  the person as a result of the commission of the offence, the court may
  order the person to pay an additional fine in an amount equal to the
  court's determination of the amount of the monetary benefits.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Officers, etc., of corporations

**[[42]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-42 .sectionLabel}** If a corporation commits
an offence under this Act, any officer or director of the corporation
who directed, authorized, assented to, acquiesced in or participated in
the commission of the offence is a party to and guilty of the offence
and is liable on conviction to the punishment provided for the offence
whether or not the corporation has been prosecuted or convicted.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Applicant for Certificate for
non-resident

**[[43]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-43 .sectionLabel}** If a Canadian Certificate
is issued to a person who has applied for it for, on behalf of or for
the use of another person who is not a resident of Canada and that other
person commits an offence under this Act, the person who applied for the
certificate is, whether or not the non-resident person has been
prosecuted or convicted, guilty of the like offence and liable, on
conviction, to the punishment provided for the offence, on proof that
the act or omission constituting the offence took place with the
knowledge or consent of the person who applied for the certificate or
that the person who applied for it failed to exercise due diligence to
prevent the commission of the offence.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Venue

**[[44]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-44 .sectionLabel}** Any proceeding in respect
of an offence under this Act may be instituted, tried or determined in
the province where the offence was committed or in the province in which
the person charged with the offence is, resides or has an office or
place of business at the time the proceedings are instituted.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Evidence

- **[[45]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-45 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  original or a copy of a bill of lading, customs form, commercial
  invoice or other document, in this section called a "shipping
  document", is admissible in evidence in any prosecution under this Act
  in respect of rough diamonds if it appears from the shipping document
  that

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the rough diamonds were sent or shipped from Canada
    or came into Canada;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} a person, as shipper, consignor or consignee, sent
    or shipped the rough diamonds from Canada or brought the rough
    diamonds into Canada; or

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} the rough diamonds were sent or shipped to a
    particular destination or person.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Proof of the facts

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} In the absence of evidence to the contrary, a
  shipping document that is admissible in evidence under subsection (1)
  is proof of any of the facts set out in paragraph (1)(a), (b) or (c).

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Review

- **[[45.1]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-45.1
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Three years after its coming into
  force, the Minister shall cause a review of the provisions and
  operation of this Act to be undertaken.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Report

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The Minister shall, within six months after causing a
  review to be undertaken pursuant to subsection (1), submit a report on
  the review to Parliament. If Parliament is not then sitting, the
  Minister shall submit the report to Parliament within the first 15
  sitting days thereafter.
