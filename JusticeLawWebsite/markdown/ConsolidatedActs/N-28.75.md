## Nunavut Planning and Project Assessment Act {#nunavut-planning-and-project-assessment-act .Title-of-Act}

[S.C.]{.abbr title="Statutes of Canada"} 2013, c. 14, s. 2
:::

Assented to 2013-06-19

An Act respecting land use planning and the assessment of ecosystemic
and socio-economic impacts of projects in the Nunavut Settlement Area
and making consequential amendments to other Acts

::: ReaderNote
\[Enacted by section 2 of chapter 14 of the Statutes of Canada, 2013, in
force July 9, 2015, see SI/2015-58.\]
:::
:::::

:::::::::::::::::::::::::: section
::: {.section .order}
###### Preamble {#preamble .MarginalNote}

Whereas Her Majesty the Queen in right of Canada and the Inuit of the
Nunavut Settlement Area have entered into a land claims agreement that
came into force on July 9, 1993 on its ratification by both parties;

And whereas the Nunavut Planning Commission and the Nunavut Impact
Review Board were established under that agreement, which provides that
the substantive powers, functions, duties and objectives of those
institutions of public government must be set out in statute;

And whereas it is desirable to set out a regime for land use planning
and project assessment that recognizes the importance of responsible
economic development and conservation and protection of the ecosystems
and that encourages the well-being and self-reliance of the Inuit and
other residents of the designated area, taking into account the
interests of all Canadians;
:::

::: Enacts
Now, therefore, Her Majesty, by and with the advice and consent of the
Senate and House of Commons of Canada, enacts as follows:
:::

## [Application]{.HTitleText1} {#h-370550 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Geographic application

- **[[5]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-5 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} This Act
  applies to the designated area.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Application outside designated area

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} This Act also applies to projects to be carried out
  wholly or partly outside the designated area and to impacts outside
  that area to the extent necessary to give effect to sections 80, 98,
  113, 133, 156 to 162, 168 and 185 to 187.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Binding on Her Majesty

**[[6]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-6 .sectionLabel}** This Act is binding on Her
Majesty in right of Canada or a province.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}[Impact Assessment Act](/eng/acts/I-2.75)

**[[7]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-7 .sectionLabel}** The [Impact Assessment
Act](/eng/acts/I-2.75) does not apply in respect of the designated area.

## [Consultation]{.HTitleText1} {#h-370560 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Amendments to this Act

**[[8]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-8 .sectionLabel}** The federal Minister must
consult closely with the territorial Minister, the designated Inuit
organization, the Commission and the Board with respect to any amendment
to this Act.

## [Delegation]{.HTitleText1} {#h-370563 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Delegation to territorial Minister

- **[[9]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-9 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  federal Minister may delegate, in writing, to the territorial Minister
  any of the federal Minister's powers, duties or functions under this
  Act, either generally or as otherwise provided in the instrument of
  delegation. However, the delegation must not abrogate or derogate from
  any Inuit rights under the Agreement.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Notice

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The federal Minister must notify the designated Inuit
  organization in writing of any delegation made under subsection (1).

## [PART 1]{.HLabel1}[Commission and Board]{.HTitleText1} {#h-370569 .Part}

### [Nunavut Planning Commission]{.HTitleText2} {#h-370570 .Subheading}

#### [Constitution]{.HTitleText3} {#h-370571 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Continuance

**[[10]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-10 .sectionLabel}** The Nunavut Planning
Commission, established under the Agreement, is continued under this
Act.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Members

- **[[11]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-11 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  federal Minister must appoint the members of the Commission, including
  the Chairperson.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Composition

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The following rules apply in respect of the
  appointment of members of the Commission, other than the Chairperson:

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} at least one member must be appointed on the
    nomination of the federal Minister;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} at least one member must be appointed on the
    nomination of the territorial Minister; and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} one half of the members must be appointed on the
    nomination of the organization referred to in paragraph (a) of the
    definition [[designated Inuit organization]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} in
    subsection 2(1).

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Substitution

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} The organization referred to in paragraph (a) of the
  definition [[designated Inuit organization]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} in
  subsection 2(1) may, for the purpose of ensuring appropriate
  representation from any planning region in the preparation or
  amendment of a land use plan, nominate one or more persons to act in
  the place of an equivalent number of members appointed under paragraph
  (2)(c). The person or persons appointed by the federal Minister on the
  nomination of that organization act in the place of the member or
  members identified by the organization.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Areas of equal use and occupancy

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} If the Commission is called on to make a decision
  under Part 3 in respect of a project to be carried out in an area of
  equal use and occupancy, Makivik may nominate a number of persons
  equal to one half the number of members appointed under paragraph
  (2)(c). The person or persons appointed by the federal Minister on the
  nomination of Makivik act in the place of an equivalent number of
  members appointed under paragraph (2)(c) and identified by the
  organization referred to in paragraph (a) of the definition
  [[designated Inuit organization]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} in subsection
  2(1).

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Residence requirement

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} At least one half of the members appointed under
  subsection (2) must reside in the designated area.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Persons not eligible for appointment

  [(6)]{.lawlabel} Employees of a department or agency are not eligible
  to be members of the Commission.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Chairperson

- **[[12]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-12 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} After
  consultation with the territorial Minister, the Chairperson of the
  Commission is to be appointed from among the persons who are nominated
  by the Commission.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Appointment of another member

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} If a member of the Commission is appointed
  Chairperson, the federal Minister must appoint another person to be a
  member of the Commission in accordance with section 11.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Acting after expiry of term

**[[13]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-13 .sectionLabel}** If a Commission member's
term expires before the review of a project by the Commission is
complete, the member continues to act as a member in relation to that
project until that review is complete. The Chairperson of the Commission
must notify the federal Minister in writing of any member acting under
this section.

#### [Powers, Duties and Functions]{.HTitleText3} {#h-370597 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Additional powers, duties and functions

**[[14]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-14 .sectionLabel}** In addition to its
powers, duties and functions specified elsewhere in this Act, the
Commission must

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} monitor projects approved under Part 3 to verify that
  they are carried out in conformity with any applicable land use plan;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} report annually in writing to the federal Minister,
  the territorial Minister and the designated Inuit organization on the
  implementation of the land use plan;

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} contribute to the development and review of marine
  policy in the Arctic;

- [(d)]{.lawlabel} exercise the powers and perform the duties and
  functions referred to in section 11.9.1 of the Agreement in relation
  to the cleanup of waste sites; and

- [(e)]{.lawlabel} exercise any powers and perform any duties and
  functions that may be agreed on by the Government of Canada or the
  Government of Nunavut, or both, taking into account their respective
  jurisdictions, and the designated Inuit organization.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Principles --- section 11.2.1 of
Agreement

**[[15]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-15 .sectionLabel}** The Commission must
exercise its powers and perform its duties and functions with a view to
fulfilling the objectives of the Agreement in relation to land use
planning in accordance with the principles referred to in section 11.2.1
of the Agreement.

#### [Meetings]{.HTitleText3} {#h-370607 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Participation by telecommunications

**[[16]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-16 .sectionLabel}** Subject to the
Commission's by-laws and rules, a member of the Commission may
participate in a meeting by means of telephone or other communications
facilities that are likely to enable all persons participating in the
meeting to hear each other, and a member so participating is deemed for
all purposes of this Act to be present at that meeting.

#### [By-laws and Rules]{.HTitleText3} {#h-370610 .Subheading}

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}[Statutory Instruments

### [Nunavut Impact Review Board]{.HTitleText2} {#h-370624 .Subheading}

#### [Constitution]{.HTitleText3} {#h-370625 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Continuance

**[[18]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-18 .sectionLabel}** The Nunavut Impact Review
Board, established under the Agreement, is continued under this Act.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Members

- **[[19]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-19 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  Board consists of nine members, including the Chairperson.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Composition

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The members of the Board, other than the Chairperson,
  must be appointed as follows:

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} two members must be appointed by the federal
    Minister;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} four members must be appointed by the federal
    Minister on the nomination of the organization referred to in
    paragraph (a) of the definition [[designated Inuit
    organization]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} in subsection 2(1);

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} one member must be appointed by the territorial
    Minister; and

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} one member must be appointed by one or more
    territorial ministers.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Additional members

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} Despite subsection (1), additional members may be
  appointed for a specific purpose in the manner and in the proportions
  set out in subsection (2).

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Areas of equal use and occupancy

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} If the Board is called on to make a decision under
  Part 3 in respect of a project to be carried out in an area of equal
  use and occupancy, Makivik may nominate a number of persons equal to
  one half the number of members appointed under paragraph (2)(b). The
  person or persons appointed by the federal Minister on the nomination
  of Makivik act in the place of an equivalent number of members
  appointed under paragraph (2)(b) and identified by the organization
  referred to in paragraph (a) of the definition [[designated Inuit
  organization]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} in subsection 2(1).

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Chairperson

- **[[20]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-20 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} After
  consulting with the territorial Minister, the federal Minister must
  appoint a Chairperson of the Board from among the persons nominated by
  the other members of the Board. If there are equally qualified
  nominees, the federal Minister must give preference to the nominees
  who reside in the designated area.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Appointment of another member

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} If a member of the Board, appointed under any of
  paragraphs 19(2)(a) to (d), is appointed Chairperson, the minister who
  appointed that member must appoint another person to be a member of
  the Board under that paragraph.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Acting after expiry of term

**[[21]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-21 .sectionLabel}** If a Board member's term
expires before the screening or review of a project by the Board is
complete, the member continues to act as a member in relation to that
project until the screening or review is complete. The Chairperson of
the Board must notify the federal Minister in writing of any member
acting under this section.

#### [Powers, Duties and Functions]{.HTitleText3} {#h-370648 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Additional powers, duties and functions

**[[22]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-22 .sectionLabel}** In addition to its
powers, duties and functions specified elsewhere in this Act, the Board
must exercise any powers and perform any duties and functions that may
be agreed on by the Government of Canada or the Government of Nunavut,
or both, taking into account their respective jurisdictions, and the
designated Inuit organization.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Primary objectives

- **[[23]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-23 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  Board must exercise its powers and perform its duties and functions in
  accordance with the following primary objectives:

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} to protect and promote the existing and future
    well-being of the residents and communities of the designated area;
    and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} to protect the ecosystemic integrity of the
    designated area.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Other residents

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} In exercising its powers or performing its duties and
  functions in accordance with the objective set out in paragraph
  (1)(a), the Board must take into account the well-being of residents
  of Canada outside the designated area.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Interpretation

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} For greater certainty, the Board must exercise its
  powers and perform its duties and functions under paragraphs 92(2)(a),
  104(1)(c) and 112(5)(b), section 124 and subsection 152(4) in
  accordance with the objectives set out in subsection (1).

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Limitation --- socio-economic benefits

**[[24]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-24 .sectionLabel}** The Board is not
authorized to establish, in the exercise of its powers or the
performance of its duties and functions, requirements relating to
socio-economic benefits.

#### [Meetings]{.HTitleText3} {#h-370662 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Nunavut Settlement Area

- **[[25]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-25 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  Board must, whenever practicable, hold its meetings within the Nunavut
  Settlement Area.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Participation by telecommunications

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} A member of the Board may, subject to the Board's
  by-laws and rules, participate in a meeting by means of telephone or
  other communications facilities that are likely to enable all persons
  participating in the meeting to hear each other, and a member so
  participating is deemed for all purposes of this Act to be present at
  that meeting.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Request by members for meeting

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} The Chairperson must call a meeting of the Board
  within 21 days after receiving a written request for a meeting,
  indicating its purpose, from at least five members.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Voting

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} Decisions of the Board must be made by a majority of
  votes, with each member, other than the Chairperson, having one vote.
  In the case of a tie vote, the Chairperson must cast the deciding
  vote.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Quorum

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} Five members of the Board constitute a quorum.

#### [By-laws and Rules]{.HTitleText3} {#h-370674 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Powers

- **[[26]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-26 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  Board may make by-laws and rules respecting the conduct and management
  of its business, including by-laws and rules respecting

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the calling of meetings of the Board and the
    conduct of business at them;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the establishment of special and standing
    committees and the fixing of quorum for meetings of those
    committees;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} procedures for making submissions and complaints to
    the Board;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} procedures and guidelines for collecting
    information and opinions, including procedures for the conduct of
    public hearings by the Board or one of its panels;

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} the establishment of guidelines for the preparation
    of impact statements;

  - [(f)]{.lawlabel} the establishment of guidelines respecting time
    limits for completing each step of a review that the Board carries
    out under Part 3 or 4; and

  - [(g)]{.lawlabel} the admissibility of evidence in public hearings
    conducted by the Board or one of its panels.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Inuit traditions

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} A by-law or rule made under paragraph (1)(d) must
  give due regard and weight to the Inuit traditions regarding oral
  communication and decision making.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Public hearings

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} By-laws and rules relating to the conduct of public
  hearings must

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} emphasize flexibility and informality to the extent
    that is consistent with the general application of the rules of
    procedural fairness and natural justice and in particular must
    allow, if appropriate, the admission of evidence that would not
    normally be admissible under the strict rules of evidence; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} with respect to any classification of intervenors,
    allow a designated Inuit organization full standing to appear at a
    public hearing for the purpose of making submissions on behalf of
    the people it represents.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}[Statutory Instruments
  Act](/eng/acts/S-22)

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} By-laws and rules made under this section are not

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Chairperson

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The Board must appoint a Chairperson for each panel.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Composition

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} In addition to the Chairperson, each panel must

### [General Provisions --- Commission and Board]{.HTitleText2} {#h-370701 .Subheading}

#### [Members]{.HTitleText3} {#h-370702 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Vacancies during term

**[[28]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-28 .sectionLabel}** If a vacancy occurs
during the term of a member of the Commission or the Board, other than a
member appointed under subsection 19(3), the minister who appointed the
member must appoint another member as soon as practicable in accordance
with section 11 or 19, as the case may be.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Term of office

- **[[29]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-29
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Members of the Commission and the
  Board, including the Chairpersons and any member appointed under

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Despite subsection (1), members appointed under

  for a term not exceeding three years.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Reappointment

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} A member is eligible to be reappointed to the
  Commission or the Board in the same or another capacity.

**[[30]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-30 .sectionLabel}** Before taking up their
duties, members of the Commission and the Board must take the oath of
office set out in Schedule 1 before a person who is authorized by law to

- **[[31]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-31 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Workers' compensation

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} A member is deemed to be an employee for the purposes

Inuit organization or the territorial Minister, the federal Minister may
remove the member only after consulting the designated Inuit
organization or the territorial minister, as the case may be.

- **[[38]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-38 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} publishing a notice in a newspaper or other

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} sending the proposed by-law or rule to the federal

    organization and the council of each municipality in the designated

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Comments invited

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The notice referred to in paragraph (1)(b) must
  include an invitation to interested persons, including corporations
  and other organizations, to make comments in writing to the Commission
  or the Board about the proposed by-law or rule within 60 days after
  publication of the notice.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Response to comments

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} The Commission or the Board may only make the by-law
  or rule if it has responded to any comments made within the time limit
  set out in subsection (2).

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Exception

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} Once a notice is published under subsection (1), the
  Commission or the Board is not required to publish any further notice
  about any amendment to the proposed by-law or rule that results from
  comments made by interested persons.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Notice

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} As soon as practicable after the by-law or rule has
  been made, the Commission or the Board must publish a notice that the
  by-law or rule has been made and is included in the public registry
  referred to in section 201 or 202, as the case may be, on its website,
  in the [Canada Gazette](http://www.gazette.gc.ca/) and in a newspaper
  or other periodical that, in its opinion, has a large circulation in
  the designated area.

## [PART 2]{.HLabel1}[Land Use Planning]{.HTitleText1} {#h-370787 .Part}

### [Interpretation]{.HTitleText2} {#h-370788 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Definition of
[[land]{.dfn}]{.DefinitionRef}

**[[40]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-40 .sectionLabel}** In this Part,
[[land]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} includes land covered by water, whether in
the onshore or offshore, waters and resources, including wildlife.

### [Policies, Priorities and Objectives]{.HTitleText2} {#h-370791 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Designated area

**[[41]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-41 .sectionLabel}** The Commission is
responsible for the establishment, in conjunction with the Government of
Canada or the Government of Nunavut, or both, taking into account their
respective jurisdictions, of broad planning policies, priorities and
objectives for the designated area regarding the conservation,
development, management and use of land.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Planning regions

- **[[42]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-42 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  Commission must identify planning regions and may, for each planning
  region, identify specific planning objectives and planning variables
  regarding the conservation, development, management and use of land.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Consistency

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The specific planning objectives must be consistent
  with the broad objectives established for the designated area.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Consultations

**[[43]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-43 .sectionLabel}** The Commission must seek
the opinions of affected municipalities, interested corporations and
organizations, residents and other interested persons regarding specific
objectives and land use planning options for the region.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Principles and factors

**[[44]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-44 .sectionLabel}** The principles and
factors set out in sections 11.2.1 and 11.2.3 of the Agreement must
guide the development of broad policies, priorities and objectives under
section 41 and specific planning objectives under section 42.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Public hearing

**[[45]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-45 .sectionLabel}** The Commission may, in
exercising its powers and performing its duties and functions under
sections 41 to 43, hold a public hearing in accordance with the by-laws
and rules made under section 17.

### [Land Use Plans]{.HTitleText2} {#h-370805 .Subheading}

#### [General Provisions]{.HTitleText3} {#h-370806 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Duty

- **[[46]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-46 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  Commission must exercise its powers and perform its duties and
  functions in relation to land use plan development under this Part so
  that the entire designated area is, as soon as practicable, subject to
  either

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} one land use plan for the entire designated area;
    or

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} two or more land use plans, each relating to one or
    more planning regions within the designated area.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}No overlap

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} For greater certainty, no portion of the designated
  area is to be subject to more than one land use plan.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Merger of plans

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} The Commission may merge the land use plans referred
  to in paragraph (1)(b) to form a land use plan referred to in
  paragraph (1)(a). If the substance of the land use plans being merged
  is not changed, such a merger is not an amendment and sections 59 to
  65 do not apply.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Purposes of plan

**[[47]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-47 .sectionLabel}** A land use plan has the
following purposes:

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} to protect and promote the existing and future
  well-being of the residents and communities of the designated area,
  taking into account the interests of all Canadians; and

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} to protect and, if necessary, restore the
  environmental integrity of the designated area or the planning region,
  as the case may be.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Contents of plan

- **[[48]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-48 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A land
  use plan must provide for the conservation and use of land and guide
  and direct resource use and development and must, in particular,
  provide for a strategy regarding the implementation of the plan and
  take into account

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the broad planning policies, priorities and
    objectives established for the designated area;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the specific planning objectives and planning
    variables identified for any applicable planning region;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} the factors referred to in section 11.3.1 of the
    Agreement; and

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} Inuit objectives for Inuit owned lands.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Permitted uses

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} A land use plan may contain descriptions of
  permitted, subject to any terms and conditions that the plan sets out,
  and prohibited uses of land.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Minor variances

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} A land use plan may authorize the Commission to grant
  minor variances and may set out the conditions subject to which they
  may be considered and granted.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Offences

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} A land use plan must identify each requirement set
  out in that plan whose contravention is prohibited under paragraph
  74(f).

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Articles 5 and 7 of Agreement

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} A land use plan must be developed and implemented in
  a manner that is consistent with the principles and requirements of
  Articles 5 and 7 of the Agreement.

#### [Development]{.HTitleText3} {#h-370835 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Preparation of draft

**[[49]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-49 .sectionLabel}** After concluding any
consultations that it considers appropriate, the Commission must prepare
a draft land use plan for the entire designated area or for one or more
planning regions.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Draft to be made public

- **[[50]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-50 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Before
  holding a public hearing in respect of a draft land use plan, the
  Commission must make the draft public and must do so in a manner
  designed to promote participation in its examination by the public.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Invitation to comment

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The Commission must solicit written and oral comments
  on the draft land use plan from appropriate departments or agencies,
  appropriate designated Inuit organizations, affected municipalities,
  interested corporations and organizations, Inuit and other residents
  of the designated area and the general public.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Public hearing

- **[[51]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-51 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} After
  allowing a reasonable period for the submission of comments on the
  draft land use plan, the Commission must hold a public hearing in
  respect of it.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Duty

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The Commission must take all necessary steps to
  promote public awareness of, and public participation in, the public
  hearing, including through the choice of the date, time and place of
  the hearing, notice given in relation to them and measures taken to
  disseminate any relevant information.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Conduct of hearing

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Effective date

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} A land use plan comes into effect when it is approved

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Publication

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} The Commission must make the land use plan public.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}[Statutory Instruments

### [Compliance]{.HTitleText2} {#h-370965 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Prohibitions

**[[74]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-74 .sectionLabel}** It is prohibited to carry
out a project, in whole or in part, if

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} a project proposal has not been submitted to the
  Commission in accordance with section 76;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} the assessment of the project under this Part has not
  been completed;

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} the assessment of the project has been terminated
  under subsection 141(2), 142(2), 143(4) or (6) or 144(3);

- [(d)]{.lawlabel} the Commission has determined, under section 77, that
  the project is not in conformity with any applicable land use plan and
  no minor variance or ministerial exemption has been granted under
  paragraph 81(2)(a) or 82(2)(a), as the case may be;

- [(e)]{.lawlabel} the responsible Minister has decided either that the
  project could be modified and an amended project proposal submitted to
  the Commission or that it is not to proceed;

- [(f)]{.lawlabel} the project is not carried out in accordance with any
  requirement identified, under subsection 48(4), in any applicable land
  use plan, other than a requirement in relation to which a minor
  variance or a ministerial exemption has been granted under paragraph
  81(2)(a) or 82(2)(a), as the case may be; or

- [(g)]{.lawlabel} the project is not carried out in accordance with the
  terms and conditions set out in the original or amended project
  certificate.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Regulatory authorities

- **[[75]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-75 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A
  regulatory authority is not authorized to issue a licence, permit or
  other authorization in respect of a project if

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} a project proposal has not been submitted to the
    Commission in accordance with section 76;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the assessment of the project under this Part has
    not been completed;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} the assessment of the project has been terminated
    under subsection 141(2), 142(2), 143(4) or (6) or 144(3);

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} the Commission has determined, under section 77,
    that the project is not in conformity with any applicable land use
    plan and no minor variance or ministerial exemption has been granted
    in respect of the project under paragraph 81(2)(a) or 82(2)(a), as
    the case may be; or

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} the responsible Minister has decided either that
    the project could be modified and an amended project proposal
    submitted to the Commission or that it is not to proceed.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}No force or effect --- licences, etc.

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} A licence, permit or other authorization issued in
  contravention of any of paragraphs (1)(a) to (e) is of no force or
  effect.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Modification not significant

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} A regulatory authority may issue any licence, permit

  that has not been significantly modified without a new assessment of

#### [Land Use Plan in Effect]{.HTitleText3} {#h-370998 .Subheading}

##### [Determination]{.HTitleText4} {#h-370999 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Conformity with plan

- **[[77]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-77 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  Commission must determine if a project is in conformity with the land
  use plan that is applicable to the place where the project is to be
  carried out.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Multiple plans

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} If different portions of the project are subject to
  different land use plans, the Commission must determine if each
  portion is in conformity with the land use plan applicable to it, and
  if one portion of the project is not in conformity with the land use
  plan applicable to it, the entire project is deemed to not be in
  conformity.

##### [Project in Conformity with Land Use Plan]{.HTitleText4} {#h-371005 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Verification --- screening

- **[[78]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-78 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} If the
  Commission determines that the project is in conformity with any
  applicable land use plan, it must verify whether the project is exempt
  from screening.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Exemption from screening

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} A project is exempt from screening if each work or
  activity that comprises the project belongs to a class of exempt works
  or activities set out in items 1 to 6 of Schedule 12-1 to the
  Agreement or in Schedule 3 and does not belong to a class of
  non-exempt works or activities prescribed by regulation.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Commission may consult Board

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} The Commission may request the Board's opinion as to
  whether a particular project is exempt from screening.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Project not exempt from screening

**[[79]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-79 .sectionLabel}** If a project is not
exempt from screening, the Commission must send the project proposal to
the Board in order for it to conduct a screening.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Project exempt from screening

- **[[80]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-80 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} If a
  project is exempt from screening and the Commission has concerns in
  respect of any cumulative ecosystemic and socio-economic impacts that
  could result from the impacts of the project combined with those of
  any other project that has been carried out, is being carried out or
  is likely to be carried out inside the designated area, or wholly or
  partly outside the designated area, it must send the project proposal
  to the Board in order for the Board to conduct a screening of the
  project.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}No concerns --- cumulative impacts

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} If a project is exempt from screening and the
  Commission does not have concerns in respect of the cumulative impacts
  referred to in subsection (1), it must indicate in the decision that
  the assessment of the project has been completed and that the
  proponent may carry out the project, subject to paragraph 74(f) and to
  obtaining any licence, permit or other authorization required by or
  under any other Act of Parliament or any territorial law and complying
  with any other requirements set out in such an Act or law.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Location of impacts

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} In performing its functions under subsections (1) and
  (2), the Commission must consider impacts both inside and outside the
  designated area.

##### [Project Not in Conformity with Land Use Plan]{.HTitleText4} {#h-371022 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Minor variance

- **[[81]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-81 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} If the
  Commission determines that the project is not in conformity with an
  applicable land use plan, it must verify whether that land use plan
  authorizes it to grant a minor variance with respect to such a project
  and whether the conditions set out under subsection 48(3), if any, are
  met.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Minor variance authorized

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} If the land use plan authorizes the granting of minor
  variances and if the conditions, if any, are met, the Commission may,
  within 20 days after its determination that the project is not in
  conformity with the plan,

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} grant a minor variance, in which case it must
    verify whether the project is exempt from screening and comply with
    the requirements of section 79 or 80, as the case may be; or

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} refuse to grant a minor variance.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Publication

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} Before granting a minor variance under paragraph
  (2)(a), the Commission must make the proposed minor variance public
  and must do so in a manner designed to promote participation in its
  examination by the public.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Objection

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} Any interested person may, within 10 days after the
  proposed minor variance is made public, indicate to the Commission in
  writing that the proposed minor variance should not be granted because

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the land use plan does not authorize the granting
    of the minor variance;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the conditions subject to which a minor variance
    may be granted are not met; or

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} the minor variance is not appropriate, in their
    opinion, for any other reason that they specify.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Reasons taken into account and public
  review

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} The Commission may only grant a minor variance under
  paragraph (2)(a) after taking into account any reasons for which an
  interested person has indicated, under subsection (4), that it should
  not be granted and, if it considers it appropriate to do so,
  conducting a public review in accordance with the by-laws and rules
  made under section 17 and taking into account any submissions made
  during that review.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Extension of time limit

  [(6)]{.lawlabel} If the Commission is of the opinion that more time is
  needed to make a decision under subsection (2), it may extend the
  period referred to in that subsection by up to 10 days and must notify

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Purpose of screening

**[[88]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-88 .sectionLabel}** The purpose of screening
a project is to determine whether the project has the potential to
result in significant ecosystemic or socio-economic impacts and,
accordingly, whether it requires a review by the Board or by a federal
environmental assessment panel, as the case may be.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Project to be reviewed

- **[[89]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-89 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  Board must be guided by the following considerations when it is called
  on to determine, on the completion of a screening, whether a review of
  the project is required:

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} a review is required if, in the Board's opinion,

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} the project may have significant adverse
      ecosystemic or socio-economic impacts or significant adverse
      impacts on wildlife habitat or Inuit harvest activities,

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} the project will cause significant public
      concern, or

    - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} the project involves technological innovations,
      the effects of which are unknown; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} a review is not required if, in the Board's
    opinion,

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} the project is unlikely to cause significant
      public concern, and

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} its adverse ecosystemic and socio-economic
      impacts are unlikely to be significant, or are highly predictable
      and can be adequately mitigated by known technologies.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Prevailing considerations

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The considerations set out in paragraph (1)(a)
  prevail over those set out in paragraph (1)(b).

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} In subparagraph (1)(a)(i),
  [[harvest]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} has the same meaning as in section
  1.1.1 of the Agreement.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Significance of impacts --- factors

**[[90]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-90 .sectionLabel}** In determining the
significance of impacts for the purposes of section 88 and subparagraphs
89(1)(a)(i) and (b)(ii), the Board must take into account the following
factors:

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} the size of the geographic area, including the size
  of wildlife habitats, likely to be affected by the impacts;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} the ecosystemic sensitivity of that area;

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} the historical, cultural and archaeological
  significance of that area;

- [(d)]{.lawlabel} the size of the human and the animal populations
  likely to be affected by the impacts;

- [(e)]{.lawlabel} the nature, magnitude and complexity of the impacts;

- [(f)]{.lawlabel} the probability of the impacts occurring;

- [(g)]{.lawlabel} the frequency and duration of the impacts;

- [(h)]{.lawlabel} the reversibility or irreversibility of the impacts;

- [(i)]{.lawlabel} the cumulative impacts that could result from the
  impacts of the project combined with those of any other project that
  has been carried out, is being carried out or is likely to be carried
  out; and

- [(j)]{.lawlabel} any other factor that the Board considers relevant to
  the assessment of the significance of impacts.

    impacts of the project, including those arising from the effects
    referred to in paragraph (d);

  - [(f)]{.lawlabel} the cumulative ecosystemic and socio-economic
    impacts that could result from the impacts of the project combined
    with those of any other project that has been carried out, is being
    carried out or is likely to be carried out;

  - [(g)]{.lawlabel} whether the impacts referred to in paragraphs (e)
    and (f) would unduly prejudice the ecosystemic integrity of the
    designated area;

  - [(h)]{.lawlabel} the measures, including those proposed by the
    proponent, that should be taken to

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} avoid and mitigate adverse ecosystemic and
      socio-economic impacts, including contingency plans,

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} optimize the benefits of the project, with
      specific consideration given to expressed community and regional
      preferences in regard to benefits,

    - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} compensate persons whose interests are
      adversely affected by the project, and

    - [(iv)]{.lawlabel} restore ecosystemic integrity after the
      permanent closure of the project;

  - [(i)]{.lawlabel} the significance of the impacts referred to in
    paragraphs (e) and (f), taking into account the measures referred to
    in paragraph (h);

  - [(j)]{.lawlabel} the capacity of renewable resources that are likely
    to be significantly affected by the project to meet the existing and
    future needs of the residents of the designated area;

  - [(k)]{.lawlabel} any monitoring program of the project's ecosystemic
    and socio-economic impacts that should be established, including one
    proposed by the proponent;

  - [(l)]{.lawlabel} the interests in land and waters that the proponent
    has acquired or seeks to acquire;

  - [(m)]{.lawlabel} the options for carrying out the project that are
    technically and economically feasible and the anticipated
    ecosystemic and socio-economic impacts of such options;

  - [(n)]{.lawlabel} the posting of performance bonds;

  - [(o)]{.lawlabel} the particular issues or concerns identified under
    subsection 96(1); and

  - [(p)]{.lawlabel} any other matter within the Board's jurisdiction
    that, in its opinion, should be considered.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Significance

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} In assessing the significance of impacts for the
  purposes of paragraph (1)(i), the Board must take into account the
  factors set out in paragraphs 90(a) to (j).

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Traditional knowledge

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} In its review of a project, the Board must take into
  account any traditional knowledge or community knowledge provided to
  it.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Determination to proceed

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} agree with that determination and either

    - [(A)]{.lawlabel} one or more of the terms or conditions are

  interest.

#### [Federal Environmental Assessment Panel]{.HTitleText3} {#h-371352 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Establishment

- **[[115]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-115
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} After receiving a project proposal
  under subparagraph 94(1)(a)(i), the Minister of the Environment must
  establish a federal environmental assessment panel consisting of
  members, including a Chairperson, appointed by that Minister.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Composition

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The following rules apply in respect of the
  appointment of members of the panel, other than the Chairperson:

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} at least one quarter of the members must be
    appointed on the nomination of the territorial Minister; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} at least one quarter of the members must be
    appointed on the nomination of the organization referred to in
    paragraph (a) of the definition [[designated Inuit
    organization]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} in subsection 2(1).

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Impartiality and qualifications

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} The Minister of the Environment must appoint as
  members of the panel persons who are unbiased and free from any
  conflict of interest relative to the project and who have special
  knowledge or experience relevant to the anticipated technical,
  environmental or social impacts of the project.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Status of Inuk

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} A panel member is not placed in a conflict of
  interest solely because the member is an Inuk as defined in section
  1.1.1 of the Agreement.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Eligibility

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} A person is not ineligible for appointment to a panel
  merely because the person is a member of the Board.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Primary objectives

- **[[116]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-116 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A
  federal environmental assessment panel must exercise its powers and
  perform its duties and functions in accordance with the following
  primary objectives:

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} to protect and promote the existing and future
    well-being of the residents and communities of the designated area;
    and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} to protect the ecosystemic integrity of the
    designated area.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Other residents

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The panel must take into account the well-being of
  residents of Canada outside the designated area when exercising its
  powers or performing its duties and functions in accordance with the
  objective set out in paragraph (1)(a).

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Interpretation

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} For greater certainty, the panel must exercise its
  powers and perform its duties and functions under paragraph 123(1)(c)
  in accordance with the objectives set out in subsection (1).

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Terms of reference

**[[117]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-117 .sectionLabel}** The Minister of the
Environment must, in consultation with the responsible Minister, fix the
terms of reference for the panel and send the project proposal to the
panel. The Minister of the Environment must include in the terms of
reference any issues or concerns identified under section 97 and may
identify other issues or concerns that must also be considered by the
panel in its review of the project.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Scope of project

- **[[118]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-118 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  Minister of the Environment must, in consultation with the responsible
  Minister, determine the scope of the project and, in making that
  determination, must

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} include within the scope of the project, in
    addition to any work or activity identified in the project proposal,
    any other work or activity that the Minister of the Environment
    considers sufficiently related to the project to form part of it;
    and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} exclude from the scope of the project any work or
    activity identified in the project proposal that the Minister of the
    Environment considers insufficiently related to the project to form
    part of it.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Consultation

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The Minister of the Environment may only make an
  inclusion under paragraph (1)(a) or an exclusion under paragraph
  (1)(b) after consulting with the proponent in respect of the
  contemplated changes and taking into account any comments the
  proponent may make in respect of them.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Process suspended

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} If the Minister of the Environment makes an inclusion
  under paragraph (1)(a), the panel must not proceed with the review and
  the Commission as well as the federal Minister or the territorial
  Minister, or both, must exercise their powers and perform their duties
  and functions under sections 77, 81 and 82 in relation to the entire
  project.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Review

**[[119]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-119 .sectionLabel}** The panel must review
the project if the Minister of the Environment has not made an inclusion
under paragraph 118(1)(a) or if that Minister made an inclusion under
that paragraph and the panel receives a decision, by reason of
subsection 118(3), that the entire project is in conformity with any
applicable land use plan, that a minor variance or a ministerial
exemption has been granted in respect of it or that there is no land use
plan applicable to it.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Impact statement --- guidelines

- **[[120]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-120 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A
  federal environmental assessment panel must issue guidelines in
  respect of the preparation of a statement by the proponent on the
  ecosystemic and socio-economic impacts of the project.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Exception

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Despite subsection (1), a panel need not issue
  guidelines if it is of the opinion that the information contained in
  the description of the project or information submitted under
  subsection 144(1) is sufficient to allow it to conduct a review of the
  project.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Content of impact statement

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} The guidelines must specify the types of information
  referred to in subsection 101(3) that the proponent must include in
  the impact statement.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Comments --- Board

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} The panel must send a draft of the guidelines to the
  Board, and the Board must provide its comments on them to the panel.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Comments --- departments or agencies,
  etc.

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} The panel must make a draft of the guidelines public,
  in both official languages of Canada and in Inuktitut and must solicit
  written and oral comments on them from appropriate departments or
  agencies, appropriate designated Inuit organizations, affected
  municipalities, interested corporations and organizations, Inuit and

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Consultation

  [(6)]{.lawlabel} After receiving the Board's comments and after
  allowing a reasonable period for the submission of comments under

  necessary and send the guidelines to the proponent.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Submission of statement

  [(7)]{.lawlabel} The proponent must submit an impact statement
  prepared in accordance with the guidelines to the panel.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Recommendations --- Board

  [(8)]{.lawlabel} As soon as practicable after receiving the impact
  statement, the panel must send it to the Board so that the Board has
  sufficient time to analyse the statement and provide its concerns or
  recommendations to the panel no later than five days before the public
  hearing.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Recommendations taken into account

  [(9)]{.lawlabel} The panel must take into account the Board's concerns
  and recommendations with respect to the impact statement.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Public hearing

- **[[121]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-121 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A
  federal environmental assessment panel must hold a public hearing in
  respect of a project.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Duty

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The panel must take all necessary steps to promote
  public awareness of and participation in the public hearing, including
  through the choice of the date, time and place of the hearing, notice
  given in relation to them and measures taken to disseminate any
  relevant information.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Informal proceedings

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} A panel must, to the extent that is consistent with
  the general application of the rules of procedural fairness and
  natural justice, emphasize flexibility and informality in the conduct
  of public hearings and in particular must

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} allow, if appropriate, the admission of evidence
    that would not normally be admissible under the strict rules of
    evidence; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} give due regard and weight to the Inuit traditions
    regarding oral communication and decision-making.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Designated Inuit organization

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} A designated Inuit organization has full standing to
  appear at a public hearing for the purpose of making submissions on
  behalf of the people it represents.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Languages

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} The panel must conduct any public hearing in both of
  the official languages of Canada in accordance with the [Official
  Languages Act](/eng/acts/O-3.01) and any directives of the responsible
  Minister and, on request by a member, a proponent or an intervenor, in
  Inuktitut.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Members

  [(6)]{.lawlabel} Nothing in subsection (5) is to be construed as
  preventing the use of translation or interpretation services if a
  member is otherwise unable to conduct business in either official
  language or in Inuktitut.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Witnesses

  [(7)]{.lawlabel} The panel has the duty to ensure that any witness
  giving evidence before it may be heard in either official language or
  in Inuktitut, and that in being so heard the witness will not be
  placed at a disadvantage by not being heard in another of those
  languages.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Summon witnesses, etc.

  [(8)]{.lawlabel} A panel has, in respect of public hearings, the power
  to summon any person to appear as a witness before the panel and to
  order the witness to

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} give evidence, orally or in writing; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} produce any documents or other things that the
    panel considers necessary to conduct its review of the project.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Enforcement of attendance, etc.

  [(9)]{.lawlabel} A panel has the same power to enforce the attendance
  of witnesses and to compel them to give evidence and produce documents
  and things as a superior court.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Hearing may be closed to public

  [(10)]{.lawlabel} A panel may close all or part of a hearing to the
  public if it is satisfied, after submissions made by a proponent or
  other witness, that the evidence, documents or things to be disclosed
  in the hearing contain

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} confidential, personal, business proprietary or
    privileged information; or

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} information whose disclosure would cause specific,
    direct and substantial harm to the witness or cause specific
    ecosystemic or socio-economic harm.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Non-disclosure

  [(11)]{.lawlabel} Evidence, documents or things referred to in
  subsection (10) must not be, or be permitted to be, disclosed without
  the authorization of the witness, by any person who has obtained the
  evidence, documents or things under this Act.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Enforcement of summonses and orders

  [(12)]{.lawlabel} A summons issued or an order made by a panel under
  subsection (8) may be made a summons or order of the Nunavut Court of
  Justice by filing a certified copy of it with the registrar of the
  court and, when so made, is enforceable in the same manner as a
  summons or order of that court.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Factors to consider

- **[[122]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-122 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} In
  conducting a review of a project, a federal environmental assessment
  panel must take into account the following factors:

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the purpose of the project, and the need for the
    project;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} whether, and to what extent, the project would
    protect and enhance the existing and future well-being of the
    residents and communities of the designated area while taking into
    account the interests of other Canadians;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} whether the project reflects the priorities and
    values of the residents of the designated area;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} the anticipated effects of the environment on the
    project, including effects associated with natural phenomena, such
    as meteorological and seismological activity, and climate change;

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} the anticipated ecosystemic and socio-economic
    impacts of the project, including those arising from the effects
    referred to in paragraph (d);

  - [(f)]{.lawlabel} the cumulative ecosystemic and socio-economic
    impacts that could result from the impacts of the project combined
    with those of any other project that has been carried out, is being
    carried out or is likely to be carried out;

  - [(g)]{.lawlabel} whether the impacts referred to in paragraphs (e)
    and (f) would unduly prejudice the ecosystemic integrity of the
    designated area;

  - [(h)]{.lawlabel} the measures, including those proposed by the
    proponent, that should be taken to

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} avoid and mitigate adverse ecosystemic and
      socio-economic impacts, including contingency plans,

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} optimize the benefits of the project, with
      specific consideration given to expressed community and regional
      preferences in regard to benefits,

    - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} compensate persons whose interests are
      adversely affected by the project, and

    - [(iv)]{.lawlabel} restore ecosystemic integrity after the
      permanent closure of the project;

  - [(i)]{.lawlabel} the significance of the impacts referred to in
    paragraphs (e) and (f), taking into account the measures referred to
    in paragraph (h);

###### [Land Use Plan in Effect]{.HTitleText5} {#h-371867 .Subheading}

  in conformity.

determines that the initiative is in conformity with any applicable land
use plan, it must send the proposal relating to the initiative to the

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Initiative not in conformity with plan

- **[[177]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-177 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} If
  the Commission determines that the initiative is not in conformity
  with an applicable land use plan, it must verify whether that land use
  plan authorizes it to grant a minor variance with respect to that
  initiative and whether the conditions set out in accordance with
  subsection 48(3), if any, are met.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Minor variance permitted

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} If the land use plan authorizes the granting of a
  minor variance and if the conditions, if any, are met, the Commission
  may, within 20 days after its determination that the initiative is not
  in conformity with the plan,

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} grant the variance, in which case it must send the
    proposal relating to the initiative to the Board in order for the
    Board to conduct a screening; or

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} refuse to grant the variance.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Publication

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} Before granting a minor variance under paragraph
  (2)(a), the Commission must make the proposed minor variance public
  and must do so in a manner designed to promote participation in its
  examination by the public.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Objection

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} Any interested person may, within 10 days after the
  proposed minor variance is made public, indicate to the Commission, in
  writing, that the proposed minor variance should not be granted
  because

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the land use plan does not authorize the granting
    of the minor variance;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the conditions subject to which a minor variance
    may be granted are not met; or

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} the minor variance is not appropriate, in their
    opinion, for any other reason that they specify.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Reasons taken into account and public
  review

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} The Commission may only grant a minor variance under
  paragraph (2)(a) after taking into account any reasons for which an
  interested person has indicated, under subsection (4), that it should
  not be granted and, if it considers it appropriate to do so,
  conducting a public review in accordance with the by-laws and rules
  made under section 17 and taking into account any submissions made
  during that review.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Extension of time limit

  [(6)]{.lawlabel} If the Commission is of the opinion that more time is
  needed to make a decision under subsection (2), it may extend the
  period referred to in that subsection by up to 10 days and must notify
  the department or agency of the extension in writing.

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} that determination, if the land use plan does not
    authorize the granting of a minor variance or if it does and the

    minor variance.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Ministerial decision

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The Minister or Ministers, as the case may be, must,
  within 120 days after the day on which they receive a request under

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} grant the exemption, in which case the Commission
    must make the decision public and send the proposal relating to the
    initiative to the Board in order for the Board to conduct a
    screening; or

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} refuse the exemption.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Consultation

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} An exemption may only be granted after consultation

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Extension of time limit

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} If any Minister referred to in subsection (1) is of

### [Administration and Enforcement]{.HTitleText2} {#h-372137 .Subheading}

#### [Powers]{.HTitleText3} {#h-372141 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Authority to enter

- **[[210]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-210 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A
  person who is designated to verify compliance or prevent
  non-compliance with this Act or orders made under section 214 may, for
  those purposes, enter a place in which they have reasonable grounds to
  believe that a project is being carried out or a document or any thing
  relating to a project is located.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Powers on entry

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The designated person may, for the purposes referred
  to in subsection (1),

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} examine anything in the place;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} use any means of communication in the place or
    cause it to be used;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} use any computer system in the place, or cause it
    to be used, to examine data contained in or available to that
    system;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} prepare a document, or cause one to be prepared,
    based on the data;

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} use any copying equipment in the place, or cause it
    to be used;

  - [(f)]{.lawlabel} remove any thing from the place for examination or
    copying;

  - [(g)]{.lawlabel} take photographs and make recordings or sketches;

  - [(h)]{.lawlabel} order the owner or person in charge of the place or
    any person at the place to establish their identity to the
    designated person's satisfaction or to stop or start an activity;

  - [(i)]{.lawlabel} order the owner or person having possession, care
    or control of any thing in the place to not move it, or to restrict
    its movement, for as long as, in the designated person's opinion, is
    necessary;

  - [(j)]{.lawlabel} direct any person to put any machinery, vehicle or
    equipment in the place into operation or to cease operating it; and

  - [(k)]{.lawlabel} prohibit or limit access to all or part of the
    place.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Certificate

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} The federal Minister must provide every designated
  person with a certificate of designation. On entering any place, that
  person must, if so requested, produce the certificate to the occupant
  or person in charge of the place.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Duty to assist

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} The owner or person in charge of the place and every
  person in the place must give all assistance that is reasonably
  required to enable the designated person to verify compliance or
  prevent non-compliance with this Act or orders made under section 214
  and must provide any documents, data or information that are
  reasonably required for that purpose.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Warrant for dwelling-house

- **[[211]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-211 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} If
  the place referred to in subsection 210(1) is a dwelling-house, the
  designated person may only enter it with the occupant's consent or
  under the authority of a warrant issued under subsection (2).

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Authority to issue warrant

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} On [ex parte]{.otherLang lang="la"} application, a
  justice of the peace may issue a warrant authorizing the designated
  person who is named in it to enter a dwelling-house, subject to any
  conditions specified in the warrant, if the justice of the peace is
  satisfied by information on oath that

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the dwelling-house is a place referred to in
    subsection 210(1);

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} entry to the dwelling-house is necessary for the
    purpose of verifying compliance or preventing non-compliance with
    this Act or orders made under section 214; and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} entry was refused by the occupant or there are
    reasonable grounds to believe that entry will be refused or that
    consent to entry cannot be obtained from the occupant.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Entering private property

- **[[212]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-212 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} For
  the purpose of gaining entry to a place referred to in subsection
  210(1), a designated person may enter and pass through private
  property. For greater certainty, no person has a right to object to
  that use of the property and no warrant is required for the entry,
  unless the property is a dwelling-house.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Person accompanying designated person

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} A person may, at the designated person's request,
  accompany the designated person to assist them in gaining entry to the

  137 and, if not, for any order that the Court considers necessary in
  the circumstances;

- [(e)]{.lawlabel} a determination of whether a project is, is likely to
  be or has been carried out in accordance with the terms and conditions
  referred to in paragraph (c) that have been implemented under section
  136 or 137 and, if not, for an order requiring any person or entity
  named in the application to

  - [(i)]{.lawlabel} refrain from doing an act that, in the opinion of
    the court, may constitute or be directed toward the breach of any of
    those terms or conditions, or

  - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} do an act that, in the opinion of the court, may
    prevent the breach of any of those terms or conditions;

- [(f)]{.lawlabel} a determination of whether a project referred to
  paragraph 152(1)(a), (b) or (c) is, or has been, carried out in
  accordance with the terms and conditions imposed under subsection
  152(6) and, if not, for any order that the Court considers necessary
  in the circumstances; or

- [(g)]{.lawlabel} judicial review of any interim or final decision or
  order made under Part 3.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Decisions final

**[[223]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-223 .sectionLabel}** Unless an exemption has
been granted under paragraph 82(2)(a), any decision made by the
Commission regarding the conformity of a project with a land use plan is
final and binding and, except for judicial review under the [Federal
Courts Act](/eng/acts/F-7) and under section 220, is not subject to
appeal to or review by any court.

#### [Immunity]{.HTitleText3} {#h-372233 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Things done in good faith

**[[224]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-224 .sectionLabel}** No action lies against
a member or employee of the Commission or the Board, a member of a
federal environmental assessment panel or joint panel or a person
designated under section 209 for anything done or omitted to be done in
good faith in the exercise or performance, or purported exercise or
performance, of any power, duty or function under this Act.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Disclosure made in good faith

**[[225]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-225 .sectionLabel}** No action lies against
the Crown, the Commission, the Board, members or employees of the
Commission or the Board or members of a federal environmental assessment
panel or joint panel for the disclosure in good faith of any document,
any part of a document or information under this Act, including by
making it available in the public registries, or for any consequences
that flow from that disclosure or for the failure to give any notice
required under section 27 or 28 of the [Access to Information
Act](/eng/acts/A-1) if reasonable care is taken to give the required
notice.

#### [Time Limits]{.HTitleText3} {#h-372238 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Authority, etc.

**[[226]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-226 .sectionLabel}** The failure of the
Commission, a responsible authority, the Board, any federal
environmental assessment panel, any joint panel or a responsible
Minister to exercise a power or perform a duty or function within a
period limited by this Act does not terminate their authority or
invalidate any document prepared or submitted or any decision or action
taken in the exercise or performance of such a power, duty or function.

### [General Monitoring]{.HTitleText2} {#h-372241 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Plan

- **[[227]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-227 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  Government of Canada and the Government of Nunavut must, in
  cooperation with the Commission, develop a plan for the general
  monitoring of the long-term state and health of the ecosystemic and
  socio-economic environment of the designated area and must direct and
  coordinate that general monitoring and the collection of information
  relating to it.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Commission

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The Commission must, in accordance with the plan
  developed under subsection (1), collate information provided by
  industry, departments or agencies and others and prepare periodic
  reports on the ecosystemic and socio-economic environment of the
  designated area. The Commission must use that information in the
  exercise of its powers and the performance of its duties and functions
  under this Act.

### [Regulations and Orders]{.HTitleText2} {#h-372247 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Regulations

- **[[228]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-228 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} establishing a funding program to facilitate the

    or a joint panel.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Consent of Tunngavik

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The Governor in Council may, on the recommendation of
  the federal Minister, after consultation by that Minister with the
  territorial Minister, the Commission and the Board and with the
  consent of Tunngavik, make regulations prescribing

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} for the purposes of the definition
    [[project]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} in subsection 2(1), classes of
    excluded works or activities; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} for the purposes of subsection 78(2), paragraph
    155(1)(a) and subsection 166(2), classes of physical works and
    activities that are not exempt from screening.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Schedule 2

**[[229]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-229 .sectionLabel}** The federal Minister
may, by order, amend Schedule 2 to add, delete or amend the name of a
designated regulatory agency.

  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Before entering into an agreement

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Comments

  activities exempted from screening under an agreement referred to in
  subsection (3).

## [PART 6]{.HLabel1}[Transitional Provisions]{.HTitleText1} {#h-372268 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Members and employees

**[[231]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-231 .sectionLabel}** Members and employees
of the Commission and the Board who occupy a position immediately before
the coming into force of this section continue in that position as if
they had been appointed or employed under this Act.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Policies, priorities and objectives
regarding planning

**[[232]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-232 .sectionLabel}** Sections 40 to 45 do
not apply in respect of policies, priorities, and broad and specific
objectives regarding land use planning established, and planning
variables identified, under Article 11 of the Agreement and in force on
the day on which this section comes into force, but those sections apply
in respect of any amendment to such a policy, priority, objective or
variable after the coming into force of this section.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Land use plans

- **[[233]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-233 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Any
  land use plan approved under section 11.5.9 of the Agreement and in
  force on the day on which this section comes into force remains in
  force subject to the following rules:

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} sections 47, 48 and 66 do not apply in respect of
    it and, for greater certainty, sections 49 to 58 do not apply in
    respect of it;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} it is taken into account for the purposes of
    sections 46, 68 to 70 and 72, Part 3, paragraph 222(d) and section
    223; and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} sections 59 to 65 apply in respect of any amendment
    to it after the coming into force of this section.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Public Registry

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The Commission must include any land use plan
  referred to in subsection (1) in the public registry established under
  subsection 201(1).

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Municipal plans

**[[234]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-234 .sectionLabel}** Sections 71 and 72 do
not apply in respect of any municipal land use plan developed in
accordance with Article 11 of the Agreement and in force on the day on
which this section comes into force. Those sections apply in respect of
any amendment to such a land use plan after the coming into force of
this section.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Projects --- assessment under Agreement

- **[[235]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-235 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} This
  Act does not apply in respect of

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} a project that is being assessed under the
    Agreement or is being, or has been, lawfully carried out on the day
    on which this section comes into force;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} a project that was approved under the Agreement
    before the day on which this section comes into force, was commenced
    and then stopped or shut down for a period of less than five years,
    calculated from that day;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} the rebuilding of a work that has been closed for a
    period of less than five years calculated from the day on which this
    section comes into force, if it relates to a project that was
    approved under the Agreement before that day and lawfully carried
    out; and

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} a project that was approved under the Agreement
    before the day on which this section comes into force and commenced
    within five years of that day.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Exception --- significant modification

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Despite subsection (1), if, after this section comes
  into force, there is a significant modification, within the meaning of
  section 145, to a project referred to in any of paragraphs (1)(a) to
  (d), this Act applies to that project.
