## National Council for Reconciliation Act {#national-council-for-reconciliation-act .Title-of-Act}

[S.C.]{.abbr title="Statutes of Canada"} 2024, c. 8
:::

Assented to 2024-04-30

An Act to provide for the establishment of a national council for
reconciliation
::::

::::: section
::: {.section .order}
###### Preamble {#preamble .MarginalNote}

Whereas, since time immemorial, First Nations and Inuit peoples --- and,
post-contact, the Métis Nation --- have thrived on and managed and
governed their Indigenous lands;

Whereas, since the arrival of settlers and colonization, Indigenous
peoples have experienced assimilationist policies, which must be
addressed through reconciliation;

Whereas reconciliation with Indigenous peoples is recognized as a
fundamental purpose of section 35 of the [Constitution Act,
1982](/eng/Const/);

Whereas that reconciliation requires collective efforts from all peoples
and the commitment of multiple generations;

Whereas Indigenous peoples have their own collective identities,
cultures, teachings and ways of life and have, throughout history and to
this day, continued to live in, use and occupy the lands that are now in
Canada;

Whereas the Government of Canada is committed to achieving
reconciliation with Indigenous peoples through renewed nation-to-nation,
government-to-government and Inuit-Crown relationships based on
recognition of rights, respect, cooperation and partnership;

Whereas the Government of Canada is committed to implementing the United
Nations Declaration on the Rights of Indigenous Peoples;

Whereas reconciliation requires the revitalization and celebration of
Indigenous languages;

Whereas the Government of Canada recognizes the need for the
establishment of an independent, non-political, permanent and
Indigenous-led organization to monitor, evaluate, conduct research and
report on the progress being made towards reconciliation, including in
relation to respect for and the protection and promotion of the rights
of Indigenous peoples, in all sectors of Canadian society and by all
governments in Canada, in order to address the Truth and Reconciliation
Commission of Canada's Call to Action number 53;

Whereas the Government of Canada recognizes the need for such an
organization to be a catalyst for innovative thought, dialogue and
action;

And whereas the Government of Canada recognizes the importance of the
work of such an organization as well as the need to cooperate with it
and support it in its contribution to advancing reconciliation,
including through the provision of information referred to in the Truth
and Reconciliation Commission of Canada's Call to Action number 55;
:::

::: Enacts
Now, therefore, His Majesty, by and with the advice and consent of the
Senate and House of Commons of Canada, enacts as follows:
:::

## [Short Title]{.HTitleText1} {#h-1450874 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Short title

**[[1]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-1 .sectionLabel}** This Act may be cited as
the [National Council for Reconciliation Act](/eng/acts/N-4.3).

## [Interpretation]{.HTitleText1} {#h-1450877 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Definitions

- **[[2]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-2 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  following definitions apply in this Act.

  [[Council]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[Council]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means the corporation incorporated
      as a result of the sending of the articles of incorporation and
      other documents referred to in section
      3. ([Conseil]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

  [[governments]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[governments]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means the Government of
      Canada, provincial and local governments and Indigenous governing
      bodies. ([gouvernements]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

  [[Indigenous governing body]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[Indigenous governing body]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means a council,
      government or other entity that is authorized to act on behalf of
      an Indigenous group, community or people that holds rights
      recognized and affirmed by section 35 of the [Constitution Act,
      1982](/eng/Const/). ([corps dirigeant autochtone]{.DefinedTermLink
      lang="fr"})

  [[Indigenous peoples]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[Indigenous peoples]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} has the meaning
      assigned by the definition [[aboriginal peoples of
      Canada]{.dfn}]{.DefinitionRef} in subsection 35(2) of the
      [Constitution Act, 1982](/eng/Const/). ([peuples
      autochtones]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

  [[Minister]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[Minister]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means the Minister of
      Crown-Indigenous Relations. ([ministre]{.DefinedTermLink
      lang="fr"})

  [[transitional committee]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[transitional committee]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means the National
      Council for Reconciliation Transitional Committee established by
      the Minister under section 19 of the [Department of
      Crown-Indigenous Relations and Northern Affairs
      Act](/eng/acts/C-49.8). ([comité de transition]{.DefinedTermLink
      lang="fr"})

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Act prevails

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} In the event of any inconsistency between this Act
  and the [Canada Not-for-profit Corporations Act](/eng/acts/C-7.75), or
  anything issued, made or established under that Act, this Act prevails
  to the extent of the inconsistency.

## [Establishment]{.HTitleText1} {#h-1450889 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Sending of articles of incorporation

**[[3]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-3 .sectionLabel}** One of the members of the
transitional committee authorized to do so by the committee must send to
the [[Director]{.dfn}]{.DefinitionRef}, as defined in subsection 2(1) of
the [Canada Not-for-profit Corporations Act](/eng/acts/C-7.75), articles
of incorporation and the other documents that are required under that
Act to effect the incorporation of the Council.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Not agent of His Majesty

**[[4]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-4 .sectionLabel}** The Council is not an agent
of His Majesty in right of Canada nor is it, for greater certainty, an
entity governed by the [Financial Administration Act](/eng/acts/F-11).

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Qualified donee

**[[5]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-5 .sectionLabel}** The Council is deemed to be
a qualified donee within the meaning of the [Income Tax
Act](/eng/acts/I-3.3).

## [Purpose and Functions]{.HTitleText1} {#h-1450896 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Purpose

**[[6]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-6 .sectionLabel}** The purpose of the Council
is to advance reconciliation between Indigenous peoples and
non-Indigenous peoples.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Functions

**[[7]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-7 .sectionLabel}** In carrying out its
purpose, the Council is to

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} monitor, evaluate and report annually on the
  Government of Canada's post-apology progress towards reconciliation,
  to ensure that government accountability for reconciling the
  relationship between Indigenous peoples and the Crown is maintained in
  the coming years;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} monitor, evaluate and report on the progress being
  made towards reconciliation across all levels of government and
  sectors of Canadian society, including the progress being made towards
  the implementation of the Truth and Reconciliation Commission of
  Canada's Calls to Action;

- [(b.1)]{.lawlabel} ensure that reconciliation is consistent with the
  protection and promotion of the rights of Indigenous peoples,
  including by advancing a rights-based approach to self-determination;

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} develop and implement a multi-year National Action
  Plan for Reconciliation that includes

  - [(i)]{.lawlabel} research on practices that advance reconciliation
    in all sectors of Canadian society, by all governments in Canada and
    at the international level,

  - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} policy development, and

  - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} public education programs;

- [(d)]{.lawlabel} monitor policies and programs of the Government of
  Canada, and federal laws, that affect Indigenous peoples;

- [(e)]{.lawlabel} recommend measures to promote, prioritize and
  coordinate reconciliation in all sectors of Canadian society and by
  all governments in Canada;

- [(f)]{.lawlabel} educate the public about Indigenous peoples'
  realities and histories and advocate for reconciliation in all sectors
  of Canadian society and by all governments in Canada;

- [(g)]{.lawlabel} stimulate and promote innovative dialogue,
  partnerships between public and private sector bodies and public
  initiatives aimed at reconciliation;

- [(h)]{.lawlabel} monitor and report on the progress made on measurable
  outcomes, including in relation to the Truth and Reconciliation
  Commission of Canada's Call to Action number 55; and

- [(i)]{.lawlabel} protect Indigenous language rights, including by
  ensuring that, to promote and support the participation of Indigenous
  persons in the work of the Council, translation and interpretation
  services are made available to them.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}For greater certainty

**[[7.1]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-7.1 .sectionLabel}** For greater certainty,

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} nothing in this Act is to be construed as authorizing
  the Council to act on behalf of, or represent the interests of, an
  Indigenous governing body; and

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} no duty to consult an Indigenous group, community or
  people that holds rights recognized and affirmed by section 35 of the
  [Constitution Act, 1982](/eng/Const/) is discharged by consulting or
  engaging with the Council.

## [Bilateral mechanisms]{.HTitleText1} {#h-1450918 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Bilateral mechanisms

**[[7.2]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-7.2 .sectionLabel}** For greater certainty,
if the Government of Canada establishes or has established a bilateral
mechanism with an Indigenous governing body, that bilateral mechanism is
not affected by this Act.

## [Board of Directors]{.HTitleText1} {#h-1450921 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}First board of directors

**[[8]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-8 .sectionLabel}** The persons whose names are
set out in the notice of directors sent under section 3 are to be
jointly selected by the Minister and the transitional committee, taking
into account sections 9, 11, 12 and 13 and the nominations referred to
in subsection 10(1).

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Composition

**[[9]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-9 .sectionLabel}** The Council's board of
directors is to be composed of a minimum of nine and a maximum of 13
directors.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Nominations

- **[[10]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-10 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  board of directors is to include

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} one director who may only be elected after having
    been nominated by the Assembly of First Nations;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} one director who may only be elected after having
    been nominated by Inuit Tapiriit Kanatami;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} one director who may only be elected after having
    been nominated by the Métis National Council; and

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} one director who may only be elected after having
    been nominated by the Native Women's Association of Canada.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Vacancy --- nominated directors

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} If there is a vacancy among the directors referred to
  in paragraphs (1)(a) to (d), the remaining directors may exercise all
  the powers of the directors if the number of directors on the
  Council's board of directors constitutes a quorum.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Application process

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} The other directors are to be elected following an
  application process established by the board of directors.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Indigenous persons

- **[[11]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-11 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} At
  least two thirds of the directors must be Indigenous persons.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Residents of territories

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} At least two of the directors must be residents of
  Yukon, the Northwest Territories or Nunavut.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Representativeness

- **[[12]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-12 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  Council's board of directors must, to the extent possible, include
  representation from

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} First Nations, Inuit and the Métis;

  - [(a.1)]{.lawlabel} Indigenous elders;

  - [(a.2)]{.lawlabel} Indigenous survivors of the discriminatory and
    assimilationist policies of the Government of Canada and their
    descendants;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} other peoples in Canada;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} [[Indigenous organizations]{.dfn}]{.DefinitionRef},
    as defined in section 2 of the [Department of Indigenous Services
    Act](/eng/acts/I-7.88), to reflect the diversity of arrangements
    that govern relationships between Indigenous communities and the
    Government of Canada;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} youth, women, men and gender-diverse persons;

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} various regions of Canada, including urban, rural
    and remote regions;

  - [(f)]{.lawlabel} Indigenous persons whose first or second language
    learned is French; and

  - [(g)]{.lawlabel} survivors of Canadian residential schools or their
    descendants.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Gender diversity

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The composition of the board of directors must also,
  to the extent possible, ensure and equitably reflect gender diversity.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Knowledge and experience

- **[[13]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-13 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Each
  director must have knowledge and experience with respect to matters
  related to Indigenous peoples and other matters related to the
  Council's purpose.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Consultation

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} To ensure that Indigenous views are heard in relation
  to the advancement of reconciliation with Indigenous peoples, the
  Council must consult with a variety of persons with relevant
  knowledge, expertise or experience, including elders, survivors of the
  discriminatory and assimilationist policies of the Government of
  Canada and Indigenous law practitioners.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Term of office

**[[14]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-14 .sectionLabel}** Directors are to hold
office for any term of not more than four years that will ensure as far
as possible the expiry in any one year of the terms of office of not
more than one third of the directors. A director is eligible to serve
for a maximum of two terms.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Election

**[[15]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-15 .sectionLabel}** Despite subsection 128(3)
of the [Canada Not-for-profit Corporations Act](/eng/acts/C-7.75), and
subject to section 8, each director is to be elected by a [[special
resolution]{.dfn}]{.DefinitionRef}, as defined in subsection 2(1) of
that Act, of the members of the Council.

## [Disclosure of Information]{.HTitleText1} {#h-1450965 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Protocol

- **[[16]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-16 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Within
  six months after the day on which the Council is incorporated, the
  Minister must, in collaboration with the Council, develop a protocol
  respecting the disclosure by the Government of Canada to the Council
  of information that is relevant to the Council's purpose.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Relevant information

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The protocol must allow, to the extent possible, the
  Council to receive all the information it judges relevant to fulfill
  its mission.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Declaration

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} If the Minister fails to comply with the obligations
  set out in subsections (1) and (2), the Council may apply to a judge
  of the Federal Court for a declaration to that effect or for any other
  appropriate order.

## [Annual Reports]{.HTitleText1} {#h-1450973 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Report of Minister

**[[16.1]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-16.1 .sectionLabel}** The Minister must,
within six months after March 31 of each year, submit to the Council an
annual report setting out

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} a comparison of the number of Indigenous and
  non-Indigenous children in care, the reasons for their apprehension
  and the total spending on preventive and care services by
  child-welfare agencies;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} a comparison of the funding for the education of
  Indigenous children on and off reserves;

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} a comparison of the educational and income
  attainments of Indigenous and non-Indigenous persons;

- [(d)]{.lawlabel} the progress made on closing the gaps between
  Indigenous and non-Indigenous communities in a number of health
  indicators, such as infant mortality, maternal health, suicide, mental
  health, addictions, life expectancy, birth rates, infant and child
  health issues, chronic diseases, illness and injury incidence, and the
  availability of appropriate health services;

- [(e)]{.lawlabel} the progress made on eliminating the
  overrepresentation of Indigenous children in youth custody;

- [(f)]{.lawlabel} the progress made on reducing the rate of criminal
  victimization of Indigenous persons, including data related to
  homicide, family violence and other crimes; and

- [(g)]{.lawlabel} the progress made on reducing the overrepresentation
  of Indigenous persons in the justice and correctional systems.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Report of Council

- **[[17]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-17 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  Council must, within three months after the end of each financial
  year, submit to the Minister an annual report setting out

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the Government of Canada's post-apology progress
    towards reconciliation;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the progress being made towards reconciliation
    across all levels of government and sectors of Canadian society; and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} the Council's recommendations respecting measures
    to promote, prioritize and coordinate reconciliation in all sectors
    of Canadian society and by all governments in Canada.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Tabling in Parliament

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The Minister must cause a copy of the report to be
  laid before each House of Parliament on any of the first 15 days on
  which that House is sitting after the day on which the report is
  received.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Government response

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} Within 60 days after the day on which the report
  under subsection (2) is laid, the Prime Minister must, on behalf of
  the Government of Canada, respond to the matters addressed by the
  report that are under the jurisdiction of Parliament by publishing an
  annual report on the state of Indigenous peoples that outlines the
  Government of Canada's plans for advancing reconciliation.

## [Financial Report]{.HTitleText1} {#h-1450993 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Contents

- **[[18]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-18 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Within
  six months after the end of each financial year, the Council must make
  public

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the documents and information that are required to
    be placed before the members of the Council under section 172 of the
    [Canada Not-for-profit Corporations Act](/eng/acts/C-7.75); and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} a detailed statement of the Council's investment
    activities during that year, its investment portfolio as at the end
    of that year and its management of funds that it has received during
    the year.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Audit engagement

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The comparative financial statements referred to in
  paragraph 172(1)(a) of the [Canada Not-for-profit Corporations
  Act](/eng/acts/C-7.75) must be subject to an audit engagement.

## [Dissolution]{.HTitleText1} {#h-1451001 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Distribution of property

**[[19]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-19 .sectionLabel}** In the case of
dissolution of the Council under Part 14 of the [Canada Not-for-profit
Corporations Act](/eng/acts/C-7.75), any property remaining on
liquidation after the discharge of any liabilities of the Council, other
than property referred to in section 234 of that Act, is to be
distributed to one or more qualified donees, within the meaning of the
[Income Tax Act](/eng/acts/I-3.3), specified by the Minister, that have
a similar purpose to the Council.
