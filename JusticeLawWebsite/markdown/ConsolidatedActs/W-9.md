## [Short Title]{.HTitleText1} {#h-469318 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Short title

**[[1]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-1 .sectionLabel}** This Act may be cited as
the [Canada Wildlife Act](/eng/acts/W-9).

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., 1985, c. W-9, s. 1
- 1994, c. 23, s. 2(F)
:::

## [Interpretation and Application]{.HTitleText1} {#h-469323 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Definitions

- **[[2]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-2 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} In this
  Act,

  [[Chief Review Officer]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[Chief Review Officer]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means the review
      officer appointed as Chief Review Officer under subsection 244(1)
      of the [Canadian Environmental Protection Act,
      1999](/eng/acts/C-15.31) and includes any review officer
      designated under subsection 244(3) of that Act to perform the
      functions of the Chief Review
      Officer; ([réviseur-chef]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

  [[conveyance]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[conveyance]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means a vehicle, aircraft or
      water-borne craft or any other contrivance that is used to move
      persons or goods; ([moyen de transport]{.DefinedTermLink
      lang="fr"})

  [[Minister]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[Minister]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means the Minister of the
      Environment or, in respect of any matter related to the Northern
      Pipeline referred to in the [Northern Pipeline
      Act](/eng/acts/N-26), the member of the Queen's Privy Council for
      Canada designated as the Minister for the purposes of that
      Act; ([ministre]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

  [[public lands]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[public lands]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means lands belonging to Her
      Majesty in right of Canada and lands that the Government of Canada
      has power to dispose of, subject to the terms of any agreement
      between the Government of Canada and the government of the
      province in which the lands are situated, and includes

      - [(a)]{.lawlabel} any waters on or flowing through the lands and
        the natural resources of the lands, and

      - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the internal waters and the territorial sea of
        Canada. ([terres domaniales]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

  [[wildlife]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[wildlife]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}[\[Repealed, 1994, c. 23, s.
      4\]]{.Repealed}

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Possession

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} For the purposes of this Act,

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} a person has anything in possession when the person
    has it in their personal possession or knowingly

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} has it in the actual possession or custody of
      another person, or

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} has it in any place, whether or not that place
      belongs to or is occupied by the person, for their own use or
      benefit or for the use or benefit of another person; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} where a person has anything in their custody or
    possession with the knowledge and consent of another person or other
    persons, it is in the custody and possession of each and all of
    them.

- [(3)]{.lawlabel} [\[Repealed, 2024, c. 30, s. 5\]]{.Repealed}

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Application

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} The provisions of this Act respecting wildlife apply
  in respect of

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} any animal, plant or other organism belonging to a
    species that is wild by nature or that is not easily distinguishable
    from such a species; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the habitat of any such animal, plant or other
    organism.

## [Powers, Duties and Functions of the Minister]{.HTitleText1} {#h-469353 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Powers of the Minister

**[[3]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-3 .sectionLabel}** The Minister may

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} undertake, promote and recommend measures for the
  encouragement of public cooperation in wildlife conservation and
  interpretation;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} initiate conferences and meetings respecting wildlife
  research, conservation and interpretation;

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} undertake programs for wildlife research and
  investigation, and establish and maintain laboratories and other
  necessary facilities for that purpose;

- [(d)]{.lawlabel} establish such advisory committees as the Minister
  deems necessary and appoint the members of those committees; and

- [(e)]{.lawlabel} coordinate and implement wildlife policies and
  programs in cooperation with the government of any province having an
  interest therein.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., 1985, c. W-9, s. 3
- 1994, c. 23, s. 6(F)
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible} 

- **[[4]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-4
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} [\[Repealed, 1999, c. 31, s.
  222\]]{.Repealed}

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Powers of Minister on public lands
  assigned

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Where the administration of any public lands has been
  assigned to the Minister pursuant to any federal law by reason of
  being required for wildlife research, conservation or interpretation,
  the Minister may

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} take charge of all wildlife research facilities
    operated on those lands;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} provide advice relating to any wildlife research,
    conservation and interpretation being carried out on those lands;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} subject to the regulations, carry out measures for
    the conservation of wildlife on those lands not inconsistent with
    any law respecting wildlife in the province in which the lands are
    situated; and

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} subject to the regulations, establish facilities or
    construct, maintain and operate works for wildlife research,
    conservation and interpretation on those lands.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Powers in relation to lands
  administered by other ministers

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} If public lands under the administration of a
  minister of the Crown other than the Minister are, in the opinion of
  the Minister and the other minister, required for wildlife research,
  conservation or interpretation, the Governor in Council may, on the
  recommendation of both ministers, by order, authorize the Minister to
  exercise, with the concurrence of the other minister, the powers
  referred to in subsection (2) in relation to those lands or any
  portion of them specified in the order.

- 1991, c. 50, s. 47
- 1994, c. 23, s. 7
- 1999, c. 31, s. 222
- 2002, c. 29, s. 134

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Protected marine areas

- **[[4.1]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-4.1 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  Governor in Council may establish protected marine areas in any area
  of the sea that forms part of the internal waters of Canada, the
  territorial sea of Canada or the exclusive economic zone of Canada.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Advice and conservation measures

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The Minister may provide advice relating to any
  wildlife research, conservation and interpretation carried out in
  protected marine areas and may carry out measures for the conservation
  of wildlife in those areas.

::: HistoricalNote
- 1994, c. 23, s. 8
- 1996, c. 31, s. 107
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Delegation by Minister

- **[[4.2]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-4.2 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  Minister may delegate to any minister of the Crown in right of Canada
  any power conferred on the Minister under this Act. The other minister
  may then exercise the power subject to any terms and conditions that
  the Minister specifies.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Sub-delegation

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The other minister may delegate any power delegated
  under subsection (1) to any person employed in any department for
  which that other minister is responsible.

::: HistoricalNote
- 2002, c. 29, s. 135

## [Agreements]{.HTitleText1} {#h-469390 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Agreements

**[[5]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-5 .sectionLabel}** The Minister may, with the
approval of the Governor in Council, enter into an agreement with the
government of any province to provide for

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} the undertaking of wildlife research, conservation
  and interpretation programs and measures, the administration of lands
  for those purposes or the construction, maintenance and operation of
  facilities and works related thereto; and

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} the payment of contributions in respect of the costs
  of those programs and measures.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., 1985, c. W-9, s. 5
- 1994, c. 23, s. 9(F)
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Provisions to be included

**[[6]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-6 .sectionLabel}** Any agreement entered into
pursuant to section 5 shall

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} specify the portions, if any, of the cost of any
  program or measure to which the agreement relates that are payable by
  the Government of Canada and the government of the province or the
  amount of any contribution in respect of the cost of any program or
  measure that is payable by the Government of Canada and the time or
  times at which any amounts under the agreement will be paid;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} specify the authority that will be responsible for
  the undertaking, operation and maintenance of any program or measure
  to which the agreement relates or any part thereof;

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} specify the proportions of the revenues from any
  program or measure to which the agreement relates that are payable to
  the Government of Canada and the government of the province; and

- [(d)]{.lawlabel} specify the terms and conditions governing the
  operation and maintenance of any program or measure to which the
  agreement relates and the charges, if any, to be charged to persons to
  whom any of the benefits of the program or measure are made available.

::: HistoricalNote
- 1973-74, c. 21, s. 6
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Other agreements

- **[[7]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-7 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Subject
  to subsection (2), the Minister may enter into an agreement for the
  purposes set out in paragraphs 5(a) and (b) with any municipal
  authority or other organization or with any person, subject to the
  provisions of section 6, with such modifications as the circumstances
  require.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Provincial approval

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The Minister shall not conclude any agreement
  referred to in subsection (1) except with the approval of the
  government of the province in which the program or measure to which
  the agreement relates is to be implemented or the property to which
  the agreement relates is situated.

::: HistoricalNote
- 1973-74, c. 21, s. 7
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Measures for protection

**[[8]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-8 .sectionLabel}** The Minister may, in
cooperation with one or more provincial governments having an interest
therein, take such measures as the Minister deems necessary for the
protection of any species of wildlife in danger of extinction.

## [Acquisition of Lands]{.HTitleText1} {#h-469417 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Acquisition of Lands

- **[[9]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-9 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  Governor in Council may autho­rize the Minister to lease any lands, or
  purchase or acquire any lands or any interests or rights in any lands,
  for the purpose of research, conservation and interpretation in
  respect of

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} migratory birds; or

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} with the agreement of the government of the
    province having an interest therein, other wildlife.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Restrictions

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Lands or interests or rights in lands purchased or
  acquired under subsection (1) shall not be disposed of, and no person
  shall use or occupy the lands, except under the authority of this Act
  or the regulations.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Disposition or lease of lands

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} The Minister may authorize the disposition or lease
  of lands purchased or acquired under subsection (1) if, in the opinion
  of the Governor in Council, the disposition or lease is compatible
  with wildlife research, conservation and interpretation.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., 1985, c. W-9, s. 9
- 1994, c. 23, s. 11(F)
- 2004, c. 25, s. 115

## [General]{.HTitleText1} {#h-469429 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Property acquired by gift or bequest

**[[10]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-10 .sectionLabel}** Where Her Majesty has
acquired any money, securities or other property by gift, bequest or
otherwise for any purpose relating to wildlife, the Minister shall
expend, administer or dispose of the money, securities or other property
subject to the terms, if any, on which the money, securities or other
property was given, bequeathed or otherwise made available to Her
Majesty.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Designation of wildlife officers and
analysts

- **[[11]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-11 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  Minister may designate any person or class of persons to act as
  wildlife officers or analysts for the purposes of this Act and the
  regulations.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Designation of provincial government
  employees

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The Minister may not designate any person or class of
  persons employed by the government of a province unless that
  government agrees.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Certificate of designation

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} Every wildlife officer and analyst must be provided
  with a certificate of his or her designation as a wildlife officer, or
  as an analyst, as the case may be, in a form approved by the Minister
  and, on entering any place under this Act, the officer or analyst
  shall, if so requested, show the certificate to the occupant or person
  in charge of the place.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Powers of peace officers

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} For the purposes of this Act and the regulations,
  wildlife officers have all the powers of a peace officer, but the
  Minister may specify limits on those powers when designating any
  person or class of persons.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Exemptions for law enforcement
  activities

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} For the purpose of investigations and other law
  enforcement activities under this Act, the Minister may, on any terms
  and conditions the Minister considers necessary, exempt wildlife
  officers who are carrying out duties or functions under this Act, and
  persons acting under their direction and control, from the application
  of any provision of this Act or the regulations.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Obstruction

  [(6)]{.lawlabel} When a wildlife officer or an analyst is carrying out
  duties or functions under this Act or the regulations, no person shall

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} knowingly make any false or misleading statement
    either orally or in writing to the wildlife officer or analyst; or

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} otherwise wilfully obstruct the wildlife officer or
    analyst.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., 1985, c. W-9, s. 11
- 1994, c. 23, s. 13
- 2009, c. 14, s. 42

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Inspections

- **[[11.1]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-11.1
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} For the purpose of ensuring
  compliance with this Act and the regulations, a wildlife officer may,
  subject to subsection (3), at any reasonable time enter and inspect
  any place in which the officer believes, on reasonable grounds, there
  is any thing to which this Act or the regulations apply or any
  document relating to the administration of this Act or the
  regulations, and the wildlife officer may

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} open or cause to be opened any container that the
    wildlife officer believes, on reasonable grounds, contains any such
    thing or document;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} inspect the thing and take samples free of charge;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} require any person to produce the document for
    inspection or copying, in whole or in part; and

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} seize any thing by means of or in relation to which
    the wildlife officer believes, on reasonable grounds, this Act or
    the regulations have been contravened or that the wildlife officer
    believes, on reasonable grounds, will provide evidence of a
    contravention.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Analysts

  [(1.1)]{.lawlabel} An analyst may, for the purposes of this Act,
  accompany a wildlife officer who is carrying out an inspection of a
  place under this section, and the analyst may, when accompanying the
  wildlife officer, enter the place and exercise any of the powers
  described in paragraphs (1)(a) and (b).

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Conveyance

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} For the purposes of carrying out the inspection, the
  wildlife officer may stop a conveyance or direct that it be moved, by
  the route and in the manner that the officer may specify, to a place
  specified by the officer where the inspection can be carried out.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Dwelling-place

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} The wildlife officer may not enter a dwelling-place
  except with the consent of the occupant or person in charge of the
  dwelling-place or under the authority of a warrant.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Warrant

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} Where on [ex parte]{.otherLang lang="la"} application
  a justice, as defined in section 2 of the [Criminal
  Code](/eng/acts/C-46), is satisfied by information on oath that

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the conditions for entry described in
    subsection (1) exist in relation to a dwelling-place,

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} entry to the dwelling-place is necessary in
    relation to the administration of this Act or the regulations, and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} entry to the dwelling-place has been refused or
    there are reasonable grounds for believing that entry will be
    refused,

  the justice may issue a warrant authorizing the wildlife officer to
  enter the dwelling-place subject to any conditions that may be
  specified in the warrant.

::: HistoricalNote
- 1994, c. 23, s. 13
- 2009, c. 14, s. 43
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Right of passage

**[[11.11]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-11.11 .sectionLabel}** While carrying out
duties or functions under this Act, wildlife officers and analysts, and
any persons accompanying them, may enter on and pass through or over
private property without being liable for doing so and without any
person having the right to object to that use of the property.

::: HistoricalNote
- 2009, c. 14, s. 44
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Assistance

**[[11.12]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-11.12 .sectionLabel}** The owner or person
in charge of a place being inspected under section 11.1, and every
person found in the place, shall

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} give the wildlife officer or analyst all reasonable
  assistance to enable the wildlife officer or analyst to carry out
  their duties or functions under this Act; and

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} provide the wildlife officer or analyst with any
  information with respect to the administration of this Act that he or
  she may reasonably require.

::: HistoricalNote
- 2009, c. 14, s. 44
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Immunity

**[[11.13]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-11.13 .sectionLabel}** Wildlife officers,
analysts and persons acting under a wildlife officer's direction and
control are not personally liable for anything they do or omit to do in
good faith under this Act.

::: HistoricalNote
- 2009, c. 14, s. 44
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Search and seizure without warrant

**[[11.2]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-11.2 .sectionLabel}** For the purpose of
ensuring compliance with this Act and the regulations, a wildlife
officer may exercise the powers of search and seizure provided in
section 487 of the [Criminal Code](/eng/acts/C-46) without a warrant if
the conditions for obtaining a warrant exist but by reason of exigent
circumstances it would not be feasible to obtain the warrant.

::: HistoricalNote
- 1994, c. 23, s. 13
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Custody of things seized

- **[[11.3]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-11.3
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Subject to subsections (2) and (3),
  where a wildlife officer seizes a thing under this Act or under a
  warrant issued under the [Criminal Code](/eng/acts/C-46),

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} sections 489.1 and 490 of the [Criminal
    Code](/eng/acts/C-46) apply; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the wildlife officer, or any person that the
    officer may designate, shall retain custody of the thing subject to
    any order made under section 490 of the [Criminal
    Code](/eng/acts/C-46).

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Forfeiture where ownership not
  ascertainable

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Where the lawful ownership of or entitlement to the
  seized thing cannot be ascertained within thirty days after its
  seizure, the thing, or any proceeds of its disposition, are forfeited
  to

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} Her Majesty in right of Canada, if the thing was
    seized by a wildlife officer employed in the federal public
    administration; or

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} Her Majesty in right of a province, if the thing
    was seized by a wildlife officer employed by the government of that
    province.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Perishable things

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} Where the seized thing is perishable, the wildlife
  officer may dispose of it or destroy it, and any proceeds of its
  disposition must be

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} paid to the lawful owner or person lawfully
    entitled to possession of the thing, unless proceedings under this
    Act are commenced within ninety days after its seizure; or

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} retained by the wildlife officer pending the
    outcome of the proceedings.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Abandonment

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} The owner of the seized thing may abandon it to Her
  Majesty in right of Canada or a province.

::: HistoricalNote
- 1994, c. 23, s. 13
- 2003, c. 22, s. 224(E)
- 2004, c. 25, s. 117(F)

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Liability for costs

**[[11.5]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-11.5 .sectionLabel}** If a thing is seized
under this Act or under a warrant issued under the [Criminal
Code](/eng/acts/C-46), the person who owned the thing at the time it was
seized, the person who had charge or control of the thing immediately
before it was seized and the person who possessed it immediately before
it was seized are jointly and severally, or solidarily, liable for all
the costs of seizure, detention, maintenance and forfeiture, including
any destruction or disposal costs, incurred by Her Majesty in right of
Canada in relation to the thing in excess of any proceeds of its
disposition, if any.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Compliance order

- **[[11.7]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-11.7
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Whenever, during the course of an
  inspection or a search, a wildlife officer has reasonable grounds to
  believe that any provision of this Act or the regulations has been
  contravened by a person who is continuing the commission of the
  offence, or that any of those provisions are likely to be contravened,
  the wildlife officer may issue a compliance order directing any person
  who causes or contributes to the alleged contravention, or who is
  likely to do so, to take any of the measures referred to in
  subsection (2) that are reasonable in the circumstances and consistent
  with wildlife conservation and public safety in order to cease or
  refrain from committing the alleged contravention.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Specific measures

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The order may specify that the person to whom the
  order is directed take one or more of the following measures:

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} refrain from doing anything in contravention of
    this Act or the regulations, or do anything to comply with this Act
    or the regulations;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} stop or shut down any activity, work, undertaking
    or thing for a specified period;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} cease the operation of any activity or any part of
    a work, undertaking or thing until the wildlife officer is satisfied
    that the activity, work, undertaking or thing will be operated in
    accordance with this Act and the regulations;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} move any conveyance to another location including,
    in the case of a vessel, moving the vessel into port or, in the case
    of an aircraft, landing the aircraft;

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} unload or reload the contents of any conveyance;
    and

  - [(f)]{.lawlabel} take any other measure that the wildlife officer
    considers necessary to facilitate compliance with the order or to
    protect and conserve wildlife and wildlife habitat, including

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} maintaining records on any relevant matter,

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} reporting periodically to the wildlife officer,
      and

    - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} submitting to the wildlife officer any
      information, proposal or plan specified by the wildlife officer
      that sets out any action to be taken by the person with respect to
      the subject matter of the order.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Contents of order

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} Subject to section 11.8, an order must be made in
  writing and must set out

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the name of the person or persons to whom the order
    is directed;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the provision of this Act or the regulations that
    is alleged to have been or that is likely to be contravened;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} the relevant facts surrounding the alleged
    contravention;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} the measures to be taken;

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} the time or the day when each measure is to begin
    or the period during which it is to be carried out;

  - [(f)]{.lawlabel} subject to subsection (4), the duration of the
    order;

  - [(g)]{.lawlabel} a statement that a request for a review may be made
    to the Chief Review Officer; and

  - [(h)]{.lawlabel} the period within which a request for a review may
    be made.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Duration of order

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} An order may not be in force for a period of more
  than 180 days.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Failing to file report

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} For the purposes of subsection (1), a person who
  commits an offence by failing to file a report required by this Act or
  the regulations is deemed to be continuing the commission of the
  offence each day that the report is not filed.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}[Statutory Instruments
  Act](/eng/acts/S-22)

  [(6)]{.lawlabel} An order is not a statutory instrument for the
  purposes of the [Statutory Instruments Act](/eng/acts/S-22).

## [Offences and Punishment]{.HTitleText1} {#h-469657 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Offence

- **[[13]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-13 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Every
  person commits an offence who contravenes

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} subsection 11(6) or 11.91(1);

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} any provision of the regulations designated by
    regulations made under paragraph 12(k); or

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} an order made by a court under this Act.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Penalty --- individuals

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Every individual who commits an offence under
  subsection (1) is liable,

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} on conviction on indictment,

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} for a first offence, to a fine of not less than
      \$15,000 and not more than \$1,000,000 or to imprisonment for a
      term of not more than five years, or to both, and

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} for a second or subsequent offence, to a fine of
      not less than \$30,000 and not more than \$2,000,000 or to
      imprisonment for a term of not more than five years, or to both;
      or

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} on summary conviction,

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} for a first offence, to a fine of not less than
      \$5,000 and not more than \$300,000 or to imprisonment for a term
      of not more than six months, or to both, and

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} for a second or subsequent offence, to a fine of
      not less than \$10,000 and not more than \$600,000 or to
      imprisonment for a term of not more than six months, or to both.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Penalty --- other persons

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} Every person, other than an individual or a
  corporation referred to in subsection (4), who commits an offence
  under subsection (1) is liable,

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} on conviction on indictment,

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} for a first offence, to a fine of not less than
      \$500,000 and not more than \$6,000,000, and

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} for a second or subsequent offence, to a fine of
      not less than \$1,000,000 and not more than \$12,000,000; or

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} on summary conviction,

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} for a first offence, to a fine of not less than
      \$100,000 and not more than \$4,000,000, and

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} for a second or subsequent offence, to a fine of
      not less than \$200,000 and not more than \$8,000,000.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Penalty --- small revenue corporations

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} Every corporation that commits an offence under
  subsection (1) and that the court determines under section 13.02 to be
  a small revenue corporation is liable,

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} on conviction on indictment,

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} for a first offence, to a fine of not less than
      \$75,000 and not more than \$4,000,000, and

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} for a second or subsequent offence, to a fine of
      not less than \$150,000 and not more than \$8,000,000; or

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} on summary conviction,

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} for a first offence, to a fine of not less than
      \$25,000 and not more than \$2,000,000, and

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} for a second or subsequent offence, to a fine of
      not less than \$50,000 and not more than \$4,000,000.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Relief from minimum fine

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} The court may impose a fine that is less than the
  minimum amount provided for in this section if it is satisfied, on the
  basis of evidence submitted to the court, that the minimum fine would
  cause undue financial hardship. The court shall provide reasons if it
  imposes a fine that is less than the minimum amount provided for in
  this section.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Fundamental purpose of sentencing

**[[13.08]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-13.08 .sectionLabel}** The fundamental
purpose of sentencing for offences under this Act is to contribute, in
light of the significant and many threats to wildlife and the importance
of wildlife to the well-being of Canadians, to respect for the law
protecting wildlife through the imposition of just sanctions that have
as their objectives

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} to deter the offender and any other person from
  committing offences under this Act;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} to denounce unlawful conduct that damages or creates
  a risk of damage to wildlife; and

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} to recover wildlife and restore wildlife habitat.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Sentencing principles

- **[[13.09]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-13.09
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} In addition to the principles and
  factors that the court is otherwise required to consider, including
  those set out in sections 718.1 to 718.21 of the [Criminal
  Code](/eng/acts/C-46), the court shall consider the following
  principles when sentencing a person who is convicted of an offence
  under this Act:

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the amount of the fine should be increased to
    account for every aggravating factor associated with the offence,
    including the aggravating factors set out in subsection (2); and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the amount of the fine should reflect the gravity
    of each aggravating factor associated with the offence.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Aggravating factors

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The aggravating factors are the following:

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the offence caused damage or risk of damage to
    wildlife or wildlife habitat;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the offence caused damage or risk of damage to any
    unique, rare, particularly important or vulnerable wildlife or
    wildlife habitat;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} the damage caused by the offence is extensive,
    persistent or irreparable;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} the offender committed the offence intentionally or
    recklessly;

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} the offender failed to take reasonable steps to
    prevent the commission of the offence despite having the financial
    means to do so;

  - [(f)]{.lawlabel} by committing the offence or failing to take action
    to prevent its commission, the offender increased revenue or
    decreased costs or intended to increase revenue or decrease costs;

  - [(g)]{.lawlabel} the offender committed the offence despite having
    been warned by a wildlife officer of the circumstances that
    subsequently became the subject of the offence;

  - [(h)]{.lawlabel} the offender has a history of non-compliance with
    federal or provincial legislation that relates to environmental or
    wildlife conservation or protection; and

  - [(i)]{.lawlabel} after the commission of the offence, the offender

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} attempted to conceal its commission,

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} failed to take prompt action to prevent,
      mitigate or remediate its effects, or

    - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} failed to take prompt action to reduce the risk
      of committing similar offences in the future.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Absence of aggravating factor

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} The absence of an aggravating factor set out in
  subsection (2) is not a mitigating factor.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Meaning of
  [[damage]{.dfn}]{.DefinitionRef}

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} For the purposes of paragraphs (2)(a) to (c),
  [[damage]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} includes loss of use value and non-use
  value.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Reasons

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} If the court is satisfied of the existence of one or
  more of the aggravating factors set out in subsection (2) but decides
  not to increase the amount of the fine because of that factor, the
  court shall give reasons for that decision.

::: HistoricalNote
- 2009, c. 14, s. 48
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Documents admissible in evidence

- **[[13.091]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-13.091
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A document made, given or issued
  under this Act and appearing to be signed by an analyst is admissible
  in evidence and, in the absence of evidence to the contrary, is proof
  of the statements contained in the document without proof of the
  signature or official character of the person appearing to have signed
  the document.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Attendance of analyst

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The party against whom the document is produced may,
  with leave of the court, require the attendance of the analyst who
  signed it.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Notice

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} No document referred to in subsection (1) may be
  received in evidence unless the party intending to produce it has
  given to the party against whom it is intended to be produced
  reasonable notice of that intention together with a copy of the
  document.

::: HistoricalNote
- 2009, c. 14, s. 48
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Proof of offence

**[[13.1]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-13.1 .sectionLabel}** In any prosecution of
an offence under this Act, it is sufficient proof of the offence to
establish that it was committed by an employee, agent or mandatary of
the accused, whether or not the employee, agent or mandatary is
identified or prosecuted for the offence.

::: HistoricalNote
- 2009, c. 14, s. 48
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Continuing offence

**[[13.11]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-13.11 .sectionLabel}** A person who
commits or continues an offence under this Act on more than one day is
liable to be convicted for a separate offence for each day on which the
offence is committed or continued.

::: HistoricalNote
- 2009, c. 14, s. 48
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Offences involving more than one animal,
plant or other organism

**[[13.12]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-13.12 .sectionLabel}** If an offence under
this Act involves more than one animal, plant or other organism, the
fine to be imposed in respect of that offence may, despite sections 13
and 13.01, be the total of the fines that would have been imposed if
each of the animals, plants or other organisms had been the subject of a
separate information.

::: HistoricalNote
- 2009, c. 14, s. 48
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Application of fines

- **[[13.13]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-13.13
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} All fines received by the Receiver
  General in respect of the commission of an offence under this Act,
  other than fines collected under the [Contraventions
  Act](/eng/acts/C-38.7), are to be credited to the Environmental
  Damages Fund, an account in the accounts of Canada, and used for
  purposes related to protecting, conserving or restoring the
  environment or for administering that Fund.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Recommendations of court

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The court imposing the fine may recommend to the
  Minister that all or a portion of the fine credited to the
  Environmental Damages Fund be paid to a person or an organization
  specified by the court for a purpose referred to in subsection (1).

::: HistoricalNote
- 2009, c. 14, s. 48
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Forfeiture

- **[[14]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-14 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Where
  a person is convicted of an offence, the convicting court may, in
  addition to any punishment imposed, order that any seized thing by
  means of or in relation to which the offence was committed, or any
  proceeds of its disposition, be forfeited to Her Majesty.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Return where no forfeiture ordered

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Where the convicting court does not order the
  forfeiture, the seized thing, or the proceeds of its disposition, must
  be returned to its lawful owner or the person lawfully entitled to it.

::: HistoricalNote
- 1994, c. 23, s. 15
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Retention or sale

**[[15]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-15 .sectionLabel}** Where a fine is imposed
on a person convicted of an offence, any seized thing, or any proceeds
of its disposition, may be retained until the fine is paid or the thing
may be sold in satisfaction of the fine and the proceeds applied, in
whole or in part, in payment of the fine.

::: HistoricalNote
- 1994, c. 23, s. 15
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Orders of court

**[[16]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-16 .sectionLabel}** Where a person is
convicted of an offence, the court may, in addition to any punishment
imposed and having regard to the nature of the offence and the
circumstances surrounding its commission, make an order containing one
or more of the following prohibitions, directions or requirements:

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} prohibiting the person from doing any act or engaging
  in any activity that could, in the opinion of the court, result in the
  continuation or repetition of the offence;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} directing the person to take any action that the
  court considers appropriate to remedy or avoid any harm to any
  wildlife that resulted or may result from the commission of the
  offence;

- [(b.1)]{.lawlabel} directing the person to carry out environmental
  effects monitoring in the manner established by the Minister, or
  directing the person to pay, in the manner specified by the court, an
  amount for the purpose of environmental effects monitoring;

- [(b.2)]{.lawlabel} directing the person to implement an environmental
  management system that meets a recognized Canadian or international
  standard specified by the court;

- [(b.3)]{.lawlabel} directing the person to pay to Her Majesty in right
  of Canada an amount of money that the court considers appropriate for
  the purpose of promoting the proper management of wildlife or the
  conservation or protection of wildlife;

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} directing the person to publish, in the manner
  specified by the court, the facts relating to the commission of the
  offence and the details of the punishment imposed, including any
  orders made under this subsection;

- [(c.1)]{.lawlabel} directing the person to notify, at the person's own
  cost and in the manner specified by the court, any person aggrieved or
  affected by the person's conduct of the facts relating to the
  commission of the offence and of the details of the punishment
  imposed, including any orders made under this subsection;

- [(d)]{.lawlabel} directing the person to compensate any person,
  monetarily or otherwise, in whole or in part, for the cost of any
  remedial or preventive action taken, caused to be taken or to be taken
  as a result of the act or omission that constituted the offence,
  including costs of assessing appropriate remedial or preventive
  action;

- [(d.1)]{.lawlabel} directing the person to pay, in a manner specified
  by the court, an amount to enable research to be conducted into the
  protection or conservation of the wildlife or the wildlife habitat in
  respect of which the offence was committed;

- [(d.2)]{.lawlabel} directing the person to pay, in the manner
  prescribed by the court, an amount to an educational institution
  including for scholarships for students enrolled in studies related to
  the environment;

- [(d.3)]{.lawlabel} directing the person to pay, in the manner
  prescribed by the court, an amount to environmental or other groups to
  assist in their work in or for the community where the offence was
  committed;

- [(e)]{.lawlabel} directing the person to perform community service in
  accordance with any reasonable conditions that may be specified in the
  order;

- [(f)]{.lawlabel} directing the person to submit to the Minister, on
  application to the court by the Minister within three years after the
  conviction, any information respecting the activities of the person
  that the court considers appropriate in the circumstances;

- [(g)]{.lawlabel} requiring the person to comply with any other
  conditions that the court considers appropriate in the circumstances
  for securing the person's good conduct and for deterring the person
  and any other persons from committing offences under this Act;

- [(h)]{.lawlabel} directing the person to post a bond or provide a
  suretyship or pay into court an amount of money that the court
  considers appropriate for the purpose of ensuring compliance with any
  prohibition, direction or requirement under this section;

- [(i)]{.lawlabel} requiring the person to surrender to the Minister any
  permit or other authorization issued under this Act to the person; and

- [(j)]{.lawlabel} prohibiting the person from applying for any new
  permit or other authorization under this Act during any period that
  the court considers appropriate.

## [Ticketable Offences]{.HTitleText1} {#h-469897 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Procedure

- **[[19]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-19 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} In
  addition to the procedures set out in the [Criminal
  Code](/eng/acts/C-46) for commencing a proceeding, proceedings in
  respect of any offence prescribed by the regulations may be commenced
  by a wildlife officer

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} completing a ticket that consists of a summons
    portion and an information portion;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} delivering the summons portion to the accused or
    mailing it to the accused at the accused's latest known address; and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} filing the information portion with a court of
    competent jurisdiction before the summons portion has been delivered
    or mailed or as soon as is practicable afterward.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Content of ticket

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The summons and information portions of the ticket
  must

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} set out a description of the offence and the time
    and place of its alleged commission;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} include a statement, signed by the wildlife officer
    who completes the ticket, that the officer has reasonable grounds to
    believe that the accused committed the offence;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} set out the amount of the fine prescribed by the
    regulations for the offence and the manner in which and period
    within which it may be paid;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} include a statement that if the accused pays the
    fine within the period set out in the ticket, a conviction will be
    entered and recorded against the accused; and

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} include a statement that if the accused wishes to
    plead not guilty or for any other reason fails to pay the fine
    within the period set out in the ticket, the accused must appear in
    the court on the day and at the time set out in the ticket.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Notice of forfeiture

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} Where a thing is seized under this Act and
  proceedings relating to it are commenced by way of the ticketing
  procedure, the wildlife officer who completes the ticket shall give
  written notice to the accused that, if the accused pays the fine
  prescribed by the regulations within the period set out in the ticket,
  the thing, or any proceeds of its disposition, will be immediately
  forfeited to Her Majesty.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Consequences of payment

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} Where an accused to whom the summons portion of a
  ticket is delivered or mailed pays the prescribed fine within the
  period set out in the ticket,

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the payment constitutes a plea of guilty to the
    offence and a conviction must be entered against the accused and no
    further action may be taken against the accused in respect of that
    offence; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} notwithstanding section 11.3, any thing seized from
    the accused under this Act that relates to the offence, or any
    proceeds of its disposition, are forfeited to

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} Her Majesty in right of Canada, if the thing was
      seized by a wildlife officer employed in the federal public
      administration, or

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} Her Majesty in right of a province, if the thing
      was seized by a wildlife officer employed by the government of
      that province.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Regulations

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} The Governor in Council may make regulations
  prescribing

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} offences in respect of which this section applies
    and the manner in which the offences are to be described in tickets;
    and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the amount of the fine for a prescribed offence,
    but the amount may not exceed \$1,000.

- 2003, c. 22, s. 224(E)
