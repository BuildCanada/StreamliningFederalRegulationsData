## Canadian Human Rights Act {#canadian-human-rights-act .Title-of-Act}

[R.S.C.]{.abbr title="Revised Statutes of Canada"}, 1985, c. H-6
:::

An Act to extend the laws in Canada that proscribe discrimination
::::

## [Short Title]{.HTitleText1} {#h-256790 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Short title

**[[1]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-1 .sectionLabel}** This Act may be cited as
the [Canadian Human Rights Act](/eng/acts/H-6).

::: HistoricalNote
- 1976-77, c. 33, s. 1
:::

## [Purpose of Act]{.HTitleText1} {#h-256795 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Purpose

**[[2]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-2 .sectionLabel}** The purpose of this Act is
to extend the laws in Canada to give effect, within the purview of
matters coming within the legislative authority of Parliament, to the
principle that all individuals should have an opportunity equal with
other individuals to make for themselves the lives that they are able
and wish to have and to have their needs accommodated, consistent with
their duties and obligations as members of society, without being
hindered in or prevented from doing so by discriminatory practices based
on race, national or ethnic origin, colour, religion, age, sex, sexual
orientation, gender identity or expression, marital status, family
status, genetic characteristics, disability or conviction for an offence
for which a pardon has been granted or in respect of which a record
suspension has been ordered.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., 1985, c. H-6, s. 2
- 1996, c. 14, s. 1
- 1998, c. 9, s. 9
- 2012, c. 1, s. 137(E)
- 2017, c. 3, ss. 9, 11, c. 13, s. 1
:::

## [PART I]{.HLabel1}[Proscribed Discrimination]{.HTitleText1} {#h-256800 .Part}

### [General]{.HTitleText2} {#h-256801 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Prohibited grounds of discrimination

- **[[3]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-3 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} For all
  purposes of this Act, the prohibited grounds of discrimination are
  race, national or ethnic origin, colour, religion, age, sex, sexual
  orientation, gender identity or expression, marital status, family
  status, genetic characteristics, disability and conviction for an
  offence for which a pardon has been granted or in respect of which a
  record suspension has been ordered.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Idem

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Where the ground of discrimination is pregnancy or
  child-birth, the discrimination shall be deemed to be on the ground of
  sex.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Idem

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} Where the ground of discrimination is refusal of a
  request to undergo a genetic test or to disclose, or authorize the
  disclosure of, the results of a genetic test, the discrimination shall
  be deemed to be on the ground of genetic characteristics.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., 1985, c. H-6, s. 3
- 1996, c. 14, s. 2
- 2012, c. 1, s. 138(E)
- 2017, c. 3, ss. 10, 11, c. 13, s. 2
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Multiple grounds of discrimination

**[[3.1]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-3.1 .sectionLabel}** For greater certainty,
a discriminatory practice includes a practice based on one or more
prohibited grounds of discrimination or on the effect of a combination
of prohibited grounds.

::: HistoricalNote
- 1998, c. 9, s. 11
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Orders regarding discriminatory practices

**[[4]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-4 .sectionLabel}** A discriminatory practice,
as described in sections 5 to 14.1, may be the subject of a complaint
under Part III and anyone found to be engaging or to have engaged in a
discriminatory practice may be made subject to an order as provided in
section 53.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., 1985, c. H-6, s. 4
- 1998, c. 9, s. 11
- 2013, c. 37, s. 1

### [Discriminatory Practices]{.HTitleText2} {#h-256819 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Denial of good, service, facility or
accommodation

**[[5]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-5 .sectionLabel}** It is a discriminatory
practice in the provision of goods, services, facilities or
accommodation customarily available to the general public

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} to deny, or to deny access to, any such good,
  service, facility or accommodation to any individual, or

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} to differentiate adversely in relation to any
  individual,

on a prohibited ground of discrimination.

::: HistoricalNote
- 1976-77, c. 33, s. 5
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Denial of commercial premises or
residential accommodation

**[[6]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-6 .sectionLabel}** It is a discriminatory
practice in the provision of commercial premises or residential
accommodation

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} to deny occupancy of such premises or accommodation
  to any individual, or

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} to differentiate adversely in relation to any
  individual,

on a prohibited ground of discrimination.

::: HistoricalNote
- 1976-77, c. 33, s. 6
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Employment

**[[7]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-7 .sectionLabel}** It is a discriminatory
practice, directly or indirectly,

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} to refuse to employ or continue to employ any
  individual, or

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} in the course of employment, to differentiate
  adversely in relation to an employee,

on a prohibited ground of discrimination.

::: HistoricalNote
- 1976-77, c. 33, s. 7
- 1980-81-82-83, c. 143, s. 3(F)
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Employment applications, advertisements

**[[8]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-8 .sectionLabel}** It is a discriminatory
practice

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} to use or circulate any form of application for
  employment, or

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} in connection with employment or prospective
  employment, to publish any advertisement or to make any written or
  oral inquiry

that expresses or implies any limitation, specification or preference
based on a prohibited ground of discrimination.

::: HistoricalNote
- 1976-77, c. 33, s. 8
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Employee organizations

- **[[9]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-9 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} It is a
  discriminatory practice for an employee organization on a prohibited
  ground of discrimination

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} to exclude an individual from full membership in
    the organization;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} to expel or suspend a member of the organization;
    or

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} to limit, segregate, classify or otherwise act in
    relation to an individual in a way that would deprive the individual
    of employment opportunities, or limit employment opportunities or
    otherwise adversely affect the status of the individual, where the
    individual is a member of the organization or where any of the
    obligations of the organization pursuant to a collective agreement
    relate to the individual.

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} [\[Repealed, 2011, c. 24, s. 165\]]{.Repealed}

- [(3)]{.lawlabel} [\[Repealed, 1998, c. 9, s. 12\]]{.Repealed}

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., 1985, c. H-6, s. 9
- 1998, c. 9, s. 12
- 2011, c. 24, s. 165
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Discriminatory policy or practice

**[[10]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-10 .sectionLabel}** It is a discriminatory
practice for an employer, employee organization or employer organization

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} to establish or pursue a policy or practice, or

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} to enter into an agreement affecting recruitment,
  referral, hiring, promotion, training, apprenticeship, transfer or any
  other matter relating to employment or prospective employment,

that deprives or tends to deprive an individual or class of individuals
of any employment opportunities on a prohibited ground of
discrimination.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., 1985, c. H-6, s. 10
- 1998, c. 9, s. 13(E)
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Equal wages

- **[[11]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-11 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} It is
  a discriminatory practice for an employer to establish or maintain
  differences in wages between male and female employees employed in the
  same establishment who are performing work of equal value.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Assessment of value of work

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} In assessing the value of work performed by employees
  employed in the same establishment, the criterion to be applied is the
  composite of the skill, effort and responsibility required in the
  performance of the work and the conditions under which the work is
  performed.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Separate establishments

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} Separate establishments established or maintained by
  an employer solely or principally for the purpose of establishing or
  maintaining differences in wages between male and female employees
  shall be deemed for the purposes of this section to be the same
  establishment.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Different wages based on prescribed
  reasonable factors

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} Notwithstanding subsection (1), it is not a
  discriminatory practice to pay to male and female employees different
  wages if the difference is based on a factor prescribed by guidelines,
  issued by the Canadian Human Rights Commission pursuant to subsection
  27(2), to be a reasonable factor that justifies the difference.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Idem

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} For greater certainty, sex does not constitute a
  reasonable factor justifying a difference in wages.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}No reduction of wages

  [(6)]{.lawlabel} An employer shall not reduce wages in order to
  eliminate a discriminatory practice described in this section.

- Definition of [[wages]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  [(7)]{.lawlabel} For the purposes of this section,
  [[wages]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means any form of remuneration payable
  for work performed by an individual and includes

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} salaries, commissions, vacation pay, dismissal
    wages and bonuses;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} reasonable value for board, rent, housing and
    lodging;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} payments in kind;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} employer contributions to pension funds or plans,
    long-term disability plans and all forms of health insurance plans;
    and

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} any other advantage received directly or indirectly
    from the individual's employer.

::: HistoricalNote
- 1976-77, c. 33, s. 11
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Publication of discriminatory notices,
etc.

**[[12]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-12 .sectionLabel}** It is a discriminatory
practice to publish or display before the public or to cause to be
published or displayed before the public any notice, sign, symbol,
emblem or other representation that

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} expresses or implies discrimination or an intention
  to discriminate, or

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} incites or is calculated to incite others to
  discriminate

if the discrimination expressed or implied, intended to be expressed or
implied or incited or calculated to be incited would otherwise, if
engaged in, be a discriminatory practice described in any of sections 5
to 11 or in section 14.

::: HistoricalNote
- 1976-77, c. 33, s. 12
- 1980-81-82-83, c. 143, s. 6
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Harassment

- **[[14]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-14 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} It is
  a discriminatory practice,

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} in the provision of goods, services, facilities or
    accommodation customarily available to the general public,

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} in the provision of commercial premises or
    residential accommodation, or

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} in matters related to employment,

  to harass an individual on a prohibited ground of discrimination.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Sexual harassment

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Without limiting the generality of subsection (1),
  sexual harassment shall, for the purposes of that subsection, be
  deemed to be harassment on a prohibited ground of discrimination.

::: HistoricalNote
- 1980-81-82-83, c. 143, s. 7
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Retaliation

**[[14.1]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-14.1 .sectionLabel}** It is a
discriminatory practice for a person against whom a complaint has been
filed under Part III, or any person acting on their behalf, to retaliate
or threaten retaliation against the individual who filed the complaint
or the alleged victim.

::: HistoricalNote
- 1998, c. 9, s. 14
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Exceptions

- **[[15]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-15 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} It is
  not a discriminatory practice if

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} any refusal, exclusion, expulsion, suspension,
    limitation, specification or preference in relation to any
    employment is established by an employer to be based on a [bona
    fide]{.otherLang lang="la"} occupational requirement;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} employment of an individual is refused or
    terminated because that individual has not reached the minimum age,
    or has reached the maximum age, that applies to that employment by
    law or under regulations, which may be made by the Governor in
    Council for the purposes of this paragraph;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} [\[Repealed, 2011, c. 24, s. 166\]]{.Repealed}

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} the terms and conditions of any pension fund or
    plan established by an employer, employee organization or employer
    organization provide for the compulsory vesting or locking-in of
    pension contributions at a fixed or determinable age in accordance
    with sections 17 and 18 of the [Pension Benefits Standards Act,
    1985](/eng/acts/P-7.01);

  - [(d.1)]{.lawlabel} the terms of any pooled registered pension plan
    provide for variable payments or the transfer of funds only at a
    fixed age under sections 48 or 55, respectively, of the [Pooled
    Registered Pension Plans Act](/eng/acts/P-15.3);

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} an individual is discriminated against on a
    prohibited ground of discrimination in a manner that is prescribed
    by guidelines, issued by the Canadian Human Rights Commission
    pursuant to subsection 27(2), to be reasonable;

  - [(f)]{.lawlabel} an employer, employee organization or employer
    organization grants a female employee special leave or benefits in
    connection with pregnancy or child-birth or grants employees special
    leave or benefits to assist them in the care of their children; or

  - [(g)]{.lawlabel} in the circumstances described in section 5 or 6,
    an individual is denied any goods, services, facilities or
    accommodation or access thereto or occupancy of any commercial
    premises or residential accommodation or is a victim of any adverse
    differentiation and there is [bona fide]{.otherLang lang="la"}
    justification for that denial or differentiation.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Accommodation of needs

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} For any practice mentioned in paragraph (1)(a) to be
  considered to be based on a [bona fide]{.otherLang lang="la"}
  occupational requirement and for any practice mentioned in paragraph
  (1)(g) to be considered to have a [bona fide]{.otherLang lang="la"}
  justification, it must be established that accommodation of the needs
  of an individual or a class of individuals affected would impose undue
  hardship on the person who would have to accommodate those needs,
  considering health, safety and cost.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Regulations

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} The Governor in Council may make regulations
  prescribing standards for assessing undue hardship.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Publication of proposed regulations

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} Each regulation that the Governor in Council proposes
  to make under subsection (3) shall be published in the [Canada
  Gazette](http://www.gazette.gc.ca/) and a reasonable opportunity shall
  be given to interested persons to make representations in respect of
  it.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Consultations

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} The Canadian Human Rights Commission shall conduct
  public consultations concerning any regulation proposed to be made by
  the Governor in Council under subsection (3) and shall file a report
  of the results of the consultations with the Minister within a
  reasonable time after the publication of the proposed regulation in
  the [Canada Gazette](http://www.gazette.gc.ca/).

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Exception

  [(6)]{.lawlabel} A proposed regulation need not be published more than
  once, whether or not it has been amended as a result of any
  representations.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Making of regulations

  [(7)]{.lawlabel} The Governor in Council may proceed to make
  regulations under subsection (3) after six months have elapsed since
  the publication of the proposed regulations in the [Canada
  Gazette](http://www.gazette.gc.ca/), whether or not a report described
  in subsection (5) is filed.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Application

  [(8)]{.lawlabel} This section applies in respect of a practice
  regardless of whether it results in direct discrimination or adverse
  effect discrimination.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Universality of service for Canadian
  Forces

  [(9)]{.lawlabel} Subsection (2) is subject to the principle of
  universality of service under which members of the Canadian Forces
  must at all times and under any circumstances perform any functions
  that they may be required to perform.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., 1985, c. H-6, s. 15
- R.S., 1985, c. 32 (2nd Supp.), s. 41
- 1998, c. 9, ss. 10, 15
- 2011, c. 24, s. 166
- 2012, c. 16, s. 83
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Special programs

- **[[16]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-16 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} It is
  not a discriminatory practice for a person to adopt or carry out a
  special program, plan or arrangement designed to prevent disadvantages
  that are likely to be suffered by, or to eliminate or reduce
  disadvantages that are suffered by, any group of individuals when
  those disadvantages would be based on or related to the prohibited
  grounds of discrimination, by improving opportunities respecting
  goods, services, facilities, accommodation or employment in relation
  to that group.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Advice and assistance

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The Canadian Human Rights Commission, may

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} make general recommendations concerning desirable
    objectives for special programs, plans or arrangements referred to
    in subsection (1); and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} on application, give such advice and assistance
    with respect to the adoption or carrying out of a special program,
    plan or arrangement referred to in subsection (1) as will serve to
    aid in the achievement of the objectives the program, plan or
    arrangement was designed to achieve.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Collection of information relating to
  prohibited grounds

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} It is not a discriminatory practice to collect
  information relating to a prohibited ground of discrimination if the
  information is intended to be used in adopting or carrying out a
  special program, plan or arrangement under subsection (1).

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., 1985, c. H-6, s. 16
- 1998, c. 9, s. 16
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Plans to meet the needs of disabled
persons

- **[[17]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-17 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A
  person who proposes to implement a plan for adapting any services,
  facilities, premises, equipment or operations to meet the needs of
  persons arising from a disability may apply to the Canadian Human
  Rights Commission for approval of the plan.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Approval of plan

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The Commission may, by written notice to a person
  making an application pursuant to subsection (1), approve the plan if
  the Commission is satisfied that the plan is appropriate for meeting
  the needs of persons arising from a disability.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Effect of approval of accommodation
  plan

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} Where any services, facilities, premises, equipment
  or operations are adapted in accordance with a plan approved under
  subsection (2), matters for which the plan provides do not constitute
  any basis for a complaint under Part III regarding discrimination
  based on any disability in respect of which the plan was approved.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Notice when application not granted

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} When the Commission decides not to grant an
  application made pursuant to subsection (1), it shall send a written
  notice of its decision to the applicant setting out the reasons for
  its decision.

::: HistoricalNote
- 1980-81-82-83, c. 143, s. 9
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Rescinding approval of plan

- **[[18]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-18 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} If the
  Canadian Human Rights Commission is satisfied that, by reason of any
  change in circumstances, a plan approved under subsection 17(2) has
  ceased to be appropriate for meeting the needs of persons arising from
  a disability, the Commission may, by written notice to the person who
  proposes to carry out or maintains the adaptation contemplated by the
  plan or any part thereof, rescind its approval of the plan to the
  extent required by the change in circumstances.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Effect where approval rescinded

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} To the extent to which approval of a plan is
  rescinded under subsection (1), subsection 17(3) does not apply to the
  plan if the discriminatory practice to which the complaint relates is
  subsequent to the rescission of the approval.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Statement of reasons for rescinding
  approval

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} Where the Commission rescinds approval of a plan
  pursuant to subsection (1), it shall include in the notice referred to
  therein a statement of its reasons therefor.

::: HistoricalNote
- 1980-81-82-83, c. 143, s. 9
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Opportunity to make representations

- **[[19]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-19 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Before
  making its decision on an application or rescinding approval of a plan
  pursuant to section 17 or 18, the Canadian Human Rights Commission
  shall afford each person directly concerned with the matter an
  opportunity to make representations with respect thereto.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Restriction on deeming plan
  inappropriate

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} For the purposes of sections 17 and 18, a plan shall
  not, by reason only that it does not conform to any standards
  prescribed pursuant to section 24, be deemed to be inappropriate for
  meeting the needs of persons arising from disability.

::: HistoricalNote
- 1980-81-82-83, c. 143, s. 9
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Certain provisions not discriminatory

**[[20]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-20 .sectionLabel}** A provision of a pension
or insurance fund or plan that preserves rights acquired before March 1,
1978 or that preserves pension or other benefits accrued before that day
does not constitute the basis for a complaint under Part III that an
employer, employee organization or employer organization is engaging or
has engaged in a discriminatory practice.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., 1985, c. H-6, s. 20
- 1998, c. 9, s. 17
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Funds and plans

**[[21]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-21 .sectionLabel}** The establishment of
separate pension funds or plans for different groups of employees does
not constitute the basis for a complaint under Part III that an
employer, employee organization or employer organization is engaging or
has engaged in a discriminatory practice if the employees are not
grouped in those funds or plans according to a prohibited ground of
discrimination.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., 1985, c. H-6, s. 21
- 1998, c. 9, s. 17
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Regulations

**[[22]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-22 .sectionLabel}** The Governor in Council
may, by regulation, prescribe the provisions of any pension or insurance
fund or plan, in addition to the provisions described in sections 20 and
21, that do not constitute the basis for a complaint under Part III that
an employer, employee organization or employer organization is engaging
or has engaged in a discriminatory practice.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., 1985, c. H-6, s. 22
- 1998, c. 9, s. 17
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Regulations

**[[23]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-23 .sectionLabel}** The Governor in Council
may make regulations respecting the terms and conditions to be included
in or applicable to any contract, licence or grant made or granted by
Her Majesty in right of Canada providing for

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} the prohibition of discriminatory practices described
  in sections 5 to 14.1; and

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} the resolution, by the procedure set out in Part III,
  of complaints of discriminatory practices contrary to such terms and
  conditions.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., 1985, c. H-6, s. 23
- 1998, c. 9, s. 18
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Accessibility standards

- **[[24]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-24 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  Governor in Council may, for the benefit of persons having any
  disability, make regulations prescribing standards of accessibility to
  services, facilities or premises.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Effect of meeting accessibility
  standards

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Where standards prescribed pursuant to subsection (1)
  are met in providing access to any services, facilities or premises, a
  matter of access thereto does not constitute any basis for a complaint
  under Part III regarding discrimination based on any disability in
  respect of which the standards are prescribed.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Publication of proposed regulations

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} Subject to subsection (4), a copy of each regulation
  that the Governor in Council proposes to make pursuant to this section

## [PART II]{.HLabel1}[Canadian Human Rights Commission]{.HTitleText1} {#h-257019 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Commission established

- **[[26]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-26 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A

  Commission, in this Act referred to as the "Commission", consisting of
  a Chief Commissioner, a Deputy Chief Commissioner, members referred to
  as the "Accessibility Commissioner" and the "Pay Equity Commissioner"

  appointed by the Governor in Council.

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The Chief Commissioner, the Deputy Chief

  consideration, in appointing the Pay Equity Commissioner, knowledge
  and experience in relation to pay equity matters.

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} Each full-time member of the Commission may be
  appointed for a term not exceeding seven years and each part-time
  member may be appointed for a term not exceeding three years.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Tenure

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} Each member of the Commission holds office during
  good behaviour but may be removed by the Governor in Council on
  address of the Senate and House of Commons.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Re-appointment

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} A member of the Commission is eligible to be
  re-appointed in the same or another capacity.

### [Powers, Duties and Functions]{.HTitleText2} {#h-257033 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Powers, duties and functions

- **[[27]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-27 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} In
  addition to its duties under Part III with respect to complaints
  regarding discriminatory practices, the Commission is generally
  responsible for the administration of this Part and Parts I and III
  and

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} shall develop and conduct information programs to
    foster public understanding of this Act and of the role and
    activities of the Commission thereunder and to foster public
    recognition of the principle described in section 2;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} shall undertake or sponsor research programs
    relating to its duties and functions under this Act and respecting
    the principle described in section 2;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} shall maintain close liaison with similar bodies or
    authorities in the provinces in order to foster common policies and
    practices and to avoid conflicts respecting the handling of
    complaints in cases of overlapping jurisdiction;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} shall perform duties and functions to be performed
    by it pursuant to any agreement entered into under subsection 28(2);

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} may consider such recommendations, suggestions and
    requests concerning human rights and freedoms as it receives from
    any source and, where deemed by the Commission to be appropriate,
    include in a report referred to in section 61 reference to and
    comment on any such recommendation, suggestion or request;

  - [(f)]{.lawlabel} shall carry out or cause to be carried out such
    studies concerning human rights and freedoms as may be referred to
    it by the Minister of Justice and include in a report referred to in
    section 61 a report setting out the results of each such study
    together with such recommendations in relation thereto as it
    considers appropriate;

  - [(g)]{.lawlabel} may review any regulations, rules, orders, by-laws
    and other instruments made pursuant to an Act of Parliament and,
    where deemed by the Commission to be appropriate, include in a
    report referred to in section 61 reference to and comment on any
    provision thereof that in its opinion is inconsistent with the
    principle described in section 2; and

  - [(h)]{.lawlabel} shall, so far as is practical and consistent with
    the application of Part III, try by persuasion, publicity or any
    other means that it considers appropriate to discourage and reduce
    discriminatory practices referred to in sections 5 to 14.1.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Complaints

- **[[40]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-40
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Subject to subsections (5) and (7),
  any individual or group of individuals having reasonable grounds for
  believing that a person is engaging or has engaged in a discriminatory
  practice may file with the Commission a complaint in a form acceptable
  to the Commission.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Consent of victim

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} If a complaint is made by someone other than the
  individual who is alleged to be the victim of the discriminatory
  practice to which the complaint relates, the Commission may refuse to
  deal with the complaint unless the alleged victim consents thereto.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Investigation commenced by Commission

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} Where the Commission has reasonable grounds for
  believing that a person is engaging or has engaged in a discriminatory
  practice, the Commission may initiate a complaint.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Limitation

  [(3.1)]{.lawlabel} No complaint may be initiated under subsection (3)
  as a result of information obtained by the Commission in the course of
  the administration of the [Employment Equity Act](/eng/acts/E-5.401).

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Complaints may be dealt with together

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} If complaints are filed jointly or separately by more
  than one individual or group alleging that a particular person is
  engaging or has engaged in a discriminatory practice or a series of
  similar discriminatory practices and the Commission is satisfied that
  the complaints involve substantially the same issues of fact and law,
  it may deal with the complaints together under this Part and may
  request the Chairperson of the Tribunal to institute a single inquiry
  into the complaints under section 49.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Multiple allegations

  [(4.1)]{.lawlabel} If a complaint alleging a discriminatory practice
  under section 11 also includes allegations to which section 11 does
  not apply, the Pay Equity Division may

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} exercise the powers and perform the duties and
    functions of the Commission under this Part with respect to the
    complaint as filed; or

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} at any stage after the filing of the complaint,
    sever the complaint and refer to the Commission some or all of the
    allegations that do not allege a discriminatory practice under
    section 11.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}New complaint

  [(4.2)]{.lawlabel} If the Pay Equity Division refers allegations
  severed from a complaint to the Commission under paragraph (4.1)(b),
  the Commission is deemed to have received a new complaint for the
  purposes of section 40.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}No complaints to be considered in
  certain cases

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} No complaint in relation to a discriminatory practice
  may be dealt with by the Commission under this Part unless the act or
  omission that constitutes the practice

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} occurred in Canada and the victim of the practice
    was at the time of the act or omission either lawfully present in
    Canada or, if temporarily absent from Canada, entitled to return to
    Canada;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} occurred in Canada and was a discriminatory
    practice within the meaning of section 5, 8, 10 or 12 in respect of
    which no particular individual is identifiable as the victim;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} occurred outside Canada and the victim of the
    practice was at the time of the act or omission a Canadian citizen
    or an individual lawfully admitted to Canada for permanent
    residence.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Determination of status

  [(6)]{.lawlabel} Where a question arises under subsection (5) as to
  the status of an individual in relation to a complaint, the Commission
  shall refer the question of status to the appropriate Minister and
  shall not proceed with the complaint unless the question of status is
  resolved thereby in favour of the complainant.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}No complaints to be dealt with in
  certain cases

  [(7)]{.lawlabel} No complaint may be dealt with by the Commission
  pursuant to subsection (1) that relates to the terms and conditions of
  a superannuation or pension fund or plan, if the relief sought would
  require action to be taken that would deprive any contributor to,
  participant in or member of, the fund or plan of any rights acquired
  under the fund or plan before March 1, 1978 or of any pension or other
  benefits accrued under the fund or plan to that date, including

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} any rights and benefits based on a particular age
    of retirement; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} any accrued survivor's benefits.

  [(2.1)]{.lawlabel} Subject to such limitations as the Governor in
  Council may prescribe in the interests of national defence or

  carry out such inquiries as are reasonably necessary for the
  investigation of a complaint.

  [(2.2)]{.lawlabel} Where on [ex parte]{.otherLang lang="la"}
  application a judge of the Federal Court is satisfied by information
  on oath that there are reasonable grounds to believe that there is in
  any premises any evidence relevant to the investigation of a
  complaint, the judge may issue a warrant under the judge's hand
  authorizing the investigator named therein to enter and search those
  premises for any such evidence subject to such conditions as may be
  specified in the warrant.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Use of force

  [(2.3)]{.lawlabel} In executing a warrant issued under subsection
  (2.2), the investigator named therein shall not use force unless the

  any premises entered pursuant to this section to produce for

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Appointment of inquirer

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} On receipt of a request referred to in paragraph
  (2)(c), the Governor in Council may, on the recommendation of the
  Minister, appoint a judge of a superior court to conduct the inquiry.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Powers

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} The judge has all the powers, rights and privileges
  that are vested in a superior court, including the power to

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} issue a summons requiring any person to appear at
    the time and place specified in the summons in order to testify
    about all matters within the person's knowledge relative to the
    inquiry and to produce any document or thing relative to the inquiry
    that the person has or controls; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} administer oaths and examine any person on oath.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Staff

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} The judge may engage the services of counsel and
  other persons having technical or specialized knowledge to assist the
  judge in conducting the inquiry, and may establish the terms and
  conditions of their engagement and, with the approval of the Treasury
  Board, fix and pay their remuneration and expenses.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Inquiry in public

  [(6)]{.lawlabel} Subject to subsections (7) and (8), an inquiry shall
  be conducted in public.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Confidentiality of inquiry

  [(7)]{.lawlabel} The judge may, on application, take any appropriate
  measures and make any order that the judge considers necessary to
  ensure the confidentiality of the inquiry if, after having considered
  all available alternative measures, the judge is satisfied that

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} there is a real and substantial risk that matters
    involving public security will be disclosed;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} there is a real and substantial risk to the
    fairness of the inquiry such that the need to prevent disclosure
    outweighs the societal interest that the inquiry be conducted in
    public; or

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} there is a serious possibility that the life,
    liberty or security of a person will be endangered.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Confidentiality of application

  [(8)]{.lawlabel} If the judge considers it appropriate, the judge may
  take any measures and make any order that the judge considers
  necessary to ensure the confidentiality of a hearing held in respect
  of an application under subsection (7).

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Rules of evidence

  [(9)]{.lawlabel} In conducting an inquiry, the judge is not bound by
  any legal or technical rules of evidence and may receive, and base a
  decision on, evidence presented in the proceedings that the judge
  considers credible or trustworthy in the circumstances of the case.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Intervenors

  [(10)]{.lawlabel} An interested party may, with leave of the judge,
  intervene in an inquiry on any terms and conditions that the judge
  considers appropriate.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Right to be heard

  [(11)]{.lawlabel} The member who is the subject of the inquiry shall
  be given reasonable notice of the subject-matter of the inquiry and of
  the time and place of any hearing and shall be given an opportunity,
  in person or by counsel, to be heard at the hearing, to cross-examine
  witnesses and to present evidence.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Report to Minister

  [(12)]{.lawlabel} After an inquiry has been completed, the judge shall
  submit a report containing the judge's findings and recommendations,
  if any, to the Minister.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Recommendations

  [(13)]{.lawlabel} The judge may, in the report, recommend that the
  member be suspended without pay or removed from office or that any
  other disciplinary measure or any remedial measure be taken if, in the
  judge's opinion, the member

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} has become incapacitated from the proper execution
    of that office by reason of infirmity;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} has been guilty of misconduct;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} has failed in the proper execution of that office;
    or

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} has been placed, by conduct or otherwise, in a
    position that is incompatible with the due execution of that office.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Transmission of report to Governor in
  Council

  [(14)]{.lawlabel} When the Minister receives the report, the Minister
  shall send it to the Governor in Council who may, if the Governor in
  Council considers it appropriate, suspend the member without pay,
  remove the member from office or impose any other disciplinary measure
  or any remedial measure.

### [Inquiries into Complaints]{.HTitleText2} {#h-257423 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Request for inquiry

- **[[49]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-49 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} At any
  stage after the filing of a complaint, the Commission may request the
  Chairperson of the Tribunal to institute an inquiry into the complaint
  if the Commission is satisfied that, having regard to all the
  circumstances of the complaint, an inquiry is warranted.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Chairperson to institute inquiry

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} On receipt of a request, the Chairperson shall
  institute an inquiry by assigning a member of the Tribunal to inquire
  into the complaint, but the Chairperson may assign a panel of three
  members if he or she considers that the complexity of the complaint
  requires the inquiry to be conducted by three members.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Chair of panel

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} If a panel of three members has been assigned to
  inquire into the complaint, the Chairperson shall designate one of
  them to chair the inquiry, but the Chairperson shall chair the inquiry
  if he or she is a member of the panel.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Copy of rules to parties

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} The Chairperson shall make a copy of the rules of
  procedure available to each party to the complaint.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Qualification of member

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} If the complaint involves a question about whether
  another Act or a regulation made under another Act is inconsistent
  with this Act or a regulation made under it, the member assigned to
  inquire into the complaint or, if three members have been assigned,
  the member chairing the inquiry, must be a member of the bar of a
  province or the Chambre des notaires du Québec.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Question raised subsequently

  [(6)]{.lawlabel} If a question as described in subsection (5) arises
  after a member or panel has been assigned and the requirements of that
  subsection are not met, the inquiry shall nevertheless proceed with
  the member or panel as designated.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Conduct of inquiry

- **[[50]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-50 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} After
  due notice to the Commission, the complainant, the person against whom
  the complaint was made and, at the discretion of the member or panel
  conducting the inquiry, any other interested party, the member or
  panel shall inquire into the complaint and shall give all parties to
  whom notice has been given a full and ample opportunity, in person or
  through counsel, to appear at the inquiry, present evidence and make
  representations.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Power to determine questions of law or
  fact

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} In the course of hearing and determining any matter
  under inquiry, the member or panel may decide all questions of law or
  fact necessary to determining the matter.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Additional powers

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} In relation to a hearing of the inquiry, the member
  or panel may

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} in the same manner and to the same extent as a
    superior court of record, summon and enforce the attendance of
    witnesses and compel them to give oral or written evidence on oath
    and to produce any documents and things that the member or panel
    considers necessary for the full hearing and consideration of the
    complaint;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} administer oaths;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} subject to subsections (4) and (5), receive and
    accept any evidence and other information, whether on oath or by
    affidavit or otherwise, that the member or panel sees fit, whether
    or not that evidence or information is or would be admissible in a
    court of law;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} lengthen or shorten any time limit established by
    the rules of procedure; and

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} decide any procedural or evidentiary question
    arising during the hearing.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Limitation in relation to evidence

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} The member or panel may not admit or accept as
  evidence anything that would be inadmissible in a court by reason of
  any privilege under the law of evidence.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Conciliators as witnesses

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} A conciliator appointed to settle the complaint is
  not a competent or compellable witness at the hearing.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Witness fees

  [(6)]{.lawlabel} Any person summoned to attend the hearing is entitled
  in the discretion of the member or panel to receive the same fees and
  allowances as those paid to persons summoned to attend before the
  Federal Court.

### [Offences and Punishment]{.HTitleText2} {#h-257530 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Offence

- **[[60]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-60 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Every
  person is guilty of an offence who

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} [\[Repealed, 1998, c. 9, s. 31\]]{.Repealed}

    59.

  organization.

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} A prosecution for an offence under this section may
  not be instituted except by or with the consent of the Attorney

      [(4)]{.lawlabel} The Board shall review the complaint in a summary
      way and shall refer it to the employer that is the subject of the
      complaint, or to the employer that is the subject of the complaint
      and the bargaining agent of the employees who filed the complaint,

      that the complaint is trivial, frivolous or vexatious or was made
      in bad faith.

    - ###### Power to assist {#power-to-assist .MarginalNote}

      [(5)]{.lawlabel} If the Board refers a complaint under
      subsection (4) to an employer, or to an employer and a bargaining

      complaint by any means that it considers appropriate.

    - ###### Hearing {#hearing .MarginalNote}

      bargaining agent, as the case may be, do not resolve the matters

      referred to them, or any longer period or periods that may be
      authorized by the Board, the Board shall schedule a hearing.

    - ###### Procedure {#procedure .MarginalNote}

      [(7)]{.lawlabel} The Board shall determine its own procedure but

    - ###### Decision {#decision .MarginalNote}

      [(8)]{.lawlabel} The Board shall make a decision in writing in
      respect of the complaint and send a copy of its decision with the

      agent, as the case may be.
