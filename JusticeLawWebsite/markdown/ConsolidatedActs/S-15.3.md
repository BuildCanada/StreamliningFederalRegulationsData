## Species at Risk Act {#species-at-risk-act .Title-of-Act}

[S.C.]{.abbr title="Statutes of Canada"} 2002, c. 29
:::

Assented to 2002-12-12

An Act respecting the protection of wildlife species at risk in Canada
::::

:::::::::::::::::::::::::::::::::::::::::::::::::::::::::::::::::::::::::::: section
::: {.section .order}
###### Preamble {#preamble .MarginalNote}

Recognizing that

Canada's natural heritage is an integral part of our national identity
and history,

wildlife, in all its forms, has value in and of itself and is valued by
Canadians for aesthetic, cultural, spiritual, recreational, educational,
historical, economic, medical, ecological and scientific reasons,

Canadian wildlife species and ecosystems are also part of the world's
heritage and the Government of Canada has ratified the United Nations
Convention on the Conservation of Biological Diversity,

providing legal protection for species at risk will complement existing
legislation and will, in part, meet Canada's commitments under that
Convention,

the Government of Canada is committed to conserving biological diversity
and to the principle that, if there are threats of serious or
irreversible damage to a wildlife species, cost-effective measures to
prevent the reduction or loss of the species should not be postponed for
a lack of full scientific certainty,

responsibility for the conservation of wildlife in Canada is shared
among the governments in this country and that it is important for them
to work cooperatively to pursue the establishment of complementary
legislation and programs for the protection and recovery of species at
risk in Canada,

it is important that there be cooperation between the governments in
this country to maintain and strengthen national standards of
environmental conservation and that the Government of Canada is
committed to the principles set out in intergovernmental agreements
respecting environmental conservation,

the Canadian Endangered Species Conservation Council is to provide
national leadership for the protection of species at risk, including the
provision of general direction to the Committee on the Status of
Endangered Wildlife in Canada in respect of that Committee's activities
and general directions in respect of the development, coordination and
implementation of recovery efforts,

the roles of the aboriginal peoples of Canada and of wildlife management
boards established under land claims agreements in the conservation of
wildlife in this country are essential,

all Canadians have a role to play in the conservation of wildlife in
this country, including the prevention of wildlife species from becoming
extirpated or extinct,

there will be circumstances under which the cost of conserving species
at risk should be shared,

the conservation efforts of individual Canadians and communities should
be encouraged and supported,

stewardship activities contributing to the conservation of wildlife
species and their habitat should be supported to prevent species from
becoming at risk,

community knowledge and interests, including socio-economic interests,
should be considered in developing and implementing recovery measures,

the traditional knowledge of the aboriginal peoples of Canada should be
considered in the assessment of which species may be at risk and in
developing and implementing recovery measures,

knowledge of wildlife species and ecosystems is critical to their
conservation,

the habitat of species at risk is key to their conservation, and

Canada's protected areas, especially national parks, are vital to the
protection and recovery of species at risk,
:::

NOW, THEREFORE, Her Majesty, by and with the advice and consent of the
Senate and House of Commons of Canada, enacts as follows:

## [Purposes]{.HTitleText1} {#h-434567 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Purposes

**[[6]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-6 .sectionLabel}** The purposes of this Act
are to prevent wildlife species from being extirpated or becoming
extinct, to provide for the recovery of wildlife species that are
extirpated, endangered or threatened as a result of human activity and
to manage species of special concern to prevent them from becoming
endangered or threatened.

## [Canadian Endangered Species Conservation Council]{.HTitleText1} {#h-434570 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Composition

- **[[7]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-7 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  Canadian Endangered Species Conservation Council consists of the
  Minister of the Environment, the Minister of Fisheries and Oceans, the
  Minister responsible for the Parks Canada Agency and ministers of the
  government of a province or a territory who are responsible for the
  conservation and management of a wildlife species in that province or
  territory.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Role

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The role of the Canadian Endangered Species
  Conservation Council is to

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} provide general direction on the activities of
    COSEWIC, the preparation of recovery strategies and the preparation
    and implementation of action plans; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} coordinate the activities of the various
    governments represented on the Council relating to the protection of
    species at risk.

## [Stewardship Action Plan]{.HTitleText1} {#h-434603 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Stewardship action plan

**[[10.1]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-10.1 .sectionLabel}** The Minister, after
consultation with the Canadian Endangered Species Conservation Council,
may establish a stewardship action plan that creates incentives and
other measures to support voluntary stewardship actions taken by any
government in Canada, organization or person. A copy of the stewardship
action plan must be included in the public registry.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Contents

**[[10.2]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-10.2 .sectionLabel}** The stewardship
action plan must include, but is not limited to, commitments to

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} regularly examine incentives and programs that
  support actions taken by persons to protect species at risk;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} provide information and increase public awareness
  about species at risk;

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} methods for sharing information about species at
  risk, including community and aboriginal traditional knowledge, that
  respect, preserve and maintain knowledge and promote their wider
  application with the approval of the holders of such knowledge, with
  other governments and persons;

- [(d)]{.lawlabel} create awards and recognition programs;

- [(e)]{.lawlabel} provide information respecting programs related to
  stewardship agreements, land conservation easements and other such
  agreements; and

- [(f)]{.lawlabel} provide information relating to the technical and
  scientific support available to persons engaged in stewardship
  activities.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Conservation agreements --- species at

  Council or any of its members if he or she considers it appropriate to

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Contents

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The agreement must provide for the taking of
  conservation measures and any other measures consistent with the

    awareness programs;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} developing and implementing recovery strategies,
    action plans and management plans;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} protecting the species' habitat, including its
    critical habitat; or

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} undertaking research projects in support of
    recovery efforts for the species.

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} developing and implementing education and public
    awareness programs;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} protecting the species' habitat; and

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} preventing the species from becoming a species at

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Provisions to be included

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the contribution towards the cost of the program or

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the authority or person who will be responsible for

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} the proportions of any revenue from the program or

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} the terms and conditions governing the operation
    and maintenance of the program or measure.

## [Wildlife Species Listing Process]{.HTitleText1} {#h-434643 .Part}

### [Committee on the Status of Endangered Wildlife in Canada]{.HTitleText2} {#h-434644 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Establishment

**[[14]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-14 .sectionLabel}** The Committee on the
Status of Endangered Wildlife in Canada is hereby established.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Functions

- **[[15]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-15 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  functions of COSEWIC are to

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} assess the status of each wildlife species
    considered by COSEWIC to be at risk and, as part of the assessment,
    identify existing and potential threats to the species and

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} classify the species as extinct, extirpated,
      endangered, threatened or of special concern,

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} indicate that COSEWIC does not have sufficient
      information to classify the species, or

    - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} indicate that the species is not currently at
      risk;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} determine when wildlife species are to be assessed,
    with priority given to those more likely to become extinct;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} conduct a new assessment of the status of species
    at risk and, if appropriate, reclassify or declassify them;

  - [(c.1)]{.lawlabel} indicate in the assessment whether the wildlife
    species migrates across Canada's boundary or has a range extending
    across Canada's boundary;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} develop and periodically review criteria for
    assessing the status of wildlife species and for classifying them
    and recommend the criteria to the Minister and the Canadian
    Endangered Species Conservation Council; and

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} provide advice to the Minister and the Canadian
    Endangered Species Conservation Council and perform any other
    functions that the Minister, after consultation with that Council,
    may assign.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Best information and knowledge

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} COSEWIC must carry out its functions on the basis of
  the best available information on the biological status of a species,
  including scientific knowledge, community knowledge and aboriginal
  traditional knowledge.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Treaties and land claims agreements

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} COSEWIC must take into account any applicable
  provisions of treaty and land claims agreements when carrying out its
  functions.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Composition

- **[[16]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-16
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} COSEWIC is to be composed of members
  appointed by the Minister after consultation with the Canadian
  Endangered Species Conservation Council and with any experts and
  expert bodies, such as the Royal Society of Canada, that the Minister
  considers to have relevant expertise.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Qualifications of members

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Each member must have expertise drawn from a
  discipline such as conservation biology, population dynamics,
  taxonomy, systematics or genetics or from community knowledge or
  aboriginal traditional knowledge of the conservation of wildlife
  species.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Term of appointment

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} The members are to be appointed to hold office for
  renewable terms of not more than four years.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Not part of the public service of
  Canada

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} The members are not, because of being a member, part
  of the public service of Canada.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Remuneration and expenses

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} The members may be paid remuneration and expenses for
  their services in amounts that the Minister may set.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Discretion

  [(6)]{.lawlabel} Each member of COSEWIC shall exercise his or her
  discretion in an independent manner.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Regulations and guidelines

**[[17]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-17 .sectionLabel}** The Minister may, after
consultation with the Canadian Endangered Species Conservation Council
and COSEWIC, establish regulations or guidelines respecting the
appointment of members and the carrying out of COSEWIC's functions.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Subcommittees

- **[[18]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-18
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} COSEWIC must establish subcommittees
  of specialists to assist in the preparation and review of status
  reports on wildlife species considered to be at risk, including
  subcommittees specializing in groups of wildlife species and a
  subcommittee specializing in aboriginal traditional knowledge, and it
  may establish other subcommittees to advise it or to exercise or
  perform any of its functions.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Membership

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Each subcommittee must be presided over by a member
  of COSEWIC, but the subcommittee may include persons who are not
  members of COSEWIC.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Aboriginal traditional knowledge
  subcommittee

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} Subject to subsection (2), the chairperson and
  members of the aboriginal traditional knowledge subcommittee must be
  appointed by the Minister after consultation with any aboriginal
  organization he or she considers appropriate.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Rules

**[[19]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-19 .sectionLabel}** COSEWIC may make rules

activities, including rules respecting

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} the selection of persons to chair its meetings; and

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} the meetings and activities of any of its
  subcommittees.

other assistance, and any facilities and supplies, that, in his or her

### [Reviews and Reports]{.HTitleText2} {#h-434710 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Review of classifications

**[[24]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-24 .sectionLabel}** COSEWIC must review the
classification of each species at risk at least once every 10 years, or
at any time if it has reason to believe that the status of the species
has changed significantly.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Copies to Minister and Council

- **[[25]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-25 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} When
  COSEWIC completes an assessment of the status of a wildlife species,

  Conservation Council with a copy of the assessment and the reasons for
  it. A copy of the assessment and the reasons must also be included in
  the public registry.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}COSEWIC list

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} COSEWIC must annually prepare a complete list of
  every wildlife species it has assessed since the coming into force of
  this section and a copy of that list must be included in the public
  registry.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Report on response

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} On receiving a copy of an assessment of the status of
  a wildlife species from COSEWIC under subsection (1), the Minister
  must, within 90 days, include in the public registry a report on how
  the Minister intends to respond to the assessment and, to the extent

## [Measures to Protect Listed Wildlife Species]{.HTitleText1} {#h-434768 .Part}

### [General Prohibitions]{.HTitleText2} {#h-434769 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Killing, harming, etc., listed wildlife
species

- **[[32]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-32 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} No
  person shall kill, harm, harass, capture or take an individual of a
  wildlife species that is listed as an extirpated species, an
  endangered species or a threatened species.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Possession, collection, etc.

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} No person shall possess, collect, buy, sell or trade
  an individual of a wildlife species that is listed as an extirpated
  species, an endangered species or a threatened species, or any part or
  derivative of such an individual.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Deeming

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} For the purposes of subsection (2), any animal, plant
  or thing that is represented to be an individual, or a part or
  derivative of an individual, of a wildlife species that is listed as
  an extirpated species, an endangered species or a threatened species
  is deemed, in the absence of evidence to the contrary, to be such an
  individual or a part or derivative of such an individual.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Damage or destruction of residence

**[[33]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-33 .sectionLabel}** No person shall damage or
destroy the residence of one or more individuals of a wildlife species
that is listed as an endangered species or a threatened species, or that
is listed as an extirpated species if a recovery strategy has
recommended the reintroduction of the species into the wild in Canada.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Application --- certain species in
provinces

- **[[34]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-34 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} With
  respect to individuals of a listed wildlife species that is not an
  aquatic species or a species of birds that are migratory birds
  protected by the [Migratory Birds Convention Act,
  1994](/eng/acts/M-7.01), sections 32 and 33 do not apply in lands in a
  province that are not federal lands unless an order is made under
  subsection (2) to provide that they apply.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Order

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The Governor in Council may, on the recommendation of
  the Minister, by order, provide that sections 32 and 33, or either of
  them, apply in lands in a province that are not federal lands with
  respect to individuals of a listed wildlife species that is not an
  aquatic species or a species of birds that are migratory birds
  protected by the [Migratory Birds Convention Act,
  1994](/eng/acts/M-7.01).

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Obligation to make recommendation

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} The Minister must recommend that the order be made if
  the Minister is of the opinion that the laws of the province do not
  effectively protect the species or the residences of its individuals.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Consultation

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} Before recommending that the Governor in Council make
  an order under subsection (2), the Minister must consult

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the appropriate provincial minister; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} if the species is found in an area in respect of
    which a wildlife management board is authorized by a land claims
    agreement to perform functions in respect of wildlife species, the
    wildlife management board.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Application --- certain species in
territories

- **[[35]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-35
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Sections 32 and 33 apply in each of
  the territories in respect of a listed wildlife species only to the
  extent that the Governor in Council, on the recommendation of the
  Minister, makes an order providing that they, or any of them, apply.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Exception

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Subsection (1) does not apply

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} in respect of individuals of aquatic species and
    their habitat or species of birds that are migratory birds protected
    by the [Migratory Birds Convention Act, 1994](/eng/acts/M-7.01); or

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} on land under the authority of the Minister or the
    Parks Canada Agency.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Obligation to make recommendation

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} The Minister must recommend that the order be made if
  the Minister is of the opinion that the laws of the territory do not
  effectively protect the species or the residences of its individuals.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Pre-conditions for recommendation

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} Before recommending that an order be made under
  subsection (1), the Minister must

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} consult the appropriate territorial minister; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} if the species is found in an area in respect of
    which a wildlife management board is authorized by a land claims
    agreement to perform functions in respect of wildlife species,
    consult the wildlife management board.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Prohibitions re provincial and
territorial classifications

- **[[36]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-36 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} If a
  wildlife species that is not listed has been classified as an
  endangered species or a threatened species by a provincial or
  territorial minister, no person shall

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} kill, harm, harass, capture or take an individual
    of that species that is on federal lands in the province or
    territory;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} possess, collect, buy, sell or trade an individual
    of that species that is on federal lands in the province or
    territory, or any part or derivative of such an individual; or

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} damage or destroy the residence of one or more
    individuals of that species that is on federal lands in the province
    or territory.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Application

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Subsection (1) applies only in respect of the
  portions of the federal lands that the Governor in Council may, on the
  recommendation of the competent minister, by order, specify.

### [Recovery of Endangered, Threatened and Extirpated Species]{.HTitleText2} {#h-434811 .Subheading}

#### [Recovery Strategy]{.HTitleText3} {#h-434812 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Preparation --- endangered or threatened
species

- **[[37]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-37 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} If a
  wildlife species is listed as an extirpated species, an endangered
  species or a threatened species, the competent minister must prepare a
  strategy for its recovery.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}More than one competent minister

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} If there is more than one competent minister with
  respect to the wildlife species, they must prepare the strategy
  together and every reference to competent minister in sections 38 to
  46 is to be read as a reference to the competent ministers.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Commitments to be considered

**[[38]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-38 .sectionLabel}** In preparing a recovery
strategy, action plan or management plan, the competent minister must
consider the commitment of the Government of Canada to conserving
biological diversity and to the principle that, if there are threats of
serious or irreversible damage to the listed wildlife species,
cost-effective measures to prevent the reduction or loss of the species
should not be postponed for a lack of full scientific certainty.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Cooperation with others

- **[[39]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-39 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} To the
  extent possible, the recovery strategy must be prepared in cooperation
  with

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the appropriate provincial and territorial minister
    for each province and territory in which the listed wildlife species
    is found;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} every minister of the Government of Canada who has
    authority over federal land or other areas on which the species is
    found;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} if the species is found in an area in respect of
    which a wildlife management board is authorized by a land claims
    agreement to perform functions in respect of wildlife species, the
    wildlife management board;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} every aboriginal organization that the competent
    minister considers will be directly affected by the recovery
    strategy; and

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} any other person or organization that the competent
    minister considers appropriate.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Land claims agreement

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} If the listed wildlife species is found in an area in
  respect of which a wildlife management board is authorized by a land
  claims agreement to perform functions in respect of wildlife species,
  the recovery strategy must be prepared, to the extent that it will
  apply to that area, in accordance with the provisions of the
  agreement.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Consultation

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} To the extent possible, the recovery strategy must be
  prepared in consultation with any landowners and other persons whom
  the competent minister considers to be directly affected by the
  strategy, including the government of any other country in which the
  species is found.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Determination of feasibility

**[[40]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-40 .sectionLabel}** In preparing the recovery
strategy, the competent minister must determine whether the recovery of
the listed wildlife species is technically and biologically feasible.
The determination must be based on the best available information,
including information provided by COSEWIC.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Contents if recovery feasible

- **[[41]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-41 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} If the
  competent minister determines that the recovery of the listed wildlife
  species is feasible, the recovery strategy must address the threats to
  the survival of the species identified by COSEWIC, including any loss
  of habitat, and must include

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} a description of the species and its needs that is
    consistent with information provided by COSEWIC;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} an identification of the threats to the survival of
    the species and threats to its habitat that is consistent with
    information provided by COSEWIC and a description of the broad
    strategy to be taken to address those threats;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} an identification of the species' critical habitat,
    to the extent possible, based on the best available information,
    including the information provided by COSEWIC, and examples of
    activities that are likely to result in its destruction;

  - [(c.1)]{.lawlabel} a schedule of studies to identify critical
    habitat, where available information is inadequate;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} a statement of the population and distribution
    objectives that will assist the recovery and survival of the
    species, and a general description of the research and management
    activities needed to meet those objectives;

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} any other matters that are prescribed by the
    regulations;

  - [(f)]{.lawlabel} a statement about whether additional information is
    required about the species; and

  - [(g)]{.lawlabel} a statement of when one or more action plans in
    relation to the recovery strategy will be completed.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Contents if recovery not feasible

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} If the competent minister determines that the
  recovery of the listed wildlife species is not feasible, the recovery
  strategy must include a description of the species and its needs, an
  identification of the species' critical habitat to the extent
  possible, and the reasons why its recovery is not feasible.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Multi-species or ecosystem approach
  permissible

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} The competent minister may adopt a multi-species or
  an ecosystem approach when preparing the recovery strategy if he or
  she considers it appropriate to do so.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Regulations

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} The Governor in Council may, on the recommendation of
  the Minister after consultation with the Minister responsible for the
  Parks Canada Agency and the Minister of Fisheries and Oceans, make
  regulations for the purpose of paragraph (1)(e) prescribing matters to
  be included in a recovery strategy.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Comments

- **[[43]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-43 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Within
  60 days after the proposed recovery strategy is included in the public
  registry, any person may file written comments with the competent
  minister.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Finalization of recovery strategy

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Within 30 days after the expiry of the period
  referred to in subsection (1), the competent minister must consider
  any comments received, make any changes to the proposed recovery
  strategy that he or she considers appropriate and finalize the
  recovery strategy by including a copy of it in the public registry.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Existing plans

- **[[44]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-44 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} If the
  competent minister is of the opinion that an existing plan relating to
  a wildlife species meets the requirements of subsection 41(1) or (2),
  and the plan is adopted by the competent minister as the proposed
  recovery strategy, he or she must include it in the public registry as
  the proposed recovery strategy in relation to the species.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Incorporation of existing plans

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The competent minister may incorporate any part of an
  existing plan relating to a wildlife species into a proposed recovery
  strategy for the species.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Amendments

- **[[45]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-45 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The

  of the amendment must be included in the public registry.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Amendments relating to time for

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} If the amendment relates to the time for completing

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Amendment procedure

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} Sections 39 and 43 apply to amendments to a recovery

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Exception

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} Subsection (3) does not apply if the competent
  minister considers the amendment to be minor.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Reporting

**[[46]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-46 .sectionLabel}** The competent minister
must report on the implementation of the recovery strategy, and the
progress towards meeting its objectives, within five years after it is
included in the public registry and in every subsequent five-year
period, until its objectives have been achieved or the species' recovery
is no longer feasible. The report must be included in the public
registry.

#### [Action Plan]{.HTitleText3} {#h-434879 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Preparation

**[[47]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-47 .sectionLabel}** The competent minister in
respect of a recovery strategy must prepare one or more action plans
based on the recovery strategy. If there is more than one competent
minister with respect to the recovery strategy, they may prepare the
action plan or plans together.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Cooperation with other ministers and
governments

- **[[48]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-48 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} To the
  extent possible, an action plan must be prepared in cooperation with

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the appropriate provincial and territorial minister
    of each province and territory in which the listed wildlife species
    is found;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} every minister of the Government of Canada who has
    authority over federal land or other areas on which the species is
    found;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} if the species is found in an area in respect of
    which a wildlife management board is authorized by a land claims
    agreement to perform functions in respect of wildlife species, the
    wildlife management board;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} every aboriginal organization that the competent
    minister considers will be directly affected by the action plan; and

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} any other person or organization that the competent
    minister considers appropriate.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Land claims agreement

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} If the listed wildlife species is found in an area in
  respect of which a wildlife management board is authorized by a land
  claims agreement to perform functions in respect of wildlife species,
  an action plan must be prepared, to the extent that it will apply to
  that area, in accordance with the provisions of the agreement.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Consultation

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} To the extent possible, an action plan must be
  prepared in consultation with any landowners, lessees and other
  persons whom the competent minister considers to be directly affected
  by, or interested in, the action plan, including the government of any
  other country in which the species is found.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Contents

- **[[49]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-49 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} An
  action plan must include, with respect to the area to which the action
  plan relates,

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} an identification of the species' critical habitat,
    to the extent possible, based on the best available information and
    consistent with the recovery strategy, and examples of activities
    that are likely to result in its destruction;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} a statement of the measures that are proposed to be
    taken to protect the species' critical habitat, including the
    entering into of agreements under section 11;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} an identification of any portions of the species'
    critical habitat that have not been protected;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} a statement of the measures that are to be taken to
    implement the recovery strategy, including those that address the
    threats to the species and those that help to achieve the population
    and distribution objectives, as well as an indication as to when
    these measures are to take place;

  - [(d.1)]{.lawlabel} the methods to be used to monitor the recovery of
    the species and its long-term viability;

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} an evaluation of the socio-economic costs of the
    action plan and the benefits to be derived from its implementation;
    and

  - [(f)]{.lawlabel} any other matters that are prescribed by the
    regulations.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Regulations

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The Governor in Council may, on the recommendation of
  the Minister after consultation with the Minister responsible for the
  Parks Canada Agency and the Minister of Fisheries and Oceans, make
  regulations for the purpose of paragraph (1)(f) prescribing matters to
  be included in an action plan.

### [Protection of Critical Habitat]{.HTitleText2} {#h-434948 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Codes of practice, national standards or
guidelines

**[[56]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-56 .sectionLabel}** The competent minister
may, after consultation with the Canadian Endangered Species
Conservation Council and any person whom he or she considers
appropriate, establish codes of practice, national standards or
guidelines with respect to the protection of critical habitat.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Purpose

**[[57]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-57 .sectionLabel}** The purpose of section 58
is to ensure that, within 180 days after the recovery strategy or action
plan that identified the critical habitat referred to in subsection
58(1) is included in the public registry, all of the critical habitat is
protected by

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} provisions in, or measures under, this or any other
  Act of Parliament, including agreements under section 11; or

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} the application of subsection 58(1).

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Destruction of critical habitat

- **[[58]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-58
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Subject to this section, no person
  shall destroy any part of the critical habitat of any listed
  endangered species or of any listed threatened species --- or of any
  listed extirpated species if a recovery strategy has recommended the
  reintroduction of the species into the wild in Canada --- if

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the critical habitat is on federal land, in the
    exclusive economic zone of Canada or on the continental shelf of
    Canada;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the listed species is an aquatic species; or

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} the listed species is a species of migratory birds
    protected by the [Migratory Birds Convention Act,
    1994](/eng/acts/M-7.01).

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Protected areas

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} If the critical habitat or a portion of the critical
  habitat is in a national park of Canada named and described in
  Schedule 1 to the [Canada National Parks Act](/eng/acts/N-14.01), the
  Rouge National Urban Park established by the [Rouge National Urban
  Park Act](/eng/acts/R-8.55), a marine protected area under the [Oceans
  Act](/eng/acts/O-2.4), a migratory bird sanctuary under the [Migratory
  Birds Convention Act, 1994](/eng/acts/M-7.01) or a national wildlife
  area under the [Canada Wildlife Act](/eng/acts/W-9), the competent
  Minister must, within 90 days after the recovery strategy or action
  plan that identified the critical habitat is included in the public
  registry, publish in the [Canada Gazette](http://www.gazette.gc.ca/) a
  description of the critical habitat or portion that is in that park,
  area or sanctuary.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Application

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} If subsection (2) applies, subsection (1) applies to
  the critical habitat or the portion of the critical habitat described
  in the [Canada Gazette](http://www.gazette.gc.ca/) under
  subsection (2) 90 days after the description is published in the
  [Canada Gazette](http://www.gazette.gc.ca/).

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Application

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} If all of the critical habitat or any portion of the
  critical habitat is not in a place referred to in subsection (2),
  subsection (1) applies in respect of the critical habitat or portion
  of the critical habitat, as the case may be, specified in an order
  made by the competent minister.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Obligation to make order or statement

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} Within 180 days after the recovery strategy or action
  plan that identified the critical habitat is included in the public
  registry, the competent minister must, after consultation with every
  other competent minister, with respect to all of the critical habitat
  or any portion of the critical habitat that is not in a place referred
  to in subsection (2),

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} make the order referred to in subsection (4) if the
    critical habitat or any portion of the critical habitat is not
    legally protected by provisions in, or measures under, this or any
    other Act of Parliament, including agreements under section 11; or

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} if the competent minister does not make the order,
    he or she must include in the public registry a statement setting
    out how the critical habitat or portions of it, as the case may be,
    are legally protected.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Habitat of migratory birds

  [(5.1)]{.lawlabel} Despite subsection (4), with respect to the
  critical habitat of a species of bird that is a migratory bird
  protected by the [Migratory Birds Convention Act,
  1994](/eng/acts/M-7.01) that is not on federal land, in the exclusive
  economic zone of Canada, on the continental shelf of Canada or in a
  migratory bird sanctuary referred to in subsection (2), subsection (1)
  applies only to those portions of the critical habitat that are
  habitat to which that Act applies and that the Governor in Council
  may, by order, specify on the recommendation of the competent
  minister.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Obligation to make recommendation

  [(5.2)]{.lawlabel} The competent minister must, within 180 days after
  the recovery strategy or action plan that identified the critical
  habitat that includes habitat to which the [Migratory Birds Convention
  Act, 1994](/eng/acts/M-7.01) applies is included in the public
  registry, and after consultation with every other competent minister,

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} make the recommendation if he or she is of the
    opinion there are no provisions in, or other measures under, this or
    any other Act of Parliament, including agreements under section 11,
    that legally protect any portion or portions of the habitat to which
    that Act applies; or

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} if the competent minister does not make the
    recommendation, he or she must include in the public registry a
    statement setting out how the critical habitat that is habitat to
    which that Act applies, or portions of it, as the case may be, are
    legally protected.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Consultation

  [(6)]{.lawlabel} If the competent minister is of the opinion that an
  order under subsection (4) or (5.1) would affect land in a territory
  that is not under the authority of the Minister or the Parks Canada
  Agency, he or she must consult the territorial minister before making
  the order under subsection (4) or the recommendation under subsection
  (5.2).

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Consultation

  [(7)]{.lawlabel} If the competent minister is of the opinion that an
  order under subsection (4) or (5.1) would affect a reserve or any
  other lands that are set apart for the use and benefit of a band under
  the [Indian Act](/eng/acts/I-5), he or she must consult the Minister
  of Indigenous Services and the band before making the order under
  subsection (4) or the recommendation under subsection (5.2).

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Consultation

  [(8)]{.lawlabel} If the competent minister is of the opinion that an
  order under subsection (4) or (5.1) would affect an area in respect of
  which a wildlife management board is authorized by a land claims
  agreement to perform functions in respect of wildlife species, he or
  she must consult the wildlife management board before making the order
  under subsection (4) or the recommendation under subsection (5.2).

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Consultation

  [(9)]{.lawlabel} If the competent minister is of the opinion that an
  order under subsection (4) or (5.1) would affect land that is under
  the authority of another federal minister, other than a competent
  minister, he or she must consult the other federal minister before
  making the order under subsection (4) or the recommendation under
  subsection (5.2).

### [Management of Species of Special Concern]{.HTitleText2} {#h-435040 .Subheading}

**[[65]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-65 .sectionLabel}** If a wildlife species is

prepare a management plan for the species and its habitat. The plan must
include measures for the conservation of the species that the competent
minister considers appropriate and it may apply with respect to more
than one wildlife species.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Cooperation with other ministers and

- **[[66]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-66 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} To the
  extent possible, the management plan must be prepared in cooperation
  with

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the appropriate provincial and territorial minister
    of each province and territory in which the listed wildlife species
    is found;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} every minister of the Government of Canada who has
    authority over federal land or other areas on which the species is
    found;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} if the species is found in an area in respect of
    which a wildlife management board is authorized by a land claims
    agreement to perform functions in respect of wildlife species, the
    wildlife management board;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} every aboriginal organization that the competent
    minister considers will be directly affected by the management plan;
    and

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} any other person or organization that the competent
    minister considers appropriate.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Land claims agreement

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} If the listed wildlife species is found in an area in
  respect of which a wildlife management board is authorized by a land
  claims agreement to perform functions in respect of wildlife species,
  the management plan must be prepared, to the extent that it will apply
  to that area, in accordance with the provisions of the agreement.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Consultation

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} To the extent possible, the management plan must be
  prepared in consultation with any landowners, lessees and other
  persons whom the competent minister considers to be directly affected
  by, or interested in, the management plan, including the government of
  any other country in which the species is found.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Multi-species or ecosystem approach
permissible

**[[67]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-67 .sectionLabel}** The competent minister
may adopt a multi-species or an ecosystem approach when preparing the
management plan if he or she considers it appropriate to do so.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Proposed management plan

- **[[68]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-68
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Subject to subsection (2), the
  competent minister must include a proposed management plan in the
  public registry within three years after the wildlife species is
  listed as a species of special concern.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}First listed species

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} With respect to a wildlife species that is set out in
  Schedule 1 as a species of special concern on the day section 27 comes
  into force, the competent minister must include a proposed management
  plan in the public registry within five years after that day.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Comments

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} Within 60 days after the proposed management plan is
  included in the public registry, any person may file written comments
  with the competent minister.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Finalization of management plan

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} Within 30 days after the expiry of the period
  referred to in subsection (3), the competent minister must consider
  any comments received, make any changes to the proposed management
  plan that he or she considers appropriate and finalize the management
  plan by including a copy of it in the public registry.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Existing plans

- **[[69]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-69 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} If the
  competent minister is of the opinion that an existing plan relating to
  a wildlife species includes adequate measures for the conservation of
  the species and the competent minister adopts the existing plan as the
  proposed management plan, he or she must include a copy of it in the
  public registry as the proposed management plan in relation to the
  species.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Incorporation of existing plans

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The competent minister may incorporate any part of an
  existing plan relating to a wildlife species into a proposed
  management plan for the species.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Amendments

- **[[70]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-70 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  competent minister may at any time amend a management plan. A copy of
  the amendment must be included in the public registry.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Amendment procedure

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Section 66 applies to amendments to the management
  plan, with any modifications that the circumstances require.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Exception

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} Subsection (2) does not apply if the competent
  minister considers the amendment to be minor.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Regulations

- **[[71]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-71 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  Governor in Council may, on the recommendation of the competent
  minister, make any regulations with respect to aquatic species or
  species of birds that are migratory birds protected by the [Migratory
  Birds Convention Act, 1994](/eng/acts/M-7.01), regardless of where
  they are located, or with respect to any other wildlife species on
  federal lands, that the Governor in Council considers appropriate for
  the purpose of implementing the measures included in the management
  plan.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Consultation

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} If the competent minister is of the opinion that a
  regulation would affect a reserve or any other lands that are set
  apart for the use and benefit of a band under the [Indian
  Act](/eng/acts/I-5), he or she must consult the Minister of Indigenous
  Services and the band before recommending the making of the
  regulation.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Consultation

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} If the competent minister is of the opinion that a
  regulation would affect an area in respect of which a wildlife
  management board is authorized by a land claims agreement to perform
  functions in respect of wildlife species, he or she must consult the
  wildlife management board before recommending the making of the
  regulation.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Incorporation by reference

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} The regulations may incorporate by reference any
  legislation of a province or territory, as amended from time to time,
  insofar as the regulations apply in that province or territory.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Consultation

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} If the competent minister is of the opinion that a
  regulation would affect land in a territory, he or she must consult
  the territorial minister before recommending the making of the
  regulation.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Exception

  [(6)]{.lawlabel} Subsection (5) does not apply

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} in respect of individuals of aquatic species and
    their habitat or species of birds that are migratory birds protected
    by the [Migratory Birds Convention Act, 1994](/eng/acts/M-7.01) and
    their habitat; or

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} in respect of land under the authority of the
    Minister or the Parks Canada Agency.

### [Agreements and Permits]{.HTitleText2} {#h-435095 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Powers of competent minister

- **[[73]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-73 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  competent minister may enter into an agreement with a person, or issue
  a permit to a person, authorizing the person to engage in an activity
  affecting a listed wildlife species, any part of its critical habitat
  or the residences of its individuals.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Purpose

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The agreement may be entered into, or the permit
  issued, only if the competent minister is of the opinion that

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the activity is scientific research relating to the
    conservation of the species and conducted by qualified persons;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the activity benefits the species or is required to
    enhance its chance of survival in the wild; or

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} affecting the species is incidental to the carrying
    out of the activity.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Pre-conditions

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} The agreement may be entered into, or the permit
  issued, only if the competent minister is of the opinion that

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} all reasonable alternatives to the activity that
    would reduce the impact on the species have been considered and the
    best solution has been adopted;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} all feasible measures will be taken to minimize the
    impact of the activity on the species or its critical habitat or the
    residences of its individuals; and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} the activity will not jeopardize the survival or
    recovery of the species.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Explanation in public registry

  [(3.1)]{.lawlabel} If an agreement is entered into or a permit is
  issued, the competent minister must include in the public registry an
  explanation of why it was entered into or issued, taking into account
  the matters referred to in paragraphs (3)(a), (b) and (c).

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Consultation

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} If the species is found in an area in respect of
  which a wildlife management board is authorized by a land claims
  agreement to perform functions in respect of wildlife species, the
  competent minister must consult the wildlife management board before
  entering into an agreement or issuing a permit concerning that species
  in that area.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Consultation

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} If the species is found in a reserve or any other
  lands that are set apart for the use and benefit of a band under the
  [Indian Act](/eng/acts/I-5), the competent minister must consult the
  band before entering into an agreement or issuing a permit concerning
  that species in that reserve or those other lands.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Terms and conditions

  [(6)]{.lawlabel} The agreement or permit must contain any terms and
  conditions governing the activity that the competent minister
  considers necessary for protecting the species, minimizing the impact
  of the authorized activity on the species or providing for its
  recovery.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Date of expiry

  [(6.1)]{.lawlabel} The agreement or permit must set out the date of
  its expiry.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Review of agreements and permits

  [(7)]{.lawlabel} The competent minister must review the agreement or
  permit if an emergency order is made with respect to the species.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Amendment of agreements and permits

  [(8)]{.lawlabel} The competent minister may revoke or amend an
  agreement or a permit to ensure the survival or recovery of a species.

- [(9)]{.lawlabel} [\[Repealed, 2012, c. 19, s. 163\]]{.Repealed}

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Regulations

  [(10)]{.lawlabel} The Minister may, after consultation with the
  Minister responsible for the Parks Canada Agency and the Minister of
  Fisheries and Oceans, make regulations respecting the entering into of
  agreements, the issuance of permits and the renewal, revocation,
  amendment and suspension of agreements and permits.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Time limits

  [(11)]{.lawlabel} The regulations may include provisions

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} respecting time limits for issuing or renewing
    permits, or for refusing to do so;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} specifying the circumstances under which any of
    those time limits does not apply; and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} authorizing the competent minister to extend any of
    those time limits or to decide that a time limit does not apply,
    when the competent minister considers that it is appropriate to do
    so.

### [Emergency Orders]{.HTitleText2} {#h-435187 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Emergency order

- **[[80]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-80 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  Governor in Council may, on the recommendation of the competent
  minister, make an emergency order to provide for the protection of a
  listed wildlife species.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Obligation to make recommendation

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The competent minister must make the recommendation
  if he or she is of the opinion that the species faces imminent threats
  to its survival or recovery.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Consultation

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} Before making a recommendation, the competent
  minister must consult every other competent minister.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Contents

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} The emergency order may

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} in the case of an aquatic species,

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} identify habitat that is necessary for the
      survival or recovery of the species in the area to which the
      emergency order relates, and

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} include provisions requiring the doing of things
      that protect the species and that habitat and provisions
      prohibiting activities that may adversely affect the species and
      that habitat;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} in the case of a species that is a species of
    migratory birds protected by the [Migratory Birds Convention Act,
    1994](/eng/acts/M-7.01),

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} on federal land or in the exclusive economic zone
      of Canada,

      - [(A)]{.lawlabel} identify habitat that is necessary for the
        survival or recovery of the species in the area to which the
        emergency order relates, and

      - [(B)]{.lawlabel} include provisions requiring the doing of
        things that protect the species and that habitat and provisions
        prohibiting activities that may adversely affect the species and
        that habitat, and

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} on land other than land referred to in
      subparagraph (i),

      - [(A)]{.lawlabel} identify habitat that is necessary for the
        survival or recovery of the species in the area to which the
        emergency order relates, and

      - [(B)]{.lawlabel} include provisions requiring the doing of
        things that protect the species and provisions prohibiting
        activities that may adversely affect the species and that
        habitat; and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} with respect to any other species,

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} on federal land, in the exclusive economic zone
      of Canada or on the continental shelf of Canada,

      - [(A)]{.lawlabel} identify habitat that is necessary for the
        survival or recovery of the species in the area to which the
        emergency order relates, and

      - [(B)]{.lawlabel} include provisions requiring the doing of
        things that protect the species and that habitat and provisions
        prohibiting activities that may adversely affect the species and
        that habitat, and

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} on land other than land referred to in
      subparagraph (i),

      - [(A)]{.lawlabel} identify habitat that is necessary for the
        survival or recovery of the species in the area to which the
        emergency order relates, and

      - [(B)]{.lawlabel} include provisions prohibiting activities that
        may adversely affect the species and that habitat.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Exemption

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} An emergency order is exempt from the application of
  section 3 of the [Statutory Instruments Act](/eng/acts/S-22).

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Equivalent measures

**[[81]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-81 .sectionLabel}** Despite subsection 80(2),
the competent minister is not required to make a recommendation for an
emergency order if he or she is of the opinion that equivalent measures
have been taken under another Act of Parliament to protect the wildlife
species.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Recommendation to repeal

**[[82]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-82 .sectionLabel}** If the competent minister
is of the opinion that the species to which the emergency order relates
would no longer face imminent threats to its survival or recovery even
if the order were repealed, he or she must make a recommendation to the
Governor in Council that the emergency order be repealed.

### [Exceptions]{.HTitleText2} {#h-435220 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}General exceptions

- **[[83]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-83
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Subsections 32(1) and (2), section
  33, subsections 36(1), 58(1), 60(1) and 61(1), regulations made under
  section 53, 59 or 71 and emergency orders do not apply to a person who
  is engaging in

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} activities related to public safety, health or
    national security, that are authorized by or under any other Act of
    Parliament or activities under the [Health of Animals
    Act](/eng/acts/H-3.3) and the [Plant Protection
    Act](/eng/acts/P-14.8) for the health of animals and plants; or

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} activities authorized under section 73, 74 or 78 by
    an agreement, permit, licence, order or similar document.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Authorization of activities under other
  Acts

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} A power under an Act described in paragraph (1)(a)
  may be used to authorize an activity prohibited by subsection 32(1) or
  (2), section 33, subsection 36(1), 58(1), 60(1) or 61(1), a regulation
  made under section 53, 59 or 71 or an emergency order only if the
  person exercising the power

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} determines that the activity is necessary for the
    protection of public safety, health, including animal and plant
    health, or national security; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} respects the purposes of this Act to the greatest
    extent possible.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Exceptions --- land claims agreements

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} Subsections 32(1) and (2), section 33, subsections
  36(1), 58(1), 60(1) and 61(1) and regulations made under section 53,
  59 or 71 do not apply to a person who is engaging in activities in
  accordance with conservation measures for wildlife species under a
  land claims agreement.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Exemptions for permitted activities

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} Subsections 32(1) and (2), section 33 and subsections
  36(1), 58(1), 60(1) and 61(1) do not apply to a person who is engaging
  in activities that are permitted by a recovery strategy, an action
  plan or a management plan and who is also authorized under an Act of
  Parliament to engage in that activity, including a regulation made
  under section 53, 59 or 71.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Additional possession exceptions

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} Subsection 32(2) and paragraph 36(1)(b) do not apply
  to a person who possesses an individual of a listed extirpated,
  endangered or threatened species, or any part or derivative of such an
  individual, if

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} it was in the person's possession when the species
    was listed;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} it is used by an aboriginal person for ceremonial
    or medicinal purposes, or it is part of ceremonial dress used for
    ceremonial or cultural purposes by an aboriginal person;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} the person acquired it legally in another country
    and imported it legally into Canada;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} the person acquired it by succession from someone
    who was entitled to possess it under this Act;

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} the person acquired it under circumstances that
    would afford them a defence under section 100 and the person
    possesses it only for as long as is necessary to donate it to a
    museum, a zoo, an educational institution, a scientific society or a
    government;

  - [(f)]{.lawlabel} the person is, or is acting on behalf of, a museum,
    zoo, educational institution, scientific society or government and
    the person acquired it from someone who was entitled to possess it
    under this Act; or

  - [(g)]{.lawlabel} it or the person is otherwise exempt by the
    regulations.

may, on the recommendation of the Minister after consultation with the
Minister responsible for the Parks Canada Agency and the Minister of
Fisheries and Oceans, make regulations for the purpose of paragraph
83(5)(g).

## [Enforcement Measures]{.HTitleText1} {#h-435247 .Part}

### [Enforcement Officers]{.HTitleText2} {#h-435248 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Enforcement officers

- **[[85]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-85 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A
  competent minister may designate any person or person of a class of
  persons to act as enforcement officers for the purposes of this Act.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Designation of provincial or
  territorial government employees

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The competent minister may not designate any person
  or person of a class of persons employed by the government of a
  province or a territory unless that government agrees.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Certificate of designation

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} An enforcement officer must be provided with a
  certificate of designation as an enforcement officer in a form
  approved by the competent minister and, on entering any place under
  this Act, the officer must, if so requested, show the certificate to
  the occupant or person in charge of the place.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Powers

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} For the purposes of this Act, enforcement officers
  have all the powers of a peace officer, but the competent minister may
  specify limits on those powers when designating any person or person
  of a class of persons to act as enforcement officers.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Exemptions for law enforcement
  activities

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} For the purpose of investigations and other law
  enforcement activities under this Act, a competent minister may, on
  any terms and conditions that he or she considers necessary, exempt
  from the application of any provision of this Act, the regulations or
  an emergency order enforcement officers whom the competent minister
  has designated and who are carrying out duties or functions under this
  Act and persons acting under the direction and control of such
  enforcement officers.

### [Inspections]{.HTitleText2} {#h-435260 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Inspections

- **[[86]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-86 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} For
  the purpose of ensuring compliance with any provision of this Act, the
  regulations or an emergency order, an enforcement officer may, subject
  to subsection (3), at any reasonable time enter and inspect any place
  in which the enforcement officer believes, on reasonable grounds,
  there is any thing to which the provision applies or any document
  relating to its administration, and the enforcement officer may

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} open or cause to be opened any container that the
    enforcement officer believes, on reasonable grounds, contains that
    thing or document;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} inspect the thing and take samples free of charge;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} require any person to produce the document for
    inspection or copying, in whole or in part; and

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} seize any thing by means of or in relation to which
    the enforcement officer believes, on reasonable grounds, the
    provision has been contravened or that the enforcement officer
    believes, on reasonable grounds, will provide evidence of a
    contravention.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Conveyance

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} For the purposes of carrying out the inspection, the
  enforcement officer may stop a conveyance or direct that it be moved
  to a place where the inspection can be carried out.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Dwelling-place

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} The enforcement officer may not enter a
  dwelling-place except with the consent of the occupant or person in
  charge of the dwelling-place or under the authority of a warrant.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Authority to issue warrant for
  inspection of dwelling-place

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} On an [ex parte]{.otherLang lang="la"} application, a
  justice, as defined in section 2 of the [Criminal
  Code](/eng/acts/C-46), may issue a warrant, subject to any conditions
  specified in it, authorizing an enforcement officer to enter a
  dwelling-place, if the justice is satisfied by information on oath
  that

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the conditions for entry described in
    subsection (1) exist in relation to the dwelling-place;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} entry to the dwelling-place is necessary for the
    purposes of the administration of this Act, the regulations or an
    emergency order; and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} entry to the dwelling-place has been refused or
    there are reasonable grounds for believing that entry will be
    refused.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Authority to issue warrant for
  inspection of non-dwellings

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} On an [ex parte]{.otherLang lang="la"} application, a
  justice, as defined in section 2 of the [Criminal
  Code](/eng/acts/C-46), may issue a warrant, subject to any conditions
  specified in it, authorizing an enforcement officer to enter a place
  other than a dwelling-place, if the justice is satisfied by
  information on oath that

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the conditions for entry described in
    subsection (1) exist in relation to that place;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} entry to that place is necessary for the purposes
    of the administration of this Act, the regulations or an emergency
    order;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} entry to that place has been refused, the
    enforcement officer is not able to enter without the use of force or
    the place was abandoned; and

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} subject to subsection (6), all reasonable attempts
    were made to notify the owner, operator or person in charge of the
    place.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Waiving notice

  [(6)]{.lawlabel} The justice may waive the requirement to give notice
  referred to in subsection (5) if the justice is satisfied that
  attempts to give the notice would be unsuccessful because the owner,
  operator or person in charge is absent from the jurisdiction of the
  justice or that it is not in the public interest to give the notice.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Use of force

  [(7)]{.lawlabel} In executing a warrant issued under subsection (4) or
  (5), an enforcement officer may not use force unless the use of force
  has been specifically authorized in the warrant.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Operation of computer system and
  copying equipment

  [(8)]{.lawlabel} In carrying out an inspection of a place under this
  section, an enforcement officer may

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} use or cause to be used any computer system at the
    place to examine any data contained in or available to the computer
    system;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} reproduce any record or cause it to be reproduced
    from the data in the form of a printout or other intelligible
    output;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} take a printout or other output for examination or
    copying; and

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} use or cause to be used any copying equipment at
    the place to make copies of the record.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Duty of person in possession or control

  [(9)]{.lawlabel} Every person who is in possession or control of a
  place being inspected under this section must permit the enforcement
  officer to do anything referred to in subsection (8).

### [Disposition of Things Seized]{.HTitleText2} {#h-435295 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Custody of things seized

- **[[87]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-87
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Subject to subsections (2) to (4),
  if an enforcement officer seizes a thing under this Act or under a
  warrant issued under the [Criminal Code](/eng/acts/C-46),

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} sections 489.1 and 490 of the [Criminal
    Code](/eng/acts/C-46) apply; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the enforcement officer, or any person that the
    officer may designate, must retain custody of the thing subject to
    any order made under section 490 of the [Criminal
    Code](/eng/acts/C-46).

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Forfeiture if ownership not
  ascertainable

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} If the lawful ownership of or entitlement to the
  seized thing cannot be ascertained within 30 days after its seizure,
  the thing or any proceeds of its disposition are forfeited to Her
  Majesty in right of Canada, if the thing was seized by an enforcement
  officer employed in the public service of Canada or by the government
  of a territory, or to Her Majesty in right of a province, if the thing
  was seized by an enforcement officer employed by the government of
  that province.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Perishable things

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} If the seized thing is perishable, the enforcement
  officer may dispose of it or destroy it, and any proceeds of its
  disposition must be paid to the lawful owner or person lawfully
  entitled to possession of the thing, unless proceedings under this Act
  are commenced within 90 days after its seizure, in which case the
  proceeds must be retained by the enforcement officer pending the
  outcome of the proceedings.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Release of individual

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} An enforcement officer who seizes an individual of a
  species at risk may, at the time of the seizure, return the individual
  to the wild if the enforcement officer believes the individual to be
  alive.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Abandonment

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} The owner of the seized thing may abandon it to Her
  Majesty in right of Canada or a province.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Disposition by competent minister

**[[88]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-88 .sectionLabel}** Any thing that has been
forfeited or abandoned under this Act is to be dealt with and disposed
of as the competent minister may direct.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Liability for costs

**[[89]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-89 .sectionLabel}** The lawful owner and any
person lawfully entitled to possession of any thing seized, forfeited or
abandoned under this Act and who has been convicted of an offence under
this Act in relation to that thing, are jointly and severally, or
solidarily, liable for all the costs of inspection, seizure,
abandonment, forfeiture or disposition incurred by Her Majesty in excess
of any proceeds of disposition of the thing that have been forfeited to
Her Majesty under this Act.

### [Investigations]{.HTitleText2} {#h-435324 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Application for investigation

- **[[93]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-93 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A
  person who is a resident of Canada and at least 18 years of age may
  apply to the competent minister for an investigation of whether an
  alleged offence has been committed or whether anything directed
  towards its commission has been done.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Statement to accompany application

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The application must be in a form approved by the
  competent minister and must include a solemn affirmation or
  declaration containing

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the name and address of the applicant;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} a statement that the applicant is at least 18 years
    old and a resident of Canada;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} a statement of the nature of the alleged offence
    and the name of each person alleged to be involved;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} a summary of the evidence supporting the
    allegations;

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} the name and address of each person who might be
    able to give evidence about the alleged offence, together with a
    summary of the evidence that the person might give, to the extent
    that information is available to the applicant;

  - [(f)]{.lawlabel} a description of any document or other material
    that the applicant believes should be considered in the
    investigation and, if possible, a copy of the document; and

  - [(g)]{.lawlabel} details of any previous contact between the
    applicant and the competent minister about the alleged offence.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Investigation

- **[[94]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-94 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  competent minister must acknowledge receipt of the application within
  20 days after receiving it and, subject to subsections (2) and (3),
  investigate all matters that he or she considers necessary to
  determine the facts relating to the alleged offence.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Frivolous or vexatious applications

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} No investigation is required if the competent
  minister decides that the application is frivolous or vexatious.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Notice of decision

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} If the competent minister decides not to conduct an
  investigation, he or she must, within 60 days after the application
  for investigation is received, give notice of the decision, with
  reasons, to the applicant.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}When notice need not be given

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} The competent minister need not give the notice if an
  investigation in relation to the alleged offence is ongoing apart from
  the application.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Competent minister may send evidence to
Attorney General

**[[95]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-95 .sectionLabel}** At any stage of the
investigation, the competent minister may send any documents or other
evidence to the Attorney General for a consideration of whether an
offence has been or is about to be committed, and for any action that
the Attorney General may wish to take.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Suspension or conclusion of investigation

- **[[96]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-96 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  competent minister may suspend or conclude the investigation if he or
  she is of the opinion that the alleged offence does not require
  further investigation or the investigation does not substantiate the
  alleged offence or any other offence.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Report if investigation suspended

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} If the investigation is suspended, the competent
  minister must prepare a written report describing the information
  obtained during the investigation and stating the reasons for its
  suspension and the action, if any, that the competent minister has
  taken or proposes to take and send a copy of the report to the
  applicant. The competent minister must notify the applicant if the
  investigation is subsequently resumed.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Report when investigation concluded

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} When the investigation is concluded, the competent
  minister must prepare a written report describing the information
  obtained during the investigation and stating the reasons for its
  conclusion and the action, if any, that the competent minister has
  taken or proposes to take and send a copy of the report to the
  applicant and to each person whose conduct was investigated.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Personal information not to be
  disclosed

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} A copy of the report sent to a person whose conduct
  was investigated must not disclose the name or address of the
  applicant or any other personal information about him or her.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}When report need not be sent

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} If another investigation in relation to the alleged
  offence is ongoing apart from the application, the competent minister
  need not send copies of a report described in subsection (2) or (3)
  until the other investigation is suspended or concluded.

## [Offences and Punishment]{.HTitleText1} {#h-435359 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Offences

- **[[97]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-97 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Every
  person commits an offence who

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} contravenes subsection 32(1) or (2), section 33,
    subsection 36(1), 58(1), 60(1) or 61(1) or section 91 or 92;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} contravenes a prescribed provision of a regulation
    or an emergency order;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} fails to comply with a term or condition of a
    permit issued under subsection 73(1); or

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} fails to comply with an alternative measures
    agreement that the person has entered into under this Act.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Penalty

  [(1.1)]{.lawlabel} Every person who commits an offence under
  subsection (1) is liable

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} on conviction on indictment,

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} in the case of a corporation, other than a
      non-profit corporation, to a fine of not more than \$1,000,000,

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} in the case of a non-profit corporation, to a
      fine of not more than \$250,000, and

    - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} in the case of any other person, to a fine of
      not more than \$250,000 or to imprisonment for a term of not more
      than five years, or to both; or

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} on summary conviction,

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} in the case of a corporation, other than a
      non-profit corporation, to a fine of not more than \$300,000,

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} in the case of a non-profit corporation, to a
      fine of not more than \$50,000, and

    - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} in the case of any other person, to a fine of
      not more than \$50,000 or to imprisonment for a term of not more
      than one year, or to both.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Exception

  [(1.2)]{.lawlabel} Paragraph (1)(c) does not apply in respect of the
  failure to comply with any term or condition of any agreement, permit,
  licence, order or other similar document referred to in section 74 or
  subsection 78(1).

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Prescription of provisions

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} A regulation or emergency order may prescribe which
  of its provisions may give rise to an offence.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Subsequent offence

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} For a second or subsequent conviction, the amount of
  the fine may, despite subsection (1.1), be double the amount set out
  in that subsection.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Continuing offence

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} A person who commits or continues an offence on more
  than one day is liable to be convicted for a separate offence for each
  day on which the offence is committed or continued.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Fines cumulative

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} A fine imposed for an offence involving more than one
  animal, plant or other organism may be calculated in respect of each
  one as though it had been the subject of a separate information and
  the fine then imposed is the total of that calculation.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Additional fine

  [(6)]{.lawlabel} If a person is convicted of an offence and the court
  is satisfied that monetary benefits accrued to the person as a result
  of the commission of the offence, the court may order the person to
  pay an additional fine in an amount equal to the court's estimation of
  the amount of the monetary benefits, which additional fine may exceed
  the maximum amount of any fine that may otherwise be imposed under
  this Act.

- Definition of [[non-profit corporation]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  [(7)]{.lawlabel} For the purposes of subsection (1.1), [[non-profit
  corporation]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means a corporation, no part of the
  income of which is payable to, or is otherwise available for, the
  personal benefit of any proprietor, member or shareholder of the
  corporation.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Due diligence

**[[100]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-100 .sectionLabel}** Due diligence is a
defence in a prosecution for an offence.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Venue

**[[101]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-101 .sectionLabel}** A prosecution for an
offence may be instituted, heard and determined in the place where the
offence was committed, the subject-matter of the prosecution arose, the
accused was apprehended or the accused happens to be or is carrying on
business.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Sentencing considerations

**[[102]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-102 .sectionLabel}** A court that imposes a
sentence shall take into account, in addition to any other principles
that it is required to consider, the following factors:

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} the harm or risk of harm caused by the commission of
  the offence;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} whether the offender was found to have committed the
  offence intentionally, recklessly or inadvertently;

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} whether the offender was found by the court to have
  been negligent or incompetent or to have shown a lack of concern with
  respect to the commission of the offence;

- [(d)]{.lawlabel} any property, benefit or advantage received or
  receivable by the offender to which, but for the commission of the
  offence, the offender would not have been entitled;

- [(e)]{.lawlabel} any evidence from which the court may reasonably
  conclude that the offender has a history of non-compliance with
  legislation designed to protect wildlife species; and

- [(f)]{.lawlabel} all available sanctions that are reasonable in the
  circumstances, with particular attention to the circumstances of
  aboriginal offenders.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Forfeiture

- **[[103]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-103 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} If a
  person is convicted of an offence, the convicting court may, in
  addition to any punishment imposed, order that any seized thing by
  means of or in relation to which the offence was committed, or any
  proceeds of its disposition, be forfeited to Her Majesty.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Return if no forfeiture ordered

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} If the convicting court does not order the
  forfeiture, the seized thing, or the proceeds of its disposition, must
  be returned to its lawful owner or the person lawfully entitled to it.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Retention or sale

**[[104]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-104 .sectionLabel}** If a fine is imposed on
a person convicted of an offence, any seized thing, or any proceeds of
its disposition, may be retained until the fine is paid or the thing may
be sold in satisfaction of the fine and the proceeds applied, in whole
or in part, in payment of the fine.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Orders of court

**[[105]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-105 .sectionLabel}** If a person is
convicted of an offence, the court may, in addition to any punishment
imposed and having regard to the nature of the offence and the
circumstances surrounding its commission, make an order having any or
all of the following effects:

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} prohibiting the person from doing any act or engaging
  in any activity that could, in the opinion of the court, result in the
  continuation or repetition of the offence;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} directing the person to take any action that the
  court considers appropriate to remedy or avoid any harm to any
  wildlife species that resulted or may result from the commission of
  the offence;

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} directing the person to have an environmental audit
  conducted by a person of a class and at the times specified by the
  court and directing the person to remedy any deficiencies revealed
  during the audit;

- [(d)]{.lawlabel} directing the person to publish, in any manner that
  the court considers appropriate, the facts relating to the commission
  of the offence;

- [(e)]{.lawlabel} directing the person to perform community service in
  accordance with any conditions that the court considers reasonable;

- [(f)]{.lawlabel} directing the person to submit to the competent
  minister, on application to the court by the competent minister within
  three years after the conviction, any information about the activities
  of the person that the court considers appropriate;

- [(g)]{.lawlabel} directing the person to pay a competent minister or
  the government of a province or a territory an amount for all or any
  of the cost of remedial or preventive action taken, or to be taken, by
  or on behalf of the competent minister or that government as a result
  of the commission of the offence;

- [(h)]{.lawlabel} directing the person to pay, in the manner prescribed
  by the court, an amount for the purpose of conducting research into
  the protection of the wildlife species in respect of which the offence
  was committed;

- [(i)]{.lawlabel} directing the person to pay, in the manner prescribed
  by the court, an amount to an educational institution for scholarships
  for students enrolled in environmental studies;

- [(j)]{.lawlabel} directing the person to post a bond or pay to the
  court an amount that the court considers appropriate for the purpose
  of ensuring compliance with any prohibition, direction or requirement
  under this section; and

- [(k)]{.lawlabel} requiring the person to comply with any other
  conditions that the court considers appropriate for securing the
  person's good conduct and for preventing the person from repeating the
  offence or committing other offences.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Suspended sentence

- **[[106]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-106 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} If a
  person is convicted of an offence and the court suspends the passing
  of sentence under paragraph 731(1)(a) of the [Criminal
  Code](/eng/acts/C-46), the court may, in addition to any probation
  order made under that Act, make an order containing one or more of the
  prohibitions, directions or requirements mentioned in section 105.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Imposition of sentence

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} If the person does not comply with the order or is
  convicted of another offence, within three years after the order is
  made, the court may, on the application of the prosecution, impose any
  sentence that could have been imposed if the passing of sentence had
  not been suspended.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Limitation period

- **[[107]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-107
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Proceedings by way of summary
  conviction in respect of an offence may be commenced at any time
  within, but not later than, two years after the day on which the
  subject-matter of the proceedings became known to the competent
  minister.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Competent minister's certificate

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} A document appearing to have been issued by the
  competent minister, certifying the day on which the subject-matter of
  any proceedings became known to the competent minister, is admissible
  in evidence without proof of the signature or official character of
  the person appearing to have signed the document and is proof of the
  matter asserted in it.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}References to the competent minister

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} A reference to the competent minister in this section
  includes a provincial or territorial minister if the competent
  minister has delegated responsibility for the enforcement of this Act,
  the regulations or an emergency order in the province or territory to
  the provincial or territorial minister and the offence is alleged to
  have been committed in the province or territory.

## [Alternative Measures]{.HTitleText1} {#h-435445 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}When alternative measures may be used

- **[[108]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-108
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Alternative measures may be used to
  deal with a person who is alleged to have committed an offence, but
  only if it is not inconsistent with the purposes of this Act to do so
  and the following conditions are met:

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the measures are part of a program of alternative
    measures authorized by the Attorney General, after consultation with
    the competent minister;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} an information has been laid in respect of the
    offence;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} the Attorney General, after consultation with the
    competent minister, is satisfied that the alternative measures would
    be appropriate, having regard to the nature of the offence, the
    circumstances surrounding its commission and the following factors,
    namely,

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} the protection of species at risk,

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} the person's history of compliance with this
      Act,

    - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} whether the offence is a repeated occurrence,

    - [(iv)]{.lawlabel} any allegation that information is being or was
      concealed or other attempts to subvert the purposes and
      requirements of this Act are being or have been made, and

    - [(v)]{.lawlabel} whether any remedial or preventive action has
      been taken by or on behalf of the person in relation to the
      offence;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} the person applies, in accordance with regulations
    made under paragraph 119(a), to participate in the alternative
    measures after having been informed of them;

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} the person and the Attorney General have concluded
    an agreement respecting the alternative measures within 180 days
    after the person has, with respect to the offence, been served with
    a summons, been issued an appearance notice or a release order or
    entered into an undertaking;

  - [(f)]{.lawlabel} before consenting to participate in the alternative
    measures, the person has been advised of the right to be represented
    by counsel;

  - [(g)]{.lawlabel} the person accepts responsibility for the act or
    omission that forms the basis of the offence;

  - [(h)]{.lawlabel} there is, in the opinion of the Attorney General,
    sufficient evidence to proceed with the prosecution of the offence;
    and

  - [(i)]{.lawlabel} the prosecution of the offence is not barred at
    law.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Restriction on use

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Alternative measures may not be used to deal with a
  person who

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} denies participation or involvement in the
    commission of the alleged offence; or

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} expresses the wish to have any charge against them
    dealt with by the court.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Admissions not admissible in evidence

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} No admission, confession or statement accepting
  responsibility for a given act or omission made by a person as a
  condition of being dealt with by alternative measures is admissible in
  evidence against the person in any civil or criminal proceedings.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Dismissal of charge

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} A court must dismiss a charge laid against a person
  in respect of an offence if alternative measures have been used to
  deal with the person in respect of the alleged offence and

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the court is satisfied on a balance of
    probabilities that the person has totally complied with the
    agreement; or

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the court is satisfied on a balance of
    probabilities that the person has partially complied with the
    agreement and, in the opinion of the court, the prosecution of the
    charge would be unfair, having regard to the circumstances and the
    person's performance with respect to the agreement.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}No bar to proceedings

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} The use of alternative measures in respect of a
  person who is alleged to have committed an offence is not a bar to any
  proceedings against the person under this Act.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Laying of information, etc.

  [(6)]{.lawlabel} This section does not prevent any person from laying
  an information, obtaining the issue or confirmation of any process, or
  proceeding with the prosecution of any offence, in accordance with
  law.

## [Assessment of Wildlife Species Mentioned in the Schedules]{.HTitleText1} {#h-435614 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Assessment of status

- **[[130]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-130
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} COSEWIC must assess the status of
  each wildlife species set out in Schedule 2 or 3, and, as part of the
  assessment, identify existing and potential threats to the species and

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} classify the species as extinct, extirpated,
    endangered, threatened or of special concern;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} indicate that COSEWIC does not have sufficient
    information to classify the species; or

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} indicate that the species is not currently at risk.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Time for assessment --- Schedule 2

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} In the case of a species set out in Schedule 2, the
  assessment must be completed within 30 days after section 14 comes
  into force.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Deemed classification

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} If an assessment of a wildlife species set out in
  Schedule 2 is not completed within the required time or, if there has
  been an extension, within the extended time, COSEWIC is deemed to have
  classified the species as indicated in Schedule 2.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Time for assessment --- Schedule 3

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} In the case of a species set out in Schedule 3, the
  assessment must be completed within one year after the competent
  minister requests the assessment. If there is more than one competent
  minister with respect to the species, they must make the request
  jointly.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Extension

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} The Governor in Council may, on the recommendation of
  the Minister after consultation with the competent minister or
  ministers, by order, extend the time provided for the assessment of

  [(6)]{.lawlabel} Subsections 15(2) and (3) and 21(1) and section 25

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Recent reports

  [(7)]{.lawlabel} In making its assessment of a wildlife species,
  COSEWIC may take into account and rely on any report on the species

  royal assent.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Section 27 applies

is added to the List by the Governor in Council as the result of an
assessment under section 130, the recovery strategy for the species must

is added to the List by the Governor in Council as a species of special
concern as the result of an assessment under section 130, the management

listing.
