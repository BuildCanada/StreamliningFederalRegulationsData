## Protecting Air Service Act {#protecting-air-service-act .Title-of-Act}

[S.C.]{.abbr title="Statutes of Canada"} 2012, c. 2
:::

Assented to 2012-03-15

An Act to provide for the continuation and resumption of air service
operations
::::

:::: section
::: Enacts
Her Majesty, by and with the advice and consent of the Senate and House
of Commons of Canada, enacts as follows:
:::

## [Short Title]{.HTitleText1} {#h-400416 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Short title

**[[1]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-1 .sectionLabel}** This Act may be cited as
the [Protecting Air Service Act](/eng/acts/P-25.6).

[[collective agreement]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

### [Air Service Operations]{.HTitleText2} {#h-400435 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Suspension of right to declare strike or
lockout

**[[4]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-4 .sectionLabel}** If, before the coming into
force of this Act, the employer has not declared or caused a lockout and
the union has not declared or authorized a strike, on the coming into
force of this Act the employer's right to declare or cause a lockout and
the union's right to declare or authorize a strike are suspended until
the day on which the collective agreement, as extended by subsection
9(1), expires.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Application of sections 6 to 8

**[[5]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-5 .sectionLabel}** Sections 6 to 8 apply if,
before the coming into force of this Act, the employer has declared or
caused a lockout or the union has declared or authorized a strike.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Continuation or resumption of air service
operations

**[[6]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-6 .sectionLabel}** On the coming into force of
this Act,

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} the employer must continue, or resume without delay,
  as the case may be, air service operations; and

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} every employee must, when so required, continue, or
  resume without delay, as the case may be, the duties of their
  employment.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Prohibitions

**[[7]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-7 .sectionLabel}** It is prohibited for the
employer and for any officer or representative of the employer to

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} in any manner impede any employee from complying with
  paragraph 6(b); or

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} discharge or in any other manner discipline, or
  authorize or direct the discharge or discipline of, any employee by
  reason of the employee having been on strike before the coming into
  force of this Act.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Obligations

**[[8]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-8 .sectionLabel}** The union and each officer
and representative of the union must

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} without delay on the coming into force of this Act,
  give notice to the employees that, by reason of that coming into
  force, air service operations are to be continued or resumed, as the
  case may be, and that the employees, when so required, are to
  continue, or resume without delay, as the case may be, the duties of
  their employment;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} take all reasonable steps to ensure that employees
  comply with paragraph 6(b); and

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} refrain from any conduct that may encourage employees
  not to comply with paragraph 6(b).

### [Extension of Collective Agreement]{.HTitleText2} {#h-400453 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Extension

- **[[9]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-9 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The term
  of the collective agreement is extended to include the period
  beginning on April 1, 2011 and ending on the day on which a new
  collective agreement between the employer and the union comes into
  effect.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Collective agreement binding for
  extended term

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Despite anything in the collective agreement or in
  Part I of the [Canada Labour Code](/eng/acts/L-2), the collective
  agreement, as extended by subsection (1), is effective and binding on
  the parties to it for the period for which it is extended. However,
  that Part applies in respect of the collective agreement, as extended,
  as if that period were the term of the collective agreement.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Strikes and lockouts prohibited

**[[10]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-10 .sectionLabel}** Until the day on which
the collective agreement, as extended by subsection 9(1), expires, it is
prohibited

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} for the employer and for any officer or
  representative of the employer to declare or cause a lockout against
  the union;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} for the union and for any officer or representative
  of the union to declare or authorize a strike against the employer;
  and

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} for an employee to participate in a strike against
  the employer.

### [Final Offer Selection]{.HTitleText2} {#h-400464 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Appointment of arbitrator

**[[11]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-11 .sectionLabel}** The Minister must appoint
as arbitrator for final offer selection a person that the Minister
considers appropriate.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Powers and duties

**[[12]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-12 .sectionLabel}** The arbitrator has, with
any modifications that the circumstances require, all the powers and
duties of an arbitrator under paragraphs 60(1)(a) and (a.2) to (a.4) and
section 61 of the [Canada Labour Code](/eng/acts/L-2).

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Obligation to provide final offer

- **[[13]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-13 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Within
  the time and in the manner that the arbitrator may specify, the
  employer and the union must each submit to the arbitrator

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} a list of the matters on which the employer and the
    union were in agreement as of a date specified by the arbitrator and
    proposed contractual language that would give effect to those
    matters;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} a list of the matters remaining in dispute on that
    date; and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} a final offer in respect of the matters referred to
    in paragraph (b).

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Contractual language

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The final offer must be submitted with proposed
  contractual language that can be incorporated into the new collective
  agreement.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Arbitrator's duties

- **[[14]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-14
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Subject to section 16, within 90
  days after being appointed, or within any longer period that may be
  specified by the Minister, the arbitrator must

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} determine the matters on which the employer and the
    union were in agreement as of the date specified for the purposes of
    paragraph 13(1)(a);

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} determine the matters remaining in dispute on that
    date;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} select, in order to resolve the matters remaining
    in dispute, either the final offer submitted by the employer or the
    final offer submitted by the union; and

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} make a decision in respect of the resolution of the
    matters referred to in this subsection and forward a copy of the
    decision to the Minister, the employer and the union.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Guiding principle

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} In making the selection of a final offer, the
  arbitrator is to take into account the tentative agreement reached by
  the employer and the union on February 10, 2012 and the report of the
  conciliation commissioner dated February 22, 2012 that was released to
  the parties, and is to be guided by the need for terms and conditions
  of employment that are consistent with those in other airlines and
  that will provide the necessary degree of flexibility to ensure

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the short-and long-term economic viability and
    competitiveness of the employer; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the sustainability of the employer's pension plan,
    taking into account any short-term funding pressures on the
    employer.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}If no final offer submitted

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} If either the employer or the union fails to provide
  the arbitrator with a final offer in accordance with paragraph
  13(1)(c), the arbitrator must select the final offer provided by the
  other party.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Contractual language

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} The arbitrator's decision must be drafted in a manner
  that constitutes a new collective agreement between the employer and
  the union and, to the extent that it is possible, incorporate the
  contractual language that is referred to in paragraph 13(1)(a) and
  that is in the final offer selected by the arbitrator.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Proceedings prohibited

**[[15]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-15 .sectionLabel}** No order is to be made,
no process is to be entered into and no proceeding is to be taken in
court

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} to question the appointment of the arbitrator; or

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} to review, prohibit or restrain any proceeding or
  decision of the arbitrator.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}New collective agreement not precluded

**[[16]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-16 .sectionLabel}** Nothing in this Part
precludes the employer and the union from entering into a new collective
agreement at any time before the arbitrator makes a decision and, if
they do so, the arbitrator's duties under this Part cease as of the day
on which the new collective agreement is entered into.

### [New Collective Agreement]{.HTitleText2} {#h-400498 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}New collective agreement

- **[[17]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-17
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Despite anything in Part I of the
  [Canada Labour Code](/eng/acts/L-2), the arbitrator's decision
  constitutes a new collective agreement between the employer and the
  union that is effective and binding on the parties beginning on the
  day on which it is made. However, that Part applies in respect of the
  new collective agreement as if it had been entered into under that
  Part.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Coming into effect of provisions

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The new collective agreement may provide that any of
  its provisions are effective and binding on a day that is before or
  after the day on which the new collective agreement becomes effective
  and binding.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Amendments

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} Nothing in this Part is to be construed so as to
  limit or restrict the rights of the parties to agree to amend any
  provision of the new collective agreement, other than a provision
  relating to its term, and to give effect to the amendment.

## [PART 3]{.HLabel1}[General]{.HTitleText1} {#h-400585 .Part}

### [Costs]{.HTitleText2} {#h-400586 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Costs

**[[33]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-33 .sectionLabel}** All costs incurred by Her
Majesty in right of Canada relating to the appointment of an arbitrator
and the performance of an arbitrator's duties under this Act are debts
due to Her Majesty in right of Canada and may be recovered as such, in
any court of competent jurisdiction, in equal parts from, in the case of
an appointment under Part 1, the International Association of Machinists
and Aerospace Workers and the employer, and in the case of an
appointment under Part 2, the Air Canada Pilots Association and the
employer.

### [Enforcement]{.HTitleText2} {#h-400589 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Individuals

- **[[34]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-34 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} An
  individual who contravenes any provision of this Act is guilty of an
  offence punishable on summary conviction and is liable, for each day
  or part of a day during which the offence continues, to a fine of

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} not more than \$50,000 if the individual was acting
    in the capacity of an officer or representative of the employer, the
    International Association of Machinists and Aerospace Workers or the
    Air Canada Pilots Association when the offence was committed; or

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} not more than \$1,000 in any other case.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Employer or union

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} If the employer, the International Association of
  Machinists and Aerospace Workers or the Air Canada Pilots Association
  contravenes any provision of this Act, it is guilty of an offence
  punishable on summary conviction and is liable, for each day or part
  of a day during which the offence continues, to a fine of not more
  than \$100,000.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}No imprisonment

**[[35]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-35 .sectionLabel}** Despite subsection 787(2)
of the [Criminal Code](/eng/acts/C-46), no term of imprisonment is to be
imposed in default of payment of a fine that is imposed under section
34.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Recovery of fines

**[[36]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-36 .sectionLabel}** If a person is convicted
of an offence under section 34 and the fine that is imposed is not paid
when required, the prosecutor may, by filing the conviction, enter as a
judgment the amount of the fine and costs, if any, in a superior court
of the province in which the trial was held, and the judgment is
enforceable against the person in the same manner as if it were a
judgment rendered against the person in that court in civil proceedings.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Presumption

**[[37]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-37 .sectionLabel}** For the purposes of this
Act, the International Association of Machinists and Aerospace Workers
and the Air Canada Pilots Association are deemed to be persons.
