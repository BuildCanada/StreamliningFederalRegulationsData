    licensed pilot or a pilotage certificate holder. ([pilotage
    obligatoire]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

[[compulsory pilotage area]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

    Minister under subsection 38.1(1). ([brevet]{.DefinedTermLink

[[Minister]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[Minister]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means the Minister of

## [Purpose and Principles]{.HTitleText1} {#h-394353 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Purpose and principles

**[[2]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-2 .sectionLabel}** The purpose of this Act is
to set out a framework for the provision of pilotage services in
accordance with the following principles:

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} that pilotage services be provided in a manner that
  promotes and contributes to the safety of navigation, including the
  safety of the public and marine personnel, and that protects human
  health, property and the environment;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} that pilotage services be provided in an efficient
  and cost-effective manner;

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} that risk management tools be used effectively and
  that evolving technologies be taken into consideration; and

- [(d)]{.lawlabel} that an Authority's pilotage charges be set at levels
  that allow the Authority to be financially self-sufficient.

::: HistoricalNote
- R.S., 1985, c. P-14, s. 2
- 2001, c. 26, s. 316

## [Pilotage Authorities]{.HTitleText1} {#h-394365 .Part}

### [Establishment]{.HTitleText2} {#h-394366 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Pilotage Authorities established

- **[[3]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-3 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Each
  Pilotage Authority named in the schedule is hereby established as a
  body corporate consisting of a Chairperson and not more than six other
  members.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Appointment of Chairperson of Authority

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} On the recommendation of the Minister and after
  consultation with members of the Authority and with the users of its
  services, the Governor in Council may appoint the Chairperson of an
  Authority to hold office during pleasure for the term that the
  Governor in Council considers appropriate.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Other members

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} Each of the other members of an Authority shall be
  appointed by the Minister, with the approval of the Governor in
  Council, to hold office during pleasure for a term, not exceeding four
  years, that will ensure, as far as possible, the expiration in any one
  year of the terms of office of not more than one half of the members.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Full- or part-time

  [(3.1)]{.lawlabel} The Chairperson and the Vice-Chairperson may be
  appointed to serve full-time or part-time.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Ineligibility

  [(3.2)]{.lawlabel} An individual who is engaged in the business of
  providing pilotage services --- or who is employed by a person that is
  engaged in the business of providing pilotage services or that uses
  pilotage services --- is not eligible to be the Chairperson or other
  member of an Authority.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Head office

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} An Authority shall have its head office at the place
  named in respect of that Authority in the schedule.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Employment of staff

- **[[15]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-15
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Subject to subsection (2), an
  Authority may employ such officers and employees, including licensed
  pilots and apprentice pilots, as are necessary for the proper conduct
  of the work of the Authority.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Contract with body corporate for
  services

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Where a majority of licensed pilots within the
  region, or any part thereof, set out in respect of an Authority in the

  elect not to become employees of the Authority, the Authority may
  contract with that body corporate for the services of licensed pilots
  and the training of apprentice pilots in the region or part thereof

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} Any body corporate that contracts, pursuant to
  subsection (2), with an Authority shall permit a licensed pilot or
  apprentice pilot in the region or part thereof to which the contract
  relates who is not a member or shareholder of that body corporate to
  become a member or shareholder on the same terms and conditions as the
  licensed pilots and apprentice pilots who formed or are members or
  shareholders of that body corporate.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Renewal of contract

- **[[15.1]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-15.1
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Where a contract for services
  referred to in subsection 15(2) does not provide a mechanism for the
  resolution of disputes in the contract renewal process, fifty days
  before the contract expires, the parties to the contract shall jointly
  choose a mediator and an arbitrator and shall refer to the mediator
  all issues related to the renewal of the contract that remain
  unresolved.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}No agreement

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The Minister shall choose the mediator or arbitrator
  if the parties cannot agree on one or if the one they choose is
  unavailable.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Mediation

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} The mediator has thirty days in which to bring the
  parties to agreement on the outstanding issues, at the end of which
  time the parties to the contract shall refer all of the remaining
  outstanding issues to the arbitrator.

::: HistoricalNote
- 1998, c. 10, s. 148
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Final offers

- **[[15.2]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-15.2
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The parties to the contract shall
  each submit a final offer in respect of the outstanding issues to each
  other and to the arbitrator within five days after the date on which
  those issues are referred to the arbitrator.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Decision of arbitrator

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The arbitrator shall consider, among other things,
  the principles set out in section 2 and, within 15 days, choose one or
  other of the final offers in its entirety.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Effect of decision

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} The final offer chosen by the arbitrator is final and
  binding and becomes part of the new contract for services that is
  effective on the day after the former contract expires.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Sharing of costs

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} The parties to the contract shall share equally the
  cost of the fees of the mediator or arbitrator.

### [Objects and Powers]{.HTitleText2} {#h-394480 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Objects

**[[18]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-18 .sectionLabel}** In keeping with the
principles set out in section 2, the objects of an Authority are to
establish, operate, maintain and administer, in the interests of safety
of navigation, an efficient pilotage service within the region set out
in respect of the Authority in the schedule.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Powers

- **[[19]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-19 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} An
  Authority may, for its use, purchase, lease or otherwise acquire

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} land, buildings, wharves or other structures;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} pilot boats;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} radio and other communication equipment; and

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} such other equipment, supplies and services as may
    be necessary for the operation of an efficient and economical
    pilotage service.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Charging principles

- **[[33.2]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-33.2 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} An
  Authority shall observe the following charging principles when
  establishing or revising pilotage charges:

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} that pilotage charges be established and revised in
    accordance with an explicit methodology --- that includes any
    conditions affecting the pilotage charges --- that the Authority has
    established and published;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} that pilotage charges be structured in a way that
    does not encourage a user to engage in practices that diminish
    safety for the purpose of avoiding a charge;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} that pilotage charges be the same for Canadian
    users or ships and foreign users or ships;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} that pilotage charges be set at levels that allow
    the Authority to be financially self-sufficient and be fair and
    reasonable; and

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} that pilotage charges not be set at levels that,
    based on reasonable and prudent projections, would generate revenues
    exceeding the Authority's current and future financial requirements
    related to the provision of compulsory pilotage services.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Financial requirements

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} For the purposes of paragraph (1)(e), financial
  requirements include

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} operations and maintenance costs;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} management and administration costs;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} debt servicing requirements and financial
    requirements arising out of contractual agreements relating to the
    borrowing of money;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} capital costs and depreciation costs on capital
    assets;

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} financial requirements necessary for the Authority
    to maintain an appropriate credit rating;

  - [(f)]{.lawlabel} tax liability;

  - [(g)]{.lawlabel} payments to the Minister for the purpose of
    defraying the costs of the administration of this Act, including the
    development of regulations, and the enforcement of this Act;

  - [(h)]{.lawlabel} reasonable reserves for future expenditures and
    contingencies; and

  - [(i)]{.lawlabel} other costs determined in accordance with
    accounting principles recommended by the Chartered Professional
    Accountants of Canada or its successor or assign.

## [Conduct of Ship Subject to Compulsory Pilotage]{.HTitleText1} {#h-1242575 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Prohibition where pilotage compulsory

- **[[38.01]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-38.01
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} No individual shall have the conduct
  of a ship within a compulsory pilotage area unless they are a licensed
  pilot for that compulsory pilotage area or a regular member of the
  ship's complement who is a pilotage certificate holder for that
  compulsory pilotage area.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Non-application

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Subsection (1) does not apply if

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the master of the ship takes the conduct of it in
    accordance with subsection 38.02(1);

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the relevant Authority waives compulsory pilotage;
    or

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} the ship is in Canadian waters that are contiguous
    with waters of the United States, the ship is under the conduct of
    an individual who is authorized to have the conduct of a ship by an
    appropriate authority of the United States and the conditions set
    out in the regulations are met.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Pilot responsible to master

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} A licensed pilot or pilotage certificate holder who
  has the conduct of a ship is responsible to the master for the safe
  navigation of the ship.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}When disqualified from pilotage

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} No licensed pilot or pilotage certificate holder
  shall have the conduct of a ship within a compulsory pilotage area or
  be on duty on board a ship under a regulation requiring a ship to have
  a licensed pilot or a pilotage certificate holder on board if

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} they know of any physical or mental disability that
    prevents them from meeting the qualifications required for their
    licence or pilotage certificate;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} their ability is impaired by alcohol or a drug or
    from any other cause; or

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} their licence or pilotage certificate is suspended.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Prohibition --- alcohol or drug
  consumption

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} No licensed pilot or pilotage certificate holder
  shall, while on duty, consume alcohol or any drug that may impair the
  ability of that pilot or holder to have the conduct of the ship.

::: HistoricalNote
- [2019, c. 29, s. 241](#1156375-1242594){.wb-lbx
  aria-controls="centred-popup" ajax-after="1156375-1242594.html"
  role="button"}
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Power to take conduct of ship

- **[[38.02]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-38.02
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} If the master of a ship believes on
  reasonable grounds that the actions of a licensed pilot or a pilotage
  certificate holder on board a ship are, in any way, endangering the
  safety of the ship, the master may, in the interest of the safety of
  the ship, take the conduct of the ship in place of the licensed pilot
  or pilotage certificate holder or relieve the licensed pilot from duty
  on board ship.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Master to report

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The master of a ship who takes the conduct of it in
  accordance with subsection (1) shall, within three days of taking the
  conduct of it, file a written report with the Minister that sets out
  the master's reasons for doing so.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Issue of pilotage certificate

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The Minister shall issue a pilotage certificate for a

  the Minister is satisfied that the applicant

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} meets the qualifications provided for in the
    regulations; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} has a degree of skill and local knowledge of the
    waters of the compulsory pilotage area that is similar to that
    required of an applicant for a licence for that compulsory pilotage
    area.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Citizenship

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} To be eligible for a licence or pilotage certificate,
  the applicant shall be

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} a Canadian citizen; or

    within the meaning of subsection 2(1) of the [Immigration and
    Refugee Protection Act](/eng/acts/I-2.5) who has not been ordinarily
    resident in Canada for six years or who has been ordinarily resident
    in Canada for six years or more and is shown, to the satisfaction of
    the Minister, not to have become a Canadian citizen as a result of
    circumstances beyond the control of the applicant.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Validity period

- **[[38.4]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-38.4 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Detention order

- **[[46.2]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-46.2 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} An
  authorized person may order the detention of a ship if the authorized
  person has reasonable grounds to believe that an offence under this
  Act has been committed by or in respect of the ship.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Order in writing

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The detention order is to be in writing and be
  addressed to every person empowered to grant clearance to the ship.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Service of detention order notice

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} Subject to subsection (4), notice of a detention
  order is to be served

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} by delivering a copy of the notice to the
    authorized representative of the ship or, in the absence of an
    authorized representative, a person in charge of the ship; or

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} if service cannot reasonably be effected in the
    manner provided in paragraph (a), by posting a copy of the notice on
    any conspicuous part of the ship.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Public notice of detention order

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} If service cannot reasonably be effected in the
  manner provided in paragraph (3)(a) or (b), public notice of the
  detention order is to be given.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Contents of notice

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} The notice is to indicate

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the measures that are to be taken for the detention
    order to be rescinded; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the amount and form of any security that is to be
    deposited with the Minister.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Notification of foreign state

  [(6)]{.lawlabel} If the ship that is subject to a detention order is
  registered in a foreign state, that state is to be notified that the
  order was made.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Movement of ship prohibited

  [(7)]{.lawlabel} Subject to subsection 46.22(1), it is prohibited for
  a person to move a ship that is subject to a detention order.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Granting clearance prohibited

  [(8)]{.lawlabel} It is prohibited for a person to which a detention
  order is addressed, after they have received notice of the order, to
  grant clearance to the ship that is subject to the order, unless they
  have been notified that the order has been rescinded.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Rescission of order

  [(9)]{.lawlabel} An authorized person may rescind a detention order
  if, in their opinion, it would be in the public interest to do so.
  However, an authorized person shall rescind a detention order if they
  are satisfied that the measures indicated in the notice referred to in
  subsection (5) have been taken and, if applicable, security in the
  amount and form indicated in the notice has been deposited with the
  Minister.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Notice of rescission

  [(10)]{.lawlabel} An authorized person who rescinds a detention order
  shall notify, in the form and manner specified by the Minister, the
  persons referred to in subsection (2) and the persons on which the
  notice was served under paragraph (3)(a) of the rescission.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Liability for costs and expenses

  [(11)]{.lawlabel} The authorized representative of a ship that is
  subject to a detention order or, in the absence of an authorized
  representative, the owner of that ship is liable for all costs and
  expenses resulting from the detention.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Return of security

  [(12)]{.lawlabel} If, in the Minister's opinion, the matter has been
  resolved, the Minister

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} may apply the security to reimburse Her Majesty in

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} shall return the security, or any part of it that

    expenses and any fine or penalty imposed under this Act have been

### [Administrative Monetary Penalties]{.HTitleText2} {#h-1242774 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Violation --- person

- **[[46.26]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-46.26
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Every person that contravenes a
  provision or direction that is designated as a violation in the
  regulations commits a violation and is liable to a penalty.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Violation --- ship

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Every ship that contravenes any of the following
  commits a violation and is liable to a penalty:

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} a direction given under paragraph 46.12(3)(b) or
    subsection 46.12(4); or

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} a provision the contravention of which is
    designated as a violation by the regulations.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Continuing violation

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} A violation that is committed or continued on more
  than one day constitutes a separate violation for each day on which it
  is committed or continued.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Penalty

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} The maximum amount payable as the penalty for a
  violation set out in subsection (1) or (2) is \$250,000 and the total
  maximum amount payable for a related series or class of violations is
  the amount set out in the regulations.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Purpose of penalty

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} The purpose of the penalty is to promote compliance
  with this Act and not to punish.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Clarification

  [(6)]{.lawlabel} If a contravention of a provision may be proceeded
  with as a violation or as an offence, proceeding with it in one manner
  precludes proceeding with it in the other.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Nature of violation

  [(7)]{.lawlabel} For greater certainty, a violation is not an offence
  and, accordingly, section 126 of the [Criminal Code](/eng/acts/C-46)
  does not apply in respect of a violation.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Due diligence defence --- person

  [(8)]{.lawlabel} A person is not to be found to be liable for a
  violation under this Act if they establish that they exercised due
  diligence to prevent its commission.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Due diligence defence --- ship

  [(9)]{.lawlabel} A ship is not to be found to be liable for a
  violation under this Act if the person that committed the act or
  omission that constitutes the violation establishes that they
  exercised due diligence to prevent its commission.

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} The burden is on the Minister to establish that the

    regulations, the member shall inform the Minister and the person or
    ship of the amount determined by the member to be payable to the

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Manner of service

- **[[46.37]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-46.37
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A notice referred to in any of
  sections 46.27, 46.29, 46.3 and 46.45 is to be served

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} in the case of an individual,

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} by leaving a copy of it with the individual at
      any place or with someone who appears to be an adult member of the
      same household at the individual's last known address or usual
      place of residence or, in the case of an individual under the age
      of 18, with a parent or other person having custody of them or
      exercising parental authority over them, or

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} by sending a copy of it by registered mail,
      courier or fax or other electronic means to the individual's last
      known address or usual place of residence;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} in the case of a person other than an individual,

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} by leaving a copy of it with their representative
      or with an officer or other individual who appears to control or
      manage the person's or representative's head office or place of
      business, or

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} by sending a copy of it by registered mail,
      courier or fax or other electronic means to their representative,
      to an individual referred to in subparagraph (i) or to the
      person's or representative's head office or place of business; and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} in the case of a ship,

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} by delivering a copy of it personally to the
      master or another individual who is, or appears to be, in charge
      of the ship,

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} by posting a copy of it on any conspicuous part
      of the ship,

    - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} by leaving a copy of it with the owner or
      operator of the ship, with the owner's or operator's
      representative or with an officer or other individual who appears
      to control or manage the owner's, operator's or representative's
      head office or place of business, or

    - [(iv)]{.lawlabel} by sending a copy of it by registered mail,
      courier or fax or other electronic means to the ship or to a
      person referred to in subparagraph (i) or (iii) or to their head
      office or place of business.

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Service may be proved by

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} in the case of service by fax or other electronic

    transmission.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Proceeding without a pilot --- persons

- **[[47]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-47 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  owner, master or person in charge of a ship commits an offence if the
  ship is subject to compulsory pilotage and proceeds through a
  compulsory pilotage area without being under the conduct of a licensed
  pilot for that compulsory pilotage area or a regular member of the
  ship's complement who is a pilotage certificate holder for that
  compulsory pilotage area.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Proceeding without a pilot --- ship

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} A ship commits an offence if it is subject to
  compulsory pilotage and proceeds through a compulsory pilotage area
  without being under the conduct of a licensed pilot for that
  compulsory pilotage area or a regular member of the ship's complement
  who is a pilotage certificate holder for that compulsory pilotage
  area.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Non-application

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} Subsections (1) and (2) do not apply if

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the master of the ship takes the conduct of it in
    accordance with subsection 38.02(1);

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the relevant Authority waives compulsory pilotage;
    or

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} the ship is in Canadian waters that are contiguous
    with waters of the United States, the ship is under the conduct of
    an individual who is authorized to have the conduct of a ship by an
    appropriate authority of the United States and the conditions set
    out in the regulations are met.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Punishment --- individuals

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} Every individual who commits an offence under

  months, or to both.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Punishment --- other persons

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} Every person, other than an individual, that commits
  an offence under subsection (1) is liable on summary conviction to a
  fine of not more than \$1,000,000.

  [(6)]{.lawlabel} Every ship that commits an offence under
  subsection (2) is liable on summary conviction to a fine of not more

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Court order

**[[48.3]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-48.3 .sectionLabel}** If a person is
convicted of an offence under this Act, in addition to any other
punishment that may be imposed under this Act, the court may, having
regard to the nature of the offence and the circumstances surrounding
its commission, make an order having any or all of the following
effects:

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} prohibiting the person from performing any act or
  engaging in any activity that may, in the opinion of the court, result
  in the continuation or repetition of the offence;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} directing the person to take any action the court
  considers appropriate to remedy or avoid any harm that results or may
  result from the commission of the offence;

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} prohibiting the person from exercising the rights or
  privileges of a licence, pilotage certificate, waiver or exemption
  order under this Act for the period or at the times and places that
  may be specified in the order; and

- [(d)]{.lawlabel} prohibiting the person from operating a ship or
  providing services essential to the operation of a ship for the period
  or at the times and places that may be specified in the order.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Service on ship

- **[[48.4]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-48.4 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} If
  a ship is charged with an offence under this Act, the summons is to be
  served

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} by delivering it to the authorized representative
    of the ship or, in the absence of an authorized representative, a
    person in charge of the ship; or

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} if service cannot reasonably be effected in the
    manner provided in paragraph (a), by posting the summons on any
    conspicuous part of the ship.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Appearance at trial

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} If a ship is charged with an offence under this Act,
  the ship may appear by counsel or representative before the court.
  Despite the [Criminal Code](/eng/acts/C-46), if the ship does not so
  appear, a court may, on proof of service of the summons, proceed to
  hold the trial.

has committed the offence to establish that the act or omission that
constitutes the offence was committed by a person in charge of the ship,
whether or not the person has been identified.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Party to offence committed by ship

- **[[48.6]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-48.6 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} If
  a ship commits an offence under this Act and a person in charge of the
  ship directed, authorized, assented to, acquiesced in or participated
  in the commission of the offence, that person is a party to and guilty
  of the offence and liable on conviction to the punishment provided for
  the offence, whether or not the ship has been prosecuted or convicted.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Party to offence committed by

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} If a corporation commits an offence under this Act,
  any director, officer or agent or mandatary of the corporation who
  directed, authorized, assented to, acquiesced in or participated in
  the commission of the offence is a party to and guilty of the offence
  and liable on conviction to the punishment provided for the offence,
  whether or not the corporation has been prosecuted or convicted.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Due diligence defence --- persons

- **[[49]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-49 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Due diligence defence --- ships

  this Act if the person that committed the act or omission that
  constitutes the offence establishes that they exercised due diligence
  to prevent its commission.

## [Regulations, Exemption Orders, Interim Orders and Directions]{.HTitleText1} {#h-394777 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Regulations

- **[[52]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-52 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  Governor in Council may make regulations respecting the provision of
  pilotage services, including regulations

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} establishing compulsory pilotage areas;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} respecting which ships or classes of ships are
    subject to compulsory pilotage;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} respecting waivers of compulsory pilotage;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} respecting master-pilot exchanges;

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} respecting the classes of licences and classes of
    pilotage certificates that are to be issued as well as the form and
    manner of their issuance;

  - [(f)]{.lawlabel} respecting the qualifications that are required of
    an applicant for a licence or pilotage certificate, or any class of
    licence or pilotage certificate, for a compulsory pilotage area,
    including the applicant's physical and mental fitness, degree of
    general and local knowledge, skill, training, experience and
    proficiency in one or both of the official languages of Canada;

  - [(g)]{.lawlabel} respecting skill and knowledge examinations,
    medical examinations, apprenticeship, the provision of documents and
    recommendations and other means of determining whether an individual
    meets the qualifications required under paragraph (f);

  - [(h)]{.lawlabel} respecting the conditions of a licence or pilotage
    certificate or the conditions of any class of licence or pilotage
    certificate;

  - [(i)]{.lawlabel} limiting the number of licences or classes of
    licences that may be issued for a compulsory pilotage area;

  - [(j)]{.lawlabel} respecting the information to be provided and the
    procedures and practices to be followed by a ship that is about to
    enter, leave or proceed within a compulsory pilotage area;

  - [(k)]{.lawlabel} respecting the minimum number of licensed pilots or
    pilotage certificate holders that are required to be on board a ship
    at any time and which class of licence or pilotage certificate that
    they are required to hold;

  - [(l)]{.lawlabel} respecting additional training and periodic medical
    examinations for licensed pilots and pilotage certificate holders;

  - [(m)]{.lawlabel} respecting risk assessments;

  - [(n)]{.lawlabel} respecting the development and implementation of
    management systems by the Authorities; and

  - [(o)]{.lawlabel} respecting fees and charges to be paid in relation
    to the administration of this Act, including the development of
    regulations, and the enforcement of this Act.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}United States pilots and waters

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} With respect to Canadian waters that are contiguous
  with waters of the United States, the regulations may set out the
  conditions under which

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} an individual who is authorized to have the conduct
    of a ship by an appropriate authority of the United States may pilot
    in Canadian waters; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} a licensed pilot or pilotage certificate holder may
    have the conduct of a ship in waters of the United States.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Paragraph (1)(l)

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} Regulations referred to in paragraph (1)(l) may
  distinguish between licensed pilots and between pilotage certificate
  holders on the basis of the class of licence or pilotage certificate
  they hold.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Exemption orders

- **[[52.1]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-52.1
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The Minister may, by order, exempt
  for a period of not more than three years any person or ship from the
  application of any provision of this Act or the regulations, subject
  to any conditions that the Minister considers appropriate, if the
  exemption would allow the undertaking of research and development,
  including in respect of any type of ships, technologies, systems,
  components or procedures and practices that may, in the Minister's
  opinion, enhance marine safety or environmental protection.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Compliance with conditions

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Every person or ship subject to a condition of the
  exemption order shall comply with that condition.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Publication

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} The Minister shall, as soon as feasible after making
  an exemption order, publish a notice of it on a Government of Canada
  website or by any other means that the Minister considers appropriate.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Interim orders

- **[[52.2]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-52.2
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The Minister may make an interim
  order that contains any provision that may be contained in a
  regulation made under this Act if the Minister believes that immediate
  action is required to deal with an urgent issue related to the
  provision of pilotage services that poses a significant risk to
  safety, human health or the environment.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Cessation of effect

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The interim order has effect from the time that it is
  made but ceases to have effect on the earliest of

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the day on which it is repealed,

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the day on which a regulation made under this Act
    that has the same effect as the interim order comes into force,

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} one year after the day on which it is made or any
    shorter period that may be specified in it, unless it is extended by
    the Governor in Council, and

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} the day that is specified in the order of the
    Governor in Council, if the Governor in Council extends the interim
    order.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Extension --- Governor in Council

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} The Governor in Council may extend the interim order
  for a period of no more than two years after the end of the applicable
  period referred to in paragraph (2)(c).

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Restriction

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} If an interim order is extended by the Governor in
  Council, the Minister is not authorized to remake the interim order
  after it ceases to have effect.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Compliance with interim order

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} Every person or ship subject to an interim order
  shall comply with it.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Contravention of unpublished order

  [(6)]{.lawlabel} No person or ship may be convicted of an offence or
  found liable for a violation consisting of a contravention of an
  interim order that, at the time of the alleged contravention, had not
  been published in the [Canada Gazette](http://www.gazette.gc.ca/)
  unless it is proved that, at the time of the alleged contravention,
  the person or ship had been notified of the interim order or
  reasonable steps had been taken to bring the purport of the interim
  order to the notice of those persons or ships likely to be affected by
  it.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Publication

  [(7)]{.lawlabel} An interim order is to be published in the [Canada
  Gazette](http://www.gazette.gc.ca/) within 23 days after it is made.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Tabling of order

  [(8)]{.lawlabel} The Minister shall ensure that a copy of each interim
  order is tabled in each House of Parliament within 15 days after it is
  made. It is to be sent to the Clerk of the House if the House is not
  sitting.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Direction to licensed pilot or pilot
corporation

- **[[52.3]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-52.3
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The Minister may, in writing, direct
  a licensed pilot or pilot corporation

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} to cease an activity that poses a risk to the
    safety of navigation, including the safety of the public and marine
    personnel, or to human health, property or the environment; or

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} to conduct an activity that protects the safety of
    navigation, including the safety of the public and marine personnel,
    or that protects human health, property or the environment.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Exceptional circumstances

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The Minister is not authorized to make the direction
  unless the Minister is satisfied that the direction is necessary to
  deal with exceptional circumstances that involve a risk to the safety
  of navigation, including the safety of the public and marine
  personnel, or to human health, property or the environment.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Validity period

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} The direction is valid for the period specified in it
  as long as that period ends no more than 30 days after the day on
  which it is made.

## RELATED PROVISIONS {#related-provisions .scheduleTitleText}

- --- 2019, c. 29, s. 259

  - ###### Non-application --- subsection 3(3.2) {#non-application-subsection-33.2 .MarginalNote}

    [259]{.lawlabel} Subsection 3(3.2) of the [Pilotage
    Act](/eng/acts/P-14), as enacted by section 227 of this Act, does
    not apply to the Chairperson or to any member of a Pilotage
    Authority on the day on which section 227 of this Act comes into
    force during the remainder of their term of appointment.

- --- 2019, c. 29, s. 260

  - ###### Tariff regulations {#tariff-regulations .MarginalNote}

    [260]{.lawlabel} Regulations made by a Pilotage Authority with the
    approval of the Governor in Council under section 33 of the
    [Pilotage Act](/eng/acts/P-14), as that section read immediately
    before the day on which section 238 of this Act comes into force,
    remain in effect until the day on which pilotage charges established
    by the Pilotage Authority in accordance with sections 33 to 35 of
    the [Pilotage Act](/eng/acts/P-14), as enacted by section 238 of
    this Act, take effect.

- --- 2019, c. 29, s. 261

  - ###### Licences and pilotage certificates {#licences-and-pilotage-certificates .MarginalNote}

    - [261]{.lawlabel} [(1)]{.lawlabel} Every licence or pilotage
      certificate issued by a Pilotage Authority under section 22 of the
      [Pilotage Act](/eng/acts/P-14), as it read immediately before the
      day on which section 242 of this Act comes into force, that was
      valid immediately before that day is deemed to have been issued,
      as applicable, by the Minister of Transport under subsection
      38.1(1) or (2) of the [Pilotage Act](/eng/acts/P-14), as enacted
      by section 242 of this Act.

    - ###### Validity period {#validity-period .MarginalNote}

      [(2)]{.lawlabel} Despite subsection 38.4(1) of the [Pilotage
      Act](/eng/acts/P-14), as enacted by section 242 of this Act, the
      validity period of a licence or pilotage certificate referred to
      in subsection (1) ends one year after the day on which section 242
      of this Act comes into force.

- --- 2019, c. 29, s. 262

  - ###### Pending applications {#pending-applications .MarginalNote}

    [262]{.lawlabel} An application for a licence or pilotage
    certificate that is pending on the day on which section 242 of this
    Act comes into force is deemed to be an application under subsection
    38.1(1) or (2) of the [Pilotage Act](/eng/acts/P-14), as enacted by
    section 242 of this Act, but the qualifications to be met by the
    applicant are the qualifications that were prescribed by the
    relevant regulations as they read immediately before the day on
    which section 242 of this Act comes into force.

- --- 2019, c. 29, s. 263

  - ###### Pending hearings {#pending-hearings .MarginalNote}

    [263]{.lawlabel} Hearings pending before a Pilotage Authority
    immediately before the day on which section 242 of this Act comes
    into force are to be continued before the Authority in accordance
    with the [Pilotage Act](/eng/acts/P-14), as it read immediately
    before that day.

- --- 2019, c. 29, s. 264

  - ###### Fines {#fines .MarginalNote}

    [264]{.lawlabel} For greater certainty, starting on the day on which
    section 252 of this Act comes into force, fines collected under the
    [Pilotage Act](/eng/acts/P-14) are no longer to be paid to the
    relevant Pilotage Authority.

- --- 2019, c. 29, s. 265

  - ###### Repeal of regulations --- section 20 [Pilotage Act](/eng/acts/P-14) {#repeal-of-regulations-section-20-pilotage-act .MarginalNote}

    - [265]{.lawlabel} [(1)]{.lawlabel} Beginning on the day on which
      section 235 of this Act comes into force, the Governor in Council
      may repeal regulations made under section 20 of the [Pilotage
      Act](/eng/acts/P-14), as that section read immediately before that
      day.

    - ###### Repeal of regulations --- section 33 [Pilotage Act](/eng/acts/P-14) {#repeal-of-regulations-section-33-pilotage-act .MarginalNote}

      [(2)]{.lawlabel} Beginning on the day on which section 238 of this
      Act comes into force, the Governor in Council may repeal
      regulations made under section 33 of the [Pilotage
      Act](/eng/acts/P-14), as that section read immediately before that
      day.

- --- 2022, c. 17, s. 76

  - ###### Clarification --- immediate application {#clarification-immediate-application .MarginalNote}

    [76]{.lawlabel} For greater certainty, but subject to sections 77
    and 78, the amendments made by this Act also apply with respect to
    proceedings that are ongoing on the day on which this Act comes into
    force.

- --- 2022, c. 17, par. 77(2)(c)

  - ###### Certain applications for warrants {#certain-applications-for-warrants .MarginalNote}

    - [77]{.lawlabel} [(2)]{.lawlabel} Each of the following provisions,
      as it read immediately before the day on which this Act comes into
      force, continues to apply with respect to an application made for
      a warrant under the provision if the application is submitted, and
      no decision has been made in respect of the application, before
      that day:

      - [(c)]{.lawlabel} subsection 46.13(4) of the [Pilotage
        Act](/eng/acts/P-14);

- --- 2022, c. 17, s. 78.1

  - ###### Impact of remote proceedings {#impact-of-remote-proceedings .MarginalNote}

    - [78.1]{.lawlabel} [(1)]{.lawlabel} The Minister of Justice must,
      no later than three years after the day on which this Act receives
      royal assent, initiate one or more independent reviews on the use
      of remote proceedings in criminal justice matters that must
      include an assessment of whether remote proceedings

      - [(a)]{.lawlabel} enhance, preserve or adversely affect access to
        justice;

      - [(b)]{.lawlabel} maintain fundamental principles of the
        administration of justice; and

      - [(c)]{.lawlabel} adequately address the rights and obligations
        of participants in the criminal justice system, including
        accused persons.

    - ###### Report {#report .MarginalNote}

      [(2)]{.lawlabel} The Minister of Justice must, no later than five
      years after the day on which a review is initiated, cause a report
      on the review --- including any findings or recommendations
      resulting from it --- to be laid before each House of Parliament.

- --- 2022, c. 17, s. 78.2

  - ###### Review by committee {#review-by-committee .MarginalNote}

    - [78.2]{.lawlabel} [(1)]{.lawlabel} At the start of the fifth year
      after the day on which this Act receives royal assent, the
      provisions enacted or amended by this Act are to be referred to a
      committee of the Senate and a committee of the House of Commons
      that may be designated or established for the purpose of reviewing
      the provisions.

    - ###### Report {#report-1 .MarginalNote}

      [(2)]{.lawlabel} The committees to which the provisions are
      referred are to review them and the use of remote proceedings in
      criminal justice matters and submit reports to the Houses of
      Parliament of which they are committees, including statements
      setting out any changes to the provisions that they recommend.
