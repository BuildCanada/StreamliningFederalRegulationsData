## [General]{.HTitleText1} {#h-1250743 .Part}

### [Interpretation]{.HTitleText2} {#h-1250744 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Definitions

- **[[1]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-1 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  following definitions apply in these Regulations.

  [[Act]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[Act]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means Part II of the [Canada Labour
      Code](/eng/acts/L-2). ([Loi]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

  [[designated recipient]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[designated recipient]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means a work unit in
      a work place or person that is designated by an employer under
      section 14. ([destinataire désigné]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

  [[occurrence]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[occurrence]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means an occurrence of
      harassment and violence in the work
      place. ([incident]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

  [[principal party]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[principal party]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means an employee or
      employer who is the object of an occurrence. ([partie
      principale]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

  [[responding party]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[responding party]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means the person who is
      alleged to have been responsible for the occurrence in notice of
      an occurrence provided under subsection 15(1). ([partie
      intimée]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

  [[witness]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[witness]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means a person who witnessed an
      occurrence or is informed of an occurrence by the principal party
      or responding party. ([témoin]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Applicable Partner

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} In these Regulations, a reference to the "applicable
  partner" is to be read as a reference to the policy committee or, if
  there is no policy committee, as a reference to the work place
  committee or the health and safety representative.

### [Joint Matters]{.HTitleText2} {#h-1250756 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}When unable to agree

**[[2]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-2 .sectionLabel}** If an employer and the
policy committee, the work place committee or the health and safety
representative are unable to agree on any matter that is required by
these Regulations to be done jointly by them, the employer's decision
prevails.

### [Former Employees]{.HTitleText2} {#h-1250759 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Prescribed circumstances --- extension

**[[3]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-3 .sectionLabel}** The Head of Compliance and
Enforcement may extend the time period referred to in subsection 125(4)
of the Act if a former employee demonstrates in an application to the
Head of Compliance and Enforcement that they were unable to make the
occurrence known to the employer within the time period because they
incurred trauma as a result of the occurrence or because of a health
condition.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Time period to make complaint

**[[4]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-4 .sectionLabel}** For the purpose of
subsection 127.1(12) of the Act, a former employee may make a complaint
under subsection 127.1(1) of the Act until the day that is the later of

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} three months after the day on which the former

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} if notice of the occurrence was provided under
  subsection 15(1), three months after the day on which the resolution
  process is completed in respect of the occurrence.

## [Prevention and Protection Measures]{.HTitleText1} {#h-1250766 .Part}

### [Work Place Assessment]{.HTitleText2} {#h-1250767 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Joint assessment

- **[[5]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-5 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} An
  employer and the applicable partner must jointly carry out a work
  place assessment that consists of the identification of risk factors
  under section 8 and the development and implementation of preventive
  measures under section 9.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Joint monitoring and updates

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} An employer and the applicable partner must jointly
  monitor the accuracy of the work place assessment and, if necessary,
  update it in order to reflect a change to the information set out in
  the assessment, including

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} a change to the risk factors identified under
    section 8; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} a change that compromises the effectiveness of a
    preventive measure developed and implemented under section 9.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Review after three years

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} An employer and the applicable partner must jointly
  review the work place assessment every three years and, if necessary,
  update it.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Joint review and update

- **[[6]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-6 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} An
  employer and the work place committee or the health and safety
  representative must jointly review and, if necessary, update the work
  place assessment if notice of an occurrence is provided under
  subsection 15(1) and

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the occurrence is not resolved under section 23 and
    the principal party ends the resolution process under section 18; or

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the responding party is not an employee or the
    employer.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Review to consider circumstances

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} A review conducted under subsection (1) must take
  into account the circumstances of the occurrence.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Multiple occurrences

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} If a review and update are being conducted under
  subsection (1) and notice is provided under subsection 15(1) of
  another occurrence that involves substantially the same matters and
  for which a review and update are also required under subsection (1),
  those occurrences may be addressed together in the same review and
  update.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Qualifications

**[[7]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-7 .sectionLabel}** An employer must ensure
that each individual who is directed by the employer to identify the
risk factors referred to in section 8, or to develop and implement the
preventive measures referred to in section 9, is qualified to do so by
virtue of their training, education or experience.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Identification of risk factors

**[[8]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-8 .sectionLabel}** An employer and the
applicable partner must jointly identify the risk factors, internal and
external to the work place, that contribute to harassment and violence
in the work place, taking into account

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} the culture, conditions, activities and
  organizational structure of the work place;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} circumstances external to the work place, such as
  family violence, that could give rise to harassment and violence in
  the work place;

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} any reports, records and data that are related to
  harassment and violence in the work place;

- [(d)]{.lawlabel} the physical design of the work place; and

- [(e)]{.lawlabel} the measures that are in place to protect
  psychological health and safety in the work place.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Preventive measures --- development and
implementation

**[[9]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-9 .sectionLabel}** Within six months after the
risk factors are identified under section 8, an employer and the
applicable partner must jointly

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} develop preventive measures that, to the extent
  feasible,

  - [(i)]{.lawlabel} mitigate the risk of harassment and violence in the
    work place, and

  - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} neither create nor increase the risk of harassment
    and violence in the work place;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} develop an implementation plan for the preventive
  measures; and

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} implement the preventive measures in accordance with
  the implementation plan.

### [Work Place Harassment and Violence Prevention Policy]{.HTitleText2} {#h-1250802 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Joint development

- **[[10]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-10 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} An
  employer and the applicable partner must jointly develop a work place
  harassment and violence prevention policy.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Policy content

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The policy must contain the following elements:

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the employer's mission statement regarding the
    prevention of and protection against harassment and violence in the
    work place;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} a description of the respective roles of the
    employer, designated recipient, employees, policy committee, work
    place committee and health and safety representative in relation to
    harassment and violence in the work place;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} a description of the risk factors, internal and
    external to the work place, that contribute to work place harassment
    and violence;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} a summary of the training that will be provided
    regarding work place harassment and violence;

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} a summary of the resolution process, including

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} the name or identity of the designated recipient,
      and

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} the manner in which a principal party or witness
      may provide the employer or the designated recipient with notice
      of an occurrence;

  - [(f)]{.lawlabel} the reasons for which a review and update of the
    work place assessment must be conducted under subsection 6(1);

  - [(g)]{.lawlabel} a summary of the emergency procedures that must be
    implemented when an occurrence poses an immediate danger to the
    health and safety of an employee or when there is a threat of such
    an occurrence;

  - [(h)]{.lawlabel} a description of the manner in which the employer
    will protect the privacy of persons who are involved in an
    occurrence or in the resolution process for an occurrence under
    these Regulations;

  - [(i)]{.lawlabel} a description of any recourse, in addition to any
    under the Act or these Regulations, that may be available to persons
    who are involved in an occurrence;

  - [(j)]{.lawlabel} a description of the support measures that are
    available to employees; and

  - [(k)]{.lawlabel} the name of the person who is designated to receive
    a complaint made under subsection 127.1(1) of the Act.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Policy to be made available

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} An employer must make the policy available to all
  employees.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Joint review and update

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} An employer and the applicable partner must jointly
  review and, if necessary, update the policy at least once every three
  years and following any change to an element of the policy.

## [Emergency Procedures]{.HTitleText1} {#h-1250825 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Joint development and implementation

- **[[11]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-11 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} An
  employer and the applicable partner must jointly develop emergency
  procedures that are to be implemented if

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} an occurrence poses an immediate danger to the
    health and safety of an employee; or

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} there is a threat of an occurrence referred to in
    paragraph (a).

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Procedures available

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} An employer must make the emergency procedures
  available to all employees.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Joint review and update

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} After every implementation of the emergency
  procedures under subsection (1), an employer and the applicable
  partner must jointly review and, if necessary, update the procedures.

## [Training]{.HTitleText1} {#h-1250835 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Joint development or identification

- **[[12]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-12 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} An
  employer and the applicable partner must jointly develop or identify
  the training on work place harassment and violence that is to be
  provided to employees, the employer and the designated recipient.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Required training elements

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The training must be specific to the culture,
  conditions and activities of the work place and include the following
  elements:

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the elements of the work place harassment and
    violence prevention policy;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} a description of the relationship between work
    place harassment and violence and the prohibited grounds of
    discrimination set out in subsection 3(1) of the [Canadian Human
    Rights Act](/eng/acts/H-6); and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} a description of how to recognize, minimize,
    prevent and respond to work place harassment and violence.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Joint review and update

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} An employer and the applicable partner must jointly
  review and, if necessary, update the training at least once every
  three years and following any change to an element of the training.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Employee

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} An employer must ensure that an employee is provided
  with the training

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} within three months after the day on which their
    employment begins or, in the case of an employee whose employment
    began before the day on which these Regulations come into force,
    within one year after the day on which these Regulations come into
    force;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} at least once every three years after that; and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} following any update to the training under
    subsection (3) or their assignment to a new activity or role for
    which there is an increased or specific risk of work place
    harassment and violence.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Designated recipient

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} An employer must ensure that the designated recipient
  is provided with the training before assuming their duties under these
  Regulations and at least once every three years after that.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Employer

  [(6)]{.lawlabel} An employer must undergo the training within one year
  after the day on which these Regulations come into force and at least
  once every three years after that.

## [Support Measures]{.HTitleText1} {#h-1250855 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Information available to employees

**[[13]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-13 .sectionLabel}** An employer must make
available to employees information respecting the medical, psychological
or other support services that are available within their geographical
area.

## [Resolution Process]{.HTitleText1} {#h-1250858 .Part}

### [Notice of an Occurrence]{.HTitleText2} {#h-1250859 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Designated recipient

**[[14]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-14 .sectionLabel}** An employer must
designate a person or work unit as the designated recipient to whom
notice of an occurrence may be provided.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Providing notice

- **[[15]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-15
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Subject to subsections (2) and (3),
  a principal party or witness may, in writing or orally, provide an
  employer or the designated recipient with notice of an occurrence.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Exception

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Notice must not be provided in respect of an
  occurrence if

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the responding party is neither the employer nor an
    employee;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} exposure to harassment and violence is a normal
    condition of the principal party's work; and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} the employer has measures in place to address that
    work place harassment and violence.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Employer is a party

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} If the principal party or the responding party is the
  employer, the notice must be provided to the designated recipient.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Anonymous notice

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} A witness may provide notice of an occurrence
  anonymously.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Content of notice

**[[16]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-16 .sectionLabel}** Notice of an occurrence
must contain the following information:

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} the name of the principal party and the responding
  party, if known;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} the date of the occurrence; and

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} a detailed description of the occurrence.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Employer or designated recipient

**[[17]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-17 .sectionLabel}** For the purposes of
sections 18 to 23, 26, 27, 29 and 34, a reference to "employer or
designated recipient" is a reference to the one to whom notice of the
occurrence was provided under subsection 15(1).

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Principal party's choices

**[[18]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-18 .sectionLabel}** The principal party may
end the resolution process at any time by informing an employer or
designated recipient that they choose not to continue with the process.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Initial review

- **[[19]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-19 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} An
  employer or designated recipient must conduct an initial review of
  every notice of an occurrence.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Occurrence deemed to be resolved

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Following the initial review, the occurrence is
  deemed to be resolved if the notice does not contain the name of the
  principal party or otherwise allow their identity to be determined.

#### [Response to Notice of an Occurrence]{.HTitleText3} {#h-1250888 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Contact with principal party

**[[20]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-20 .sectionLabel}** An employer or designated
recipient must, within seven days after the day on which notice of an
occurrence is provided, contact the principal party to inform them

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} that their notice has been received or that they have
  been named or identified as the principal party in notice provided by
  a witness, as the case may be;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} of the manner in which the work place harassment and
  violence prevention policy is accessed;

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} of each step of the resolution process; and

- [(d)]{.lawlabel} that they may be represented during the resolution
  process.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Contact with witness

**[[21]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-21 .sectionLabel}** If notice of an
occurrence is provided by a witness who is not anonymous, an employer or
designated recipient must, within seven days after the day on which the
notice is provided, contact the witness to confirm that notice was
received.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Contact with responding party

**[[22]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-22 .sectionLabel}** On the first occasion
that an employer or designated recipient contacts the responding party
regarding the occurrence, they must inform them

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} that they have been named or identified as the
  responding party in the notice of an occurrence;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} of the manner in which the work place harassment and
  violence prevention policy is accessed;

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} of each step of the resolution process; and

- [(d)]{.lawlabel} that they may be represented during the resolution
  process.

#### [Negotiated Resolution]{.HTitleText3} {#h-1250903 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Reasonable effort

- **[[23]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-23 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} An
  employer or designated recipient, the principal party and, if
  contacted under section 22, the responding party, must make every
  reasonable effort to resolve an occurrence for which notice is
  provided under subsection 15(1) and those efforts must begin no later
  than 45 days after the day on which that notice is provided. However,
  if the occurrence is also investigated, it cannot be resolved under
  this section after the investigator has provided their report under
  subsection 30(1).

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Review required

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} For the purposes of subsection (1), the reasonable
  effort includes a review by the principal party and the employer or
  designated recipient to determine whether the notice of occurrence
  provided under subsection 15(1) describes an action, conduct or
  comment that constitutes [[harassment and
  violence]{.dfn}]{.DefinitionRef} as defined in subsection 122(1) of
  the Act.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Joint determination --- harassment and
  violence

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} For the purpose of subsection (1), resolution of the
  occurrence includes, but is not limited to, a joint determination by
  the principal party and the employer or designated recipient that the
  notice of occurrence provided under subsection 15(1) does not describe
  an action, conduct or comment that constitutes [[harassment and
  violence]{.dfn}]{.DefinitionRef} as defined in subsection 122(1) of
  the Act.

#### [Conciliation]{.HTitleText3} {#h-1250911 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Conditions

**[[24]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-24 .sectionLabel}** The principal party and
the responding party may attempt to resolve an occurrence for which
notice is provided under subsection 15(1) by conciliation if they agree
to conciliation and on a person to facilitate it. However, if the
occurrence is also investigated, it cannot be resolved by conciliation
after the investigator has provided their report under subsection 30(1).

#### [Investigation]{.HTitleText3} {#h-1250914 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}When investigation is required

- **[[25]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-25
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Subject to subsection (2), if an
  occurrence is not resolved under section 23 or 24, an investigation of
  the occurrence must be carried out if the principal party requests it.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Investigation discontinued

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} If the occurrence being investigated is resolved
  under section 23 or 24 before the investigator has provided their
  report under subsection 30(1), the investigation must be discontinued.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Notice of investigation

**[[26]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-26 .sectionLabel}** An employer or the
designated recipient must provide the principal party and the responding
party with notice that an investigation is to be carried out.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Selection of investigator

- **[[27]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-27
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Subject to subsection (2), an
  employer or designated recipient must select one of the following
  persons to act as the investigator:

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} in the case where the employer and the applicable
    partner have jointly developed or identified a list of persons who
    may act as an investigator, a person from that list; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} in any other case,

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} a person that is agreed to by the employer or
      designated recipient, the principal party and the responding
      party, or

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} if there is no agreement within 60 days after
      the day on which the notice is provided under section 26, a person
      from among those whom the Canadian Centre for Occupational Health
      and Safety identifies as having the knowledge, training and
      experience referred to in subsection 28(1).

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Limit

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} An employer or designated recipient may select a
  person to act as the investigator only if the person

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} possesses the knowledge, training and experience
    referred to in subsection 28(1); and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} provides the employer or designated recipient,
    principal party and responding party with a written statement
    indicating that the person is not in a conflict of interest in
    respect of the occurrence.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Investigator's qualifications

- **[[28]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-28 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} For
  the purposes of these Regulations, an investigator must

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} be trained in investigative techniques;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} have knowledge, training and experience that are
    relevant to harassment and violence in the work place; and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} have knowledge of the Act, the [Canadian Human
    Rights Act](/eng/acts/H-6) and any other legislation that is
    relevant to harassment and violence in the work place.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Statement of qualifications

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} A person or party referred to in subparagraph
  27(1)(b)(i) who proposes that a person act as the investigator must
  provide the other persons and parties referred to in that subparagraph
  with the following information about the proposed investigator:

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} their name;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} if they are an employee of the employer, their job
    title and the name of their immediate supervisor;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} a description of their knowledge, training and
    experience demonstrating that they meet the requirements of
    subsection (1); and

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} a description of any experience that they have
    which is relevant to the nature of the occurrence that is to be
    investigated.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Information for investigator

**[[29]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-29 .sectionLabel}** An employer or the
designated recipient must provide the investigator with all information
that is relevant to the investigation.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Investigator's report

- **[[30]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-30 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} An
  investigator's report regarding an occurrence must set out the
  following information:

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} a general description of the occurrence;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} their conclusions, including those related to the
    circumstances in the work place that contributed to the occurrence;
    and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} their recommendations to eliminate or minimize the
    risk of a similar occurrence.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Identity of persons

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} An investigator's report must not reveal, directly or
  indirectly, the identity of persons who are involved in an occurrence
  or the resolution process for an occurrence under these Regulations.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Copies of report

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} An employer must provide a copy of the investigator's
  report to the principal party, responding party, the work place
  committee or health and safety representative and, if they were
  provided with notice under subsection 15(1), the designated recipient.

#### [Implementation of Recommendations]{.HTitleText3} {#h-1250957 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Joint determination

- **[[31]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-31 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} An
  employer and the work place committee or the health and safety
  representative must jointly determine which of the recommendations set
  out in the report are to be implemented.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Implementation

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The employer must implement all recommendations that
  are determined under subsection (1).

#### [Completion of Resolution Process]{.HTitleText3} {#h-1250963 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Completion of process

**[[32]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-32 .sectionLabel}** The resolution process
for an occurrence is completed when

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} if a work place assessment is required under
  subsection 6(1), the review and, if necessary, update of the
  assessment are carried out;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} the occurrence is resolved under subsection 19(2) or
  under section 23 or 24;

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} if an investigator has provided a report in
  accordance with subsection 30(1), the employer implements the
  recommendations referred to in subsection 31(2).

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Time limit

- **[[33]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-33
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Subject to subsection (2), an
  employer must ensure that the resolution process is completed within
  one year after the day on which notice of the occurrence is provided
  under subsection 15(1).

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Temporary absence

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} If the principal party or responding party is
  temporarily absent from work for more than 90 consecutive days after
  the day on which notice of the occurrence is provided under subsection
  15(1), the employer must ensure that the resolution process is
  completed within the later of

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} one year after the day on which notice of the
    occurrence is provided under subsection 15(1), and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} six months after the day on which the party returns
    to work.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Monthly status updates

**[[34]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-34 .sectionLabel}** For every occurrence for
which notice is provided under subsection 15(1), an employer or
designated recipient must provide monthly updates regarding the status
of the resolution process to

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} the principal party, beginning on the first month
  after the month in which the notice is provided and ending on the
  month in which the resolution process is completed; and

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} the responding party, beginning on the first month
  after the month in which the responding party is first contacted by
  the employer or designated recipient concerning the occurrence and
  ending on the month in which the resolution process is completed.

## [Records and Reports]{.HTitleText1} {#h-1250980 .Part}

### [Health and Safety Records]{.HTitleText2} {#h-1250981 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Records to be kept

- **[[35]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-35 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} An
  employer must keep the following health and safety records:

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the work place harassment and violence prevention
    policy;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} a copy of the documents that form part of the work
    place assessment;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} a copy of the documents that form part of each
    review and update of the work place assessment;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} for each instance where the employer and the policy
    committee, the work place committee or the health and safety
    representative are unable to agree on a matter that is required by
    these Regulations to be jointly done by them, a record of the
    employer's decision in that matter and the reasons for that
    decision;

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} a record of each notice provided under subsection
    15(1) and of each action taken in response to the notice;

  - [(f)]{.lawlabel} for each instance where a time limit set out in
    section 33 is not met, a document that sets out the reason for the
    delay;

  - [(g)]{.lawlabel} a copy of each report that is prepared by an
    investigator under subsection 30(1);

  - [(h)]{.lawlabel} a copy of each annual report; and

  - [(i)]{.lawlabel} a copy of each fatality report provided under
    subsection 37(1).

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Time period

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} An employer must keep the records referred to in
  paragraphs (1)(c) to (i) for a period of 10 years.

### [Annual Report to Head of Compliance and Enforcement]{.HTitleText2} {#h-1250996 .Subheading}

::: HistoricalNote
\[

- SOR/2021-118, s. 14

\]
:::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Content

**[[36]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-36 .sectionLabel}** On or before March 1 of
each year, an employer must provide the Head of Compliance and
Enforcement with an annual report that sets out

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} their name or business name;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} their [[business number]{.dfn}]{.DefinitionRef}, as
  defined in subsection 248(1) of the [Income Tax Act](/eng/acts/I-3.3);

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} the name of a person who can be contacted in respect
  of the report; and

- [(d)]{.lawlabel} the following information respecting the occurrences
  for which notice was provided under subsection 15(1) in the preceding
  calendar year:

  - [(i)]{.lawlabel} the total number of occurrences,

  - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} the number of occurrences that were related,
    respectively, to sexual harassment and violence and non-sexual
    harassment and violence,

  - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} the number of occurrences that resulted in the
    death of an employee,

  - [(iv)]{.lawlabel} if known, the number of occurrences that fell
    under each prohibited ground of discrimination set out in subsection
    3(1) of the [Canadian Human Rights Act](/eng/acts/H-6),

  - [(v)]{.lawlabel} the locations where the occurrences took place,
    specifying the total number of occurrences that took place in each
    location,

  - [(vi)]{.lawlabel} the types of professional relationships that
    existed between the principal and responding parties, specifying the
    total number for each type,

  - [(vii)]{.lawlabel} the means set out in section 32 by which
    resolution processes were completed and, for each of those means,
    the number of occurrences involved, and

  - [(viii)]{.lawlabel} the average time, expressed in months, that it
    took to complete the resolution process for an occurrence.

### [Fatality Report]{.HTitleText2} {#h-1251011 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Time period

- **[[37]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-37 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} If an
  occurrence results in the death of an employee, an employer must
  report the occurrence to the Head of Compliance and Enforcement within
  24 hours after becoming aware of the employee's death.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Content

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The report must set out the following information:

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the employer's name or business name;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the employer's [[business
    number]{.dfn}]{.DefinitionRef}, as defined in subsection 248(1) of
    the [Income Tax Act](/eng/acts/I-3.3);

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} a general description of the occurrence;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} the date and time of the occurrence; and

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} the name of a person who can be contacted in
    respect of the report.
