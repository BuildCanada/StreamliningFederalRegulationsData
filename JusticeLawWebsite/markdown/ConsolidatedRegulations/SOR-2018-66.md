## [Purpose and Overview]{.HTitleText1} {#h-858524 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Protection of environment and reduction
of harmful effects

**[[1]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-1 .sectionLabel}** For the purpose of
protecting the environment on which life depends and of reducing the
immediate or long-term harmful effects of the emission of methane and
certain volatile organic compounds on the environment or its biological
diversity, these Regulations

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} impose certain requirements on the oil and gas sector
  in order to reduce emissions of methane and certain volatile organic
  compounds; and

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} designate the contravention of certain of its
  provisions as serious offences by adding them to the schedule to the
  [Regulations Designating Regulatory Provisions for Purposes of
  Enforcement (Canadian Environmental Protection Act,
  1999)](/eng/regulations/SOR-2012-134).

## [Responsibility]{.HTitleText1} {#h-858588 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Operator

**[[3]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-3 .sectionLabel}** An operator for an upstream
oil and gas facility must ensure that a requirement set out in these
Regulations in respect of the facility or equipment at the facility ---
along with any related requirement in respect of recording information,
keeping documents and providing reports --- is complied with.

## [PART 1]{.HLabel1}[Onshore Upstream Oil and Gas Facilities]{.HTitleText1} {#h-858591 .Part}

### [Application]{.HTitleText2} {#h-858592 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Onshore facilities

**[[4]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-4 .sectionLabel}** This Part applies in
respect of upstream oil and gas facilities other than those located
offshore.

### [General Requirements]{.HTitleText2} {#h-858595 .Subheading}

#### [Hydrocarbon Gas Conservation and Destruction Equipment]{.HTitleText3} {#h-858596 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Hydrocarbon gas conservation equipment

- **[[5]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-5
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Hydrocarbon gas conservation
  equipment that is used at an upstream oil and gas facility must

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} be operated in such a manner that at least 95% of
    the hydrocarbon gas that is routed to the equipment --- based on a
    calculation of the volumetric flow rates at standard conditions ---
    is captured and conserved;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} be operating continuously, other than during
    periods when it is undergoing normal servicing or timely repairs;
    and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} be operated and maintained in accordance with the
    applicable recommendations of its manufacturer.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Exception to paragraph (1)(c)

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Despite paragraph (1)(c), no recommendation referred
  to in that paragraph need be treated as a requirement and complied
  with if the operator for a facility has a record that establishes that
  without that compliance the hydrocarbon gas conservation equipment's
  ability to respect paragraph (1)(a) is unaffected.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Records --- conservation equipment

**[[6]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-6 .sectionLabel}** A record in respect of any
hydrocarbon gas conservation equipment used at an upstream oil and gas
facility must be made that indicates

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} for each month during which the equipment is used,
  the percentage, at any given moment, of the hydrocarbon gas routed to
  the equipment that is captured and conserved, along with a calculation
  of the volumetric flow rates on which that percentage is based, with
  supporting documents; and

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} how the equipment was operated and maintained, along
  with an indication of any recommendations of its manufacturer for its
  operation and maintenance, with supporting documents.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Conserved gas --- use

**[[7]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-7 .sectionLabel}** Hydrocarbon gas that has
been captured and conserved in hydrocarbon gas conservation equipment
must be conserved until it is

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} used at the facility as fuel in a combustion device
  that releases at most 5% of the combusted hydrocarbon gas to the
  atmosphere as hydrocarbon gas;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} delivered; or

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} injected into an underground geological deposit for a
  purpose other than to dispose of the gas as waste.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Records --- conserved gas used as fuel

**[[8]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-8 .sectionLabel}** A record in respect of any
hydrocarbon gas that is combusted as fuel in a combustion device
referred to in paragraph 7(a) must be made that indicates for each month
during which the device is used, the percentage, at any given moment, of
the combusted hydrocarbon gas that is released as hydrocarbon gas, with
supporting documents, based on

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} tests conducted when the device operates under
  conditions recommended by the manufacturer for determining this
  percentage; or

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} measurements taken when the device operates under
  those conditions.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Hydrocarbon gas destruction equipment

**[[9]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-9 .sectionLabel}** Hydrocarbon gas destruction
equipment that is used at an upstream oil and gas facility must satisfy
the requirements related to the destruction of hydrocarbon gas set out
in

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} Sections 3.6 and 7 of Version 4.5 of the guideline
  entitled Flaring and Venting Reduction Guideline, published by the Oil
  and Gas Commission of British Columbia in June 2016, if the facility
  is located in British Columbia;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} section 3 of the directive entitled Directive S-20:
  Saskatchewan Upstream Flaring and Incineration Requirements, published
  by the Government of Saskatchewan on November 1, 2015, if the facility
  is located in Manitoba or Saskatchewan; and

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} sections 3.6 and 7 of the directive entitled
  Directive 060: Upstream Petroleum Industry Flaring, Incinerating, and
  Venting, published by the Alberta Energy Regulator on March 22, 2016,
  in any other case.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Records --- hydrocarbon gas destruction
equipment

**[[10]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-10 .sectionLabel}** A record in respect of
any hydrocarbon gas destruction equipment used at an upstream oil and
gas facility must be made that demonstrates, with supporting documents,
that the requirements related to the destruction of hydrocarbon gas set
out in the applicable document referred to in section 9 are satisfied.

#### [Well Completion involving Hydraulic Fracturing]{.HTitleText3} {#h-858625 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Application

- **[[11]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-11 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} This
  section applies in respect of an upstream oil and gas facility that
  includes a well that undergoes hydraulic fracturing and whose
  production has a gas-to-oil ratio of at least 53:1, based on the most
  recent determination of the gas-to-oil ratio prior to the hydraulic
  fracturing.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}No venting

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Hydrocarbon gas associated with flowback at a well at
  an upstream oil and gas facility must not be vented during flowback
  but must instead be captured and routed to hydrocarbon gas
  conservation equipment or hydrocarbon gas destruction equipment.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Exception

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} Subsection (2) does not apply if all the gas
  associated with flowback at the well does not have sufficient heating
  value to sustain combustion.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Records --- hydraulic fracturing

**[[12]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-12 .sectionLabel}** A record in respect of
each well at an upstream oil and gas facility that undergoes hydraulic
fracturing must be made

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} that indicates the gas-to-oil ratio, based on the
  most recent determination of the gas-to-oil ratio prior to the
  hydraulic fracturing;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} if that gas-to-oil ratio is at least 53:1, that
  demonstrates, with supporting documents, that the hydrocarbon gas
  associated with flowback was captured and routed to hydrocarbon gas
  conservation equipment or hydrocarbon gas destruction equipment; and

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} if hydrocarbon gas associated with flowback at the
  well is vented, the heating value of that gas.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Non-application --- British Columbia and
Alberta

**[[13]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-13 .sectionLabel}** Sections 11 and 12 do not
apply in respect of an upstream oil and gas facility that is located in

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} British Columbia, if the facility is subject to the
  requirements with respect to well completion involving hydraulic
  fracturing that are set out in the guideline entitled Flaring and
  Venting Reduction Guideline, published by the Oil and Gas Commission
  of British Columbia in June 2016; and

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} Alberta, if the facility is subject to the
  requirements with respect to well completion involving hydraulic
  fracturing that are set out in the directive entitled Directive 060:
  Upstream Petroleum Industry Flaring, Incinerating, and Venting,
  published by the Alberta Energy Regulator on March 22, 2016.

#### [Compressors]{.HTitleText3} {#h-858642 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Capture or venting of emissions

**[[14]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-14 .sectionLabel}** The emissions of
hydrocarbon gas from the seals of a centrifugal compressor, or from the
rod packings and distance pieces of a reciprocating compressor, that has
a rated brake power of 75 kW or more at an upstream oil and gas facility
must be

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} captured and routed to hydrocarbon gas conservation
  equipment or hydrocarbon gas destruction equipment; or

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} routed to vents that release those emissions to the
  atmosphere.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Measurement of flow rate

**[[15]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-15 .sectionLabel}** The flow rate of
emissions of hydrocarbon gas released from the vents referred to in
paragraph 14(b) of a compressor must be measured by means of

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} a flow meter, other than a calibrated bag, in
  accordance with section 16; or

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} a continuous monitoring device in accordance with
  section 17.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Flow meters

- **[[16]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-16 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  flow meter must be calibrated in accordance with the manufacturer's
  recommendations such that its measurements have a maximum margin of
  error of ±10%.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Measurements by flow meters

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Those measurements must be made

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} the 365th day after the day on which the

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} subsequently, the period that ends on the 365th day

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the average of the flow rates measured, if the

#### [Determination of Volume of Gas]{.HTitleText3} {#h-858757 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Applicable methods

- **[[23]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-23 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} For
  the purpose of sections 20 and 26, the volume of hydrocarbon gas
  produced, received, vented or destroyed at, or delivered from, an
  upstream oil and gas facility must be determined in accordance with
  the applicable method set out in

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the document entitled Measurement Guideline for
    Upstream Oil and Gas Operations, published by the Oil and Gas
    Commission of British Columbia on March 1, 2017, if the facility is
    located in British Columbia;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the document entitled Measurement Requirements for
    Oil and Gas Operations and commonly referred to as Directive PNG017,
    published by the Government of Saskatchewan on August 1, 2017
    (version 2.1), if the facility is located in Manitoba or
    Saskatchewan; and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} the document entitled Measurement Requirements for
    Oil and Gas Operations and commonly referred to as AER Directive
    017, published by the Alberta Energy Regulator on March 31, 2016, in
    any other case.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Directive PNG017 and AER 017

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Despite paragraphs (1)(b) and (c), for the purpose of
  sections 12.2.2.1 and 12.2.2.2 of the Saskatchewan Directive PNG017
  and of the AER Directive 017, the gas production per well per day is
  to be determined

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} if the expected gas production is greater than 2
    000 standard m^3^ per day, by direct measurement; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} in any other case,

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} by direct measurement, or

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} by means of an estimate based on a gas-to-oil
      ratio determined

      - [(A)]{.lawlabel} in accordance with section 24, or

      - [(B)]{.lawlabel} by the formula

        ::: {#858772 .Clause}
        −0.5P~w~ + 150

        where

        [P~w~]{.dfn}
        :   is the average volume, expressed in standard m^3^, of oil
            produced by the well for a day during the most recent month
            of production.
        :::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Determination of gas-to-oil ratio

- **[[24]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-24 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  determination of a gas-to-oil ratio for the purpose of clause
  23(2)(b)(ii)(A) is made using the formula

  ::: {#858778 .Subsection}
  G/O

  where

  [G]{.dfn}
  :   is the average volume of gas produced by the well measured over a
      continuous period --- of at least 72 hours or at least 24 hours,
      determined, as the case may be, in accordance with subsection (2)
      or (3) --- under conditions, in particular in respect of flow rate
      and operating conditions, that are representative of the
      conditions that occurred during the most recent month of
      production; and

  [O]{.dfn}
  :   is the average volume of oil produced by the well over the period
      that is used for the determination of G, based on measurements
      taken in accordance with subsection (4) as prorated to that period
      and under conditions, in particular in respect of flow rate and
      operating conditions, that are representative of the conditions
      during the most recent month of production.
  :::

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Determination of value of G

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The measurements to determine the value of G must be
  taken over a continuous period of at least 72 hours with a continuous
  measuring device or using a flow meter with at least one reading taken
  every 20 minutes.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Exception

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} Despite subsection (2), the measurements to determine
  the value of G may be taken over a continuous period of at least 24
  hours, if

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the flow rate of gas from the well is greater than
    100 standard m^3^ per day; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the measurement is taken

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} with a continuous measuring device and the
      variation of flow rate in that continuous period is such that the
      average flow rate for any 20-minute period is within ±5% of the
      average flow rate, or

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} using a flow meter with at least one reading
      taken every 20 minutes within that continuous period and the
      variation of flow rate in that continuous period is such that 95%
      of the readings taken are within ±5% of the average flow rate.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Determination of the value of O

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} The measurements to determine the value of O must be
  taken after the water has been separated from the liquid produced from
  the well and taken

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} over the continuous period used to determine the
    value of G with a continuous measuring device that has a maximum
    margin of error of ±0.1 standard m^3^; or

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} over a continuous period of at least 10 days that
    includes the continuous period used to measure G with a continuous
    measuring device that has a maximum margin of error of ±1 standard
    m^3^ and with the variation of flow rate in that continuous period
    such that the measured volume of oil produced for any day is within
    ±5% of the measured volume of oil produced for any other day in that
    continuous period.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Steady state

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} A measurement taken under any of subsections (2)
  to (4) must be taken while the well is operating in a steady state,
  that is, it must be taken only if no adjustment that could result in a
  change to the oil or gas production rates has been made to the
  production parameters for at least 48 hours before the measurement is
  taken.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Measuring equipment --- directives

  [(6)]{.lawlabel} The continuous measuring device or flow meter used to

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Frequency of determination

  [(7)]{.lawlabel} A determination of the gas-to-oil ratio must be made

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} at least once per year and at least 90 days after a

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} in the case of an initial determination, the

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} in any other case, the flow rate of the gas

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} at least once every six months and at least 45 days

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} in the case of an initial determination, the

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} in any other case, the flow rate of the gas
      according to the most recent determination was greater than 500
      standard m^3^ per day and at most 1 000 standard m^3^ per day; and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} at least once every month and at least seven days

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} in the case of an initial determination, the

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} in any other case, the flow rate of the gas

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Excluded volumes

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} glycol dehydration, that is, the use of a liquid

    liquids;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} the use of a pneumatic controller, pneumatic pump
    or compressor;

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} the start-up and shutdown of equipment;

  - [(f)]{.lawlabel} well completion; and

  - [(g)]{.lawlabel} venting in order to avoid serious risk to human
    health or safety arising from an emergency situation.

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} Subsection (1) does not apply in respect of a
  facility, as of a given month, if the combined volume of hydrocarbon

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} Despite subsection (3), subsection (1) does apply in

##### [Regulatory LDAR Programs]{.HTitleText4} {#h-858856 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Obligation to inspect

- **[[30]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-30 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} An
  equipment component at an upstream oil and gas facility must be
  inspected, during the periods referred to in subsection (3), for the
  release of hydrocarbons by means of an eligible leak detection
  instrument.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Eligible leak detection instruments

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The following leak detection instruments are
  eligible:

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} a portable monitoring instrument if it

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} meets the specifications set out in Section 6 of
      EPA Method 21,

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} is operated in accordance with the requirements
      of Section 8.3 of EPA Method 21 to the extent that those
      requirements are consistent with its manufacturer's
      recommendations,

    - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} is calibrated in accordance with Sections 7,
      8.1, 8.2 and 10 of EPA Method 21 before it is used, for each day
      on which it is used, and

    - [(iv)]{.lawlabel} undergoes a calibration drift assessment after
      its last use on each of those days in accordance with the
      requirements set out in Section 60.485a(b)(2) of Subpart VVa,
      entitled Standards of Performance for Equipment Leaks of VOC in
      the Synthetic Organic Chemicals Manufacturing Industry for which
      Construction, Reconstruction, or Modification Commenced After
      November 7, 2006, in Part 60 of Title 40, Chapter I of the Code of
      Federal Regulations of the United States; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} an optical gas-imaging instrument if it is capable
    of imaging gas that is

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} in the spectral range for the compound of highest
      concentration in the hydrocarbon gas to be measured,

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} half methane and half propane at a total
      concentration of at most 500 ppmv and at a flow rate of at least
      60 g/h leaking from an orifice that is 0.635 cm in diameter, and

    - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} at the viewing distance determined in
      accordance with the requirements of the alternative work practice
      of the Environmental Protection Agency of the United States set
      out in Sections 60.18(h)(7)(i)(2)(i) to (v) of Section 60.18,
      entitled General control device and work practice requirements, in
      Part 60 of Title 40, Chapter I of the Code of Federal Regulations
      of the United States.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Period for inspections

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} The period for inspections is as follows:

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} for the first inspection, on or before the later of
    May 1, 2020 and the day that occurs 60 days after the day on which
    production at the facility first began; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} for subsequent inspections, at least three times
    per year and at least 60 days after a previous inspection.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Operation and maintenance

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} An eligible leak detection instrument must be
  operated and maintained in accordance with the recommendations, if
  any, of its manufacturer.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Training

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} The inspection must be conducted by an individual
  who, not more than five years before the inspection, has received
  training in

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the operation and maintenance, in accordance with
    subsection (4), of eligible leak detection instruments; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the calibration requirements set out in
    subparagraphs (2)(a)(iii) and (iv), if an eligible portable
    monitoring instrument is used.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Leaks

- **[[31]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-31 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A
  release of hydrocarbons from an equipment component is a leak if

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the release consists of at least 500 ppmv of
    hydrocarbons, as determined by an inspection conducted by means of
    an eligible portable monitoring instrument in accordance with EPA
    Method 21; or

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the release is detected

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} during an inspection conducted by means of an
      eligible optical gas-imaging instrument, or

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} by means of an auditory method, an olfactory
      method or a visual method, including the observation of the
      dripping of hydrocarbon liquids from the equipment component.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Release not considered a leak

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} A release that is detected under paragraph (1)(b) is
  no longer considered to be a leak if the equipment component undergoes
  an inspection conducted by means of an eligible portable monitoring
  instrument in accordance with EPA Method 21 and the release is
  determined to consist of less than 500 ppmv of hydrocarbons.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Period for repair

- **[[32]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-32 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A leak
  from an equipment component that is detected, whether as a result of
  an inspection or otherwise, must be repaired

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} if the repair can be carried out while the
    equipment component is operating, within 30 days after the day on
    which it was detected; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} in any other case, within the period before the end
    of the next planned shutdown unless that period is extended under
    section 33.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Next planned shutdown

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The next planned shutdown must be scheduled not later
  than the date on which the estimated volume of hydrocarbon gas,
  expressed in standard m^3^, that, beginning from the day on which the
  leak is detected, would if no repairs are made be emitted from the
  leaking equipment component in question and from all other equipment
  components that are also leaking as of that day is equal to the volume
  of hydrocarbon gas, expressed in standard m^3^, that would be emitted
  due to purging of hydrocarbon gas from equipment components in order
  to carry out the repair.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Repair

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} A leak in an equipment component is considered to be
  repaired if the release is determined to consist of less than 500 ppmv
  of hydrocarbons based on an inspection of the equipment component by
  means of an eligible portable monitoring instrument in accordance with
  EPA Method 21 that is capable of measuring hydrocarbon concentrations
  in ppmv.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Renewal

#### [Pneumatic Controllers and Pneumatic Pumps]{.HTitleText3} {#h-858975 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Pneumatic controllers --- bleed rate

- **[[37]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-37 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A
  pneumatic controller at an upstream oil and gas facility must not
  operate using hydrocarbon gas, other than propane, unless

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} it is operated at an operational setting such that
    its bleed rate for that operational setting is less than or equal to
    0.17 standard m^3^/h according to the manufacturer's operating
    manual or according to a written demonstration, with supporting
    documents, made by the operator for the facility; or

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the hydrocarbon emissions from it are captured and
    routed to hydrocarbon gas conservation equipment or hydrocarbon gas
    destruction equipment.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Exception --- control of production
  processes

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Despite paragraph (1)(a), a pneumatic controller at
  an upstream oil and gas facility may operate using hydrocarbon gas,
  other than propane, at an operational setting such that its bleed rate
  for that operational setting is more than 0.17 standard m^3^/h if the
  operator for the facility has a written record, with supporting
  documents, that demonstrates that the pneumatic controller must
  operate at that operational setting because of the need for the
  pneumatic controller to have a sufficient response time to control a
  process in the facility's production activities.

  - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} for controlling temperature,

  - [(iv)]{.lawlabel} as a transducer,

  - [(v)]{.lawlabel} as a positioner, or

### [Venting Limit]{.HTitleText2} {#h-859053 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}15 000 standard m^3^ per year

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Records --- emergency situation

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} For each volume of vented hydrocarbon gas arising
  from an emergency situation referred to in subsection 47(2), a record
  must be made that indicates:

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the name of the offshore facility;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the volume of hydrocarbon gas that was vented,
    expressed in standard m^3^; and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} a description of the emergency situation.

### [Compressors]{.HTitleText2} {#h-859067 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Capture or venting of emissions

**[[49]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-49 .sectionLabel}** The emissions of
hydrocarbon gas from the seals of a centrifugal compressor at an
offshore facility must be

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} captured and routed to hydrocarbon gas conservation
  equipment or hydrocarbon gas destruction equipment; or

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} routed to vents that release those emissions to the
  atmosphere.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Continuous monitoring devices

- **[[50]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-50 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  flow rate of emissions of hydrocarbon gas from vents referred to in
  paragraph 49(b) must be measured by means of a continuous monitoring
  device.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Requirements

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} A continuous monitoring device must

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} be calibrated in accordance with the manufacturer's
    recommendations such that its measurements have a maximum margin of
    error of ±10%;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} be operated continuously, other than during periods
    when it is undergoing normal servicing or timely repairs; and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} be equipped with an alarm that is triggered when
    the applicable flow rate limit referred to in subsection (3) for the
    vents of the compressor is reached.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Flow rate limit

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} The flow rate limit of emissions of hydrocarbon gas
  from the vents of a compressor is

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} for a compressor that was installed before January
    1, 2023,

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} 0.68 standard m^3^/min, if the compressor has a
      rated brake power of greater than or equal to 5 MW, and

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} 0.34 standard m^3^/min, if the compressor has a
      rated brake power of less than 5 MW; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} for a compressor that was installed on or after
    January 1, 2023, 0.14 standard m^3^/min.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Corrective action

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} If the alarm is triggered, corrective action must be
  taken to reduce the flow rate to below or equal to the applicable flow
  rate limit, as demonstrated by the absence of an alarm when the
  compressor begins to operate following the taking of that action.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Records

**[[51]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-51 .sectionLabel}** A record must be made
that indicates the following information concerning centrifugal
compressors:

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} for each compressor referred to in section 49,

  - [(i)]{.lawlabel} its serial number,

  - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} its make and model,

  - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} whether it was installed at the facility before
    January 1, 2023 or on or after January 1, 2023,

  - [(iv)]{.lawlabel} if it was installed at the facility before January
    1, 2023, its rated brake power, and

  - [(v)]{.lawlabel} an indication of the manufacturer's recommendations
    for the calibration of the continuous monitoring device, along with
    a demonstration, with supporting documents, that the measurements
    taken with that calibration have a maximum margin of error of ±10%;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} for each compressor for which an alarm referred to
  subsection 50(4) was triggered,

  - [(i)]{.lawlabel} its serial number, make and model,

  - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} the date on which the alarm was triggered,

  - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} the flow rate indicated by the continuous
    monitoring device when the alarm was triggered, and

  - [(iv)]{.lawlabel} a description of the corrective action that was
    taken, along with the dates on which that action was taken.

### [Gas Detection System and Repair of Leaks]{.HTitleText2} {#h-859101 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Requirements

- **[[52]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-52 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} An
  offshore facility must be equipped with a gas detection system that
  satisfies the requirements of section 32 of the [Newfoundland Offshore
  Petroleum Installations Regulations](/eng/regulations/SOR-95-104) and
  section 32 of the [Nova Scotia Offshore Petroleum Installations
  Regulations](/eng/regulations/SOR-95-191).

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Repair

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} A leak must be repaired within 730 days after the day
  on which it is detected by the gas detection system or is detected by
  means of an auditory method, an olfactory method or a visual method,
  including the observation of the dripping of hydrocarbon liquids from
  the equipment component.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Records

**[[53]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-53 .sectionLabel}** A record must be made
that indicates the following information concerning the detection and
repair of leaks:

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} the date on which each leak was detected;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} the type of equipment that was leaking, along with
  its location within the facility or its identifier;

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} the means by which the leak was identified; and

- [(d)]{.lawlabel} the steps that were taken to repair each leak
  detected, along with the dates on which those steps were taken.

## [PART 3]{.HLabel1}[Administration]{.HTitleText1} {#h-859113 .Part}

### [Registration]{.HTitleText2} {#h-859114 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Registration report

- **[[54]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-54 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} An
  upstream oil and gas facility in respect of which any of sections 5,
  9, 11, 14 and 15 apply or in respect of which sections 26 to 45 apply
  and an offshore facility in respect of which section 46 applies must
  be registered by providing the Minister with a registration report for
  the facility that contains the information set out in Schedule 3.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Date of registration

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The facility must be registered not later than 120
  days after the later of

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} January 1, 2020, and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the earlier of

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} the first day on which any of sections 5, 9, 11,
      14, 15 and 46 apply in respect of the facility, and

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} the first day of the month referred to in
      subsection 20(1) as of which sections 26 to 45 apply in respect of
      the facility.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Updated information

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} If there is a change such that the information
  provided in the facility's registration report is no longer accurate,
  a notice to that effect that contains the updated information, along
  with the information referred to in item 4 of Schedule 3, must be sent
  to the Minister not later than 90 days after the change.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Provision of information

- **[[55]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-55
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Information that is required under
  section 54 to be in a registration report provided to the Minister may
  be provided to the Minister via an approved entity.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Deemed provision of registration report

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} If all of the information required to be in a
  registration report is provided to the Minister via an approved
  entity, the operator for that facility must notify the Minister to
  that effect. The registration report is deemed to have been provided
  to the Minister on the day on which the Minister receives that notice.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Approval of entity

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} The Minister may approve an entity for the purpose of
  subsection (1) if the Minister concludes an arrangement with the
  entity under which information referred to in section 54 that is
  provided to the entity is accessible to the Minister.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Publication of approved entities

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} The Minister must publish a list of approved entities
  in the Environmental Registry established under section 12 of the
  [Canadian Environmental Protection Act, 1999](/eng/acts/C-15.31).

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Withdrawal of approval

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} The Minister may withdraw the approval of an entity
  and publish a notice to that effect in the Environmental Registry.

### [Record-making and Updating and Keeping of Documents]{.HTitleText2} {#h-859137 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Record-making and updates

- **[[56]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-56 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A
  record that is required to be made under these Regulations must be
  made within 30 days after the day on which the information to be
  recorded becomes available. The record must be updated within 30 days
  after the information to be updated becomes available.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Record-keeping --- indefinite

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} A record, along with supporting documents, of
  information that applies on an ongoing basis must be kept indefinitely
  until an update is required.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Record-keeping --- five years

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} If an update referred to in subsection (2) is
  required, the record of the information, along with its supporting
  documents, as recorded before the updating must be kept for five years
  after the updating.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Record-keeping --- five years

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} A record, along with supporting documents, of
  information that applies only in respect of a given day, must be kept
  for five years after that given day.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Document-keeping

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} A document that is required to be kept under these
  Regulations must be kept for five years.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Place kept

  [(6)]{.lawlabel} The records and documents must be kept at the
  upstream oil and gas facility to which they relate or at another place
  in Canada where they can be inspected.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Provision of records

  [(7)]{.lawlabel} On the Minister's request, the operator must, within
  60 days after the day on which the request was made, provide any of
  the records or documents kept to the Minister.
