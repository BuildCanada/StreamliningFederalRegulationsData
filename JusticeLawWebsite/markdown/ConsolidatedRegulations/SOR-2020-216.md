Whereas the annexed [Navigation Safety Regulations,
2020](/eng/regulations/SOR-2020-216) establish additional or
complementary standards to those set out in the International Convention
for the Safety of Life At Sea of 1974 and the Protocol of 1988 relating
to the International Convention for the Safety of Life At Sea of 1974,
and the Governor in Council is satisfied that those standards meet the
objectives of the Convention and Protocol;

Therefore, Her Excellency the Governor General in Council, on the
recommendation of the Minister of Transport and the Minister of Natural
Resources with respect to the provisions of the annexed Regulations
other than sections 425 to 427 and on the recommendation of the Minister
of Natural Resources with respect to sections 425 to 427, makes the
annexed [Navigation Safety Regulations,
2020](/eng/regulations/SOR-2020-216) pursuant to

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Composite unit

- **[[2]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-2 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} For the
  purposes of these Regulations, a composite unit of a pushing vessel
  and a pushed vessel that are rigidly connected and designed as a
  dedicated and integrated tug-and-barge combination is considered to be
  a single vessel that is not a towboat, the length and gross tonnage of
  which is the aggregate length and gross tonnage of the two vessels
  composing the unit.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Documents --- amended from time to time

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Any reference in the Regulations to a document is a
  reference to the document as amended from time to time, except when
  the reference is to the document as it read on a specified date.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Incorporated documents --- meaning of
  "should"

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} For the purpose of interpreting a document
  incorporated by reference into these Regulations, "should" is to be
  read as "must" and recommendations are to be considered mandatory.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Incorporated documents --- meaning of
  "ship"

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} For the purpose of interpreting a document
  incorporated by reference into these Regulations, "ship" is to be read
  as "vessel".

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Incorporated documents --- exclusion of
  certain expressions

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} A document incorporated by reference into these
  Regulations is to be read without reference to the expressions "at the
  discretion of the Administration", "in the opinion of the
  Administration", "or other means", "satisfactory to the
  Administration" and "unless the Administration decides otherwise",
  including any necessary adaptations to these expressions found in the
  document.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Interpretation --- vessel date of
  construction

  [(6)]{.lawlabel} For the purposes of these Regulations, the date of
  construction of a vessel is the earliest of the dates on which

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} its keel is laid,

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} construction of the vessel begins, and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} the assembly of the vessel has reached the lesser
    of 50 tonnes and 1% of the estimated mass of all structural material
    of the vessel.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Definition of
[[vessel]{.dfn}]{.DefinitionRef} under the Act

**[[3]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-3 .sectionLabel}** For the purposes of these
Regulations, barges that are not self-propelled are prescribed as a
class of floating object that is excluded from the definition of
[[vessel]{.dfn}]{.DefinitionRef} in section 2 of the Act.

## [General Requirements]{.HTitleText1} {#h-1262678 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Compliance

**[[4]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-4 .sectionLabel}** Except as otherwise
provided, the authorized representative of a vessel must ensure that the
requirements of these Regulations are met in respect of the vessel.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}General requirement

- **[[5]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-5 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Unless
  under *[force majeure]{.otherLang lang="fr"}* or to save life or
  property, the master of a vessel must ensure that the vessel does not
  engage on a voyage unless it is fitted with the equipment required
  under these Regulations.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Effective operating condition

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The master and authorized representative of a vessel
  must take all reasonable steps to ensure that all equipment required
  under these Regulations is installed, tested and maintained in a
  manner that ensures it is in effective operating condition.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Effective operating condition ---
  restoration

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} If any equipment required by these Regulations ceases
  to be in effective operating condition, the master of the vessel must,
  as soon as possible, restore the equipment to an effective operating
  condition.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Voyage to repair facilities

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} If the vessel is in a port where repair facilities to
  restore the equipment to an effective operating condition are not
  readily available, the master must plan and execute a safe voyage to a
  port where such facilities are readily available, taking into account
  the fact that the equipment is not in effective operating condition.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Section 112 of the Act --- shore station

- **[[6]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-6 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} For the
  purposes of section 112 of the Act, the authority on shore that must
  be notified of a direct danger to navigation is the shore station for
  the area in which the vessel is navigating.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Procedures for giving notice to vessels

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} A master who gives notice under section 112 of the
  Act to all vessels in the vicinity and the shore station must give the
  notice in accordance with the danger message reporting procedures set
  out in Section A5, entitled Navigation Safety, of the annual edition
  of Notices to Mariners.

## [PART 1]{.HLabel1}[Marine Navigation]{.HTitleText1} {#h-1262696 .Part}

### [Application]{.HTitleText2} {#h-1262697 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Application

- **[[100]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-100 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} This
  Part applies in respect of the following vessels:

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} Canadian vessels everywhere; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} vessels that are not Canadian vessels and that are
    in Canadian waters.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Activities related to oil or gas

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} This Part applies in respect of a vessel referred to
  in subsection (1) that is capable of engaging in the drilling for, or
  the production, conservation or processing of, oil or gas, except a
  vessel that is on location and engaged in one of those activities in
  an area referred to in

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} section 3 of the [Canada Oil and Gas Operations
    Act](/eng/acts/O-7);

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} subsection 8(1) of the [Canada-Newfoundland and
    Labrador Atlantic Accord Implementation Act](/eng/acts/C-7.5); or

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} subsection 8(1) of the [Canada-Nova Scotia Offshore
    Petroleum Resources Accord Implementation Act](/eng/acts/C-7.8).

### [DIVISION 1]{.HLabel2}[Maintenance and Standards]{.HTitleText2} {#h-1262708 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Standards

- **[[101]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-101
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Every type of equipment referred to
  in column 1 of Schedule 1 with which a vessel of 150 gross tonnage or
  more is fitted to comply with this Part and every AIS that is fitted
  on a vessel to which this Part applies must be type approved by a
  competent authority as meeting the following standards:

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the performance standards set out in the Annex to
    IMO resolution A.694(17), General Requirements for Shipborne Radio
    Equipment Forming Part of the Global Maritime Distress and Safety
    System (GMDSS) and for Electronic Navigational Aids;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the testing standards set out in IEC 60945,
    Maritime Navigation and Radiocommunication Equipment and Systems --
    General Requirements -- Methods of Testing and Required Test
    Results; and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} every standard set out in columns 2 to 4 of
    Schedule 1 for the equipment or AIS.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Exception --- equivalent standard

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The equipment and the AIS may be of a type approved
  by a competent authority as meeting a standard that provides a level
  of safety that is equivalent to or higher than that provided by the
  standards referred to in paragraph (1)(c) instead of the standards
  referred to in that paragraph.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Type approval

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} The type approval must be evidenced by a label or a
  document issued by the competent authority.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Placement of document or label

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} The type approval must be

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} if it is evidenced by a label, securely affixed to
    the equipment in a readily visible location; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} if it is evidenced by a document, kept in a readily
    accessible location on board the vessel.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}English or French translation

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} If the label or document is written in a language
  other than English or French, it must be accompanied by an English or
  French translation.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Equipment that is not required

  [(6)]{.lawlabel} Subsections (1) to (5) apply in respect of equipment
  that is fitted on a vessel even if the equipment is not required to be
  fitted on the vessel under this Part, if

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} performance standards for that equipment are
    specified in Schedule 1;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the vessel is of 500 gross tonnage or more and
    engaged on a voyage other than a sheltered waters voyage;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} the vessel is not a fishing vessel; and

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} the equipment was fitted on or after July 1, 2002.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Grandfathering

  [(7)]{.lawlabel} Paragraphs (1)(b) and (c) and subsections (2) to (4)
  do not apply to equipment referred to in column 1 of Schedule 2 that
  was fitted before July 1, 2002, if the equipment is of a type approved
  by a competent authority as meeting the following standards:

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the performance standards set out in the Annex to
    IMO resolution A.281(VIII), Recommendation on General Requirements
    for Electronic Navigational Aids; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the standards set out in column 2 of Schedule 2 for
    that equipment.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Maintenance record

- **[[102]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-102
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Every Canadian vessel of 150 gross
  tonnage or more that is engaged on an international voyage and every
  Canadian vessel of 500 gross tonnage or more must keep on board a
  maintenance record for the equipment required under this Part that
  shows all periodic testing and servicing, all defects, repairs and
  parts replacements and the dates and locations of each event and the
  personnel involved.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Manuals

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Every vessel must keep on board the manufacturer's
  operating and maintenance manuals for the equipment that it must be
  fitted with under this Part.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Spare parts

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} Every vessel that is engaged on a voyage other than a
  sheltered waters voyage must carry the spare parts recommended by the
  manufacturer or by the operating or maintenance manuals for the
  equipment that it must be fitted with under this Part.

### [DIVISION 2]{.HLabel2}[Equipment Required for Vessels Subject to Chapter V of SOLAS]{.HTitleText2} {#h-1262742 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Application

- **[[103]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-103 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} This
  Division applies in respect of the following vessels:

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} Canadian vessels of 150 gross tonnage or more but
    less than 500 gross tonnage that are engaged on an international
    voyage;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} Canadian vessels of 500 gross tonnage or more; and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} foreign vessels that are subject to Chapter V of
    SOLAS.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Application --- exceptions

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Despite subsection (1), this Division does not apply
  in respect of the following Canadian vessels:

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} fishing vessels;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} cable ferries;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} pleasure crafts; or

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} vessels operating exclusively in the waters of the
    Great Lakes, their connecting and tributary waters, and the waters
    of the St. Lawrence River as far seaward as a straight line drawn

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} from Cap-des-Rosiers to Pointe Ouest, Anticosti
      Island, and

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} from Anticosti Island to the north shore of the
      St. Lawrence River along a meridian of longitude 63° W.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Compliance

- **[[104]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-104
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Subject to subsections (2) to (5),
  an authorized representative of a vessel must ensure that the
  following requirements are met in respect of the vessel:

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} for a Canadian vessel, the requirements set out in
    Regulations 15 to 19 and 20 to 35 of Chapter V of SOLAS, except the
    requirements of Regulation 19.2.4; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} for a foreign vessel, the requirements set out in
    Chapter V of SOLAS.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Regulation 18 of Chapter V of SOLAS ---
  type approval

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} For the purposes of this section, the words "type
  approved by the Administration" used in Regulation 18 of Chapter V of
  SOLAS must be read as "type approved by a competent authority" when
  that Regulation applies to a Canadian vessel.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Exception --- Regulation 18.9 of
  Chapter V of SOLAS

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} A Canadian vessel of 500 gross tonnage or more must
  comply with Regulation 18.9 of Chapter V of SOLAS only if it is
  engaged on an international voyage.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Exception --- Regulation 19.2.2.3 of
  Chapter V of SOLAS

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} The requirements set out in Regulation 19.2.2.3 of
  Chapter V of SOLAS do not apply

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} to a ferry that engages solely on voyages of less
    than five nautical miles; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} until January 1, 2022, to a vessel other than a

    voyages that are not international voyages.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Exception --- Regulation 19.2.7.1 of

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} For the purposes of this section, Regulation 19.2.7.1
  of Chapter V of SOLAS must be read without reference to "or, where
  considered appropriate by the Administration, a second 9 GHz radar".

  [(6)]{.lawlabel} Every vessel that is fitted with a standard magnetic
  compass must be fitted with a means of communication between the
  standard magnetic compass position and the position from which the
  vessel is normally navigated.

  Manoeuvring Information on Board Ships before the vessel enters into
  service and must keep that information on board, if

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} it is of 1 600 gross tonnage or more and was
    constructed on or after March 1, 2001;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} it is a chemical carrier or gas carrier that was
    constructed on or after March 1, 2001; or

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} it is a Safety Convention vessel.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Definitions

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The following definitions apply in paragraph (1)(b).

  [[chemical carrier]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[chemical carrier]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means a vessel that is
      constructed or adapted for the carriage of dangerous chemicals and
      that is is engaged in the carriage of those
      chemicals. ([transporteur de produits chimiques]{.DefinedTermLink
      lang="fr"})

  [[gas carrier]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[gas carrier]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means a cargo vessel that was
      constructed or adapted for the carriage in bulk of any liquefied
      gas or other products listed in Chapter 19 of the International
      Code for the Construction and Equipment of Ships Carrying
      Liquefied Gases in Bulk, published by the IMO, and that is engaged
      in the carriage of those products. ([transporteur de
      gaz]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Modification or conversion of vessel

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} If a vessel is modified or converted such that its
  dimensions or other characteristics are altered in a way affecting the
  manoeuvring capability of the vessel, the manoeuvring information
  referred to in subsection (1) must be updated.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Exception

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} If it is not practicable to establish the manoeuvring
  information required by subsection (1) in its final form before the
  vessel enters into service, the information must be

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} established in a preliminary form before the vessel
    enters into service;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} established in its final form as soon as
    circumstances permit after the vessel enters into service; and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} verified in its final form within 18 months after
    the vessel enters into service.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Manoeuvring information --- IMO
  resolution A.209(VII)

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} Every vessel of 1 600 gross tonnage or more that was
  constructed before March 1, 2001, other than a Safety Convention
  vessel, must establish and display manoeuvring information as set out
  in the Annex to IMO resolution A.209(VII), Recommendation on
  Information to Be Included in the Manoeuvring Booklets and must keep
  the manoeuvring information on board.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Canadian towboats --- radar

- **[[125]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-125 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A
  Canadian vessel that is a towboat must be fitted with

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} if it is of 5 gross tonnage or more and is engaged
    on an inland voyage or a near coastal voyage, Class 2, a radar to
    determine and display the range and bearing of radar transponders
    and of other surface craft, obstructions, buoys, shorelines and
    navigational marks;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} if it is engaged on a near coastal voyage, Class 1,

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} echo-sounding equipment to measure and display
      the available depth of water, and

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} two radars that meet the requirements set out in
      paragraph (a) and that are functionally independent of each other;
      and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} if it is engaged on an unlimited voyage, the
    equipment set out in paragraph (b) and a gyro-compass to determine
    and display its heading by shipborne non-magnetic means.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Exception

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} A Canadian vessel that is a towboat does not have to
  meet the equipment requirements set out in subsection (1) if it is
  engaged in a towing operation in an emergency situation on an
  exceptional basis.

### [DIVISION 5]{.HLabel2}[Additional Requirements --- Vessels Not Subject to Chapter V of SOLAS]{.HTitleText2} {#h-1262967 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Non-application

**[[126]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-126 .sectionLabel}** This Division does not
apply in respect of vessels subject to the requirements of Division 2 of
this Part.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Guidelines and standards -- bridge

**[[127]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-127 .sectionLabel}** On every vessel of 150
gross tonnage or more that is engaged on an international voyage and on
every vessel of 500 gross tonnage or more that is engaged on a voyage
other than a sheltered waters voyage, all decisions that affect bridge
design, bridge procedures and the design and arrangement of navigational
systems and equipment on the bridge must take into consideration the
following documents:

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} the Annex to IMO circular MSC/Circ.982, Guidelines on
  Ergonomic Criteria for Bridge Equipment and Layout;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} if the vessel is fitted with an integrated bridge
  system, the Annex to IMO circular SN.1/Circ.288, Guidelines for Bridge
  Equipment and Systems, Their Arrangement and Integration (BES); and

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} if the vessel is fitted with an integrated navigation
  system, Annex 3 to IMO resolution MSC.86(70), Adoption of New and
  Amended Performance Standards for Navigational Equipment.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Electromagnetic compatibility

**[[128]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-128 .sectionLabel}** The electrical and
electronic equipment installed on a vessel of 150 gross tonnage or more
that is engaged on an international voyage, or on a vessel of 500 gross
tonnage or more, must meet the following requirements, if the equipment
has not been type approved by a competent authority as meeting testing
standard IEC 60945, Maritime Navigation and Radiocommunication Equipment
and Systems -- General Requirements -- Methods of Testing and Required
Test Results:

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} the equipment must be installed so that
  electromagnetic interference does not affect the proper functioning of
  navigational systems and equipment;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} if the equipment is portable, it must not be operated
  on the bridge if it might affect the proper functioning of
  navigational systems and equipment; and

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} if the equipment is on the bridge or in the vicinity
  of the bridge on a vessel constructed on or after July 1, 2002, it
  must be tested for electromagnetic compatibility when the equipment is
  installed, taking into account testing standard IEC 60533, Electrical
  and Electronic Installations in Ships --- Electromagnetic
  Compatibility (EMC) -- Ships with a Metallic Hull.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Modes of operation --- equipment

**[[129]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-129 .sectionLabel}** The mode of operation
being used must be indicated if equipment that is referred to in this
Part and fitted on a vessel on or after July 1, 2002 offers alternative
modes of operation.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Integrated bridge systems

**[[130]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-130 .sectionLabel}** Integrated bridge
systems that are fitted on a vessel on or after July 1, 2002 must be so
arranged that failure of any subsystem is brought to the immediate
attention of the person in charge of the deck watch by audible and
visual alarms and does not cause the failure of any other subsystem.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Failure of integrated navigation system

**[[131]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-131 .sectionLabel}** In the case of a
failure in one part of an integrated navigation system, it must be
possible to operate every other individual piece of equipment or part of
the system separately.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Compass inspection

- **[[132]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-132
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} During an inspection of the
  compasses on a vessel that is not a pleasure craft, the master of the
  vessel must

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} show the inspector the most recent deviation card
    for each compass, signed and dated by the master and a deck officer;
    or

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} provide the inspector with a statement, signed by
    the master and a deck officer, confirming that the compasses have
    been found to be satisfactory to provide heading direction.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Inspector

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} For the purposes of subsection (1), an inspector is a
  marine safety inspector referred to in section 11 of the Act or a
  person, classification society or other organization authorized to
  carry out inspections under section 12 of the Act.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Heading or track control system

- **[[133]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-133 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} When
  the heading or track control system of a vessel is used in an area of
  high traffic density, under conditions of restricted visibility or in
  any other hazardous navigational situation, means must be provided to
  enable the immediate changeover to manual steering.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Qualified helmsman

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} In any situation described in subsection (1), the
  person in charge of the deck watch must ensure that the services of a
  qualified helmsman are available at all times to take over steering
  control.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Qualified person

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} Every changeover from the heading or track control
  system to manual steering of a vessel and vice versa must be made by,
  or under the supervision of, the person in charge of the deck watch.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Manual steering --- testing

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} The manual steering of a vessel must be tested, while
  the heading or track control system is not in use, before the vessel
  enters any area where navigation demands special caution, and at least
  once a day, to ensure the manual steering is in effective operating
  condition.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Steering gear

**[[134]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-134 .sectionLabel}** If a vessel is fitted
with two or more steering gear power units that are capable of
simultaneous operation, the vessel must have at least two of those units
in operation in areas where navigation demands special caution.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Steering gear changeover procedures

- **[[135]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-135 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A
  vessel that is fitted with a remote steering gear control system or a
  steering gear power unit must have permanently displayed, on its
  navigating bridge and in its steering gear compartment, if any,
  simple, brief operating instructions and a block diagram showing the
  changeover procedures for the system or unit.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Steering system familiarity

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The master and any person on board the vessel who is
  responsible for the operation or maintenance of the steering gear must
  be familiar with the operation of the steering systems fitted on the
  vessel and with the procedures for changing from one system to
  another.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Steering gear checks and tests

- **[[136]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-136
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Within 12 hours before the departure
  of a vessel, the steering gear of the vessel must be checked and
  tested and the checks and tests must include

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} verifying the operation of

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} the main steering gear,

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} the auxiliary steering gear, unless the gear
      includes tackle,

    - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} the remote steering gear control systems,

    - [(iv)]{.lawlabel} the steering controls located at different
      positions on the navigating bridge,

    - [(v)]{.lawlabel} the emergency power supply,

    - [(vi)]{.lawlabel} the rudder angle indicators in relation to the
      actual position of the rudder,

    - [(vii)]{.lawlabel} the remote steering gear control system power
      failure alarms,

    - [(viii)]{.lawlabel} the steering gear power unit failure alarms,
      and

    - [(ix)]{.lawlabel} automatic isolating arrangements and other
      automatic equipment required for the steering gears;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the full movement of the rudder according to the
    capabilities of the steering gear as designed;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} a visual inspection of the steering gear and its
    connecting linkage; and

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} verifying the operation of the means of
    communication between the navigating bridge and the steering gear
    compartment.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Exception --- regular voyages

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} For a vessel that regularly engages on voyages of
  less than one week, the checks and tests referred to in subsection (1)
  do not need to be carried out within 12 hours before departure if
  those checks and tests have been carried out at least once every week.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Emergency steering drills

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} In addition to the checks and tests referred to in
  subsections (1) and (2), emergency steering drills must be carried out
  at least once every three months and must include direct control from
  within the steering gear compartment, communications procedures with
  the navigating bridge and, where applicable, the operation of
  alternative power supplies.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Log book

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} The person in charge of the deck watch must record
  the dates on which the checks and tests referred to in subsections (1)
  and (2) are carried out and the dates and details of the drills
  referred to in subsection (3) in the official log book, referred to in
  Division 7 of Part 3 of the [Marine Personnel
  Regulations](/eng/regulations/SOR-2007-115), if that Division applies
  to the vessel, or any log book if the Division does not apply to it.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Working language

- **[[137]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-137 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  master or authorized representative of a vessel must determine and
  record in the official log book referred to in Division 7 of Part 3 of
  the [Marine Personnel Regulations](/eng/regulations/SOR-2007-115) or,
  if the Division does not apply, any log book, the appropriate working
  language for the purposes of navigational safety matters, and must
  ensure that each crew member is able to

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} understand the working language;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} give orders and instructions in that language, if
    appropriate; and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} report back in that language, if appropriate.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Translations

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} If the working language is not an official language
  of the state whose flag the vessel is entitled to fly, all plans and
  lists required to be posted, and all documents establishing
  procedures, must include a translation into the working language in
  addition to a copy in an official language.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}English as working language

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} Despite subsection (1), English must be used for
  bridge-to-bridge and bridge-to-shore safety communications, as well as
  for communications on board between the pilot and bridge watchkeeping
  personnel, unless the individuals directly involved in the
  communication speak a common language other than English.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Record of navigational activities

- **[[138]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-138
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Every vessel of 150 gross tonnage or
  more that is engaged on an international voyage must keep a record on
  board of navigational activities and events that are of importance to
  the safety of navigation.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Contents of record

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The record must contain

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the information set out in section 1 of the Annex
    to IMO resolution A.916(22), Guidelines for the Recording of Events
    Related to Navigation; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the dates on which the checks and tests referred to
    in subsections 136(1) and (2) were carried out and the dates and
    details of the emergency steering drills referred to in subsection
    136(3).

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Maintenance of record

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} The record must be maintained in accordance with
  section 4 of the Annex referred to in subsection (2) and kept for at
  least five years.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Plan for cooperation --- search and
rescue

- **[[139]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-139
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Every passenger vessel of less than
  150 gross tonnage that is engaged on an international voyage must keep
  on board a plan for cooperation with the search and rescue services
  for each area in which the vessel navigates in the event of an
  emergency.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Plan for cooperation --- requirements

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The plan for cooperation must

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} be developed by the authorized representative of
    the vessel in accordance with the Annex to IMO circular
    MSC.1/Circ.1079, Guidelines for Preparing Plans for Cooperation
    Between Search and Rescue Services and Passenger Ships; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} include provisions for periodic exercises to test
    its effectiveness.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Visibility requirements from navigating
bridge

- **[[140]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-140
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Every Canadian vessel that is 55 m
  or more in length and that is constructed on or after July 1, 2002,
  must comply with the following requirements:

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the view of the sea surface from the conning
    position must not be obscured for more than two vessel lengths or
    500 m, whichever is lesser, forward of the bow to 10° on either side
    under all conditions of draught, trim and deck cargo;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} if there are blind sectors caused by cargo, cargo
    gear or other obstructions outside the wheelhouse forward of the
    beam that obstruct the view of the sea surface from the conning
    position,

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} no blind sector may exceed 10° except that in the
      case of the view referred to in paragraph (a) a blind sector may
      not exceed 5°,

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} the total arc of blind sectors must not exceed
      20°, and

    - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} the clear sectors between blind sectors must be
      at least 5°;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} the horizontal field of vision from the conning
    position must extend over an arc of not less than 225° that is from
    right ahead to not less than 22.5° abaft the beam on either side of
    the vessel;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} from each bridge wing, the horizontal field of
    vision must extend over an arc of at least 225° that is from at
    least 45° on the opposite side through right ahead and then from
    right ahead to right astern through 180° on the same side of the
    vessel;

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} from the main steering position, the horizontal
    field of vision must extend over an arc from right ahead to at least
    60° on each side of the vessel;

  - [(f)]{.lawlabel} the vessel's side must be visible from the bridge
    wing;

  - [(g)]{.lawlabel} the height of the lower edge of the navigation
    bridge front windows above the bridge deck must be kept as low as
    possible without resulting in an obstruction of the forward view
    that contravenes this subsection;

  - [(h)]{.lawlabel} the upper edge of the navigation bridge front
    windows must allow a forward view of the horizon, for a person with
    a height of eye of 1 800 mm above the bridge deck at the conning
    position, when the vessel is pitching in heavy seas; and

  - [(i)]{.lawlabel} with respect to windows,

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} the navigation bridge front windows must be
      inclined from the vertical plane, top out, at an angle of not less
      than 10° but not more than 25°,

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} the framing between navigation bridge windows
      must be kept to a minimum and not be installed immediately forward
      of any workstation,

    - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} the windows must not be polarized or tinted,
      and

    - [(iv)]{.lawlabel} it must be possible, at all times and regardless
      of weather conditions, to have a clear view through at least two
      of the navigation bridge front windows and, depending on the
      bridge configuration, through an additional number of clear-view
      windows.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Canadian vessels constructed before
  July 1, 2002

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Every Canadian vessel that is 55 m or more in length
  that was constructed before July 1, 2002 must comply with the
  requirements set out in paragraphs (1)(a) and (b), unless structural
  alterations or additional equipment are required to comply with those
  requirements.

### [DIVISION 6]{.HLabel2}[Charts and Publications]{.HTitleText2} {#h-1263078 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Definitions

**[[141]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-141 .sectionLabel}** The following
definitions apply in this Division.

[[reference catalogue]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[reference catalogue]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means, in respect of an
    area to be navigated by a vessel, a catalogue of nautical charts and
    related publications for that area, published by or under the
    authority of the Canadian Hydrographic Service or published by or
    under the authority of a hydrographic office or other relevant
    government institution of a foreign state. ([catalogue de
    référence]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

[[waters under Canadian jurisdiction]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[waters under Canadian jurisdiction]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means

    - [(a)]{.lawlabel} Canadian waters; and

    - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the exclusive economic zone of Canada. ([eaux de
      compétence canadienne]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Charts, documents and publications on
board

- **[[142]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-142 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  master and authorized representative of a vessel must ensure that the
  most recent versions of the following charts, documents and
  publications, in respect of each area where the vessel is scheduled to
  be navigated, are kept on board:

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the reference catalogue and the applicable charts
    referred to in the catalogue in their largest scale, that are
    published by or under the authority of

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} in the case of a Canadian vessel in Canadian
      waters, the Canadian Hydrographic Service, and

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} in any other case, the Canadian Hydrographic
      Service or the hydrographic office or other relevant government
      institution of a foreign state;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the annual edition of Notices to Mariners;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} in the case of a Canadian vessel in waters under
    Canadian jurisdiction and a vessel that is not a Canadian vessel,

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} Canadian Sailing Directions, published by the
      Canadian Hydrographic Service,

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} Canadian Tide and Current Tables, published by
      the Canadian Hydrographic Service,

    - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} List of Lights, Buoys and Fog Signals,
      published by the Canadian Coast Guard, and

    - [(iv)]{.lawlabel} if the vessel is required under these
      Regulations or any foreign law to be fitted with radio equipment,
      Radio Aids to Marine Navigation, published by the Canadian Coast
      Guard;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} in the case of a Canadian vessel not in waters
    under Canadian jurisdiction, the following publications referred to
    in the reference catalogue:

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} sailing directions,

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} tide and current tables,

    - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} lists of lights, and

    - [(iv)]{.lawlabel} if the vessel is required to be fitted with
      radio equipment under these Regulations, the list of radio aids to
      navigation;

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} in the case of a vessel, other than a pleasure
    craft of less than 150 gross tonnage, that is engaged on a voyage
    other than a sheltered waters voyage, an illustrated table of
    life-saving signals;

  - [(f)]{.lawlabel} in the case of a Canadian vessel of 150 gross
    tonnage or more, a printed copy of the International Aeronautical
    and Maritime Search and Rescue Manual, Volume III, Mobile
    Facilities, published by the IMO and the International Civil
    Aviation Organization;

  - [(g)]{.lawlabel} in the case of a Canadian vessel that is required
    to be fitted with radio equipment under Part 2 and that is engaged
    on a near coastal voyage, Class 1 or an unlimited voyage, or a
    Safety Convention vessel, a printed copy of the International Code
    of Signals published by the IMO and a printed copy of Annex 1 to IMO
    resolution A.918(22), IMO Standard Marine Communication Phrases; and

  - [(h)]{.lawlabel} in the case of a vessel that is engaged on a voyage
    during which ice may be encountered, the document entitled Ice
    Navigation in Canadian Waters, published by the Canadian Coast
    Guard.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Document availability --- paragraphs
  (1)(e) and (f)

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The master and authorized representative of a vessel
  must ensure that the table required under paragraph (1)(e) and the
  publication required under paragraph (1)(f) are readily available to
  the person in charge of navigation.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Exception --- less than 100 gross
  tonnage

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} Subsection (1) does not apply in the case of a vessel
  of less than 100 gross tonnage, if the person in charge of navigation
  has sufficient knowledge of the following about where the vessel is to
  be navigated such that the safety and efficiency of navigation in that
  area will not be compromised:

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the location and character of charted

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} shipping routes,

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} lights, buoys and marks, and

    - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} navigational hazards; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the prevailing navigational conditions, taking into
    account such factors as tides, currents, and ice and weather
    patterns.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Exception --- charts, documents and
  publications

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} Subsection (1) does not apply if, after reasonable
  efforts, the master and authorized representative of a vessel are
  unable to obtain at any place where the vessel calls, the charts,
  documents or publications required under this Division and the safety
  and efficiency of navigation will not be compromised. However, they
  must comply with subsection (1) as soon as circumstances permit.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Exception --- foreign state
  publications

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} The publications referred to in paragraphs (1)(c)
  and (d) may be replaced by similar publications issued officially by
  or on the authority of a hydrographic office or other relevant
  government institution of a foreign state, if the information
  contained in them that is necessary for the safe navigation of a
  vessel in the area in which the vessel is to be navigated is as
  complete, accurate, intelligible and up-to-date as the information
  contained in the publications referred to in those paragraphs.

        Performance Standards for Electronic Chart Display and
        Information Systems (ECDIS),

      - [(B)]{.lawlabel} is connected to the vessel's main and emergency
        sources of electrical energy, and

      - [(C)]{.lawlabel} is provided with an emergency source of
        electrical energy providing uninterrupted transitional power for
        a period of not less than 30 minutes, or

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} by other printed charts that meet the
      requirements of paragraph 142(1)(a) and on which the position of
      the vessel is being plotted at intervals that will enable a safe
      and immediate takeover in the event of an ECDIS failure.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Definition of
  [[ENC]{.dfn}]{.DefinitionRef}

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} For the purposes of subsection (1),
  [[ENC]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means an electronic navigational chart
  database that

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} is standardized as to content, structure and
    format;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} is issued for use with an ECDIS by or under the
    authority of the Canadian Hydrographic Service or by or under the
    authority of a hydrographic office or other relevant government
    institution of a foreign state; and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} contains all the chart information necessary for
    safe navigation.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Planning a voyage

- **[[144]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-144 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  master of a vessel must, before the vessel embarks on a voyage, plan
  the voyage taking into account the Annex to IMO resolution A.893(21),
  Guidelines for Voyage Planning, and if charts, documents and
  publications are required to be kept on board under section 142, by
  using those charts, documents and publications to the extent that they
  relate to voyage planning.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Identifying a route

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} When planning the voyage, the master must identify a
  route taking the following factors into account:

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} any relevant routing systems;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} sufficient sea room for the safe passage of the
    vessel throughout the intended voyage;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} all known navigational hazards and adverse weather
    conditions;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} any marine environmental protection measures that
    apply; and

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} actions and activities that could cause damage to
    the environment and measures to be taken to avoid those actions and
    activities.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Voyage display and position monitoring

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} The master of a vessel must display the vessel's
  route for the voyage using a chart referred to in paragraph 142(1)(a)
  and plot and monitor the vessel's position throughout the voyage on
  that chart.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Navigation accessories

**[[145]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-145 .sectionLabel}** Any vessel that is
required under section 142 to keep charts, documents and publications on
board must be fitted with

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} the navigation accessories necessary to permit the
  proper use of the charts so as to precisely determine the position of
  the vessel;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} the navigation accessories necessary to determine the
  accuracy of compass readings; and

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} binoculars.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Up-to-date charts, documents and
publications

- **[[146]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-146 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  master of a vessel must ensure that the charts, documents and
  publications required under this Division, before being used to plan
  and execute a voyage, are correct and up-to-date, based on information
  that is contained in Notices to Mariners or a navigational warning.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Exception

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Subsection (1) does not apply if the circumstances of
  the voyage are such that it is impossible for the master, after making
  reasonable efforts, to receive Notices to Mariners or navigational
  warnings.

## [PART 2]{.HLabel1}[Marine Radiocommunications]{.HTitleText1} {#h-1263160 .Part}

### [Definitions]{.HTitleText2} {#h-1263161 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Definitions

**[[200]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-200 .sectionLabel}** The following
definitions apply in this Part.

[[appropriate VHF channel]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[appropriate VHF channel]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means the channel
    specified in Notices to Mariners or in any law of Canada or of any
    foreign state for use in a traffic zone, or part of it, for vessel
    traffic management purposes. ([voie VHF appropriée]{.DefinedTermLink
    lang="fr"})

[[continuous watch]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[continuous watch]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means a radio watch that is
    uninterrupted except for brief intervals when the receiving
    capability of the radio installations are impaired or blocked by the
    installations' own communications or by periodic maintenance or
    checks. ([veille permanente]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

[[DSC]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[DSC]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means digital selective calling, which
    is a system that uses digital codes and conforms to Recommendation
    ITU-R M.493, Digital Selective-calling System for use in the
    Maritime Mobile Service, published by the International
    Telecommunication Union, Radiocommunication
    Sector. ([ASN]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

[[enhanced group call equipment]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[enhanced group call equipment]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means a radio
    installation capable of receiving maritime safety information and
    search and rescue information broadcast by an enhanced group call
    system. ([équipement d'appel de groupe amélioré]{.DefinedTermLink
    lang="fr"})

[[enhanced group call system]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[enhanced group call system]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means a system
    that allows for the broadcast of coordinated maritime safety
    information and search and rescue information to a defined
    geographical area using a mobile satellite service recognized by the
    IMO. ([système d'appel de groupe amélioré]{.DefinedTermLink
    lang="fr"})

[[EPIRB]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[EPIRB]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means an emergency position indicating
    radio beacon operating in the 406 MHz band. ([RLS]{.DefinedTermLink
    lang="fr"})

[[international NAVTEX service]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[international NAVTEX service]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means the
    coordinated broadcast of maritime safety information in English on
    the radio frequency of 518 kHz that is automatically received on any
    vessel through NBDP. ([service NAVTEX
    international]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

[[maritime safety information]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[maritime safety information]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means
    navigational and meteorological warnings, meteorological forecasts
    and other urgent safety-related messages broadcast to
    vessels. ([renseignements sur la sécurité maritime]{.DefinedTermLink
    lang="fr"})

[[MF/HF radio installation]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[MF/HF radio installation]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means a radio
    installation capable of transmitting and receiving voice
    communications and communications using NBDP and DSC in the MF/HF
    bands. ([installation radio MF/HF]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

[[MF radio installation]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[MF radio installation]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means a radio
    installation capable of transmitting and receiving voice
    communications and communications using DSC in the MF
    bands. ([installation radio MF]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

[[NAVTEX receiver]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[NAVTEX receiver]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means a receiver that is
    capable of receiving an international NAVTEX service
    message. ([récepteur NAVTEX]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

[[NBDP]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[NBDP]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means the process of message
    transmission known as narrow-band direct-printing telegraphy that
    uses the International Telegraph Alphabet Number 2 Code in such a
    way that receiving equipment automatically produces a printed
    version of the transmitted message. ([IDBE]{.DefinedTermLink
    lang="fr"})

[[PLB]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[PLB]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means a personal locator beacon
    operating in the 406 MHz band. ([BLP]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

[[radio operator]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[radio operator]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means a person who is the
    holder of a radio operator certificate issued under subparagraph
    5(1)(a)(iii) of the [Radiocommunication Act](/eng/acts/R-2) or an
    equivalent certificate issued by the appropriate authority of a
    foreign state and who is in charge of a radio watch on a
    vessel. ([opérateur radio]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

[[SART]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[SART]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means a transponder designed for search
    and rescue purposes that operates on a radar frequency or an AIS
    frequency. ([répondeur SAR]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

[[sea area A1]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[sea area A1]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means an area within the
    radiotelephone coverage of at least one VHF coast station in which
    continuous DSC alerting is available. ([zone océanique
    A1]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

[[sea area A2]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[sea area A2]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means an area, excluding sea
    area A1, within the radiotelephone coverage of at least one medium
    frequency (MF) coast station in which continuous DSC alerting is
    available. ([zone océanique A2]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

[[sea area A3]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[sea area A3]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means an area, excluding sea
    area A1 and sea area A2, within the coverage of a mobile-satellite
    service that is recognized by the IMO and supported by the ship
    earth station carried on board in which continuous alerting is
    available. ([zone océanique A3]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

[[sea area A4]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[sea area A4]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means an area outside of sea
    area A1, sea area A2 and sea area A3. ([zone océanique
    A4]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

[[ship earth station]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[ship earth station]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means a mobile earth
    station that is located on board a vessel and part of a
    mobile-satellite service that has been recognized by the
    IMO. ([station terrienne de navire]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

[[survival craft]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[survival craft]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}[\[Repealed, SOR/2023-257, s.
    533\]]{.Repealed}

[[traffic centre]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[traffic centre]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means a centre established by
    a government for the purpose of regulating marine traffic within a
    traffic zone. ([centre de gestion du trafic]{.DefinedTermLink
    lang="fr"})

[[traffic zone]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[traffic zone]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means an area under the
    responsibility of a traffic centre described in Notices to Mariners
    or in any law of Canada or of a foreign state and established for
    the purpose of directing vessel traffic. ([zone de gestion du
    trafic]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

[[VHF radio installation]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[VHF radio installation]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means a radio
    installation capable of transmitting and receiving voice
    communications on VHF frequencies. ([installation radio
    VHF]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

### [DIVISION 1]{.HLabel2}[General Requirements]{.HTitleText2} {#h-1263190 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Application

- **[[201]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-201 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} This
  Division applies in respect of Canadian vessels everywhere

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Application --- sections 203 to 207

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Sections 203 to 207 also apply in respect of foreign
  vessels in Canadian waters.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Non-application

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} This Division does not apply in respect of

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} a dredge or a floating plant unless it is located
    in a place where it constitutes a collision hazard to other vessels;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} a towboat, if the towboat and its tow are located
    within a booming ground; or

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} a pleasure craft.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Towboats

**[[202]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-202 .sectionLabel}** A towboat that is not a
Safety Convention vessel and that is engaged in a towing operation
outside the sea area in which it normally operates is not required to
meet any additional radio equipment requirements for outside that sea
area if

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} one of the vessels engaged in the towing operation
  with the towboat meets the requirements for that sea area; or

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} it is engaged in a towing operation in an emergency
  situation on an exceptional basis.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Responsibility of authorized
representative

**[[203]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-203 .sectionLabel}** The authorized
representative must ensure that the vessel is equipped with radio
equipment in accordance with this Part before the vessel embarks on a
voyage and throughout the voyage.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}VHF radio installation

- **[[204]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-204 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A
  vessel on a voyage, any part of which is in sea area A1, within VHF
  coverage of a Canadian Coast Guard station or on a voyage more than
  five nautical miles from shore on the sea coasts of Canada, must be
  fitted with a VHF radio installation capable of DSC if the vessel is

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} more than 8 m in length;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} carrying passengers; or

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} a towboat.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Exception

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Subsection (1) does not apply to a vessel that
  carries six passengers or less and is not more than 8 m in length if
  it is equipped with a portable VHF handheld radio capable of DSC.

    radiotelephone or a waterproof portable VHF handheld radio capable
    of DSC.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}General requirements --- radio
installation

**[[219]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-219 .sectionLabel}** A radio installation
must be

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} installed on board a vessel so as to

  - [(i)]{.lawlabel} protect it from mechanical, electrical or any other
    interference that would adversely affect its functioning,

  - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} be compatible with other radio and electronic
    equipment installed on the vessel and not cause adverse
    electromagnetic or electrostatic interaction, and

  - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} be readily accessible for maintenance purposes
    and inspection;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} clearly marked with the vessel's name, call sign,
  maritime mobile service identity number and any other identifier that
  may be used in the course of transmitting or receiving communications;
  and

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} located on a vessel so as to

  - [(i)]{.lawlabel} maximize its safety and operational accessibility,

  - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} enable the monitoring of the distress and safety
    frequencies, including frequencies on which maritime safety
    information is broadcasted, and the sending of distress signals or
    distress alerts, from the position from which the vessel is normally
    navigated, and

  - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} protect it from the harmful effects of water,
    extremes of temperature and other adverse environmental conditions.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}VHF radio accessibility

**[[220]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-220 .sectionLabel}** A VHF radiotelephone
and a VHF radio installation must be accessible from the vessel's
conning position and its operation, including the selection of channels,
must be possible from that position.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Position of vessel

**[[221]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-221 .sectionLabel}** If radio equipment is
capable of automatically providing the vessel's position when
transmitting a distress alert, the vessel's position and the time the
vessel was at that position must be made available from a GNSS receiver
for transmission by the radio equipment.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Radio equipment standards

- **[[222]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-222 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  radio equipment referred to in column 1 of Schedule 3 must be of a
  type approved by a competent authority as meeting the following
  standards:

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the performance standards set out in the Annex to
    IMO resolution A.694(17), General Requirements for Shipborne Radio
    Equipment Forming Part of the Global Maritime Distress and Safety
    System (GMDSS) and for Electronic Navigational Aids;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the testing standards set out in IEC 60945,
    Maritime Navigation and Radiocommunication Equipment and Systems --
    General Requirements -- Methods of Testing and Required Test
    Results;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} the standards set out for that equipment in column
    2 of Schedule 3; and

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} the standards set out for that equipment in column
    3 or 4 of Schedule 3.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Exception --- equivalent standard

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The equipment may be type approved by a competent
  authority as meeting a standard that provides a level of safety that
  is equivalent to or higher than that provided by the standards
  referred to in paragraph (1)(c) or (d) instead of the standards
  referred to paragraph (1)(c) or (d), as the case may be.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Exception --- VHF radio installation
  capable of DSC

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} The VHF radio installation capable of DSC on board a
  vessel referred to in subsection 204(1) or paragraph 207(1)(b) may
  meet either of the following standards instead of the standards
  referred to in paragraphs (1)(c) and (d):

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the testing standards set out in IEC 62238,
    Maritime Navigation and Radiocommunication Equipment and Systems --
    VHF Radiotelephone Equipment Incorporating Class "D" Digital
    Selective Calling (DSC) -- Methods of Testing and Required Test
    Results; or

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the testing standards set out in EN 301 025: VHF
    Radiotelephone Equipment for General Communications and Associated
    Equipment for Class "D" Digital Selective Calling (DSC); Harmonised
    Standard Covering the Essential Requirements of Articles 3.2 and
    3.3(g) of the Directive 2014/53/EU.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Type Approval

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} The type approval must be evidenced by a label or
  document issued by the competent authority.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Placement of document or label

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} The type approval must be

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} if it is evidenced by a label, securely affixed to
    the equipment in a readily visible location; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} if it is evidenced by a document, kept in a readily
    accessible location on board the vessel.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}English or French translation

  [(6)]{.lawlabel} If the label or document is written in a language
  other than English or French, it must be accompanied by an English or
  French translation.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Portable VHF handheld radio with DSC

- **[[223]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-223 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A
  portable VHF handheld radio capable of DSC must

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} be capable of transmitting and receiving distress
    and safety alerts using DSC on frequency 156.525 MHz (channel 70);

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} be capable of transmitting and receiving
    radiotelephony on

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} the distress and safety frequency of 156.8 MHz
      (channel 16),

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} the primary inter-vessel safety communication
      frequency of 156.3 MHz (channel 6),

    - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} the bridge-to-bridge communication frequency of
      156.65 MHz (channel 13),

    - [(iv)]{.lawlabel} the public correspondence frequency specifically
      assigned for the area in which the vessel is navigating, and

    - [(v)]{.lawlabel} any other VHF frequencies that are necessary for
      safety purposes in the area in which the vessel is navigating;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} be capable of receiving communications on

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} a VHF frequency specifically assigned for the
      transmission of maritime safety information in the area in which
      the vessel is navigating, or

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} if no frequency is specifically assigned in the
      area in which the vessel is navigating, any other frequency used
      for transmitting maritime safety information;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} be certified as waterproof by the manufacturer;

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} not be unduly affected by exposure to seawater, oil
    or the sun;

  - [(f)]{.lawlabel} be of robust mechanical construction;

  - [(g)]{.lawlabel} have an internal GNSS receiver capable of providing
    an accurate position within 90 seconds of being switched on, when
    provided with an unobstructed view of the sky; and

  - [(h)]{.lawlabel} be capable of six hours of operation, with a duty
    cycle of 10% transmitting, 10% receiving above squelch level and 80%
    receiving below squelch level.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Rechargeable battery

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} A portable VHF handheld radio capable of DSC that is
  powered by a rechargeable battery must be accompanied by a device
  capable of fully charging the battery from empty within 10 hours.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Supplementary VHF radio

- **[[224]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-224 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Any
  supplementary VHF radio installation that a vessel is fitted with
  under section 205 must be functionally independent from the VHF radio
  installation required under section 204.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Source of energy

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The VHF radio installations referred to in
  subsection (1) may be connected to the main source of energy for the
  vessel, but one of them must have another source of energy that is
  located in the upper part of the vessel.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}VHF radio antenna

**[[225]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-225 .sectionLabel}** The antenna of a VHF
radio installation must be

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} capable of transmitting and receiving a vertically
  polarized signal;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} installed as high as possible on a vessel and in such
  a manner as to provide an omnidirectional radiation pattern; and

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} connected to the radio installation by the shortest
  possible transmission line.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Battery-powered VHF radio

- **[[226]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-226 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} If
  batteries are the main source of energy for a VHF radio installation
  on board a vessel, the batteries must

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} be located in the upper part of the vessel;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} have sufficient capacity to operate the VHF radio
    installation; and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} be accompanied by a device capable of fully
    charging the batteries from empty within 10 hours.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Exception to paragraph (1)(a)

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} If it is impracticable to locate the batteries in the
  upper part of a vessel whose construction began before June 1, 1978 or
  that is less than 20 m in length, they must be located as high in the
  hull as is possible.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}MF/HF radio installations

**[[227]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-227 .sectionLabel}** An MF/HF radio
installation's transmitter on board a vessel must be capable of
delivering 125 W peak envelope power at the output of the transmitter.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Beacon registration

- **[[228]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-228 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  authorized representative of a vessel, or in the case of a pleasure
  craft that is not a Canadian vessel, the owner of the vessel, must
  register an EPIRB or PLB with the Canadian Beacon Registry, which is
  maintained by the Department of National Defence.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Updating the Canadian Beacon Registry

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The authorized representative or the owner, as the
  case may be, must update the information contained in the Canadian
  Beacon Registry with respect to the registration within 30 days after
  a change in the information.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}EPIRB and PLB --- Type Approved

- **[[229]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-229
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Every EPIRB and PLB must be of a
  type approved by Cospas-Sarsat as meeting the requirements of standard
  C/S T.007 entitled Cospas-Sarsat 406 MHz Distress Beacons Type
  Approval Standard, published by Cospas-Sarsat.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Type approval certificate

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The type approval referred to in subsection (1) must
  be evidenced by a type approval certificate that is kept in a readily
  accessible location on board.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Replacing the automatic release mechanism
--- EPIRB

- **[[230]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-230 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  automatic release mechanism of an EPIRB must be marked with the
  replacement date determined by the manufacturer and be replaced on or
  before that date in accordance with the manufacturer's instructions.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Replacing the battery --- EPIRB and PLB

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The battery of an EPIRB and the battery of a PLB must
  be replaced in accordance with the manufacturer's instructions

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} after the use of the EPIRB or PLB in an emergency;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} after an inadvertent activation of the EPIRB or
    PLB;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} if required as a result of an inspection or testing
    of the EPIRB or PLB; and

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} on or before the expiry date indicated on the
    battery.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Replacing the battery --- SART

**[[231]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-231 .sectionLabel}** The battery of a SART
must be replaced in accordance with the manufacturer's instructions on
or before the expiry date indicated on the battery.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Battery --- survival craft VHF
radiotelephone

**[[232]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-232 .sectionLabel}** A battery used to power
a survival craft VHF radiotelephone must

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} if the battery is non-rechargeable, be replaced, on
  or before the expiry date indicated on the battery; and

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} if the battery is rechargeable, be maintained in a
  fully charged condition while the vessel is at sea and the
  radiotelephone is not being used for communication purposes.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Search and rescue VHF radio
direction-finding equipment

**[[233]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-233 .sectionLabel}** Search and rescue VHF
radio direction-finding equipment must

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} be capable of receiving amplitude-modulated signals
  on frequency 121.5 MHz;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} be capable of receiving frequency or phase-modulated
  signals on frequency 156.8 MHz (channel 16); and

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} provide an automatic indication-of-direction signal
  that is received when the source of the signal is located within an
  arc of 30° on either side of the vessel's bow.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Reserve source of energy

- **[[234]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-234 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} If a
  vessel is required to have a reserve source of energy under section
  217, the reserve source must be capable of

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} being used immediately in the event of a failure of
    the main source of electrical energy; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} simultaneously operating

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} the VHF radio installation,

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} the lighting required under paragraph 217(1)(b),
      and

    - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} the radio equipment that the vessel must be
      fitted with under paragraph 207(1)(b), in the case of a vessel
      referred in that paragraph.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Capacity

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The reserve source of energy must have a capacity to
  provide electrical energy for

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} one hour, if the radio equipment is provided with
    an emergency source of electrical energy that meets the standards
    set out in TP 127, Ship Electrical Standards, published by the
    Department of Transport; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} six hours, in any other case.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Electrical capacity

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} The electrical capacity of the reserve source of
  energy under distress conditions must, for the period prescribed in
  subsection (2), be equal to the total of

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} one half of the electrical capacity necessary for
    the transmission of communications,

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the electrical capacity necessary for the
    continuous reception of communications, and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} the electrical capacity necessary for the
    continuous operation of any additional radio equipment or lighting
    connected to that reserve source of energy.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Independence

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} The reserve source of energy must be independent of
  the propelling power of the vessel and the vessel's electrical system.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Simultaneous supply

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} The reserve source of energy must, for the period
  prescribed by subsection (2), be capable of

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} simultaneously supplying energy to all radio
    installations that can be connected to the reserve source of energy
    at the same time; or

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} if only one radio installation can be connected to
    the reserve source of energy at the same time as a VHF radio
    installation, supplying energy to the radio installation that uses
    the most power.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Electrical lighting

  [(6)]{.lawlabel} The reserve source of energy must be used to supply
  the electrical lighting for a radio installation unless the electrical
  lighting has an independent source of energy that meets the capacity
  requirements set out in subsection (2).

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Reserve source of energy --- rechargeable
batteries

- **[[235]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-235 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} If
  the reserve source of energy consists of rechargeable batteries, a
  vessel must have

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} a means of recharging the batteries from empty to
    the minimum capacity required within 10 hours; or

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} for a vessel that is less than 20 m in length, a
    reserve battery of a capacity sufficient for the duration of the
    voyage.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Installation of rechargeable batteries

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Rechargeable batteries that constitute the reserve
  source of energy for a radio installation must be installed so that
  they

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} are maintained at all times at the temperature
    specified by the manufacturer of the batteries; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} provide, when fully charged, the minimum number of
    hours of operation required by subsection 234(2), regardless of
    meteorological conditions.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Failure of electrical energy source

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} If a radio installation requires an uninterrupted
  input of data from the vessel's GNSS receiver in order to function
  properly, the radio installation must have a means to ensure that
  uninterrupted input of data in the event of a failure of the vessel's
  main or emergency source of electrical energy.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Assessments and tests before a voyage

**[[236]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-236 .sectionLabel}** A radio operator must
ensure that the radio equipment is in proper operating condition before
the vessel embarks on a voyage.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Assessment of radio installation at sea

- **[[237]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-237
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} While a vessel is at sea, a radio
  operator must assess

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} daily the operating condition of a radio
    installation; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} subject to subsection (2), in the case of a VHF
    radio installation, MF radio installation or MF/HF radio
    installation, weekly by means of normal communications or a test
    call carried out within the communication range of either a radio
    installation or a coast station that is capable of DSC, the
    operating condition of the radio installation.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Test call

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} When a vessel has been out of the communication range
  of a radio installation or a coast station that is capable of DSC for
  longer than a week, a radio operator must make the test call at the
  first opportunity after the vessel is within the communication range
  of a coast station in order to assess the operating condition of the
  radio installation.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Testing transmitter functioning

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} A radio operator must, when testing the operating
  condition of a radio installation transmitter, use the antenna
  normally used for the transmitter.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Restoring to proper functioning

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} If an assessment referred to in subsection (1) or (2)
  indicates that the radio equipment or the reserve source of energy is
  not operating properly, the equipment or source of energy must be
  restored to its proper operating condition without delay.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Out-of-service radio installation

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} If a radio installation is out-of-service for more
  than 30 days, a radio operator must verify, within the seven days
  before the vessel embarks on a voyage, that the radio equipment is in
  proper operating condition and must enter that information in the
  radio log.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Radio equipment batteries

- **[[238]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-238 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} If
  batteries constitute a source of electrical energy for radio
  equipment, other than an EPIRB or PLB, they must be

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} tested daily, to determine the state of their
    charge;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} checked once each month, to determine the physical
    condition of the batteries, their connections and their compartment;
    and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} sufficiently charged to permit the continuous
    operation of the radio equipment for the duration of the voyage.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Reserve rechargeable batteries

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Rechargeable batteries that constitute a reserve
  source of electrical energy for any radio equipment must undergo

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} a check of their capacity, once a year, when the
    vessel is not at sea, by fully discharging and recharging them,
    using normal operating current and their battery rating period,
    unless the battery manufacturer advises otherwise; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} an assessment of their charge without any
    significant discharge of the batteries immediately before the vessel
    embarks on a voyage, and weekly while at sea.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Inspection and testing of EPIRB or PLB

- **[[239]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-239 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} An
  EPIRB or PLB, other than an EPIRB or PLB stowed in an inflatable life
  raft, must be inspected and tested by a radio operator on installation
  and at least once every six months after that, in accordance with the
  manufacturer's instructions.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Inspection and testing of a SART

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} A SART must be inspected and tested by a radio
  operator on installation and at least once every six months after
  that, in accordance with the manufacturer's instructions.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}VHF radiotelephone testing

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} A survival craft VHF radiotelephone must be tested by
  a radio operator during each boat and fire drill held on board the
  vessel, if the radiotelephone has a power source that can be replaced
  or recharged by the user.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Radio inspection certificate

- **[[240]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-240 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  following vessels, other than a Safety Convention vessel, must have a
  radio inspection certificate to engage on a voyage:

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} a vessel that is 20 m or more in length;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} a towboat; and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} a passenger vessel that is engaged on a voyage any
    part of which is in Sea Area A1 or more than five nautical miles
    from shore on the sea coasts of Canada.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Safety certificate

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The master of a Safety Convention vessel must keep on
  board any safety certificate issued under subsection 7(1) of the
  [Vessel Safety Certificates
  Regulations](/eng/regulations/SOR-2021-135) that relates to radio
  installation requirements.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Inspection certificate --- Great Lakes
  Agreement

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} The master of a vessel that is required to be
  inspected under the Agreement between Canada and the United States of
  America for Promotion of Safety on the Great Lakes by Means of Radio,
  1973, must keep on board an inspection certificate issued by the
  Minister evidencing compliance with subsection 205(3) of these
  Regulations.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Issuance of radio inspection
  certificate

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} The Minister must issue a radio inspection
  certificate to a vessel if the radio installation meets the
  requirements of this Part.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Application --- Canadian vessels

- **[[241]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-241
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Sections 242 to 248 apply in respect
  of Canadian vessels that are required under Division 1 of this Part to
  be fitted with radio equipment.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Application --- foreign vessels

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Sections 243 to 245, paragraph 246(1)(b) and
  subsection 246(2) also apply in respect of foreign vessels that are
  required under Division 1 of this Part to be fitted with radio
  equipment.

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} Sections 249 to 254 apply in respect of any vessel

  Canadian vessel that is required under Division 1 of this Part to be

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Officers responsible

**[[242]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-242 .sectionLabel}** The master of a vessel
and the person in charge of the deck watch must ensure that the
requirements of this Division are met.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Communications of distress, urgency or
safety

**[[243]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-243 .sectionLabel}** A vessel must make
communications of distress, urgency or safety in accordance with the
requirements of articles 32 to 34 of Chapter VII of the Radio
Regulations that are published by the Secretary General of the
International Telecommunication Union.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Cancellation of distress signals and
alerts

**[[244]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-244 .sectionLabel}** A person who has
inadvertently transmitted a distress alert from a vessel or, after
transmitting an alert, determines that assistance is no longer required,
must immediately cancel that alert in accordance with the instructions
set out in the Appendix to IMO resolution A.814(19), Guidelines for the
Avoidance of False Distress Alerts.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Communication power

**[[245]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-245 .sectionLabel}** Except in a case of
distress, a radio installation must not radiate more power than the
power required to ensure clear communication.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Time

- **[[246]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-246 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A
  radio operator using a radio installation, must, when stating the time
  during voice communications on board a vessel, observe the time in the
  following manner:

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} if the vessel is engaged on an international
    voyage, observe coordinated universal time (UTC); and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} if the vessel is engaged on any other type of
    voyage, observe the local time of the area in which the vessel is
    navigating.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}24-hour system

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} A radio operator of a radio installation must, when
  stating the time, use the 24-hour system expressed by means of four
  figures from 00:01 to 24:00 followed by the time zone identifier.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Radio record

- **[[247]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-247 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A
  vessel must maintain a record in which the radio operator keeps the
  following information:

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the vessel's name and registration number, its port
    of registry, gross tonnage and length and any of its Global Maritime
    Distress and Safety System identities as defined in Regulation 2 of
    Chapter IV of SOLAS;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the period covered by the record;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} the time of each entry made in the radio record,
    stated in accordance with section 246;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} a summary of all radio communications, including
    the date, time, frequency used and details with respect to

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} distress and urgency communications,

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} safety communications respecting the vessel,

    - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} abnormal radio propagation conditions that may
      reduce the effectiveness of the radio installation, and

    - [(iv)]{.lawlabel} any other important service incident;

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} the names of the radio operators, the dates each
    operator is on board and the name of each certificate that each
    radio operator holds;

  - [(f)]{.lawlabel} the name of the radio operator designated for
    operating the radio equipment during emergencies as required under
    the [Marine Personnel Regulations](/eng/regulations/SOR-2007-115);

  - [(g)]{.lawlabel} the date and time a check, test or inspection
    required under this Part was carried out and the results obtained
    including, for each day that the vessel is at sea,

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} the operating condition of the radio equipment
      determined by normal communication or a test call, as well as the
      position of the vessel at the time the determination is made,

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} the assessment of the reserve source of energy,
      and

    - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} if any of the radio equipment is found not to
      be in operating condition, a notation that the master was
      informed;

  - [(h)]{.lawlabel} the time of an inadvertent transmission of a
    distress, urgency or safety communication and the time and method of
    its cancellation;

  - [(i)]{.lawlabel} the date, time and details of any significant
    maintenance carried out on the radio installation, including the
    name of the person or the company that performed the maintenance
    tasks; and

  - [(j)]{.lawlabel} any corrective action taken to remedy any
    deficiency in the radio equipment required under this Part.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Radio record entry

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The radio operator making an entry in a radio record
  must initial the entry.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Accessibility

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} The radio record must be kept for a period of not
  less than 12 months after the day on which the last entry is made, in
  a place accessible to a marine safety inspector referred to in section
  11 of the Act or a person, classification society or other
  organization authorized to carry out inspections under section 12 of
  the Act, and in the case of a paper record, it must be in its original
  format.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Continuous watch

- **[[248]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-248 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A
  vessel that is fitted with any of the following types of radio
  equipment must, while on a voyage, maintain a continuous watch on the
  frequencies specifically assigned for the transmission of maritime
  safety information that are appropriate to the time of day, the
  position of the vessel and the equipment carried:

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} a VHF radio installation;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} an MF radio installation;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} an MF/HF radio installation;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} a NAVTEX receiver or other means that is used to
    automatically receive maritime safety information for visual
    display;

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} enhanced group call equipment; and

  - [(f)]{.lawlabel} radio equipment capable of transmitting and
    receiving communications using NBDP.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}MF/HF radio installation

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} A vessel fitted with an MF/HF radio installation may
  keep the continuous watch by means of a scanning receiver.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Delayed radio transmission

**[[249]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-249 .sectionLabel}** If, at any time, a
radio transmission required to be made under these Regulations is
prohibited by any other law or may cause a fire or an explosion, the
transmission must be made as soon as it is permitted by that law and is
not likely to cause a fire or an explosion.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Radio operator

- **[[250]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-250 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  master of a vessel or a person authorized by the master to maintain a
  continuous listening watch or to make a navigation safety call
  required under this Division must be a radio operator.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Obligation to inform

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} If the person maintaining a continuous listening
  watch or making a navigation safety call is not the person in charge
  of the deck watch, they must, without delay, inform the person in
  charge of the deck watch of any information they receive or any
  navigation safety call they make that may affect the safe navigation
  of the vessel.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Steering and sailing rules

**[[251]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-251 .sectionLabel}** Nothing in this
Division may be construed as relieving a vessel of its obligation under
the provisions of the [Collision
Regulations](/eng/regulations/C.R.C.,_c._1416) to sound the appropriate
whistle signals or as permitting the vessel to carry out manoeuvres that
contravene the steering and sailing rules of those Regulations.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Continuous listening watch

- **[[252]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-252
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Every vessel that is required under
  section 204 to be fitted with a VHF radio installation must maintain a
  continuous listening watch during the period beginning 15 minutes
  before the vessel is underway and ending when the vessel is

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} securely anchored, moored or made fast to the shore
    or secured to the bottom; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} in a place where its presence does not constitute a
    hazard to passing vessels.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}VHF channel

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} A VHF radio installation, referred to in subsection
  (1), on board a vessel described in column 1 of Schedule 4 must be set
  to the appropriate VHF channel set out in column 2 of that Schedule
  for that vessel and must operate with sufficient gain to permit an
  effective continuous listening watch.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Interrupted watch

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} If a vessel is not fitted with an additional VHF
  radio installation, the continuous listening watch referred to in
  subsection (1) may be interrupted for short periods while the
  installation is being used to transmit or receive communications on
  another channel.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Navigation safety call

- **[[253]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-253
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Every vessel that is required under
  section 204 to have a VHF radio installation and that is a vessel
  described in column 1 of Schedule 4, other than a dredge or floating
  plant, must make a navigation safety call on the appropriate VHF
  channel set out in column 3 for that vessel, under the following
  circumstances:

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} a risk of collision with another vessel exists
    under the provisions of the [Collision
    Regulations](/eng/regulations/C.R.C.,_c._1416) that apply in the
    area being navigated by the vessel;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the navigation safety call of another vessel
    indicates that a close-quarters situation may develop;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} the vessel is in a narrow channel or fairway and is
    about to overtake another vessel, or be overtaken by another vessel;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} doubt exists as to the actions or the intentions of
    another vessel;

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} the vessel is nearing a bend in a river, channel or
    fairway or an obstruction around which an approaching vessel cannot
    adequately be seen;

  - [(f)]{.lawlabel} the vessel is approaching, in restricted
    visibility, a charted route, including a ferry route, or a
    concentration of vessels;

  - [(g)]{.lawlabel} the vessel will commence a manoeuvre that could
    impede the safe navigation of other vessels;

  - [(h)]{.lawlabel} the vessel is engaged in fishing with nets, lines,
    trawls, trolling lines or other apparatus, or is a vessel restricted
    in its ability to manoeuvre in or near a routing system and is being
    approached by another vessel, other than a vessel engaged in
    fishing;

  - [(i)]{.lawlabel} the vessel is approaching a dredge or floating
    plant in or near a river, channel or fairway;

  - [(j)]{.lawlabel} the vessel will depart from a berth, anchorage,
    mooring area, booming ground, dredge or floating plant; and

  - [(k)]{.lawlabel} under any other circumstance when a navigation
    safety call is necessary for the safe navigation of the vessel or
    any other vessel.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Call --- paragraph (1)(j)

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The navigation safety call required under the
  circumstances specified in paragraph (1)(j) must be made 15 minutes
  before and again immediately before departure from the berth,
  anchorage, mooring area, booming ground, dredge or floating plant.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Content of call

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} The navigation safety call must not exceed one minute
  in duration and must contain only information that is essential for
  safe navigation, including the following, in sequence:

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the identity of the vessel;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the location of the vessel;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} the intended course of action;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} the present course and speed of the vessel; and

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} whether the vessel is

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} engaged in a towing operation,

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} not under command,

    - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} restricted in its ability to manoeuvre,

    - [(iv)]{.lawlabel} engaged in fishing, other than trolling,

    - [(v)]{.lawlabel} severely restricted in its ability to deviate
      from the course it is following because of its draft in relation
      to the available depth of water,

    - [(vi)]{.lawlabel} engaged in pilotage duty,

    - [(vii)]{.lawlabel} at anchor, or

    - [(viii)]{.lawlabel} aground.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Exception

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} A vessel is not required to make a navigation safety
  call in the circumstances described in paragraph (1)(i) or (j) if the
  vessel

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} is a towboat and is manoeuvring in or around any
    berth, anchorage, mooring area, booming ground, dredge or floating
    plant and the vessel

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} manoeuvres in such a manner that it will not
      impede the safe navigation of other vessels, and

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} makes a navigation safety call 15 minutes before
      its final departure from that berth, anchorage, mooring area,
      booming ground, dredge or floating plant; or

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} is reporting to a traffic centre.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Call power --- Great Lakes Basin

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} The transmission power of a navigation safety call on
  Channel 13 within the Great Lakes Basin must not exceed 1 W except in
  the following cases when that power must not exceed 25 W:

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} in the case of an emergency;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the vessel being called to does not respond to a
    second call at a transmission power of 1 W or less; or

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} a broadcast in blind situations such as when the
    vessel is rounding a bend in a channel.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Definition of [[vessel restricted in
  its ability to manoeuvre]{.dfn}]{.DefinitionRef}

  [(6)]{.lawlabel} For the purposes of subsections (1) and (3), [[vessel
  restricted in its ability to manoeuvre]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} has the
  meaning assigned by the definition [[vessel restricted in her ability
  to manoeuvre]{.dfn}]{.DefinitionRef} in Rule 3(g) of Schedule 1 to the
  [Collision Regulations](/eng/regulations/C.R.C.,_c._1416).

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Dredges and floating plants

- **[[254]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-254
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Every dredge or floating plant that
  is required under section 204 to have a VHF radio installation and
  that is a vessel described in column 1 of Schedule 4 must make a
  navigation safety call on the appropriate VHF channel set out in
  column 3 for that vessel under the following circumstances:

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} when approached by another vessel not engaged in an
    operation related to the dredge or floating plant;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} when requested to do so by another vessel; and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} under any other circumstance in which it is
    necessary for the safety of the dredge or floating plant or the safe
    navigation of any other vessel.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Content of call

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} A navigation safety call referred to in
  subsection (1) must not exceed one minute in duration and must contain
  only information that is essential for safe navigation, including the
  following, in sequence:

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the identity of the dredge or floating plant;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} its location; and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} in the case of a dredge or floating plant that is
    on a river, channel or fairway, to the extent possible, whether any
    part of the river, channel or fairway is obstructed and, if so, the
    side on which the obstruction exists and the side on which another
    vessel may pass.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Exception

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} A dredge or floating plant is not required to make a
  navigation safety call in the circumstances described in paragraph
  (1)(a) if it is reporting to a traffic centre.

### [DIVISION 2]{.HLabel2}[Burlington Canal]{.HTitleText2} {#h-1263653 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Speed limit

**[[303]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-303 .sectionLabel}** It is prohibited for a
vessel to navigate in the Burlington Canal at a speed greater than

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} seven knots, if the vessel is 80 m or less in length;
  or

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} the lowest speed at which the vessel can be navigated
  safely, if the vessel is more than 80 m in length.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Passing prohibited

- **[[304]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-304 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A
  vessel heading towards the Burlington Canal must not, if it is within
  0.5 nautical miles of the canal, pass another vessel going in the same
  direction.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Exception

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Subsection (1) does not apply in respect of vessels
  that are less than 15 m in length.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Request to lift the bridge

**[[305]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-305 .sectionLabel}** If the person who has
the conduct of a vessel requires the lift bridge over the Burlington
Canal to be raised, the person must make a request to the bridgemaster
by radio communication or, if such communication is not possible, the
person must sound three long blasts on the whistle or horn.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Entry of vessel 15 m or more

- **[[306]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-306
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Subject to subsection (2), it is
  prohibited for a vessel that is 15 m or more in length to enter the
  Burlington Canal unless the signal light on the lift bridge shows
  green in the direction of the vessel.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Exception in case of emergency

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} In the case of an emergency, a vessel that is 15 m or
  more in length may enter the Burlington Canal while the signal light
  on the lift bridge does not show green in its direction, but it must
  moor at the north wall of the canal and must not proceed until the
  signal light shows green in its direction.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Entry of vessel less than 15 m

**[[307]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-307 .sectionLabel}** It is prohibited for a
vessel that is less than 15 m in length to enter the Burlington Canal
while the lift bridge is not raised or while a flashing blue light is
not shown in its direction, unless it waits at the side of the canal to
its starboard at a distance of 90 m or more from the lift bridge until
the bridge is raised or a flashing blue light is shown in its direction.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Prohibition --- under sail

**[[308]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-308 .sectionLabel}** It is prohibited for a
vessel to navigate under sail in the Burlington Canal.

### [DIVISION 3]{.HLabel2}[St. Clair River and Detroit River]{.HTitleText2} {#h-1263674 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Application

- **[[309]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-309 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} This
  Division, other than section 314, applies in respect of

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} all vessels in Canadian waters between Lake Erie
    and Lake Huron; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} all Canadian vessels in the waters of the United
    States between buoy "1" of the East Outer and West Outer Channels at
    the Lake Erie entrance to the Detroit River and Lake Huron Cut
    Lighted Buoy "11" and including the Rouge River and Short Cut Canal
    from Detroit Edison Cell Light "1" to the head of navigation.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Exception --- section 311

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Section 311 does not apply in respect of a vessel
  referred to in subsection (1) unless the vessel is required under Part
  2 to be fitted with a VHF radio installation or VHF radio telephone.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Exception --- sections 312 and 313

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} Sections 312 and 313 do not apply in respect of a
  vessel referred to in subsection (1) that is

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} a vessel engaged in icebreaking, search and rescue
    or servicing aids to navigation that is

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} a government vessel,

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} owned by and in the service of the government of
      the United States, or

    - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} in the exclusive possession of the government
      of the United States; or

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} a vessel engaged in river or harbour improvements,
    if it is operated in a safe and prudent manner and other vessels
    have been warned of those operations.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Application --- section 314

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} Section 314 applies in respect of a vessel referred
  to in subsection (1) that is

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} a power-driven vessel that is 55 m or more in
    length;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} a vessel that is 20 m or more in length propelled
    only by sails;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} a towboat; or

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} a floating plant.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Conflict

**[[310]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-310 .sectionLabel}** In the event of any
inconsistency between this Division and the laws of the United States,
the laws of the United States prevail to the extent of the inconsistency
in respect of a Canadian vessel while it is in United States waters.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Continuous listening watch

**[[311]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-311 .sectionLabel}** Every vessel must
maintain a continuous listening watch in accordance with Radio Aids to
Marine Navigation published by the Canadian Coast Guard and make traffic
reports to the Canadian Coast Guard Marine Communications and Traffic
Services Centre located in Sarnia, Ontario, under the circumstances set
out in that publication.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Detroit River restrictions

- **[[312]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-312 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} It
  is prohibited for a vessel in the Detroit River to

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} proceed upbound in the West Outer Channel;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} proceed downbound in the Amherstburg Channel east
    of Bois Blanc Island;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} proceed upbound in the Livingstone Channel west of
    Bois Blanc Island; and

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} overtake another vessel between Bar Point Pier
    Light "D33" and Fighting Island South Light if there is a risk that
    the vessel will meet a third vessel proceeding in the opposite
    direction while the overtaking is taking place.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Exception --- paragraph (1)(b)

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Despite paragraph (1)(b), the Assistant Commissioner
  may authorize a vessel to proceed downbound in the Amherstburg Channel
  east of Bois Blanc Island if the safety of navigation is not
  compromised.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Overtaking prohibited

**[[313]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-313 .sectionLabel}** It is prohibited for a
vessel to overtake another vessel

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} in the St. Clair River between St. Clair Flats Canal
  Light "2" and Russell Island Light "33", unless the vessel being
  overtaken is a towboat;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} in the Rouge River; and

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} in the Detroit River between the west end of Belle
  Isle and Peche Island Light, unless the vessel being overtaken

  - [(i)]{.lawlabel} is a towboat; or

  - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} has checked down its speed to await berth
    availability or to make the turn for Rouge River and has so advised
    the Canadian Coast Guard Marine Communications and Traffic Services
    Centre located in Sarnia, Ontario.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Area of one-way traffic

**[[314]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-314 .sectionLabel}** The waters between the
St. Clair/Black River Junction Light and Lake Huron Cut Lighted Buoy "1"
constitute an area of alternating one-way traffic where the following
rules apply

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} a vessel must not, in that area, overtake or meet
  another vessel or come about;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} a moored vessel must not get underway until it is
  able to proceed through those waters without passing or being passed
  by another vessel;

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} a downbound vessel that has reached Lake Huron Cut
  Light "7" has the right-of-way over an upbound vessel that has not yet
  reached the St. Clair/Black River Junction Light; and

- [(d)]{.lawlabel} an upbound vessel awaiting the transit of a downbound
  vessel must wait its turn below the St. Clair/Black River Junction
  Light.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Embark, disembark or exchange pilot

**[[315]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-315 .sectionLabel}** It is prohibited for a
vessel to embark, disembark or exchange a pilot between the St.
Clair/Black River Junction Light and Lake Huron Cut Lighted Buoy "1"
unless, because of the weather, it is unsafe to carry out that activity
at the normal pilotage ground above Lake Huron Cut Lighted Buoy "1".

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Navigation safety calls

**[[316]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-316 .sectionLabel}** Every vessel must, by
using navigation safety calls, communicate its intentions to any other
vessel in the vicinity and ensure that the movements of the vessels are
coordinated and there is an agreement between the vessels before
proceeding to overtake or meet the other vessel.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Anchorage rules

**[[317]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-317 .sectionLabel}** In the St. Clair and
Detroit Rivers, it is prohibited for a vessel to anchor in such a manner
that it could swing into the channel or across steering courses.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Floating plant activities

**[[318]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-318 .sectionLabel}** A floating plant may be
operated, anchored or moored for the purposes of engaging in dredging,
construction or wrecking only if the person having conduct of the
floating plant obtains an authorization from the Assistant Commissioner,
the District Commander, the Captain of the Port or the Windsor harbour
master having jurisdiction in waters in which the floating plant will
operate, anchor or moor, on such terms and conditions as are necessary
to ensure the safety of navigation.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Speed limits

**[[319]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-319 .sectionLabel}** Except when required
for the safety of the vessel or any other vessel, a vessel that is 20 m
or more in length must not proceed at a speed greater than

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} 10.4 knots between

  - [(i)]{.lawlabel} Fort Gratiot and St. Clair Flats Canal Light "2",
    and

  - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} Peche Island Light and Bar Point Pier Light "D33";

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} 3.5 knots in the Rouge River; and

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} 5 knots in the navigable channel south of Peche
  Island.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Towboat

- **[[320]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-320 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} It
  is prohibited for a towboat to drop or anchor its tows in such a
  manner that they could swing into a channel or across steering
  courses.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Prohibition against obstructing
  navigation

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} It is prohibited for a towboat engaged in arranging
  its tow to obstruct the navigation of other vessels.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Temporary instructions

**[[321]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-321 .sectionLabel}** Despite anything in
this Division, a vessel must comply with temporary instructions to
proceed in a certain manner or by a certain route, to anchor in a
certain place or not to proceed or anchor except as specified in lieu of
or in addition to any provisions of this Division if because of channel
obstructions, a casualty, the weather, ice conditions, water levels or
other unforeseen or temporary circumstances, compliance with this
Division would be impossible, impracticable or unsafe or would cause a
risk of pollution, and the instructions are

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} given by the Assistant Commissioner, in the case of
  Canadian waters, or the District Commander or the Captain of the Port,
  in the case of the waters of the United States; and

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} published in Notices to Mariners or a navigational
  warning.

## [SCHEDULE 5]{.scheduleLabel}[(Sections 301 and 302)]{.OriginatingRef}[Prohibited Waters]{.scheduleTitleText}  {#h-1263890 .scheduleLabel}
:::

- [1]{.lawlabel} Conception Bay --- Newfoundland

  Beginning at the lighted cable sign in Broad Cove, Newfoundland, in
  the position latitude 47°35′45″N., longitude 52°53′10″W., as shown on
  Canadian Hydrographic Service Chart 4566, edition dated November 23,
  1962; thence in a straight line to the position latitude 47°35′45″N.,
  longitude 52°53′37″W.; thence in a straight line to the position
  latitude 47°37′12″N., longitude 52°55′44″W.; thence in a straight line
  to the cable landing at Bell Island, Newfoundland, in position
  latitude 47°37′37″N., longitude 52°56′00″W.; thence in a straight line
  to the position latitude 47°37′37″N., longitude 52°55′16″W.; thence in
  a straight line to the position on the shore latitude 47°36′09″N.,
  longitude 52°53′06″W.; thence following the high water mark in a
  southwesterly direction to the point of beginning.

- [2]{.lawlabel} Random Sound --- Newfoundland

  Beginning at a point on the high-water mark at the easterly extremity
  of East Random Head, as shown on Canadian Hydrographic Service Charts
  4545 and 4546, editions dated May 6, 1983 and January 24, 1964,
  respectively; thence in a straight line to a point on the high-water
  mark at the easterly extremity of West Random Head; thence following
  the high-water mark in a westerly direction to a point on the shore at
  latitude 48°03′04″N., longitude 53°38′42″W.; thence in a straight line
  to a point on the high-water mark of Middle Cliff at latitude
  48°03′54″N., longitude 53°40′26″W.; thence following the high-water
  mark in a westerly and northerly direction to the easterly extremity
  of Red Point; thence in a straight line to a point on the high-water
  mark at the westerly extremity of Brown Mead; thence following the
  high-water mark in a southerly and easterly direction to the point of
  beginning.

- [3]{.lawlabel} Northumberland Strait --- Between New Brunswick and
  Prince Edward Island

  Beginning at a point on the high-water mark at Cape Bruin, New
  Brunswick, located at latitude 46°10′58″N., longitude 63°58′42″W., as
  shown on Canadian Hydrographic Service Chart 4406, edition dated July
  12, 1985; thence in a straight line to a point on the high-water mark
  at Fernwood, P.E.I., latitude 46°19′15″N., longitude 63°48′55″W.;
  thence following the high-water mark in a southerly and easterly
  direction to a point on the shore at latitude 46°18′30″N., longitude
  63°46′09″W.; thence in a straight line to a point on the high-water
  mark at Botsford, N.B., latitude 46°10′01″N., longitude 63°56′00″W.;
  thence following the high-water mark in a northwesterly direction to
  the point of beginning.

- [4]{.lawlabel} Welland Canal entrances --- Ontario

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} Lake Ontario: beginning at a point north of the
    Port Weller Entrance to the Welland Canal located at latitude
    43°14′40″N., longitude 79°13′40″W., as shown on Canadian
    Hydrographic Service Chart 2063, edition dated December 2, 1983;
    thence in a 090° direction for approximately 0.9 nautical miles to a
    position located at latitude 43°14′40″N., longitude 79°12′28″W.;
    thence in a 009° direction for approximately 2.6 nautical miles to a
    position located at latitude 43°17′16″N., longitude 79°11′50″W.;
    thence in a 270° direction for approximately 1.7 nautical miles to a
    position located at latitude 43°17′16″N., longitude 79°14′15″W.;
    thence in a 171° direction for approximately 2.6 nautical miles to
    the point of beginning; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} Lake Erie: beginning at a point south of the Port
    Colborne Entrance to the Welland Canal located at latitude
    42°51′55″N., longitude 79°15′53″W., as shown on Canadian
    Hydrographic Service Chart 2120, edition dated March 7, 1986; thence
    in a 110° direction for approximately 0.9 nautical miles to a
    position located at latitude 42°51′37″N., longitude 79°14′44″W.;
    thence in a 191° direction for approximately 2.6 nautical miles to a
    position located at latitude 42°49′3″N., longitude 79°15′24″W.;
    thence in a 290° direction for approximately 1.7 nautical miles to a
    position located at latitude 42°49′38″N., longitude 79°17′36″W.;
    thence in a 029° direction for approximately 2.6 nautical miles to
    the point of beginning.

- [5]{.lawlabel} Parry Bay --- British Columbia

  Beginning at a point on the high-water mark of Albert Head located at
  latitude 48°23′05″N., longitude 123°28′48″W., as shown on Canadian
  Hydrographic Service Chart 3440, edition dated March 11, 1983; thence
  in a straight line to a point at latitude 48°21′12″N., longitude
  123°30′54″W.; thence in a straight line to a point on the high-water
  mark at latitude 48°21′51″N., longitude 123°31′57″W.; thence in a
  northerly and easterly direction along the high-water mark of Parry
  Bay to the point of beginning.
