## [Interpretation]{.HTitleText1} {#h-855961 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Definitions

**[[1]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-1 .sectionLabel}** The following definitions
apply in these Regulations.

[[Act]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[Act]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means the [Aeronautics
    Act](/eng/acts/A-2). ([Loi]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

[[air traffic services]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[air traffic services]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means the issuance of
    air traffic control clearances or other aerospace control
    instructions or the provision of aeronautical safety information,
    including aviation weather information and serviceability reports in
    respect of aerodromes and radio navigation aids. ([services de
    circulation aérienne]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

[[owner]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[owner]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}, in respect of an aircraft, means the
    person who has legal custody and control of the
    aircraft. ([propriétaire]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

## [Report to Authority]{.HTitleText1} {#h-855967 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Mandatory reporting

- **[[2]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-2 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Any
  civilian who is an owner, pilot-in-command or crew member of an
  aircraft, has possession of an aircraft as lessee or otherwise, holds
  a Canadian aviation document with respect to an airport or heliport,
  is in charge of an airport or heliport, whether as employee, agent or
  representative of the holder of the Canadian aviation document, or
  provides air traffic services or maintenance services in respect of an
  aircraft or other aeronautical product must report to the Authority if
  they have direct knowledge of

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} any of the following accidents or incidents that is
    a military-civilian occurrence:

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} a person sustaining an injury that requires
      medical intervention, becoming ill or dying as a result of being
      on board the aircraft, coming into contact with any part of the
      aircraft or its contents, conducting maintenance on the aircraft
      or other aeronautical product or being directly exposed to the
      aircraft's jet or propeller blast or rotor down wash,

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} the aircraft sustaining damage, or experiencing
      a failure, that adversely affects the structural strength, flight
      characteristics or performance of the aircraft and that requires
      repair or replacement of any affected component,

    - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} the aircraft going missing or being
      inaccessible,

    - [(iv)]{.lawlabel} an engine failing or being shut down as a
      precautionary measure,

    - [(v)]{.lawlabel} a transmission gearbox malfunctioning,

    - [(vi)]{.lawlabel} smoke or fire that is not related to the normal
      operation of an aircraft or other aeronautical product being
      detected,

    - [(vii)]{.lawlabel} difficulties in controlling the aircraft as a
      result of any aircraft system malfunction, weather phenomena, wake
      turbulence, uncontrolled vibrations or operations outside the
      flight envelope,

    - [(viii)]{.lawlabel} the aircraft landing or taking off outside or
      partially outside the area intended for that purpose or landing
      with all or part of its landing gear retracted,

    - [(ix)]{.lawlabel} the aircraft dragging any of its parts,

    - [(x)]{.lawlabel} any crew member being unable to perform their
      duties as a result of a physical incapacitation or a malfunction
      of their life-support equipment,

    - [(xi)]{.lawlabel} a depressurization requiring an emergency
      descent, the activation of emergency systems or the use of
      emergency life-support equipment,

    - [(xii)]{.lawlabel} a fuel shortage requiring a diversion of the
      aircraft or approach and landing priority at the aircraft's
      destination,

    - [(xiii)]{.lawlabel} the aircraft being serviced with the incorrect
      type of fuel or lubricant or with contaminated fuel or lubricant,

    - [(xiv)]{.lawlabel} a collision, other than a collision associated
      with normal operating circumstances, occurring between aircraft or
      between an aircraft and another object or terrain,

    - [(xv)]{.lawlabel} an aircraft coming so close to being involved in
      a collision described in subparagraph (xiv) that the safety of any
      person, property or the environment is compromised,

    - [(xvi)]{.lawlabel} the distance separating two aircraft being less
      than the minimum established in the most recent version of the
      Canadian Domestic Air Traffic Control Separation Standards,
      published by the Department of Transport,

    - [(xvii)]{.lawlabel} a crew member declaring an emergency or
      indicating any situation that requires priority handling by an air
      traffic control unit or the standing by of emergency response
      services,

    - [(xviii)]{.lawlabel} an accident or incident involving any device
      that is installed on an aircraft for emergency activation or for
      deployment or deployment and recovery during flight, including
      ejection seats, bombs, rockets, missiles, cartridge activated
      devices, jammers and targets,

    - [(xix)]{.lawlabel} a slung load being released from the aircraft
      unintentionally or as a precautionary or emergency measure,

    - [(xx)]{.lawlabel} [[dangerous goods]{.dfn}]{.DefinitionRef}, as
      defined in section 2 of the [Transportation of Dangerous Goods
      Act, 1992](/eng/acts/T-19.01), being released inside or from the
      aircraft,

    - [(xxi)]{.lawlabel} a defect or deterioration of an aeronautical
      product that is not expected as a result of normal operations or
      maintenance being discovered,

    - [(xxii)]{.lawlabel} an aeronautical product sustaining damage
      during maintenance,

    - [(xxiii)]{.lawlabel} an unauthorized deviation from an approved
      maintenance program for an aeronautical product,

    - [(xxiv)]{.lawlabel} any object being found in an aircraft that is
      out of place and could result in damage to the aircraft with or
      without the intervention of a person, or

    - [(xxv)]{.lawlabel} a tool being lost during maintenance of an
      aeronautical product; or

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} an accident or incident referred to in one or more
    of subparagraphs (a)(i) to (xxv) that involves an aircraft or
    installation referred to in subparagraph (a)(i) of the definition
    [[military-civilian occurrence]{.dfn}]{.DefinitionRef} in subsection
    10(1) of the Act.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Notification --- situation or condition

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} A civilian referred to in subsection (1) must report
  to the Authority any situation or condition of which they have direct
  knowledge if the Authority has informed them in writing that the
  Authority has reasonable grounds to believe that the situation or
  condition could, if left unattended, induce an accident or incident
  referred to in any of subparagraphs (1)(a)(i) to (xxv).

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Exceptions

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} A civilian is not required to report an accident,
  incident, situation or condition to the Authority if

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the civilian has reported it to the Canadian
    Transportation Accident Investigation and Safety Board in accordance
    with subsection 2(1) of the [Transportation Safety Board
    Regulations](/eng/regulations/SOR-92-446); or

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the Authority has already received a report of it
    from another person.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Required contents

- **[[3]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-3 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} For the
  purposes of section 2, the civilian must report, as soon as feasible
  and by the quickest means available, all of the following information
  that is available to them at the time of the accident, incident,
  situation or condition:

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the aircraft's type, model, registration mark and
    country of registration;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the name of the aircraft's owner and
    pilot-in-command and of any person who has possession of the
    aircraft as lessee or otherwise;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} the aircraft's last point of departure, the date
    and time of departure and the intended destination;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} the date and time of the accident, incident,
    situation or condition;

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} the name of the person providing air traffic
    services in relation to the accident, incident, situation or
    condition;

  - [(f)]{.lawlabel} in the case of an accident or incident, the number
    of crew members, passengers and other persons that were involved and
    the number of those who died or sustained injuries that required
    medical intervention;

  - [(g)]{.lawlabel} the location of the accident, incident, situation
    or condition by reference to an easily defined geographical point or
    by latitude and longitude;

  - [(h)]{.lawlabel} a description of the accident, incident, situation
    or condition and the extent of any resulting damage to the
    environment and to the aircraft and any other property;

  - [(i)]{.lawlabel} a list of any [[dangerous
    goods]{.dfn}]{.DefinitionRef}, as defined in section 2 of the
    [Transportation of Dangerous Goods Act, 1992](/eng/acts/T-19.01),
    carried on board or released from the aircraft, including the
    shipping name or UN number and consignor and consignee information;

  - [(j)]{.lawlabel} if the aircraft is missing, the last known position
    of the aircraft by reference to an easily defined geographical point
    or by latitude and longitude, the date and time that the aircraft
    was at that position and the actions taken or planned to locate the
    aircraft;

  - [(k)]{.lawlabel} if the aircraft is inaccessible, the actions taken
    or planned to gain access to the aircraft;

  - [(l)]{.lawlabel} a description of any action taken or planned to
    protect persons, property and the environment; and

  - [(m)]{.lawlabel} the name and title of the person making the report
    and the telephone number and address at which they can be reached.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Remainder of information

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Any information referred to in subsection (1) that is
  not available to the civilian at the time of the accident, incident,
  situation or condition must be reported to the Authority as soon as it
  becomes available to them.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Voluntary reporting

- **[[4]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-4 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} If a
  civilian other than a civilian referred to in subsection 2(1) wishes
  to report any information concerning an accident or incident referred
  to in that subsection or a situation or condition referred to in
  subsection 2(2), they may report it to the Authority by any means.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Identity protected

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The identity of a civilian who reports information
  under subsection (1) is protected unless the civilian waives that
  protection in writing.

## [Investigations by the Authority]{.HTitleText1} {#h-856027 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Preservation of evidence

- **[[5]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-5 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Every
  person that has the possession or control of evidence relating to a
  military-civilian occurrence must preserve that evidence until the
  Authority determines that the evidence is no longer required.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Exception --- safety measures

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Subsection (1) is not to be construed as preventing
  any person from taking necessary measures to ensure the safety of any
  person or the protection of property or the environment.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Record of evidence

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} Any person who takes the measures referred to in
  subsection (2) must, to the extent possible in the circumstances and
  before taking those measures, record the evidence by the best means
  available and, as soon as feasible, advise the Authority of their
  actions.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Exemption

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} The Authority may exempt any person from the
  requirement to preserve evidence if another person has already
  preserved that evidence.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Observers

- **[[6]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-6 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} When the
  Authority is conducting an investigation of a military-civilian
  occurrence, the Authority may authorize an observer referred to in
  subsection 17(2) of the Act to do, under the supervision of an
  investigator, any or all of the following:

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} attend at the location of the occurrence;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} examine the aircraft involved in the occurrence and
    the component parts and contents of the aircraft;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} to the extent permitted by the Act, examine any
    document or other information that is relevant and that relates to

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} the activity during which the occurrence took
      place,

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} the crew members involved in the occurrence, and

    - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} the aircraft and its component parts and
      contents; and

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} attend during the performance of laboratory tests
    or analyses.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Communications and use of information

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} An observer may communicate or use, or permit to be
  communicated or used, information that they have obtained during the
  investigation only if the communication or use of that information is
  permitted under the Act and the Authority authorizes the communication
  or use in the interests of aviation safety.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Witness fees and expenses

**[[7]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-7 .sectionLabel}** A person who is required to
attend before an investigator under paragraph 14(10)(a) of the Act is to
be paid witness fees in the amounts set out in section 3 of Tariff A of
the [Federal Courts Rules](/eng/regulations/SOR-98-106) and travel and
living expenses determined in accordance with the rates and allowances
set out in the National Joint Council Travel Directive that are in
effect at the time of the attendance.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Test under subsection 14(6) of the Act

**[[8]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-8 .sectionLabel}** A person who is invited to
be present at a test to destruction conducted under subsection 14(6) of
the Act may

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} be represented by a person having technical knowledge
  and expertise in the subject matter of the test; and

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} record or cause to be recorded the condition of the
  thing being tested prior to, during and after the test.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Warrant

- **[[9]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-9 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A
  warrant issued under subsection 14(4) of the Act must be in the form
  set out in

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} Form 1 of the schedule, in the case of a warrant
    requested by an investigator who appears personally; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} Form 2 of the schedule, in the case of a warrant
    obtained by a means of telecommunication.

  >   [(14)]{.lawlabel} In any proceeding in which it is material for a
  >   court to be satisfied that a search or seizure was authorized by a
  >   warrant issued by telephone or other means of telecommunication,
  >   the absence of the information or warrant, signed by the justice
  >   of the peace and carrying on its face a notation of the time, date
  >   and location of issuance, is, in the absence of evidence to the
  >   contrary, proof that the search or seizure was not authorized by a
  >   warrant issued by telephone or other means of telecommunication.
  >
  > - [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Duplicates and facsimiles --
  >   probative force
  >
  >   [(15)]{.lawlabel} A duplicate or a facsimile of information or of
  >   a warrant has the same probative force as the original for the
  >   purposes of subsection (14).
  > :::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Notice --- production of information or
attendance

- **[[10]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-10 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A
  notice under paragraph 14(10)(a) of the Act, requiring the production
  of information or attendance before the investigator, must be in the
  form set out in Form 3 of the schedule.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Notice --- medical examination

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} A notice under paragraph 14(10)(b) of the Act,
  requiring a person directly or indirectly involved in the operation of
  an aircraft to submit to a medical examination, must be in the form
  set out in Form 4 of the schedule.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Notice --- information concerning a
  patient

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} A notice under paragraph 14(10)(c) of the Act,
  requiring a physician or other health practitioner to provide
  information concerning a patient, must be in the form set out in Form
  5 of the schedule.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Notice --- autopsy or other medical
  examination

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} A notice under paragraph 14(10)(d) of the Act,
  requiring the person who has custody of the body of a deceased person
  or other human remains to permit the performance of an autopsy on the
  body or other medical examination on the remains, must be in the form
  set out in Form 6 of the schedule.

## [Investigation File]{.HTitleText1} {#h-856120 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Creation of investigation file

- **[[11]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-11 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} If the
  Authority conducts an investigation into a military-civilian
  occurrence, the Authority must create and maintain a file relating to
  the investigation.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Required contents

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The file must contain all the information that is
  relevant to the military-civilian occurrence and records of any
  representations made under subsection 18(2) of the Act.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Period of retention

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} The Authority must keep the file for

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} in the case of an investigation that is completed
    by the Authority, not less than 20 years after the date of the
    military-civilian occurrence; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} in the case of an investigation that is completed
    by a person who is authorized to complete investigations on behalf
    of the Authority, not less than five years after the date of the
    military-civilian occurrence.
