### [Interpretation]{.HTitleText2} {#h-927282 .Subheading}

- **[[4]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-4 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} These
  rules shall be liberally construed to secure the just, most
  expeditious and least expensive determination of every proceeding on
  its merits.

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} Where matters are not provided for in these rules,
  the practice shall be determined by the Court, either on a motion for
  directions or after the event if no such motion has been made.

::: HistoricalNote
- SOR/2004-100, s. 3(F)
:::

### [Forms]{.HTitleText2} {#h-927288 .Subheading}

**[[5]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-5 .sectionLabel}** The forms in Schedule I
shall be used where applicable and with such variations as the
circumstances require.

## [Associate Judges]{.HTitleText1} {#h-1418693 .Part}

- **[[6.1]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-6.1 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} An
  associate judge of the Court has all the powers of a judge of the
  Court under these Rules.

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} Any associate judge may sit and act at any time and
  at any place in Canada for the transaction of the business of the
  Court, and, where the associate judge so sits or acts, that associate
  judge constitutes the Court.

## [Non-compliance with the Rules]{.HTitleText1} {#h-927294 .Part}

### [Effect of Non-compliance]{.HTitleText2} {#h-927295 .Subheading}

**[[7]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-7 .sectionLabel}** A failure to comply with
these rules is an irregularity and does not render a proceeding or a
step, document or direction in a proceeding a nullity, and the Court,

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} may grant all necessary amendments or other relief,
  on such terms as are just, to secure the just determination of the
  real matters in dispute, or

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} only where and as necessary in the interests of
  justice, may set aside the proceeding or a step, document or direction
  in the proceeding in whole or in part.

### [Attacking Irregularity]{.HTitleText2} {#h-927299 .Subheading}

**[[8]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-8 .sectionLabel}** A motion to attack a
proceeding or a step, document or direction in a proceeding for
irregularity shall not be made,

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} after the expiry of a reasonable time after the
  moving party knows or ought reasonably to have known of the
  irregularity, or

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} if the moving party has taken any further step in the
  proceeding after obtaining knowledge of the irregularity,

except with leave of the Court.

### [Court may Dispense with Compliance]{.HTitleText2} {#h-927304 .Subheading}

**[[9]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-9 .sectionLabel}** The Court may, where and as
necessary in the interests of justice, dispense with compliance with any
rule at any time.

::: HistoricalNote
- SOR/2004-100, s. 4
:::

### [Remedying Default]{.HTitleText2} {#h-927308 .Subheading}

**[[10]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-10 .sectionLabel}** When a notice of motion
for a direction under section 91 or section 110 has been filed and
served, the person or party against whom the motion is made may not,
without the consent of the other party or leave of the Court, remedy any
default on the basis of which relief is sought by such proposed motion.

## [Time]{.HTitleText1} {#h-927310 .Part}

### [Computation]{.HTitleText2} {#h-927311 .Subheading}

**[[11]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-11 .sectionLabel}** In the computation of
time under these rules or a direction, except where a contrary intention
appears,

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} where the time limited for the doing of a thing
  expires or falls on a holiday or a Saturday, the thing may be done on
  the day next following that is not a holiday or Saturday, and

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} the period beginning on December 21 in any year and
  ending on January 7 of the next year shall be excluded.

### [Extension or Abridgement]{.HTitleText2} {#h-927315 .Subheading}

- **[[12]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-12 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  Court may extend or abridge any time prescribed by these rules or a
  direction, on such terms as are just.

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} A motion for a direction extending time may be made
  before or after the expiration of the time prescribed.

- [(3)]{.lawlabel} A time prescribed by these rules for filing, serving
  or delivering a document may be extended or abridged by consent in
  writing.

### [Hearing in Absence of Opposite Party]{.HTitleText2} {#h-927320 .Subheading}

**[[13]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-13 .sectionLabel}** No motion, examination,
taxation of costs or other matter except a motion made without notice,
shall proceed before a judge or an officer in the absence of the
opposite party until thirty minutes after the time fixed for it.

### [Court Office Hours]{.HTitleText2} {#h-927322 .Subheading}

**[[14]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-14 .sectionLabel}** Unless otherwise directed
by the Chief Justice, every office of the Court shall be open for the
transaction of business each day except holidays and Saturdays, from 9
in the forenoon until 5 o'clock in the afternoon, and at such other
times as the Court may, for special reason, direct.

::: HistoricalNote
- SOR/2004-100, s. 44(E)
:::

## [Court Documents]{.HTitleText1} {#h-927326 .Part}

### [Format]{.HTitleText2} {#h-927327 .Subheading}

**[[15]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-15 .sectionLabel}** A document prepared for
use in a proceeding shall be of good quality paper, 215 mm in width and
not exceeding 279 mm in length and the text shall be printed,
typewritten, written or reproduced legibly on one side only, with double
spaces between the lines and a margin of approximately 40 mm on the
left-hand side.

### [Photocopies of Court Documents]{.HTitleText2} {#h-927331 .Subheading}

**[[16]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-16 .sectionLabel}** Subject to any order that
the Court, in special circumstances, may make restricting access to a
particular file by persons other than the parties to a matter before the
Court, any person may, subject to appropriate supervision, and when the
facilities of the Court permit without interfering with the ordinary
work of the Court,

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} inspect any Court file relating to a matter before
  the Court; and

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} on payment of \$0.40 per page, obtain a photocopy of
  any document on a Court file.

### [Confidentiality Order]{.HTitleText2} {#h-927337 .Subheading}

- **[[16.1]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-16.1 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} On
  motion, the Court may order that a document or part of a document
  shall be treated as confidential at the time of filing of the document
  or part of the document and determines the conditions in relation to
  its reproduction, destruction and non-disclosure.

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} Where the Court makes an order pursuant to
  subsection (1), a party or solicitor of record may have access to the
  confidential document or part of the confidential document only on
  conditions determined by the Court in relation to its reproduction,
  destruction and non-disclosure.

- [(3)]{.lawlabel} The order remains in effect until the Court orders
  otherwise.

::: HistoricalNote
- SOR/2007-142, s. 3
:::

### [Notice to be in Writing]{.HTitleText2} {#h-927344 .Subheading}

**[[17]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-17 .sectionLabel}** Where these rules require
notice to be given, it shall be given in writing.

### [Filing of Documents]{.HTitleText2} {#h-927346 .Subheading}

- **[[18]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-18 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Except
  as otherwise provided in these rules and unless otherwise directed by
  the Court, a document other than a notice of appeal may be filed using
  one of the following methods:

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} depositing it with the Registry;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} sending it by mail to the Registry; or

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} sending it by fax or by electronic filing to the
    Registry.

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} Except as otherwise provided in these rules and
  unless otherwise directed by the Court, the date of filing of a
  document is deemed to be

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} in the case of a document filed with the Registry
    or sent by mail or by fax, the date shown by the date received stamp
    placed on the document by the Registry at the time of filing; or

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} in the case of a document filed by electronic
    filing, the date shown on the acknowledgment of receipt issued by
    the Court.

- [(3)]{.lawlabel} Except as otherwise provided in these rules and
  unless otherwise directed by the Court, where a document is filed by
  electronic filing, the copy of the document that is printed by the
  Registry and placed in the Court file is deemed to be the original
  version of the document.

- [(4)]{.lawlabel} A party who files a document by electronic filing
  shall, if required by these rules or at the request of a party or the
  Court, provide a paper copy of the document and file it with the
  Registry.

- [(5)]{.lawlabel} Where the Registry has no record of the receipt of a
  document, it is deemed not to have been filed, unless the Court
  directs otherwise.

- SOR/2007-142, s. 4
- SOR/2008-303, s. 3

### [Affidavits]{.HTitleText2} {#h-927360 .Subheading}

- **[[19]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-19 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} An
  affidavit used in a proceeding shall be in Form 19.

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} An affidavit shall be confined to a statement of
  facts within the personal knowledge of the deponent or to other
  evidence that the deponent could give if testifying as a witness in
  Court, except where these rules provide otherwise.

- [(3)]{.lawlabel} Where there is a reference in an affidavit to an
  exhibit, that reference shall be made by words identifying the
  exhibit, as, for example, "Exhibit A to this my affidavit".

- [(4)]{.lawlabel} Where there is a reference in an affidavit to an
  exhibit, there shall be endorsed on or attached to the exhibit a
  certificate identifying the exhibit and signed by the person before
  whom the affidavit is sworn or affirmed, as, for example, "This is
  Exhibit A to the affidavit of ![blank
  line](/img/ii_spacer.gif){.Leader}, sworn before me the ![blank
  line](/img/ii_spacer.gif){.Leader} day of ![blank
  line](/img/ii_spacer.gif){.Leader} 20![blank
  line](/img/ii_spacer.gif){.Leader}".

- [(5)]{.lawlabel} Where an exhibit is referred to in an affidavit and
  the exhibit,

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} is referred to as being attached to the affidavit,
    it shall be attached to and files with the affidavit,

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} is referred to as being produced and shown to the
    deponent, it shall not be attached to the affidavit or filed with
    it, but shall be left with the Registrar for the use of the Court,
    and on the disposition of the matter in respect of which the
    affidavit was filed, the exhibit shall be returned to the counsel or
    party who filed the affidavit, unless the Court directs otherwise,
    and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} is a document, a copy shall be served with the
    affidavit, unless it is not practical to do so.

- [(6)]{.lawlabel} Where it appears to a person taking an affidavit that
  the deponent is illiterate or blind, the person shall certify in the
  jurat that the affidavit was read in his or her presence to the
  deponent, that the deponent appeared to understand it, and that the
  deponent signed the affidavit or placed his or her mark on it in the
  presence of the person taking the affidavit.

- [(7)]{.lawlabel} Where it appears to a person taking an affidavit that

## [Joinder, Consolidation, Intervention and Transfer or Transmission of Interest]{.HTitleText1} {#h-927404 .Part}

### [Joinder of Claims]{.HTitleText2} {#h-927405 .Subheading}

**[[25]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-25 .sectionLabel}** A party may join in a
notice of appeal all assessments under appeal unless otherwise directed.

### [When Proceedings May be Consolidated]{.HTitleText2} {#h-927407 .Subheading}

**[[26]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-26 .sectionLabel}** Where two or more
proceedings are pending in the Court and

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} they have in common a question of law or fact or
  mixed law and fact arising out of one and the same transaction or
  occurrence or series of transactions or occurrences, or

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} for any other reason, a direction ought to be made
  under this section,

the Court may direct that,

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} the proceedings be consolidated or heard at the same
  time or one immediately after the other, or

- [(d)]{.lawlabel} any of the proceedings be stayed until the
  determination of any other of them.

### [Discretion of Presiding Judge]{.HTitleText2} {#h-927414 .Subheading}

**[[27]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-27 .sectionLabel}** Where a direction has
been made that proceedings be heard either at the same time or one
immediately after the other, the judge presiding at the hearing
nevertheless has the discretion to direct otherwise.

### [Leave to Intervene]{.HTitleText2} {#h-927416 .Subheading}

- **[[28]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-28 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Where
  it is claimed by a person who is not a party to a proceeding

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} that such person has an interest in the subject
    matter of the proceeding,

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} that such person may be adversely affected by a
    judgment in the proceeding, or

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} that there exists between such person and any one
    or more parties to the proceeding a question of law or fact or mixed
    law and fact in common with one or more of the questions in issue in
    the proceeding,

  such person may move for leave to intervene.

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} On the motion, the Court shall consider whether the
  intervention will unduly delay or prejudice the determination of the
  rights of the parties to the proceeding, and the Court may,

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} allow the person to intervene as a friend of the
    Court and without being a party to the proceeding, for the purpose
    of rendering assistance to the Court by way of evidence or argument,
    and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} give such direction for pleadings, discovery or
    costs as is just.

### [Transfer or Transmission of Interest]{.HTitleText2} {#h-927426 .Subheading}

- **[[29]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-29 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Where
  at any stage of a proceeding the interest or liability of a person who
  is a party to a proceeding in the Court is transferred or transmitted
  to another person by assignment, bankruptcy, death or other means, no
  other proceedings shall be instituted until the Registrar is notified
  of the transfer or transmission and the particulars of it.

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} On receipt of the notice and particulars referred to
  in subsection (1) the Registrar shall consult with the parties
  regarding the circumstances under which the proceeding shall continue
  and he shall report on these consultations to the Chief Justice.

- [(3)]{.lawlabel} The Chief Justice or a judge designated by him to
  deal with the matter may direct the continuation of the proceeding or
  give such other direction as is just.

### [Counsel of Record]{.HTitleText2} {#h-927445 .Subheading}

- **[[31]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-31
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Subject to the other provisions of
  this section where a party has taken any step in a proceeding by a
  document signed by counsel, that person shall be the counsel of record
  for that party until a change is effected in a manner provided for by
  this section.

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} A counsel of record shall act as and remain the

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the client delivers a notice under section 32,

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} such counsel has served a notice of intention to
    cease to act as counsel and the provisions of subsection 33(1) have
    been complied with, or

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} a direction removing the counsel from the record
    has been entered, served on the client and every other party and
    filed with proof of service.

### [Change in Representation by a Party]{.HTitleText2} {#h-927452 .Subheading}

- **[[32]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-32 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A
  party who has a counsel of record may change the counsel of record by
  serving on the counsel and every other party and filing, with proof of
  service, a notice giving the name, address for service and telephone
  number of the new counsel.

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} A party acting in person may appoint a counsel of
  record by serving on every other party and filing, with proof of
  service, a notice giving the name, address for service and telephone
  number of the counsel of record.

- [(3)]{.lawlabel} A party who has a counsel of record may elect to act
  in person by serving on the counsel and every other party and filing
  with proof of service, a notice of intention to act in person giving
  the party's address for service and telephone number.

### [Removal of Counsel of Record --- By Notice]{.HTitleText2} {#h-927457 .Subheading}

- **[[33]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-33 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A
  counsel of record may at any time before

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} a joint application has been made to fix the time
    and place of hearing, or

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} a proceeding has been listed for hearing,

  whichever is earlier, serve on a party who is the client and upon all
  other parties, a written notice of intention to cease to act as
  counsel of record which notice shall state the last known address of
  the client.

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} Service of the notice shall be made on the client
  personally or by mailing a copy to the last known address of the
  client.

- [(3)]{.lawlabel} Upon filing the notice with proof of service, and
  upon the expiry of ten days after service upon the client, the counsel
  shall cease to be counsel of record and his or her address shall cease
  to be the address for service of the client.

- [(4)]{.lawlabel} The address for service of the client shall
  thereafter be the address contained in the notice, until the client
  has filed a document that sets out another address for service.

### [Removal of Counsel of Record --- By Application]{.HTitleText2} {#h-927466 .Subheading}

- **[[34]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-34 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} At any
  time after,

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} a joint application has been made to fix the time
    and place of hearing, or

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} a proceeding has been listed for hearing,

  whichever is earlier, a counsel may move, on notice to his or her
  client, for a direction to remove him or her as counsel of record.

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} A notice of motion for the removal of a counsel from
  the record and a direction under subsection (1) shall be served on the
  client personally or by sending a copy by mail to the last known
  address of the client.

- [(3)]{.lawlabel} The direction to remove a counsel from the record
  shall set out the last known address of the client.

- [(4)]{.lawlabel} The address for service of the client shall
  thereafter be the address contained in the direction, until the client
  has filed a document that sets out another address for service.

    in control or management of the place of business, and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} in any other case in such manner as the Court may,
    on a motion without notice, direct.

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} A person effecting personal service of a document
  need not produce the original document or have it in his or her
  possession.

- [(3)]{.lawlabel} The written admission or acceptance of service of
  counsel of record is sufficient proof of service and need not be

### [Rules of Pleadings --- Applicable to Answer]{.HTitleText2} {#h-927589 .Subheading}

### [Striking out a Pleading or other Document]{.HTitleText2} {#h-927612 .Subheading}

- **[[53]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-53 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  Court may, on its own initiative or on application by a party, strike
  out or expunge all or part of a pleading or other document with or
  without leave to amend, on the ground that the pleading or other
  document

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} may prejudice or delay the fair hearing of the
    appeal;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} is scandalous, frivolous or vexatious;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} is an abuse of the process of the Court; or

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} discloses no reasonable grounds for appeal or
    opposing the appeal.

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} No evidence is admissible on an application under
  paragraph (1)(d).

- [(3)]{.lawlabel} On application by the respondent, the Court may quash
  an appeal if

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the Court has no jurisdiction over the subject
    matter of the appeal;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} a condition precedent to instituting an appeal has
    not been met; or

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} the appellant is without legal capacity to commence
    or continue the proceeding.

- **[[67]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-67 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The

  material to be used at the hearing of the motion shall be served on
  any person or party who will be affected by the direction sought.

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} Where the nature of the motion or the circumstances
  render service of the notice of motion impractical or unnecessary, the
  Court may grant judgment without notice.

- [(3)]{.lawlabel} Where the delay necessary to effect service might

  be used at the hearing of the motion shall be filed and served at
  least seven days before the date on which the motion is to be heard.

- [(7)]{.lawlabel} Proof of service of the notice of motion shall be

  motion is to be heard.

## [Inspection of Property]{.HTitleText1} {#h-927737 .Part}

### [Direction for Inspection]{.HTitleText2} {#h-927738 .Subheading}

- **[[77]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-77 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The

  property where it appears to be necessary for the proper determination
  of an issue in a proceeding.

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} For the purpose of the inspection, the Court may,

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} authorize entry on or into and the taking of

    notice, might entail serious consequences to the moving party, or

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the Court dispenses with service of notice for any
    other sufficient reason.

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} a corporation is affiliated with another

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} both corporations are subsidiaries of the same

    - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} both corporations are controlled directly or

in compliance with section 81 or 82 shall enumerate the documents in a

in the case of bundles of documents of the same nature, each bundle
shall be described sufficiently to enable it to be identified.

### [Inspection]{.HTitleText2} {#h-927801 .Subheading}

- **[[85]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-85 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A
  party who has delivered a list of documents to any other party shall
  allow the other party to inspect and copy the documents listed, except
  those which he objects to produce, and when he delivers the list he
  shall also deliver a notice stating a place where the documents may be
  inspected and copied during normal business hours.

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} Where a party is entitled to inspect the documents to
  which reference is made in the list of documents, the other party
  shall, on request and on payment in advance of the cost of
  reproduction and delivery, deliver copies of any of the documents.

- [(3)]{.lawlabel} All documents listed in a party's list of documents
  under section 81 or under section 82 and that are not privileged, and
  all documents previously produced for inspection by the party shall,
  without notice, subpoena or direction, be taken to and produced at,

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the examination for discovery of the party or a
    person on behalf of, in place of, or in addition to the party, and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the hearing of the appeal,

  unless the parties otherwise agree.

### [Document in Possession of Non-Party]{.HTitleText2} {#h-927809 .Subheading}

- **[[86]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-86 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} When a
  document is in the possession of a person not a party to the appeal
  and the production of such document at a hearing might be compelled,
  the Court may at the instance of any party, on notice to such person,
  direct the production of a certified copy which may be used for all
  purposes in lieu of the original.

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} Where an application under subsection (1) is in
  respect of a document in the possession of the Crown, the notice to
  the Crown shall be directed to, and served on, the Deputy Attorney
  General of Canada.

### [List Incomplete]{.HTitleText2} {#h-927813 .Subheading}

**[[87]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-87 .sectionLabel}** Where, after the list of
documents has been served under either section 81 or section 82, it
comes to the attention of the party serving it that the list has for any
reason become inaccurate or incomplete, that party shall serve forthwith
a supplementary list specifying the inaccuracy or describing the
document.

### [Where Affidavit Incomplete or Privilege Improperly Claimed]{.HTitleText2} {#h-927815 .Subheading}

**[[88]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-88 .sectionLabel}** Where the Court is
satisfied by any evidence that a relevant document in a party's
possession, control or power may have been omitted from the party's
affidavit of documents, or that a claim of privilege may have been
improperly made, the Court may,

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} order cross-examination on the affidavit of
  documents,

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} order service of a further and better affidavit of
  documents,

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} order the disclosure or production for inspection of
  the document or a part of the document, if it is not privileged, and

- [(d)]{.lawlabel} inspect the document for the purpose of determining
  its relevance or the validity of a claim of privilege.

### [Use at Hearing]{.HTitleText2} {#h-927821 .Subheading}

- **[[89]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-89 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Unless
  the Court otherwise directs, except with the consent in writing of the
  other party or where discovery of documents has been waived by the
  other party, no document shall be used in evidence by a party unless

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} reference to it appears in the pleadings, or in a
    list or an affidavit filed and served by a party to the proceeding,

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} it has been produced by one of the parties, or some
    person being examined on behalf of one of the parties, at the
    examination for discovery, or

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} it has been produced by a witness who is not, in
    the opinion of the Court, under the control of the party.

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} Unless the Court otherwise directs, subsection (1)
  does not apply to a document that is used solely as a foundation for
  or as part of a question in cross-examination or re-examination.

### [Disclosure or Production not Admission of Relevance]{.HTitleText2} {#h-927830 .Subheading}

**[[90]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-90 .sectionLabel}** The disclosure or
production of a document for inspection shall not be taken as an
admission of its relevance or admissibility.

### [Effect of Failure to Disclose or Produce for Inspection]{.HTitleText2} {#h-927832 .Subheading}

**[[91]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-91 .sectionLabel}** Where a person or party
who is required to make discovery of documents under sections 78 to 91
fails or refuses without reasonable excuse to make a list or affidavit
of documents or to disclose a document in a list or affidavit of
documents or to produce a document for inspection and copying, or to
comply with a judgment of the Court in relation to the production or
inspection of documents, the Court may,

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} direct or permit the person or party to make a list
  or affidavit of documents, or a further list or affidavit of
  documents,

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} direct the person or party to produce a document for
  inspection and copying,

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} except where the failure or refusal is by a person
  who is not a party, dismiss the appeal or allow the appeal as the case
  may be,

- [(d)]{.lawlabel} direct any party or any other person to pay
  personally and forthwith the costs of the motion, any costs thrown
  away and the costs of any continuation of the discovery necessitated
  by the failure to disclose or produce, and

- [(e)]{.lawlabel} give such other direction as is just.

## [Examination for Discovery]{.HTitleText1} {#h-927839 .Part}

### [General]{.HTitleText2} {#h-927840 .Subheading}

**[[92]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-92 .sectionLabel}** An examination for
discovery may take the form of an oral examination or, at the option of
the examining party, an examination by written questions and answers,
but the examining party is not entitled to subject a person to both
forms of examination except with leave of the Court.

### [Who May be Examined]{.HTitleText2} {#h-927842 .Subheading}

- **[[93]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-93 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A
  party to a proceeding may examine for discovery an adverse party once,
  and may examine that party more than once only with leave of the
  Court.

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} A party to be examined, other than an individual or
  the Crown, shall select a knowledgeable current or former officer,
  director, member or employee, to be examined on behalf of that party,
  but, if the examining party is not satisfied with that person, the
  examining party may apply to the Court to name some other person.

- [(3)]{.lawlabel} The Crown, when it is the party to be examined, shall
  select a knowledgeable current or former officer, servant or employee,
  nominated by the Deputy Attorney General of Canada, to be examined on
  behalf of that party, but, if the examining party is not satisfied
  with that person, the examining party may apply to the Court to name
  some other person.

- [(4)]{.lawlabel} If a current or former officer, director or employee
  of a corporation or of the Crown has been examined, no other person
  may be examined without leave of the Court.

- [(5)]{.lawlabel} Where an appeal is brought by a party under a legal
  disability,

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the representative for that party may be examined
    in place of that party; or

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} at the option of the examining party, the party
    under a legal disability may be examined if that party is competent
    to give evidence.

  If the representative referred to in paragraph (a) is a public
  official, that person may be examined only with leave of the Court.

- [(6)]{.lawlabel} Where an appeal is brought by an assignee, the
  assignor may be examined in addition to the assignee.

- [(7)]{.lawlabel} Where an appeal is brought by a trustee of the estate
  of a bankrupt, the bankrupt may be examined in addition to the
  trustee.

- [(8)]{.lawlabel} Where a party is entitled to examine for discovery,

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} more than one person under this section, or

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} multiple parties who are in the same interest,

  but the Court is satisfied that multiple examinations would be
  oppressive, vexatious or unnecessary, the Court may impose such limits
  on the right of discovery as are just.

### [Scope of Examination]{.HTitleText2} {#h-927867 .Subheading}

- **[[95]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-95 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A
  person examined for discovery shall answer, to the best of that
  person's knowledge, information and belief, any proper question
  relevant to any matter in issue in the proceeding or to any matter
  made discoverable by subsection (3) and no question may be objected to
  on the ground that

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the information sought is evidence or hearsay,

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the question constitutes cross-examination, unless
    the question is directed solely to the credibility of the witness,
    or

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} the question constitutes cross-examination on the
    affidavit of documents of the party being examined.

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} Prior to the examination for discovery, the person to
  be examined shall make all reasonable inquiries regarding the matters
  in issue from all of the party's officers, servants, agents and
  employees, past or present, either within or outside Canada and, if
  necessary, the person being examined for discovery may be required to
  become better informed and for that purpose the examination may be
  adjourned.

- [(3)]{.lawlabel} [\[Repealed, SOR/2014-26, s. 10\]]{.Repealed}

- [(4)]{.lawlabel} A party may on an examination for discovery obtain
  disclosure of the names and addresses of persons who might reasonably
  be expected to have knowledge of transactions or occurrences in issue
  in the proceeding, unless the Court orders otherwise.

### [Effect of Refusal]{.HTitleText2} {#h-927878 .Subheading}

- **[[96]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-96 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Where
  a party, or a person examined for discovery on behalf or in place of a
  party, has refused to answer a proper question or to answer a question
  on the ground of privilege, and has failed to furnish the information
  in writing not later than ten days after the proceeding is set down
  for hearing, the party may not introduce at the hearing the
  information refused on discovery, except with leave of the judge.

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} The sanction provided by subsection (1) is in
  addition to the sanctions provided by section 110.

### [Effect of Counsel Answering]{.HTitleText2} {#h-927882 .Subheading}

**[[97]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-97 .sectionLabel}** Questions on an oral
examination for discovery shall be answered by the person being examined
but, where there is no objection, the question may be answered by
counsel and the answer shall be deemed to be the answer of the person
being examined unless, before the conclusion of the examination, the
person repudiates, contradicts or qualifies the answer.

### [Information Subsequently Obtained]{.HTitleText2} {#h-927884 .Subheading}

- **[[98]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-98 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Where
  a party has been examined for discovery or a person has been examined
  for discovery on behalf or in place of, or in addition to the party,
  and the party subsequently discovers that the answer to a question on
  the examination,

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} was incorrect or incomplete when made, or

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} is no longer correct and complete,

  the party shall forthwith provide the information in writing to every
  other party.

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} Where a party provides information in writing under
  subsection (1),

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the adverse party may require that the information
    be verified by affidavit of the party or be the subject of further
    examination for discovery, and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the writing may be treated at a hearing as if it
    formed part of the original examination of the person examined.

- [(3)]{.lawlabel} Where a party has failed to comply with
  subsection (1) or a requirement under paragraph (2)(a), and the
  information subsequently discovered is,

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} favourable to that party's case, the party may not
    introduce the information at the hearing, except with leave of the
    judge, or

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} not favourable to that party's case, the Court may
    give such direction as is just.

### [Discovery of Non-Parties with Leave]{.HTitleText2} {#h-927896 .Subheading}

- **[[99]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-99 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  Court may grant leave, on such terms respecting costs and other
  matters as are just, to examine for discovery any person who there is
  reason to believe has information relevant to a material issue in the
  appeal, other than an expert engaged by or on behalf of a party in
  preparation for contemplated or pending litigation.

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} Leave under subsection (1) shall not be granted
  unless the Court is satisfied that,

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the moving party has been unable to obtain the
    information from other persons whom the moving party is entitled to
    examine for discovery, or from the person sought to be examined,

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} it would be unfair to require the moving party to
    proceed to hearing without having the opportunity of examining the
    person, and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} the examination will not,

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} unduly delay the commencement of the hearing of
      the proceeding,

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} entail unreasonable expense for other parties,
      or

    - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} result in unfairness to the person the moving
      party seeks to examine.

- [(3)]{.lawlabel} A party who examines a person orally under this
  section shall, if requested, serve any party who attended or was
  represented on the examination with the transcript free of charge,
  unless the Court directs otherwise.

- [(4)]{.lawlabel} The examining party is not entitled to recover the
  costs of the examination from another party unless the Court expressly
  directs otherwise.

- [(5)]{.lawlabel} The evidence of a person examined under this section
  may not be read into evidence at the hearing under subsection 100(1).

### [Use of Examination for Discovery at Hearing]{.HTitleText2} {#h-927909 .Subheading}

- **[[100]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-100 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} At
  the hearing, a party may read into evidence as part of that party's
  own case, after that party has adduced all of that party's other
  evidence in chief, any part of the evidence given on the examination
  for discovery of

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the adverse party, or

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} a person examined for discovery on behalf of or in
    place of, or in addition to the adverse party, unless the judge
    directs otherwise,

  if the evidence is otherwise admissible, whether the party or person
  has already given evidence or not.

- [(1.1)]{.lawlabel} The judge may, on request, allow the part of
  evidence referred to in subsection (1) to be read into evidence at a
  time other than that specified in that subsection.

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} Subject to the provisions of the [Canada Evidence
  Act](/eng/acts/C-5), the evidence given on an examination for
  discovery may be used for the purpose of impeaching the testimony of
  the deponent as a witness in the same manner as any previous
  inconsistent statement by that witness.

- [(3)]{.lawlabel} Where only part of the evidence given on an
  examination for discovery is read into or used in evidence, at the
  request of an adverse party the judge may direct the introduction of
  any other part of the evidence that qualifies or explains the part
  first introduced.

- [(3.1)]{.lawlabel} A party who seeks to read into evidence under
  subsection (1) or who requests the judge to direct the introduction of
  evidence under subsection (3) may, with leave of the judge, instead of
  reading into evidence, file with the Court a photocopy or other copy
  of the relevant extracts from the transcripts of the examination for
  discovery, and when the copy is filed such extracts shall form part of
  the record.

- [(4)]{.lawlabel} A party who reads into evidence as part of that
  party's own case evidence given on an examination for discovery of an
  adverse party, or a person examined for discovery on behalf of or in
  place of or in addition to an adverse party, may rebut that evidence
  by introducing any other admissible evidence.

- [(5)]{.lawlabel} The evidence given on the examination for discovery
  of a party under legal disability may be read into or used in evidence
  at the hearing only with leave of the judge.

- [(6)]{.lawlabel} Where a person examined for discovery,

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} has died,

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} is unable to testify because of infirmity or
    illness,

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} for any other sufficient reason cannot be compelled
    to attend at the hearing, or

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} refuses to take an oath or make an affirmation or
    to answer any proper question,

  any party may, with leave of the judge, read into evidence all or part
  of the evidence given on the examination for discovery as the evidence
  of the person examined, to the extent that it would be admissible if
  the person were testifying in Court.

- [(7)]{.lawlabel} In deciding whether to grant leave under
  subsection (6), the judge shall consider,

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the extent to which the person was cross-examined
    on the examination for discovery,

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the importance of the evidence in the proceeding,

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} the general principle that evidence should be
    presented orally in Court, and

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} any other relevant factor.

- [(8)]{.lawlabel} Where an appeal has been discontinued or dismissed
  and another appeal involving the same subject matter is subsequently
  brought between the same parties or their representatives or
  successors in interest, the evidence given on an examination for
  discovery taken in the former appeal may be read into or used in
  evidence at the hearing of the subsequent appeal as if it had been
  taken in the subsequent appeal.

## [Examinations Out of Court]{.HTitleText1} {#h-927935 .Part}

### [Application of Sections 102 to 112]{.HTitleText2} {#h-927936 .Subheading}

**[[101]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-101 .sectionLabel}** Sections 102 to 112
apply to all oral examinations for which provision is made in these
rules including,

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} an oral examination for discovery,

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} the taking of evidence before hearing,

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} the cross-examination on an affidavit, and

- [(d)]{.lawlabel} the examination out of Court of a witness before
  hearing of a pending motion.

### [Mode of Examination]{.HTitleText2} {#h-927942 .Subheading}

- **[[102]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-102 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} An
  oral examination shall be held before a person agreed upon by the
  parties, who may be the verbatim reporter, or some other person if
  directed by the Court.

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} Unless otherwise directed by the Court or the parties
  otherwise agree, an examination that takes place in Canada shall be
  under oath or upon affirmation as provided in the [Canada Evidence
  Act](/eng/acts/C-5).

- [(3)]{.lawlabel} Unless otherwise directed by the Court, or the
  parties otherwise agree, an examination shall be recorded by a
  verbatim reporter and arrangements for the attendance of a reporter
  shall be made by the party conducting the examination, who shall pay
  the reporter's fees.

- [(4)]{.lawlabel} If the person being examined understands neither
  English nor French, or is deaf or mute, the examining party shall
  provide and pay the fees and disbursements of a competent and
  independent interpreter who shall take an oath or make an affirmation
  to interpret accurately the administration of the oath or affirmation,
  the questions to be put to the person being examined and the person's
  answers.

- [(5)]{.lawlabel} Where the examination is to be conducted in one of
  the official languages and the person to be examined would prefer to
  be examined in the other official language, the examining party shall
  advise the Registrar, and the Registrar shall then appoint an
  interpreter, at no cost to the parties, who shall take an oath or make
  an affirmation to interpret accurately the administration of the oath
  or affirmation and the questions to be put to the person being
  examined and the person's answers.

- [(6)]{.lawlabel} The transcript of the examination shall be certified
  as correct by the person who recorded the examination, but need not be
  read to or signed by the person examined.

### [Manner of Requiring Attendance]{.HTitleText2} {#h-927952 .Subheading}

  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Where the person to be examined is a

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} on the counsel for the party who filed the

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} where the party who filed the affidavit acts in

### [Notice of Time and Place]{.HTitleText2} {#h-927969 .Subheading}

**[[104]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-104 .sectionLabel}** The person to be
examined shall be given not less than ten days notice of the time and
place of the examination, unless the Court directs otherwise.

### [Production of Documents on Examination]{.HTitleText2} {#h-927971 .Subheading}

- **[[105]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-105
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Unless the parties otherwise agree,
  or the Court otherwise directs, the person to be examined shall bring
  to the examination and produce for inspection,

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} on an examination for discovery, all documents as
    required by subsection 85(3), and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} on all other examinations, such documents as may be
    required by subsection 105(3).

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} Where a person admits, on an examination, that he or
  she has possession or control of or power over any other document that

  privileged, the person shall produce it for inspection by the
  examining party forthwith, if the person has the document at the
  examination, and if not, within ten days thereafter, unless the Court
  directs otherwise.

- [(3)]{.lawlabel} The notice to attend for examination or subpoena may
  require the person to be examined to bring to the examination and
  produce for inspection,

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} all documents and things relevant to any matter in
    issue in the proceeding that are in that person's possession,
    control or power and that are not privileged, or

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} such documents or things described in paragraph (a)
    as are specified in the notice or subpoena,

  unless the Court directs otherwise.

### [Re-Examination]{.HTitleText2} {#h-927983 .Subheading}

- **[[106]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-106 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A
  person being examined for discovery may be re-examined by his or her
  own counsel.

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} A person being cross-examined on his or her affidavit
  may be re-examined by his or her own counsel.

- [(3)]{.lawlabel} The re-examination shall take place immediately after
  the examination or cross-examination and shall not take the form of a
  cross-examination.

### [Objections and Rulings]{.HTitleText2} {#h-927988 .Subheading}

- **[[107]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-107
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Where a question is objected to, the
  objector shall state briefly the reason for the objection, and the
  question and the brief statement shall be recorded.

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} A question that is objected to may be answered with
  the objector's consent, and where the question is answered, a ruling
  shall be obtained from the Court before the evidence is used at a
  hearing.

- [(3)]{.lawlabel} A ruling on the propriety of a question that is
  objected to and not answered may be obtained on motion to the Court.

### [Improper Conduct of Examination]{.HTitleText2} {#h-927993 .Subheading}

- **[[108]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-108 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} An
  examination may be adjourned by the person being examined or by a
  party present or represented at the examination, for the purpose of
  moving for directions with respect to the continuation of the
  examination or for an order terminating the examination or limiting
  its scope, where,

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the right to examine is being abused by an excess
    of improper questions or interfered with by an excess of improper
    interruptions or objections,

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the examination is being conducted in bad faith, or
    in an unreasonable manner so as to annoy, embarrass or oppress the
    person being examined,

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} many of the answers to the questions are evasive,
    unresponsive or unduly lengthy, or

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} there has been a neglect or improper refusal to
    produce a relevant document on the examination.

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} Where the Court finds that,

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} a person's improper conduct necessitated a motion
    under subsection (1), or

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} a person improperly adjourned an examination under
    subsection (1),

  the Court may direct the person to pay personally and forthwith the
  costs of the motion, any costs thrown away and the costs of any
  continuation of the examination and the Court may fix the costs and
  give such other direction as is just.

### [Videotaping or other Recording of Examination]{.HTitleText2} {#h-928004 .Subheading}

- **[[109]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-109 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} On
  consent of the parties or by direction of the Court, an examination
  may be recorded by videotape or other similar means and the tape or
  other recording may be filed for the use of the Court along with the
  transcript.

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} Section 111 applies, with necessary modifications, to
  a tape or other recording made under subsection (1).

### [Sanctions for Default or Misconduct by Person to be Examined]{.HTitleText2} {#h-928008 .Subheading}

**[[110]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-110 .sectionLabel}** Where a person fails to
attend at the time and place fixed for an examination in the notice to
attend or subpoena, or at the time and place agreed on by the parties,
or refuses to take an oath or make an affirmation, to answer any proper
question, to produce a document or thing that that person is required to
produce or to comply with a direction under section 108, the Court may,

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} where an objection to a question is held to be
  improper, direct or permit the person being examined to reattend at
  that person's own expense and answer the question, in which case the
  person shall also answer any proper questions arising from the answer,

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} where the person is a party or, on an examination for
  discovery, a person examined on behalf of or in place of a party,
  dismiss the appeal or allow the appeal as the case may be,

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} strike out all or part of the person's evidence,
  including any affidavit made by the person, and

- [(d)]{.lawlabel} direct any party or any other person to pay
  personally and forthwith costs of the motion, any costs thrown away
  and the costs of any continuation of the examination.

### [Filing of Transcript]{.HTitleText2} {#h-928014 .Subheading}

- **[[111]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-111 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} It
  is the responsibility of a party who intends to refer to evidence
  given on an examination to have a copy of the transcript of the
  examination available for filing with the Court.

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} A copy of a transcript for the use of the Court at
  hearing shall not be filed until a party refers to it at hearing, and
  the presiding judge may read only the portions to which a party
  refers.

### [Examination Where Person Outside Canada]{.HTitleText2} {#h-928018 .Subheading}

- **[[112]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-112
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Where the person to be examined
  resides outside of Canada the Court may determine,

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} whether the examination is to take place in or
    outside of Canada,

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the time and place of the examination,

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} the minimum notice period,

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} the amount of witness fees and expenses to be paid
    to the person to be examined, and

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} any other matter respecting the holding of the
    examination.

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} Where the person is to be examined outside of Canada,
  the direction under subsection (1) shall, if the moving party requests
  it, provide for the issuing of,

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} a commission (Form 112(2)(a)) authorizing the
    taking of evidence before a named commissioner, and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} a letter of request (Form 112(2)(b) --- REQUEST)
    directed to the judicial authorities of the jurisdiction in which
    the person is to be found, requesting the issuing of such process as
    is necessary to compel the person to attend and be examined before
    the commissioner, and the direction shall be in Form 112(2)(b)A ---
    DIRECTION.

- [(3)]{.lawlabel} The commission and letter of request shall be
  prepared and issued by the Registrar.

- [(4)]{.lawlabel} Where the person to be examined resides outside of
  Canada and is not a party or a person to be examined on behalf of or
  in place of a party, the examining party shall pay or tender to the
  person to be examined the amount of witness fees and expenses fixed
  under subsection (1).

- [(5)]{.lawlabel} A commissioner shall, to the extent that it is
  possible to do so, conduct the examination in the form of oral
  questions and answers in accordance with these rules, the laws of
  evidence of Canada and the terms of the commission, unless some other
  form of examination is required by the judgment or the law of the
  place where the examination is conducted.

- [(6)]{.lawlabel} As soon as the transcript of the examination is
  prepared the commissioner shall,

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} return the commission, together with the original
    transcript and exhibits, to the Registrar who issued it,

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} keep a copy of the transcript and, where
    practicable, the exhibits, and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} notify the parties who appeared at the examination
    that the transcript is complete and has been returned to the
    Registrar who issued the commission.

- [(7)]{.lawlabel} The Registrar shall send the transcript to the
  counsel for the examining party and the counsel shall, if requested,
  forthwith serve every other party with the transcript free of charge.

## [Procedure on Examination for Discovery by Written Questions]{.HTitleText1} {#h-928039 .Part}

### [Questions]{.HTitleText2} {#h-928040 .Subheading}

**[[113]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-113 .sectionLabel}** An examination for
discovery by written questions and answers shall be conducted by serving
a list of the questions to be answered on the person to be examined.
(Form 113)

### [Answers]{.HTitleText2} {#h-928042 .Subheading}

**[[114]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-114 .sectionLabel}** Written questions shall
be answered by the affidavit of the person being examined, served on the
examining party within thirty days after service of the list of
questions. (Form 114)

### [Objections]{.HTitleText2} {#h-928044 .Subheading}

**[[115]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-115 .sectionLabel}** An objection to
answering a written question shall be made in the affidavit of the
person being examined, with a brief statement of the reason for the
objection.

### [Failure to Answer]{.HTitleText2} {#h-928046 .Subheading}

- **[[116]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-116
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Where the examining party is not
  satisfied with an answer or where an answer suggests a new line of
  questioning, the examining party may, within fifteen days after
  receiving the answer, serve a further list of written questions which
  shall be answered within thirty days after service.

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} Where the person being examined refuses or fails to
  answer a proper question or where the answer to a question is
  insufficient, the Court may direct the person to answer or give a
  further answer to the question or to answer any other question either
  by affidavit or on oral examination.

- [(3)]{.lawlabel} Where the Court is satisfied, on reading all the
  answers to the written questions, that some or all of them are
  evasive, unresponsive or otherwise unsatisfactory, the Court may
  direct the person examined to submit to oral examination on such terms
  respecting costs and other matters as are just.

- [(4)]{.lawlabel} Where a person refuses or fails to answer a proper
  question on a written examination or to produce a document which that
  person is required to produce, the Court may, in addition to imposing
  the sanctions provided in subsections (2) and (3),

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} if the person is a party or a person examined on
    behalf of or in place of a party, dismiss the appeal or allow the
    appeal as the case may be,

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} strike out all or part of the person's evidence,
    and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} give such other direction as is just.

### [Improper Conduct of Written Examination]{.HTitleText2} {#h-928055 .Subheading}

**[[117]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-117 .sectionLabel}** On motion by the person
being examined, or by any party, the Court may terminate the written
examination or limit its scope where,

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} the right to examine is being abused by an excess of
  improper questions, or

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} the examination is being conducted in bad faith, or
  in an unreasonable manner so as to annoy, embarrass or oppress the
  person being examined.

### [Filing Questions and Answers]{.HTitleText2} {#h-928059 .Subheading}

**[[118]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-118 .sectionLabel}** Section 111 applies,
with necessary modifications, to the filing of written questions and
answers for the use of the Court.

## [Taking Evidence Before Hearing]{.HTitleText1} {#h-928061 .Part}

### [Where Available]{.HTitleText2} {#h-928062 .Subheading}

- **[[119]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-119 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A
  party who intends to introduce the evidence of a person at a hearing
  may, with leave of the Court or the consent of the parties, examine
  the person on oath or affirmation before the hearing for the purpose
  of having the person's testimony available to be tendered as evidence
  at the hearing.

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} In exercising its discretion to direct an examination
  under subsection (1), the Court shall take into account,

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the convenience of the person whom the party seeks
    to examine,

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the possibility that the person will be unavailable
    to testify at the hearing by reason of death, infirmity or sickness,

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} the possibility that the person will be beyond the
    jurisdiction of the Court at the time of the hearing,

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} the expense of bringing the person to the hearing,

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} whether the witness ought to give evidence in
    person at the hearing, and

  - [(f)]{.lawlabel} any other relevant consideration.

- [(3)]{.lawlabel} Before moving for leave to examine an expert witness
  under subsection (1), the moving party shall serve on all other
  parties the report of the expert witness referred to in
  subsection 145(7) unless the Court directs otherwise.

## [Listing for Hearing]{.HTitleText1} {#h-928087 .Part}

### [How Appeal is Set Down for Hearing]{.HTitleText2} {#h-928088 .Subheading}

- **[[123]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-123
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} After the close of pleadings, any
  party to an appeal, who is not in default under these rules or a
  judgment of the Court and who is ready for hearing, may apply in
  writing to the Registrar to fix the time and place of hearing.

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} Where all parties agree on the making of a joint
  application, it shall be made in Form 123.

- [(3)]{.lawlabel} Where all parties do not agree on making a joint
  application, the party making the application shall file a memorandum
  containing the information, as far as the applicant is concerned,
  required in Form 123 and shall serve a copy of the memorandum on all
  other parties and those parties shall, within ten days of service of
  the memorandum, file and serve on the party applying a similar
  memorandum.

- [(4)]{.lawlabel} Subject to any direction by the Court, the Registrar,
  or a person designated by the Registrar or by the Chief Justice, may
  fix the time and place for the hearing

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} on receipt of a joint application;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} on receipt of an application and of a separate
    memorandum from each party; or

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} on receipt of an application and after the time has
    expired for the filing of a separate memorandum by each party.

- [(4.1)]{.lawlabel} Despite subsection (4), the Court may, on its own
  initiative, fix the time and place for the hearing.

- [(5)]{.lawlabel} The Registrar shall forthwith send, by registered
  mail, notice of the time and place fixed for the hearing to all
  parties.

- [(6)]{.lawlabel} If the time and place for a hearing have been fixed
  on the joint application of the parties, the hearing shall not be
  adjourned unless the Court is satisfied that there are special
  circumstances that justify the adjournment and it is in the interests
  of justice to adjourn it.

### [Status Hearing]{.HTitleText2} {#h-928106 .Subheading}

- **[[125]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-125 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} If
  an appeal has not been set down for hearing or terminated by any means
  within 60 days after the filing of the reply or after the last day for
  the filing of the reply, whichever is later, subject to any direction
  by the Chief Justice, the Registrar or a person designated by the
  Registrar or by the Chief Justice may serve on the Deputy Attorney
  General of Canada and on the counsel of record for the appellant or,
  if the appellant acts in person, on the appellant, a notice of status
  hearing at least 30 days before the date fixed for that hearing, and
  the hearing shall be held before a judge.

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} A counsel who receives a notice of status hearing
  shall forthwith give a copy of the notice to that counsel's client.

- [(3)]{.lawlabel} Unless the appeal has been set down for hearing or
  terminated by any means before the date fixed for the status hearing,
  the counsel of record shall attend the status hearing and the parties
  may attend the hearing.

- [(4)]{.lawlabel} Where a party represented by counsel does not attend
  the hearing, that counsel shall file proof that a copy of the notice
  was given to the party.

- [(5)]{.lawlabel} At the status hearing,

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} if a reply has been filed, the judge may

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} set time periods for the completion of any
      remaining steps in the appeal,

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} dismiss the appeal for delay, or

    - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} make any order or give any other direction that
      is appropriate; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} if a reply has not been filed, the judge may,

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} direct that the appeal be allowed if the facts
      alleged in the notice of appeal entitle the appellant to the
      judgment sought,

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} direct that the appeal be heard on the basis
      that the facts alleged in the notice of appeal are presumed to be
      true and make a direction regarding the hearing fee, or

    - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} make any order or give any other direction that
      is appropriate.

- [(6)]{.lawlabel} The presumption in subparagraph (5)(b)(ii) is a
  rebuttable presumption.

- [(7)]{.lawlabel} Despite subsection (1), the Court may, on its own
  initiative or at the request of a party, at any time after the expiry
  of the period for serving a notice of status hearing provided in that
  subsection, direct counsel for the parties, either with or without the
  parties, and any party not represented by counsel, to appear before a
  judge who may

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} set time periods for the completion of any
    remaining steps before the hearing of the appeal;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} determine the advisability of amending the
    pleadings;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} attempt to identify any issue and shorten the
    hearing;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} attempt to obtain admissions of fact or documents;

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} consider directing that a settlement conference be
    held regarding any issue in the appeal;

  - [(f)]{.lawlabel} determine if the parties are ready to proceed with
    the hearing of the appeal by

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} identifying the parties' potential witnesses and
      the documents which may be filed as exhibits,

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} confirming that all required steps in the appeal
      have been completed before setting down the appeal for hearing,

    - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} determining the approximate duration of the
      hearing, and

    - [(iv)]{.lawlabel} fixing a time and place for the hearing; or

  - [(g)]{.lawlabel} make any other order or give any direction that the
    judge considers appropriate.

- [(8)]{.lawlabel} If a party fails to comply with an order made or
  direction given under subsection (5) or (7) or fails to appear at a
  status hearing at the time and place fixed for it, the Court may, on
  application or on its own initiative, allow or dismiss the appeal or
  make any other order that is appropriate.

### [Case Management]{.HTitleText2} {#h-928137 .Subheading}

- **[[126]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-126 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  Chief Justice may, on his or her own initiative or at the request of a
  party, at any time order that an appeal or a group of appeals be
  subject to case management and may designate one or more judges to act
  as the case management judge.

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} The case management judge shall convene a case
  management conference as soon as possible after the close of pleadings
  for the purpose of establishing, in conjunction with the parties, a
  timetable for the conduct of the appeal or group of appeals.

- [(3)]{.lawlabel} The case management judge may deal with all issues
  that arise prior to the hearing of the appeal, including by

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} convening case management conferences as necessary,
    either on their initiative or at the request of a party;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} giving any directions that are necessary for the
    just, most expeditious and least expensive determination of the
    appeal on its merits, including consolidating two or more appeals or
    parts of appeals that raise common issues or deal with common facts;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} determining all motions that are made prior to the
    appeal hearing date or arranging for them to be heard by another
    judge;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} despite any other time requirement provided in
    these rules, setting the time for the completion of any steps in the
    appeal; and

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} making any order or giving any direction that the
    case management judge considers appropriate.

- [(4)]{.lawlabel} If a party fails to comply with the time requirements
  set out in a timetable established under this section or with any
  requirement of these rules, or fails to attend any case management
  conference, the case management judge may

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} strike out any document or portion of a document
    filed by that party;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} dismiss the appeal or give judgment in favour of
    the appellant;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} amend the timetable in order to allow the party to
    comply with it;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} order the party to pay costs, either in a fixed
    amount or in an amount to be taxed; or

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} make any other order that the case management judge
    considers just in the circumstances.

- [(5)]{.lawlabel} A case management judge hearing any motion may
  dispense in whole or in part with the requirement to file a notice of
  motion together with the affidavits or other documentary material.

- [(6)]{.lawlabel} A case management judge shall not preside at the
  hearing of the appeal except with the consent of the parties.

### [Trial Management Conference]{.HTitleText2} {#h-928157 .Subheading}

- **[[126.1]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-126.1
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A trial management conference may be
  held as soon as a date for hearing the appeal has been fixed, at the
  request of one of the parties or on the initiative of the judge
  presiding at the hearing.

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} At the trial management conference, the judge may

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} obtain from the parties the names and contact

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} consider the possibility of obtaining admissions

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} consider alternative methods of adducing evidence,

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} consider expeditious means for adducing evidence;

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} give directions that would facilitate the orderly
    and expeditious conduct of the hearing;

  - [(f)]{.lawlabel} identify and hear, if necessary, any pre-trial
    motions which the judge considers ought to be dealt with and
    disposed of before the hearing commences;

  - [(g)]{.lawlabel} address the issues referred to in
    subsection 145(9); and

  - [(h)]{.lawlabel} give any direction for the conduct of the hearing
    that the judge considers just in the circumstances.

### [Settlement Conference]{.HTitleText2} {#h-928171 .Subheading}

- **[[126.2]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-126.2
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The Court may, at any time, on its
  own initiative or at the request of a party, direct that a conference
  be held to consider the possibility of settling any or all of the
  issues.

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} The judge who presides at a settlement conference
  shall not preside at the hearing of the appeal and shall not
  communicate with the judge hearing the appeal concerning anything that
  was said or done at the settlement conference.

- [(3)]{.lawlabel} Unless otherwise directed by the settlement

  represented by counsel, shall attend the settlement conference.

- [(4)]{.lawlabel} Each party shall, at least 14 days before the date of

  the Court a settlement conference brief containing

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} an explanation of the party's theory of the case;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} a statement of the material facts that the party
    expects to establish at the hearing of the appeal and how they will
    be established;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} a statement of the issues to be determined at the
    hearing; and

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} a statement of the law and authorities that the
    party will rely on at the hearing of the appeal.

- [(5)]{.lawlabel} A settlement conference brief shall not exceed
  10 pages except with leave of the settlement conference judge, which
  may be applied for by informal communication with the Registry.

- [(6)]{.lawlabel} The settlement conference judge may adjourn a
  settlement conference and reconvene it at a later date.

## [Admissions]{.HTitleText1} {#h-928197 .Part}

### [Interpretation]{.HTitleText2} {#h-928198 .Subheading}

**[[129]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-129 .sectionLabel}** In sections 130 to 132,
[[authenticity]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} includes the fact that,

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} a document that is said to be an original was
  printed, written, signed or executed as it purports to have been,

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} a document that is said to be a copy is a true copy
  of the original, and

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} where the document is a copy of a letter, telegram,
  telecommunication or a fax, the original was sent as it purports to
  have been sent and was received by the person to whom it is addressed.

### [Request to Admit Fact or Document]{.HTitleText2} {#h-928203 .Subheading}

- **[[130]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-130 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A
  party may, at any time, by serving a request to admit, request any
  other party to admit, for the purposes of the appeal only, the truth
  of a fact or the authenticity of a document. (Form 130)

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} A copy of any document mentioned in the request to
  admit shall, where practical, be served with the request, unless a
  copy is already in the possession of the other party.

### [Effect of Request to Admit]{.HTitleText2} {#h-928207 .Subheading}

- **[[131]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-131 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A
  party on whom a request to admit is served shall respond to it within
  fifteen days after it is served by serving on the requesting party, a
  response to request to admit. (Form 131)

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} Where the party on whom the request is served fails
  to serve a response as required by subsection (1), the party shall be
  deemed, for the purposes of the appeal only, to admit the truth of the
  facts or the authenticity of the documents mentioned in the request to
  admit.

- [(3)]{.lawlabel} A party shall also be deemed, for the purposes of the
  appeal only, to admit the truth of the facts or the authenticity of
  the documents mentioned in the request to admit, unless the party's
  response

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} specifically denies the truth of a fact or the
    authenticity of a document mentioned in the request, or

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} refuses to admit the truth of a fact or the
    authenticity of a document and sets out the reason for the refusal.

::: HistoricalNote
- SOR/2004-100, s. 20(E)
:::

### [Withdrawal of Admission]{.HTitleText2} {#h-928216 .Subheading}

**[[132]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-132 .sectionLabel}** A party may withdraw an
admission made in response to a request to admit, a deemed admission or
an admission in the party's pleading on consent or with leave of the
Court.

## [Procedure At Hearing]{.HTitleText1} {#h-928218 .Part}

### [Exclusion of Witnesses]{.HTitleText2} {#h-928219 .Subheading}

- **[[133]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-133 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A
  judge may exclude from a courtroom,

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} a witness until called to give evidence, and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} a person who is interfering with the proper conduct
    of a hearing.

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} A direction under subsection (1) may not be made in
  respect of a party to the appeal or a witness whose presence is
  essential to instruct counsel for the party calling the witness, but
  the presiding judge may require any such party or witness to give
  evidence before any other witnesses are called to give evidence on
  behalf of that party.

- [(2.1)]{.lawlabel} Where an order is made excluding a witness from the
  courtroom, there shall be no communication to the witness of any
  evidence given during the absence of the witness from the courtroom,
  except with leave of the presiding judge, until after the witness has
  been called and has given evidence.

- [(3)]{.lawlabel} Where an appeal is heard in camera under the
  authority of an enactment, disclosure of information relating to the
  appeal is not contempt of court unless the Court has expressly
  prohibited the disclosure of the information.

### [Return of Exhibits]{.HTitleText2} {#h-928229 .Subheading}

**[[134]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-134 .sectionLabel}** Subject to any
direction by the Chief Justice, at any time following the judgment, on
requisition by the counsel or party who put an exhibit in evidence, or
the person who produced it, the Registrar shall return the exhibit to
the person making the requisition.

### [Order of Presentation at Hearing]{.HTitleText2} {#h-928233 .Subheading}

- **[[135]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-135 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} If
  at a hearing a party proposes to adduce evidence, the party or the
  party's counsel shall, unless the judge directs otherwise, immediately
  before adducing the evidence, open his case by making a short
  statement giving a concise outline of the facts that the party
  proposes to prove and of the applicable law.

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} Unless the judge directs otherwise, the parties shall
  put in their respective cases by evidence or by putting before the
  Court the facts on which they rely, in the following order,

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the appellant,

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the respondent, and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} the appellant in respect of rebuttal evidence.

- [(3)]{.lawlabel} Unless the judge directs otherwise, after all parties
  have adduced their evidence, they shall be heard in argument in the
  order in which they adduced their evidence and the party who was first
  heard in argument may reply and an opposing party may answer a new
  point of law raised in the reply.

### [View by Judge]{.HTitleText2} {#h-928241 .Subheading}

**[[136]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-136 .sectionLabel}** The judge may, in the
presence of the parties or their counsel, inspect any place or thing
concerning which any question may arise.

### [Adjournment of Hearing]{.HTitleText2} {#h-928243 .Subheading}

**[[137]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-137 .sectionLabel}** A judge may postpone or
adjourn a hearing to such time and place and on such terms as are just.

### [Reopening of Hearing]{.HTitleText2} {#h-928245 .Subheading}

- **[[138]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-138 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  judge may reopen a hearing before judgment has been pronounced for
  such purposes and upon such terms as are just.

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} The judge may, at any time before judgment, draw the
  attention of the parties to any failure to prove some fact or document
  material to a party's case, or to any defect in the proceeding, and
  permit a party to remedy the failure or defect for such purposes and
  upon such terms as are just.

### [Justifying Absence of Witness]{.HTitleText2} {#h-928249 .Subheading}

- **[[139]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-139
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Where a party shows that through no
  lack of diligence, a witness whose evidence is material has failed to
  attend the hearing, the judge may adjourn the hearing.

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} Upon an application for an adjournment under
  subsection (1) another party may require the party applying, on oath
  or on the oath of some other person, to state the facts that it is
  believed the witness would have stated, and the other party may admit
  those facts, or admit that the witness would have stated those facts
  and the judge may refuse to adjourn the hearing.

### [Failure to Appear]{.HTitleText2} {#h-928253 .Subheading}

- **[[140]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-140 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} If
  at a hearing, either party fails to appear, the Court may allow the
  appeal, dismiss the appeal or give such other direction as is just.

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} The Court may set aside or vary, on such terms as are
  just, a judgment or order obtained against a party who failed to
  attend a hearing, a status hearing or a pre-hearing conference on the
  application of the party if the application is made within thirty days
  after the pronouncement of the judgment or order.

### [Compelling Attendance at Hearing]{.HTitleText2} {#h-928259 .Subheading}

- **[[141]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-141 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A
  party who requires the attendance of a person as a witness at a
  hearing may serve the person with a subpoena requiring the person to
  attend the hearing at the time and place stated in the subpoena and
  the subpoena may also require the person to produce at the hearing the
  documents or other things in the person's possession, control or power
  relevant to the matters in question in the appeal that are specified
  in the subpoena. (Form 141(1))

- [(1.1)]{.lawlabel} Unless otherwise directed by the Court, a subpoena
  must be served on a person whose attendance is required at the
  hearing, at least five days before the day on which that person is
  required to appear.

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} On the request of a party or of counsel, the
  Registrar, or some other person authorized by the Chief Justice, shall
  sign, seal and issue a blank subpoena and the party or counsel may
  complete the subpoena and insert the names of any number of witnesses.

- [(3)]{.lawlabel} No subpoena for the production or an original record
  or document that may be proved by a certified copy shall be served
  without leave of the Court.

- [(4)]{.lawlabel} No person is required to attend at a hearing unless
  the subpoena has been served on that person personally in accordance
  with subsection (1.1) and, at the same time, witness fees and expenses
  calculated in accordance with Schedule II, Tariff A have been paid or
  tendered to the person.

- [(5)]{.lawlabel} Service of a subpoena and the payment or tender of
  the witness fees and expenses may be proved by affidavit.

- [(6)]{.lawlabel} A subpoena continues to have effect until the
  attendance of the witness is no longer required.

- [(7)]{.lawlabel} Where a witness whose evidence is material to a
  proceeding is served with a subpoena and the proper witness fees and
  expenses are paid or tendered to the witness, and the witness fails to
  attend the hearing or to remain in attendance in accordance with the
  requirement of the subpoena, the judge may by a warrant for arrest
  cause the witness to be apprehended and forthwith brought before the
  Court. (Form 141(7))

- [(8)]{.lawlabel} On being apprehended, the witness may be detained in
  custody until the presence of the witness is no longer required, or
  the witness is released on such terms as are just, and the witness may
  be ordered to pay the costs arising out of the failure to attend or
  remain in attendance.

## [Evidence At Hearing of an Appeal]{.HTitleText1} {#h-928274 .Part}

### [Evidence of Particular Facts]{.HTitleText2} {#h-928275 .Subheading}

- **[[143]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-143
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Before or at the hearing, the Court
  may direct that evidence of a particular fact shall be given in such
  manner and subject to such conditions as the Court may specify and in
  particular that the evidence shall be given,

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} by affidavit,

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} by statement on oath or affirmation of information
    and belief,

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} by the production of documents or of entries in
    books or of copies thereof,

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} where a fact is a matter of common knowledge,
    either generally or in a particular place, by the production of a
    newspaper which contains a statement of that fact.

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} A direction made before the hearing under
  subsection (1) may be set aside or varied by the judge when it appears
  necessary to do so in the interests of justice.

### [Evidence by Witnesses]{.HTitleText2} {#h-928283 .Subheading}

- **[[144]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-144
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Unless these rules provide
  otherwise, witnesses at a hearing shall be examined orally in Court
  and the examination may consist of direct examination,
  cross-examination and re-examination.

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} The judge shall exercise reasonable control over the
  mode of interrogation of a witness so as to protect the witness from
  undue harassment or embarrassment and may disallow a question put to a
  witness that is vexatious or irrelevant to any matter that may
  properly be enquired into at the hearing.

- [(3)]{.lawlabel} The judge may, at any time, direct that a witness be
  recalled for further examination.

- [(4)]{.lawlabel} Where a witness appears unwilling or unable to give
  responsible answers, the judge may permit the party calling the
  witness to examine him or her by means of leading questions.

### [Expert Witnesses]{.HTitleText2} {#h-928289 .Subheading}

- **[[145]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-145 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} In
  this section, [[expert report]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} a solemn declaration made by a proposed expert
    witness under section 41 of the [Canada Evidence
    Act](/eng/acts/C-5);

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} a written statement signed by a proposed expert
    witness accompanied by a certificate of counsel indicating that
    counsel is satisfied that the statement represents evidence that the
    proposed expert witness is prepared to give in the matter; or

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} a written statement in any other form authorized by
    direction of the Court in a particular case and for special reasons.

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} An expert report shall

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} set out in full the evidence of the expert;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} set out the expert's qualifications and the areas
    in respect of which it is proposed that they be qualified as an
    expert witness; and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} be accompanied by a certificate in Form 145(2)
    signed by the expert acknowledging that they have read the Code of
    Conduct for Expert Witnesses set out in Schedule III and agree to be
    bound by it.

- [(3)]{.lawlabel} If an expert fails to comply with the Code of Conduct
  for Expert Witnesses, the Court may exclude some or all of their
  expert report.

- [(4)]{.lawlabel} A party intending to call more than five expert
  witnesses at a hearing shall seek leave of the Court under section 7
  of the [Canada Evidence Act](/eng/acts/C-5).

- [(5)]{.lawlabel} In deciding whether to grant leave, the Court shall
  consider all relevant matters, including

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the nature of the proceeding, its public
    significance and any need to clarify the law;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the number, complexity and technical nature of the
    issues in dispute; and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} the likely expense involved in calling the expert
    witnesses in relation to the amounts in issue.

- [(6)]{.lawlabel} Two or more of the parties may jointly name an expert
  witness.

- [(7)]{.lawlabel} Unless otherwise directed by the Court, no evidence
  in chief of an expert witness shall be received at the hearing in
  respect of an issue unless

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the issue has been defined by the pleadings or by
    written agreement of the parties stating the issues;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} their expert report prepared in accordance with
    subsection (2) has been served on all other parties not less than
    90 days before the commencement of the hearing; and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} the expert witness is available at the hearing for
    cross-examination.

- [(8)]{.lawlabel} Subject to subsection (7), evidence in chief of an
  expert witness may be given at the hearing

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} by the reading of all or part of the expert report
    by the witness, unless the Court, with the consent of the parties,
    permits it to be taken as read; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} if the party calling the witness so elects, by the
    verbal testimony of the expert witness

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} explaining or demonstrating what is in the expert
      report or in the part that has been given in evidence, and

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} in respect of other matters, by special leave of
      the Court, if it considers it appropriate.

- [(9)]{.lawlabel} A litigation process conference other than a
  settlement conference may address

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} any objection to the evidence of an adverse party's
    proposed expert witness and the basis of the objection;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} any benefit to the conduct of the proceeding that
    would be achieved by ordering that the proposed expert witnesses
    confer with one another before the hearing in order to narrow the
    issues and identify the points on which their views differ;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} the need for any additional or rebuttal expert
    witness evidence;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} the number of proposed expert witnesses and the
    manner of presentation of their evidence; and

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} any other issue arising from the expert reports of
    proposed expert witnesses.

- [(10)]{.lawlabel} The Court, at the request of a party or on its own
  initiative, may at any time order that expert witnesses confer with
  one another before the hearing in order to narrow the issues and
  identify the points on which their views differ.

- [(11)]{.lawlabel} The parties and their counsel may attend an expert
  conference but the conference may take place in the parties' absence
  if the parties agree.

- [(12)]{.lawlabel} The Court may order that an expert conference take
  place in the presence of a judge.

- [(13)]{.lawlabel} A joint statement prepared by two or more expert
  witnesses following an expert conference is admissible in evidence at
  the hearing.

- [(14)]{.lawlabel} Discussions in an expert conference and documents
  prepared for the conference are confidential and shall not be
  disclosed to the judge presiding at the hearing of the appeal except
  with consent of the parties.

- [(15)]{.lawlabel} Unless otherwise directed by the Court, no evidence
  of an expert witness, including their expert report, shall be led in
  rebuttal of any evidence referred to in paragraph (7)(b) unless the
  rebuttal evidence has been reduced to writing in accordance with this
  section and a copy is served on the other parties not less than
  60 days before the commencement of the hearing.

- [(16)]{.lawlabel} Unless otherwise directed by the Court, no evidence
  of an expert witness, including an expert report, shall be led in
  surrebuttal of any evidence tendered under subsection (15) unless the
  surrebuttal evidence has been reduced to writing in accordance with
  this section and a copy has been served on the other parties not less
  than 30 days before the commencement of the hearing.

- [(17)]{.lawlabel} The Court may require that some or all of the expert
  witnesses testify as a panel after the completion of the testimony of
  the non-expert witnesses of each party or at any other time that the
  Court determines.

- [(18)]{.lawlabel} The Court may identify matters that are within the
  area of expertise of the panel members and pose questions to the panel
  members about those matters.

- [(19)]{.lawlabel} Expert witnesses shall give their views and may be
  directed to comment on the views of other panel members and to make
  concluding statements. They may, with leave of the Court, pose
  questions to other panel members.

- [(20)]{.lawlabel} On completion of the testimony of the panel, panel
  members may be cross-examined and re-examined in the sequence directed
  by the Court.

### [Calling Adverse Party as Witness]{.HTitleText2} {#h-928334 .Subheading}

- **[[146]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-146 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A
  party may secure the attendance of a person who is,

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} an adverse party, or

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} an officer, director or employee of an adverse
    party,

  as a witness at a hearing by,

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} serving the person with a subpoena, or

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} serving on the adverse party or the counsel of

    commencement of the hearing, a notice of intention to call the

- [(3)]{.lawlabel} A party calling a witness referred to in
  subsection (1) may cross-examine the witness.

- [(4)]{.lawlabel} Where a person required to testify under this
  section,

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} refuses or neglects to attend at the hearing or to
    remain in attendance at the hearing,

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} refuses to be sworn, or

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} refuses to answer any proper question put to that
    person or to produce any document or other thing which that person
    is required to produce,

  the Court may grant judgment in favour of the party calling the
  witness, adjourn the hearing or give such other direction as is just.

### [Lead Cases]{.HTitleText2} {#h-928352 .Subheading}

- **[[146.1]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-146.1
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} This section applies if

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} two or more appeals have been filed before the
    Court;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the Court has not made a decision disposing of any
    of the appeals; and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} the appeals give rise to one or more common or
    related issues of fact or law.

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} The Court may give a direction

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} specifying one or more of the appeals referred to
    in subsection (1) as a lead case or lead cases; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} staying the related appeals.

- [(3)]{.lawlabel} If the Court gives a direction, each party in a
  related appeal who agrees to be bound, in whole or in part, by the
  decision in the lead case shall, within 10 days, file Form 146.1 with
  the Court.

- [(4)]{.lawlabel} If a party does not agree to be bound by the decision
  in the lead case, in whole or in part, or does not file Form 146.1
  with the Court, the Court shall give a direction that the appeal is no
  longer stayed.

- [(5)]{.lawlabel} The Court may, on its own initiative or at the
  request of a party, give directions with respect to the related
  appeals, provide for their disposal or take further steps with respect
  to those appeals.

- [(6)]{.lawlabel} If a lead case or lead cases are withdrawn or
  disposed of before the Court makes a decision in relation to the
  common or related issues, the Court shall give directions as to

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} whether another appeal or other appeals are to be
    heard as the lead case or lead cases; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} whether any direction affecting the related appeals
    should be set aside or amended.

## [Costs]{.HTitleText1} {#h-928369 .Part}

### [General Principles]{.HTitleText2} {#h-928370 .Subheading}

- **[[147]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-147 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  Court may determine the amount of the costs of all parties involved in
  any proceeding, the allocation of those costs and the persons required
  to pay them.

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} Costs may be awarded to or against the Crown.

- [(3)]{.lawlabel} In exercising its discretionary power pursuant to
  subsection (1) the Court may consider,

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the result of the proceeding,

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the amounts in issue,

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} the importance of the issues,

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} any offer of settlement made in writing,

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} the volume of work,

  - [(f)]{.lawlabel} the complexity of the issues,

  - [(g)]{.lawlabel} the conduct of any party that tended to shorten or
    to lengthen unnecessarily the duration of the proceeding,

  - [(h)]{.lawlabel} the denial or the neglect or refusal of any party
    to admit anything that should have been admitted,

  - [(i)]{.lawlabel} whether any stage in the proceedings was,

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} improper, vexatious, or unnecessary, or

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} taken through negligence, mistake or excessive
      caution,

  - [(i.1)]{.lawlabel} whether the expense required to have an expert
    witness give evidence was justified given

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} the nature of the proceeding, its public
      significance and any need to clarify the law,

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} the number, complexity or technical nature of
      the issues in dispute, or

    - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} the amount in dispute; and

  - [(j)]{.lawlabel} any other matter relevant to the question of costs.

- [(3.1)]{.lawlabel} Unless otherwise ordered by the Court, if an
  appellant makes an offer of settlement and obtains a judgment as
  favourable as or more favourable than the terms of the offer of
  settlement, the appellant is entitled to party and party costs to the
  date of service of the offer and substantial indemnity costs after
  that date, as determined by the Court, plus reasonable disbursements
  and applicable taxes.

- [(3.2)]{.lawlabel} Unless otherwise ordered by the Court, if a
  respondent makes an offer of settlement and the appellant obtains a
  judgment as favourable as or less favourable than the terms of the
  offer of settlement or fails to obtain judgment, the respondent is
  entitled to party and party costs to the date of service of the offer
  and substantial indemnity costs after that date, as determined by the
  Court, plus reasonable disbursements and applicable taxes.

- [(3.3)]{.lawlabel} Subsections (3.1) and (3.2) do not apply unless the
  offer of settlement

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} is in writing;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} is served no earlier than 30 days after the close
    of pleadings and at least 90 days before the commencement of the
    hearing;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} is not withdrawn; and

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} does not expire earlier than 30 days before the
    commencement of the hearing.

- [(3.4)]{.lawlabel} A party who is relying on subsection (3.1) or (3.2)
  has the burden of proving that

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} there is a relationship between the terms of the
    offer of settlement and the judgment; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the judgment is as favourable as or more favourable
    than the terms of the offer of settlement, or as favourable or less
    favourable, as the case may be.

- [(3.5)]{.lawlabel} For the purposes of this section, [[substantial
  indemnity costs]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means 80% of solicitor and
  client costs.

- [(3.6)]{.lawlabel} In ascertaining whether the judgment granted is as
  favourable as or more favourable than the offer of settlement for the
  purposes of applying subsection (3.1) or as favourable as or less
  favourable than the offer of settlement for the purposes of applying
  subsection (3.2), the Court shall not have regard to costs awarded in
  the judgment or that would otherwise be awarded, if an offer of
  settlement does not provide for the settlement of the issue of costs.

- [(3.7)]{.lawlabel} For greater certainty, if an offer of settlement
  that does not provide for the settlement of the issue of costs is
  accepted, a party to the offer may apply to the Court for an order
  determining the amount of costs.

- [(3.8)]{.lawlabel} No communication respecting an offer of settlement
  shall be made to the Court, other than to a judge in a litigation
  process conference who is not the judge at the hearing, until all of
  the issues, other than costs, have been determined.

- [(4)]{.lawlabel} The Court may fix all or part of the costs with or
  without reference to Schedule II, Tariff B and, further, it may award
  a lump sum in lieu of or in addition to any taxed costs.

- [(5)]{.lawlabel} Notwithstanding any other provision in these rules,
  the Court has the discretionary power,

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} to award or refuse costs in respect of a particular
    issue or part of a proceeding,

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} to award a percentage of taxed costs or award taxed
    costs up to and for a particular stage of a proceeding, or

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} to award all or part of the costs on a solicitor
    and client basis.

- [(6)]{.lawlabel} The Court may give directions to the taxing officer
  and, without limiting the generality of the foregoing, the Court in
  any particular proceeding may give directions,

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} respecting increases over the amounts specified for
    the items in Schedule II, Tariff B,

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} respecting services rendered or disbursements
    incurred that are not included in Schedule II, Tariff B, and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} to permit the taxing officer to consider factors
    other than those specified in section 154 when the costs are taxed.

- [(7)]{.lawlabel} Any party may,

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} within thirty days after the party has knowledge of
    the judgment, or

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} after the Court has reached a conclusion as to the
    judgment to be pronounced, at the time of the return of the motion
    for judgment,

  whether or not the judgment included any direction concerning costs,
  apply to the Court to request that directions be given to the taxing
  officer respecting any matter referred to in this section or in
  sections 148 to 152 or that the Court reconsider its award of costs.

## [Contempt of Court]{.HTitleText1} {#h-928609 .Part}

- **[[172.4]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-172.4
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A person is guilty of contempt of
  court who

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} at a hearing of the Court fails to maintain a
    respectful attitude, remain silent or refrain from showing approval
    or disapproval of the proceeding;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} wilfully disobeys a process or order of the Court;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} acts in such a way as to interfere with the orderly
    administration of justice or to impair the authority or dignity of
    the Court;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} is an officer of the Court and fails to perform his
    or her duties;

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} is a sheriff or bailiff and does not execute a writ
    forthwith or does not make a return thereof; or

  - [(f)]{.lawlabel} contrary to these rules and without lawful excuse,

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} refuses or neglects to obey a subpoena or to
      attend at the time and place appointed for his or her examination
      for discovery,

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} refuses to be sworn or to affirm or to answer
      any question put to him or her,

    - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} refuses or neglects to produce or permit to be
      inspected any document or other property, or

    - [(iv)]{.lawlabel} refuses or neglects to answer interrogatories or
      to make discovery of documents.

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} Subject to subsection (6), before a person may be
  found in contempt of court, the person alleged to be in contempt shall
  be served with an order, made on the motion of a person who has an
  interest in the proceeding or at the Court's own initiative, requiring
  the person alleged to be in contempt

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} to appear before a judge at a time and place
    stipulated in the order;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} to be prepared to hear proof of the act with which
    the person is charged, which shall be described in the order with
    sufficient particularity to enable the person to know the nature of
    the case against the person; and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} to be prepared to present any defence that the
    person may have.

- [(3)]{.lawlabel} A motion for an order under subsection (2) may be
  made [ex parte]{.otherLang lang="la"}.

- [(4)]{.lawlabel} An order may be made under subsection (2) if the
  Court is satisfied that there is a [prima facie]{.otherLang lang="la"}
  case that contempt has been committed.

- [(5)]{.lawlabel} An order under subsection (2) shall be personally
  served, together with any supporting documents, unless otherwise
  ordered by the Court.

- [(6)]{.lawlabel} In a case of urgency, a person may be found in
  contempt of court for an act committed in the presence of a judge in
  the exercise of his or her functions and condemned at once, provided
  that the person has first been called on to justify his or her
  behaviour.

- [(7)]{.lawlabel} A finding of contempt shall be based on proof beyond
  a reasonable doubt.

- [(8)]{.lawlabel} A person alleged to be in contempt may not be
  compelled to testify.

- [(9)]{.lawlabel} Where the Court considers it necessary, it may
  request the assistance of the Attorney General of Canada or any other
  person in relation to any proceedings for contempt.

- [(10)]{.lawlabel} Where a person is found to be in contempt, a judge
  may order, in addition to any other order made in respect of the
  proceedings, any or all of the following:

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} that the person be imprisoned for a period of less
    than two years;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} that the person pay a fine;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} that the person do or refrain from doing any act;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} that the person's property be sequestered; and

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} that the person pay costs.

- SOR/2004-100, s. 24

## [SCHEDULE II]{.scheduleLabel}[(Sections 103, 141, 146, 147, 154 and 176)]{.OriginatingRef} {#h-929372 .scheduleLabel}

### [TARIFF A]{.HLabel1}[Tariff of fees]{.HTitleText1} {#tariff-atariff-of-fees .SchedHeadL1}

###### [Classes of Proceedings]{.HTitleText5}

- **1** Subject to section 1.1, for purposes of this Tariff and
  Tariff B, there are three classes of proceedings as follows:

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} Class A proceedings which include

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} appeals in which the aggregate of all amounts in
      issue is less than \$50,000, and

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} appeals in which a loss has been determined
      under subsection 152(1.1) of the [Income Tax Act](/eng/acts/I-3.3)
      and the amount that is in issue is less than \$100,000; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} Class B proceedings which include

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} appeals in which the aggregate of all amounts in
      issue is \$50,000 or more but less than \$150,000,

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} appeals in which a loss has been determined
      under subsection 152(1.1) of the [Income Tax Act](/eng/acts/I-3.3)
      and the amount that is in issue is \$100,000 or more but less than
      \$300,000,

    - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} a reference under section 173 or 174 of the
      [Income Tax Act](/eng/acts/I-3.3), section 310 or 311 of the
      [Excise Tax Act](/eng/acts/E-15), section 97.58 of the [Customs
      Act](/eng/acts/C-52.6), section 51 or 52 of the [Air Travellers
      Security Charge Act](/eng/acts/A-10.5), section 204 or 205 of the
      [Excise Act, 2001](/eng/acts/E-14.1) or section 62 or 63 of the
      [Softwood Lumber Products Export Charge Act,
      2006](/eng/acts/S-12.55), and

    - [(iv)]{.lawlabel} any proceeding not otherwise specifically
      provided for under this section; and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} Class C proceedings which include

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} appeals in which the aggregate of all amounts in
      issue is \$150,000, or more, and

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} appeals in which a loss has been determined
      under subsection 152(1.1) of the [Income Tax Act](/eng/acts/I-3.3)
      and the amount that is in issue is \$300,000 or more.

- **1.1** For the purposes of applying this Tariff and Tariff B with
  respect to appeals from a determination or redetermination of the fair
  market value of an object by the Canadian Cultural Property Export
  Review Board established under the [Cultural Property Export and
  Import Act](/eng/acts/C-51), or from a confirmation or redetermination
  of the fair market value of an ecological gift by the Minister of the
  Environment pursuant to subsection 118.1(10.4) of the [Income Tax
  Act](/eng/acts/I-3.3), the three classes of proceedings are as
  follows:

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} Class A, where the amount in issue is less than
    \$50,000;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} Class B, where the amount in issue is \$50,000 or
    more but less than \$150,000; and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} Class C, where the amount in issue is \$150,000 or
    more.

###### [Court Fees]{.HTitleText5}

- - **2** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The party who commences a proceeding shall
    pay to the Registry:

    - [(a)]{.lawlabel} in the case of a Class A proceeding --- \$250;

    - [(b)]{.lawlabel} in the case of a Class B proceeding --- \$400;
      and

    - [(c)]{.lawlabel} in the case of a Class C proceeding --- \$550.

  - [(2)]{.lawlabel} Subsection (1) shall come into force on March 15,
    1995, and applies to proceedings instituted on or after that date.

- **3** [\[Repealed, SOR/99-209, s. 9\]]{.Repealed}

###### [Witness Fees and Expenses]{.HTitleText5}

- - **4** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A witness, other than a witness who appears
    to give evidence as an expert, is entitled to be paid by the party
    who arranged for his or her attendance \$75 per day, plus reasonable
    and proper transportation and living expenses.

  - [(1.1)]{.lawlabel} An amount is not payable under subsection (1) in
    respect of an appellant unless the appellant is called upon to
    testify by counsel for the respondent.

  - [(2)]{.lawlabel} The party who arranges for the attendance of a
    witness may pay such witness, in lieu of the amount prescribed by
    subsection 4(1) of this Tariff a larger reasonable amount for
    attending as witness in that proceeding, but he must, forthwith
    after he makes such payment, or makes payment of the excess of the
    amount over the amount provided for in subsection 4(1) of this
    Tariff, file in the Court Office a statement of that payment and of
    the way in which he satisfied himself as to the amount payable under
    this subsection.

###### [Expert Witnesses]{.HTitleText5} {#expert-witnesses}

- - **5** [(1)]{.lawlabel} When a witness, other than a party, is called
    on to give evidence in consequence of any professional or technical
    services rendered by that witness, there shall be substituted for
    the amount of \$75 in subsection 4(1) of this Tariff an amount not
    exceeding \$350 per day subject to increase in the discretion of the
    taxing officer but otherwise section 4 is applicable to such a
    witness.

  - [(2)]{.lawlabel} In lieu of making a payment under section 4 of this
    Tariff, there may be paid to a witness who appears to give evidence
    as an expert a reasonable payment not exceeding \$300 per day,
    unless the taxing officer otherwise directs, for the services
    performed by the witness in preparing to give evidence and giving
    evidence.

###### [No Preparation Fee]{.HTitleText5}

- **6** No payment shall be made to or received by a witness for what a
  witness has done in preparing to give evidence or giving evidence
  except as permitted by this Tariff.

###### [Attendance of Witness]{.HTitleText5}

- **7** For the purpose of this Tariff, a party arranges for the
  attendance of a witness by causing a subpoena to be served on the
  witness or by arranging either directly or indirectly to have the
  witness attend.

###### [Sheriffs and Bailiffs]{.HTitleText5}

- **8** A sheriff or bailiff may take and receive for a service rendered
  by him the fees or allowances permitted by law for a like service in
  the superior court of the province (territory) in which the service
  was rendered.

###### [Commissioners]{.HTitleText5}

- **9** A commissioner for taking evidence or before whom an examination
  for discovery is taken or other person performing a similar function,
  other than a salaried officer of the Court, may be paid by the party
  who arranged for his services:

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} if the services are performed in Canada, such fees
    and allowances as are payable for similar services in the superior
    court of the province (territory) in which the services are
    performed, and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} if the services are performed outside Canada, the
    amount reasonably necessary in order to obtain the services of a
    properly qualified person.

## [SCHEDULE III]{.scheduleLabel}[(Paragraph 145(2)(c) and Form 145(2) of Schedule I)]{.OriginatingRef}[Code of Conduct for Expert Witnesses]{.scheduleTitleText}  {#h-929459 .scheduleLabel}
:::

![blank line](/img/ii_spacer.gif){.Leader-dash}

#### [General Duty to the Court]{.HTitleText2} {#general-duty-to-the-court .SchedHeadL2}

- **1** An expert witness has an overriding duty to assist the Court
  impartially on matters relevant to his or her area of expertise.

- **2** This duty overrides any duty to a party to the proceeding,
  including the person retaining the expert witness. An expert witness
  must be independent and objective and must not be an advocate for a
  party.

#### [Expert Reports]{.HTitleText2} {#expert-reports .SchedHeadL2}

- **3** An expert report referred to in subsection 145(1) of the Rules
  shall include

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} a statement of the issues addressed;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} a description of the qualifications of the expert
    on the issues addressed;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} the expert's current curriculum vitae attached as a
    schedule;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} the facts and assumptions on which the opinions in
    the report are based;

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} a summary of the opinions expressed;

  - [(f)]{.lawlabel} in the case of a report that is provided in
    response to another expert's report, an indication of the points of
    agreement and disagreement with the other expert's opinions;

  - [(g)]{.lawlabel} the reasons for each opinion expressed;

  - [(h)]{.lawlabel} any literature or other materials specifically
    relied on in support of the opinions;

  - [(i)]{.lawlabel} a summary of the methodology used, including any
    examinations, tests or other investigations on which the expert has
    relied, including details of the qualifications of the person who
    carried them out, and whether a representative of any other party
    was present; and

  - [(j)]{.lawlabel} any caveats or qualifications necessary to render
    the report complete and accurate, including those relating to any
    insufficiency of data or research and an indication of any matters
    that fall outside the expert's field of expertise.

- **4** An expert witness must report without delay to persons in
  receipt of the expert report any material changes affecting the
  expert's qualifications or the opinions expressed or the data
  contained in the expert report.

#### [Expert Conferences]{.HTitleText2} {#expert-conferences .SchedHeadL2}

- **5** An expert witness who is ordered by the Court to confer with
  another expert witness shall

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} exercise independent, impartial and objective
    judgment on the issues addressed;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} endeavour to clarify with the other expert witness
    the points on which they agree and those on which their views
    differ; and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} not act on any instruction or request to withhold
    or avoid agreement with another expert witness.
