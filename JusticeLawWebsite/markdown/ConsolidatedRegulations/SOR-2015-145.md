## [Application]{.HTitleText1} {#h-817265 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Application

- **[[2]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-2 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Subject
  to subsection (2), these Regulations apply to the packaging and
  transport of

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} prescribed equipment; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} nuclear substances, including

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} the design, production, use, inspection,
      maintenance and repair of packaging and packages for nuclear
      substances, and

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} the preparation, consigning, handling, loading,
      carriage, storage during transport, receipt at final destination
      and unloading of packages and unpacking of their contents.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Exception

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} These Regulations, except for sections 6 and 7, do
  not apply to the packaging and transport of a nuclear substance

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} that is naturally occurring, provided that it has

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} a specific activity that is less than or equal to
      70 kBq/kg, or

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} an activity concentration that does not exceed
      10 times the activity concentration limit for exempt material
      values set out in the IAEA Regulations;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} that was implanted in or administered to a person
    or an animal for medical diagnosis or treatment purposes, or that
    subsists in their remains;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} that is contained in a sample of material taken for
    bioassay purposes;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} that is used by a holder of a licence on private
    property for the purpose of an activity described in paragraphs
    26(a) to (c) of the Act that the licence authorizes the holder to
    carry on, if access to the property is controlled;

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} that is contained in human or animal tissue samples
    or animal remains, or a liquid scintillation medium, if the specific
    activity of the nuclear substance averaged over the mass of the
    material does not exceed 10^-6^ A~2~/kg;

  - [(f)]{.lawlabel} that is contained in a product for which no licence
    is required under sections 6 to 8 of the [Nuclear Substances and
    Radiation Devices Regulations](/eng/regulations/SOR-2000-207)
    following the sale of the product to an end user;

  - [(g)]{.lawlabel} that is an integral part of a conveyance and is
    required for transport purposes;

  - [(h)]{.lawlabel} that has an activity concentration that does not
    exceed the values for an exempt material set out in the IAEA
    Regulations or in a certificate for a basic radionuclide value that
    is not listed in the IAEA Regulations;

  - [(i)]{.lawlabel} that is in a consignment that has a total activity
    that does not exceed the values for an activity limit for an exempt
    consignment set out in the IAEA Regulations, in a certificate for a
    basic radionuclide value that is not listed in the IAEA Regulations
    or in a certificate for an instrument or article that has an
    alternative activity limit for an exempt consignment;

  - [(j)]{.lawlabel} that is contained in a check source for which no
    licence is required under section 8.1 of the [Nuclear Substances and
    Radiation Devices Regulations](/eng/regulations/SOR-2000-207)
    following the sale of the check source to an end user;

  - [(k)]{.lawlabel} that is contained in a radiation device for which
    no licence is required under paragraph 5(1)(c) of the [Nuclear
    Substances and Radiation Devices
    Regulations](/eng/regulations/SOR-2000-207) following the sale of
    the device to an end user;

  - [(l)]{.lawlabel} that consists of non-radioactive solid objects with
    radioactive material present on any surface in quantities not
    exceeding 0.4 Bq/cm^2^ for beta and gamma emitters and low toxicity
    alpha emitters and 0.04 Bq/cm^2^ for all other alpha emitters;

  - [(m)]{.lawlabel} that is in or on a person who is transported for
    medical treatment because the person has been subject to an
    accidental or deliberate intake or contamination;

  - [(n)]{.lawlabel} that is present in a load of waste that is in
    transport, is not classified as radioactive material and has
    triggered a radiation monitor alarm if the nuclear substance in the
    load has been determined only to be one or more of the following
    medical isotopes and if there is no loss or dispersal of the
    material during the transport:

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} Chromium 51,

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} Indium 111,

    - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} Iodine 123, 124 or 131,

    - [(iv)]{.lawlabel} Gallium 67,

    - [(v)]{.lawlabel} Technetium 99m,

    - [(vi)]{.lawlabel} Thallium 201;

  - [(o)]{.lawlabel} that is being transported to a location for proper
    characterization in accordance with section 3, if

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} it is present in a load that was already in
      transport,

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} it is not classified as radioactive material,

    - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} it has triggered a radiation monitor alarm and
      the maximum dose rate on any external surface of the vehicle that
      is transporting it is less than or equal to 500 µSv/h, and

    - [(iv)]{.lawlabel} there is no loss or dispersal of the material
      during the transport; or

  - [(p)]{.lawlabel} that is being transported by a peace officer to a
    location for proper characterization, if

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} it is a forensic sample,

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} the peace officer has reason to believe that it
      is radioactive material,

    - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} the maximum dose rate on contact is less than
      or equal to 2 mSv/h on any external surface of the container,

    - [(iv)]{.lawlabel} there is no loss or dispersal of the material
      during the transport, and

    - [(v)]{.lawlabel} the Commission is advised immediately of the
      transport.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Characterization

- **[[3]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-3 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  nuclear substance referred to in paragraph 2(2)(o) must be
  characterized at the earliest possible time to determine the extent to
  which it is subject to these Regulations and the [Nuclear Substances
  and Radiation Devices Regulations](/eng/regulations/SOR-2000-207).

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Licensable quantity

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} For the purpose of this section, a licensable
  quantity of a nuclear substance is a quantity

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} in respect of which the activity exceeds the
    exemption quantity, as defined in section 1 of the [Nuclear
    Substances and Radiation Devices
    Regulations](/eng/regulations/SOR-2000-207); or

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} in respect of which there is no exemption from
    licensing under sections 5 to 8.1 of those Regulations.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Documentation of characterization

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} The person who performs the characterization must

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} keep a record documenting the detection of the
    radiation and the disposal of the nuclear substance for two years;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} file an annual report with the Commission by April
    30 that contains a summary of radiation detections for the calendar
    year before the date of the report; and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} immediately notify the Commission if the source of
    the radioactivity in the load is determined to be a licensable
    quantity of a nuclear substance.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Dose rate greater than 5 µSv/h and less
  than or equal to 25 µSv/h

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} If the measured dose rate at the time that the alarm
  is triggered is greater than 5 µSv/h and less than or equal to 25
  µSv/h and there is no loss or dispersal of the nuclear substance
  during the transport, the consignor, the carrier and the consignee
  must

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} immediately make a preliminary report to the
    Commission indicating the alarm level, the details of the transport,
    information on the location and circumstances of the detected
    radiation and any action that they have taken or propose to take in
    respect of it; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} characterize the source of the radiation within 10
    days after its detection and make a follow-up report

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} immediately, if the characterization of the
      source of the radioactivity in the load indicates that it is a
      licensable quantity of a nuclear substance, or

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} within 21 days after the initial detection, if
      the nuclear substance in the load is determined not to be of a
      licensable quantity, with a summary of the radiation detection and
      the disposal of the substance and a confirmation that it is not of
      a licensable quantity.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Dose rate greater than 25 µSv/h and
  less than or equal to 500 µSv/h

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} If the measured dose rate at the time that the alarm
  is triggered is greater than 25 µSv/h but less than or equal to 500
  µSv/h and there is no loss or dispersal of the nuclear substance
  during the transport, the consignor, carrier and consignee must

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} immediately make a preliminary report to the
    Commission indicating the alarm level, the details of the transport,
    information on the location and circumstances of the detected
    radiation and any action that they have taken or propose to take in
    respect of it;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} isolate the load, prevent dispersal of the nuclear
    substance and control access to it to ensure that persons are not
    exposed to effective doses that exceed the limits set out in section
    13 of the [Radiation Protection
    Regulations](/eng/regulations/SOR-2000-203);

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} have an expert in radiation protection assess the
    situation; and

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} report the results of the assessment to the
    Commission within 10 days after the detection and make a follow-up
    report

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} immediately, if the characterization of the
      source of the radioactivity in the load indicates that it is a
      licensable quantity of a nuclear substance, or

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} within 21 days after the initial detection, if
      the nuclear substance in the load is determined not to be of a
      licensable quantity, with a summary of the radiation detection and
      the disposal of the substance and a confirmation that it is not of
      a licensable quantity.

## [Classification of Material and Packages]{.HTitleText1} {#h-817334 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}General classification

**[[4]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-4 .sectionLabel}** Subject to section 5,
packages and radioactive material must be classified in accordance with
the IAEA Regulations.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}LSA-I material

- **[[5]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-5 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} LSA
  material is classified as LSA-I material if it is either non-fissile
  material or fissile-excepted radioactive material and if it consists
  of

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} ores that contain naturally occurring radionuclides
    with a uranium and thorium concentration not greater than 3% by
    mass;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} radioactive material for which the A~2~ value is
    unlimited, except for ores that contain naturally occurring
    radionuclides with a uranium and thorium concentration greater than
    3% by mass;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} concentrates of unirradiated thorium, of natural
    uranium or of depleted uranium, as those terms are defined in the
    IAEA Regulations, or their unirradiated compounds or mixtures in
    solid or liquid form;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} any mill tailings, contaminated earth, concrete,
    rubble, other debris and activated materials in which the
    radioactive material is essentially uniformly distributed and for
    which the average specific activity does not exceed 10^-6^ A~2~/g;
    or

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} other radioactive material in which the activity is
    distributed throughout and for which the estimated average specific
    activity does not exceed 30 times the activity concentration limit
    for exempt material values set out in the IAEA Regulations or in a
    certificate for a basic radionuclide value that is not listed in
    those Regulations.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}LSA-II material

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} LSA material is classified as LSA-II material if it
  consists of material

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} that is less than 225 L of water with a tritium
    concentration that has an activity level not greater than 0.8 TBq/L;
    or

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} in which the activity is distributed throughout and
    for which the estimated average specific activity does not exceed
    10^-4^ A~2~/g for solids and gases and 10^-5^ A~2~/g for liquids.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}LSA-III material

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} LSA material is classified as LSA-III material if it
  consists of solid material that is not in powder form and that meets
  the applicable requirements of the IAEA Regulations.

## [Licence]{.HTitleText1} {#h-817351 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Licensing requirements

- **[[6]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-6 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A person
  may transport a nuclear substance without a licence issued under
  subsection 24(2) of the Act for that purpose, except in the following
  cases:

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the nuclear substance is a Category I, II or III
    nuclear material, as defined in section 1 of the [Nuclear Security
    Regulations](/eng/regulations/SOR-2000-209), and is transported
    outside the area in which the material is required, under section 7
    of those Regulations, to be processed, used or stored;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the nuclear substance is in transit in a package of
    a certified design or in a package that has been approved as Type
    B(U)-96, Type C-96 or Type H(U)-96 by a foreign competent authority
    in accordance with the IAEA Regulations unless, in the case of
    transport by aircraft or ship, there is no scheduled stop in Canada;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} the nuclear substance is contained in a large
    object;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} the transport of the nuclear substance cannot meet
    the requirements of these Regulations;

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} the transport of the nuclear substance requires a
    special use vessel; or

  - [(f)]{.lawlabel} the transport of the nuclear substance requires
    multilateral approval of shipments in accordance with the IAEA
    Regulations.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Activity exemptions

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} A person may possess, transfer, import, export or use
  prescribed equipment without a licence issued under subsection 24(2)
  of the Act for that purpose.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Packaging exemptions

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} A person may package a nuclear substance without a
  licence issued under subsection 24(2) of the Act for that purpose.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Prohibitions imposed by section 26 of
  Act

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} For greater certainty, the exemptions established in
  subsections (1) to (3) relate only to the packaging and transport of
  nuclear substances and do not otherwise derogate from the prohibitions
  imposed by section 26 of the Act.

    - [(A)]{.lawlabel} radiation protection,

## [Packaging Requirements]{.HTitleText1} {#h-817418 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Type H(M) packages

**[[8]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-8 .sectionLabel}** Type H(M) packages must
meet the following requirements:

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} they must be designed and maintained to meet national
  or international standards other than the International Organization
  for Standardization standard ISO 7195 entitled Nuclear Energy ---
  Packaging of uranium hexafluoride (UF6) for transport, as amended from
  time to time, provided that an equivalent level of safety is
  maintained;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} they must withstand, without leakage and without
  unacceptable stress, a hydraulic test at an internal pressure of at
  least 1.38 MPa;

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} they must withstand, without loss or dispersal of
  uranium hexafluoride, the free drop test set out in the IAEA
  Regulations for normal conditions of transport;

- [(d)]{.lawlabel} they must withstand, without rupture of the
  containment system, the thermal test set out in the IAEA Regulations
  for accident conditions of transport, unless they are designed to
  contain 9 000 kg or more of uranium hexafluoride; and

- [(e)]{.lawlabel} they must not be equipped with pressure relief
  devices.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Type H(U) packages

**[[9]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-9 .sectionLabel}** Type H(U) packages must
meet the following requirements:

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} they must be designed and maintained to meet the
  International Organization for Standardization standard ISO 7195
  entitled Nuclear Energy --- Packaging of uranium hexafluoride (UF6)
  for transport, as amended from time to time;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} they must withstand, without leakage and without
  unacceptable stress, as specified in standard ISO 7195, as amended
  from time to time, the hydraulic test set out in the IAEA Regulations;

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} they must withstand, without loss or dispersal of
  uranium hexafluoride, the free drop test set out in the IAEA
  Regulations for normal conditions of transport;

- [(d)]{.lawlabel} they must withstand, without rupture of the
  containment system, the thermal test set out in the IAEA Regulations
  for accident conditions of transport; and

- [(e)]{.lawlabel} they must not be equipped with pressure relief
  devices.

  certified by the Commission or a designated officer before the value

    mass of uranium-235 provided that

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} there is no more than 3.5 g of uranium-235 per

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} fissile nuclides with a total mass not greater than
    2.0 g per package, provided that the total mass of fissile nuclides
    in the consignment is limited to 15 g; and

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} fissile nuclides with a total mass not greater than

      and production of the instrument or article, and

    - [(v)]{.lawlabel} instructions for the use, inspection, maintenance
      and disposal of the instrument or article; and

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} any other information necessary to demonstrate that
    the calculation meets the applicable requirements of these
    Regulations.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}New application for certification

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} No later than 60 days after the day on which the
  certificate expires, an applicant may make a new application for
  certification to the Commission or a designated officer if the
  calculation has not been modified. The application must include

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} a statement confirming that the calculation of the
    basic radionuclide value, including the principles used, assumptions
    made, scenarios considered and any data or formulae used to
    determine the calculation, has not been modified;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} in respect of an instrument or article, a statement
    confirming that the information previously submitted has not been
    modified or, if it has been modified, the revised information and a
    statement confirming that the modifications are without technical
    significance and do not affect safety;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} a copy of any applicable approval issued by the
    foreign competent authority in accordance with the IAEA Regulations
    since the last certification;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} a statement confirming that the instructions
    previously submitted in respect of the certified calculation have
    not been modified; and

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} any other information necessary to demonstrate that
    the calculation meets the applicable requirements of these
    Regulations.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Application for certification

- **[[14]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-14 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Modifications requiring new

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Right to be heard

## [Packaging and Transport of Radioactive Material]{.HTitleText1} {#h-817591 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}General obligations

- **[[25]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-25 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Every
  person who transports, or presents for transport, radioactive material
  must comply with the requirements of the [Transportation of Dangerous
  Goods Regulations](/eng/regulations/SOR-2001-286).

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Consignor's responsibilities

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Every consignor, other than a consignor of an
  excepted package, must comply with the requirements of the IAEA
  Regulations in respect of

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the provision of information for carriers;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the notification of competent authorities; and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} the possession of certificates and instructions.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Advising consignee

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} The consignor must advise the consignee that the
  material is going to be transported.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Carrier's responsibilities

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} Every carrier of radioactive material must

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} comply, in respect of transport and storage, with
    the requirements of the IAEA Regulations except in respect of
    placarding;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} transport the material in accordance with the
    consignor's instructions; and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} implement and maintain work procedures to ensure
    compliance with these Regulations and keep a record documenting
    those procedures.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Packages for transport

- **[[26]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-26 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A
  consignor may present for transport and a carrier may transport

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} radioactive material if the material is contained
    in

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} an excepted package,

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} a Type IP-1, Type IP-2 or Type IP-3 package,

    - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} a Type A package,

    - [(iv)]{.lawlabel} a Type B or Type C package of a certified
      design,

    - [(v)]{.lawlabel} a package of a certified design used to transport
      fissile material, or

    - [(vi)]{.lawlabel} a package of a certified design used to
      transport 0.1 kg or more of uranium hexafluoride;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the following if a licence has been issued for that
    purpose under subsection 24(2) of the Act:

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} a nuclear substance contained in a large object,

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} a nuclear substance whose transport does not
      meet all of the requirements of these Regulations,

    - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} a nuclear substance whose transport requires a
      special use vessel,

    - [(iv)]{.lawlabel} a nuclear substance whose transport requires
      multilateral approval of shipments in accordance with the IAEA
      Regulations, and

    - [(v)]{.lawlabel} a package that is in transit and is of a design
      that has been approved as a Type B(U)-96 or Type C-96 package by a
      foreign competent authority in accordance with the IAEA
      Regulations;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} unpackaged LSA-I material or an unpackaged SCO-I,
    in accordance with the IAEA Regulations; and

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} a package that contains 0.1 kg or more of uranium
    hexafluoride and that is of a design that has been approved as a
    Type H(U)-96 package by a foreign competent authority in accordance
    with the IAEA Regulations.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Activity or mass limits

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The activity or mass of the radioactive material
  contained in the package must be within the applicable limit for that
  type of package as set out

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} in the IAEA Regulations;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} in any applicable certificate; and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} in any applicable approval issued by a foreign
    competent authority in accordance with the IAEA Regulations.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Previously not requiring certification

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} Despite subsection (1), a package that does not
  require certification by the Commission and whose design meets the
  requirements of the 1985 or 1985 (as amended in 1990) edition of the
  IAEA Regulations may be used if

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the package meets the applicable requirements of
    section 25; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the packaging was neither manufactured nor modified
    after December 31, 2003.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Previously certified

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} Despite subsection (1), a package manufactured to a
  package design certified under the requirements of the 1973, 1973 (as
  amended), 1985 or 1985 (as amended in 1990) edition of the IAEA
  Regulations may continue to be used if

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the package meets the applicable requirements of
    section 25;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} its manufacture began before

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} January 1, 1996, for designs that meet the
      requirements of the 1973 or 1973 (as amended) edition of the IAEA
      Regulations, or

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} January 1, 2007, for designs that meet the
      requirements of the 1985 or 1985 (as amended in 1990) edition of
      the IAEA Regulations; or

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} it contains fissile material that meets the
    applicable requirements for fissile material of the editions of the
    IAEA Regulations issued after 2009.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Requirements

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} For packages prepared in accordance with the
  requirements of an edition of the IAEA Regulations before the 2012
  edition, if the material was considered fissile-excepted radioactive
  material under that earlier edition, and if it is neither excluded
  from the definition of fissile material nor excepted from the
  provisions applicable to fissile material in the editions of those
  Regulations issued after 2009, the package may be transported,
  provided that it is under exclusive use and the following formula
  yields a result of less than one:

  ::: {#817643 .Subsection}
  (A/B) + (C/D)

  where

  [A]{.dfn}
  :   is the mass in grams of uranium-235;

  [B]{.dfn}
  :   is 400 if the fissile material is mixed with substances that have
      an average hydrogen density less than or equal to water, or 290 in
      all other cases;

  [C]{.dfn}
  :   is the mass in grams of all other fissile nuclides, as defined in
      the IAEA Regulations; and

  [D]{.dfn}
  :   is 250 if the fissile material is mixed with substances that have
      an average hydrogen density less than or equal to water, or 180 in
      all other cases.
  :::

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Transport requirements for LSA and SCO

- **[[27]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-27
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Subject to subsections (2) and (3),
  both LSA material and an SCO must be transported in Type IP-3
  packages.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}No escape of radioactive contents

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} LSA-I material and an SCO-I may be transported
  unpackaged in accordance with the IAEA Regulations, but must be
  transported in a manner that ensures that, under routine conditions of

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Transport in accordance with IAEA
  Regulations

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} LSA material and an SCO may be transported in Type
  IP-1 packages and Type IP-2 packages in accordance with the IAEA
  Regulations if the LSA material and the SCO

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} are transported in conveyances that are not
    carrying passengers;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} are transported in conveyances or freight
    containers from one consignor only; and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} are only loaded at the consignor's location and
    unloaded at the consignee's location.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Responsibilities of consignors and
carriers under IAEA Regulations

- **[[28]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-28
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Consignors and carriers of

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Exceptions

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Despite subsection (1), a consignor may present for

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} the package or overpack is transported with goods

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} the package or overpack is only loaded at the

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Definition

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} For the purpose of subsection (2),
  [[certified]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} and [[exposure
  device]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} have the same meaning as in section 1 of

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Exceptions

## [Radiation Protection]{.HTitleText1} {#h-817705 .Part}

### [Definitions]{.HTitleText2} {#h-817706 .Subheading}

sections 31 and 33, [[committed]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}, [[equivalent

### [Radiation Protection Program]{.HTitleText2} {#h-817709 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Radiation protection program

- **[[31]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-31 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Every
  consignor, carrier or consignee of radioactive material, other than
  one who only handles or transports excepted packages, must implement a
  radiation protection program and must, as part of that program,

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} keep the amount of exposure to radon progeny and
    the effective dose and equivalent dose received by and committed to
    persons as low as reasonably achievable, taking into account social
    and economic factors, through the implementation of

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} management control over work practices,

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} personnel qualification and training,

    - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} control of exposure to radiation by personnel
      and the public, and

    - [(iv)]{.lawlabel} planning for unusual situations;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} prevent persons from receiving doses of radiation
    higher than the radiation dose limits prescribed by the [Radiation
    Protection Regulations](/eng/regulations/SOR-2000-203);

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} assess the radiation at the workplace and

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} conduct workplace or individual monitoring if it
      may reasonably be expected that the doses of radiation received by
      persons at the workplace will be 1 mSv or more but less than 5 mSv
      a year, or

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} conduct individual monitoring if it may
      reasonably be expected that the doses of radiation received by
      persons at the workplace will be 5 mSv a year or more; and

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} train the persons referred to in the program on the
    application of the program.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Requirement to keep records

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Every consignor, carrier or consignee must

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} keep a record documenting their radiation
    protection program and of any information collected under it; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} retain the record for a period ending two years
    after the day on which the information is collected.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}When dose limit exceeded

**[[32]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-32 .sectionLabel}** Every consignor, carrier
or consignee who becomes aware that a dose of radiation received by a
person may have exceeded an applicable dose limit prescribed by the
[Radiation Protection Regulations](/eng/regulations/SOR-2000-203) must

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} immediately notify the person and the Commission of
  the dose;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} conduct an investigation to determine the magnitude
  of the dose and to establish the causes of the exposure;

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} take any action required to prevent the occurrence of
  a similar incident; and

- [(d)]{.lawlabel} within 21 days after becoming aware that the dose
  limit may have been exceeded, report to the Commission the results of
  the investigation or the progress that has been made in conducting it.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Provision of information

- **[[33]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-33 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Every
  consignor, carrier or consignee must inform every nuclear energy
  worker that they employ, in writing,

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} of the fact that the worker is a nuclear energy
    worker;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} of the risks associated with the radiation to which
    the worker may be exposed in the course of their work, including the
    risks associated with the exposure of embryos and foetuses to
    radiation;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} of the applicable effective dose limits and
    equivalent dose limits prescribed by sections 13 and 14,
    respectively, of the [Radiation Protection
    Regulations](/eng/regulations/SOR-2000-203); and

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} of the worker's radiation dose levels.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Obligation to inform

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} In the case of a female nuclear energy worker, the
  consignor, carrier or consignee must

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} inform her, in writing, that she is required, as
    soon as she is aware that she is pregnant, to inform her employer of
    that fact in writing;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} inform her, in writing, of the applicable effective
    dose limits prescribed by the [Radiation Protection
    Regulations](/eng/regulations/SOR-2000-203); and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} on being informed of the pregnancy, make any
    accommodation that will not result in costs or business
    inconvenience constituting undue hardship to it to comply with the
    effective dose limits prescribed by section 13 of the [Radiation
    Protection Regulations](/eng/regulations/SOR-2000-203).

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Acknowledgement of information

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} Every consignor, carrier or consignee must obtain
  from each nuclear energy worker that they employ who is informed of
  the matters referred to in paragraphs (1)(a) and (b) and
  subsection (2) a written acknowledgement that the worker has received
  the information.

### [Personal Information]{.HTitleText2} {#h-817747 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Collection of personal information

- **[[34]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-34 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} If a
  consignor, carrier or consignee collects personal information, as
  defined in section 3 of the [Privacy Act](/eng/acts/P-21), that may be
  required to be disclosed to the Commission, another government
  institution, as defined in that section, or a dosimetry service, the
  consignor, carrier or consignee must inform the person to whom the
  information relates of the purpose for which it is being collected.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Required information

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Every nuclear energy worker whose work requires that
  they engage in activities that are subject to these Regulations must
  provide the following information to their employer:

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} their given names, surname and any previous
    surname;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} their Social Insurance Number;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} their gender;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} their date, province and country of birth; and

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} their dose record, as applicable, for the current
    one-year dosimetry period and five-year dosimetry period as those
    terms are defined in subsection 1(1) of the [Radiation Protection
    Regulations](/eng/regulations/SOR-2000-203).

### [Dangerous Occurrences]{.HTitleText2} {#h-817758 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Interpretation

**[[35]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-35 .sectionLabel}** For the purpose of
sections 36 to 38, a dangerous occurrence is any of the following
situations:

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} a conveyance carrying radioactive material is
  involved in an accident;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} a package shows evidence of damage, tampering or
  leakage of its contents, or its integrity is degraded in a manner that
  may reasonably be expected to impair its ability to comply with these
  Regulations or its certificate;

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} radioactive material is lost, stolen or no longer in
  the control of a person who is required to have control of it under
  the Act;

- [(d)]{.lawlabel} radioactive material has escaped from a containment
  system, a package or a conveyance during transport;

- [(e)]{.lawlabel} fissile material is outside the confinement system
  during transport;

- [(f)]{.lawlabel} the level of non-fixed contamination, as defined in
  the IAEA Regulations, during transport exceeds the following limits as
  applicable when averaged over any area of 300 cm^2^ of any part of the
  surface of the package or the conveyance:

  - [(i)]{.lawlabel} 4 Bq/cm^2^ for beta and gamma emitters and low
    toxicity alpha emitters, or

  - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} 0.4 Bq/cm^2^ for all other alpha emitters;

- [(g)]{.lawlabel} there is a failure to comply with the provisions of
  the Act, the provisions of these Regulations or any licence or
  certificate that is applicable to a package that may reasonably be
  expected to lead to a situation in which the environment, the health
  and safety of persons or national security is adversely affected.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Obligations in event of dangerous
occurrence

- **[[36]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-36 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  consignor, carrier or consignee of a package or radioactive material
  involved in a dangerous occurrence or any other person who controls
  any area affected by a dangerous occurrence must immediately

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} limit, to the extent possible, the dispersal of any
    radioactive material;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} place barriers, signs or personnel at every point
    of entry into the affected area to control the entry of persons into
    that area; and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} record the name, postal address and telephone
    number of any person who may have been exposed to or contaminated by
    radioactive material and request that the person remain available
    for assessment by an expert in radiation protection.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Expert assessment

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} As soon as feasible after a dangerous occurrence has
  occurred the consignor, carrier or consignee of the package or
  radioactive material involved in the occurrence must have an expert in
  radiation protection assess the situation. The expert must report the
  results of the assessment to the Commission as soon as feasible.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Preliminary report

- **[[37]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-37
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Immediately after becoming aware of
  a failure to comply with the requirements of section 26 or after the
  obligations set out in subsection 36(1) have been discharged, every
  consignor, carrier, consignee and holder of a licence to transport a
  package while in transit must make a preliminary report of the
  situation to the Commission.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Exception

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} No preliminary report is required for the dangerous
  occurrence referred to in paragraph 35(f) in respect of the internal
  surfaces of a tank or intermediate bulk container, as those terms are
  defined in the IAEA Regulations --- or of a freight container or
  conveyance --- that is dedicated to the transport of unpackaged
  radioactive material under exclusive use for as long as it remains
  under that specific exclusive use.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Informing consignor

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} Every carrier, consignee and holder of a licence
  referred to in subsection (1) must immediately notify the consignor if
  that person is not already aware of the failure to comply or of the
  dangerous occurrence.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Contents of preliminary report

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} All preliminary reports must include information on
  the location and circumstances of the failure to comply or of the
  dangerous occurrence and on any action that the consignor carrier,
  consignee or holder of a licence referred to in subsection (1) has
  taken or proposes to take with respect to it.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Full report

**[[38]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-38 .sectionLabel}** Within 21 days after the
failure to comply with the requirements of section 26 or after the
dangerous occurrence, the consignor, carrier and consignee and any
holder of a licence to transport a package while in transit must file a
full report with the Commission that includes the following information:

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} the date, time and location of the failure to comply
  or of the dangerous occurrence;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} the names of the persons involved;

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} the details of the packaging and packages;

- [(d)]{.lawlabel} the probable cause;

- [(e)]{.lawlabel} the effects on the environment, the health and safety
  of persons, and national or international security that have resulted
  or may result;

- [(f)]{.lawlabel} the doses of radiation that any person has received
  or is likely to have received; and

- [(g)]{.lawlabel} the actions taken to remedy the failure to comply or
  the dangerous occurrence and to prevent its recurrence.

## [Miscellaneous Provisions]{.HTitleText1} {#h-817796 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}[[Release]{.dfn}]{.DefinitionRef} under
Transportation of Dangerous Goods Act, 1992

**[[39]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-39 .sectionLabel}** For the purpose of the
definition [[release]{.dfn}]{.DefinitionRef} in section 2 of the
[Transportation of Dangerous Goods Act, 1992](/eng/acts/T-19.01), the
following levels of ionizing radiation are established:

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} in respect of a package that is being transported
  under exclusive use,

  - [(i)]{.lawlabel} 10 mSv/h on the external surface of the package,

  - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} 2 mSv/h on the surface of the conveyance, and

  - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} 0.1 mSv/h at a distance of 2 m from the surface
    of the conveyance; and

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} in respect of a package that is not being transported
  under exclusive use,

  - [(i)]{.lawlabel} 2 mSv/h on the external surface of the package,

  - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} 0.1 mSv/h at a distance of 1 m from the package,

  - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} 2 mSv/h on the surface of the conveyance, and

  - [(iv)]{.lawlabel} 0.1 mSv/h at a distance of 2 m from the surface of
    the conveyance.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Opening of packages

- **[[40]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-40 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A
  person, other than the consignor or the consignee of the package, may
  open a package only if

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} measures are taken to prevent persons from
    receiving doses of radiation higher than the radiation dose limits
    prescribed by the [Radiation Protection
    Regulations](/eng/regulations/SOR-2000-203); and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the package is opened in the presence of an expert
    in radiation protection.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Restoring opened package

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} If a person other than the consignor or the consignee
  opens a package while in transport, the person must restore the
  package to a condition that meets the requirements of these
  Regulations before forwarding it to the consignee.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Responsibilities on opening package

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} Every person who receives a package or who opens a
  package must, at that time, determine if any of the following
  conditions exist:

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the package is damaged;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the package has been tampered with;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} if the package contains fissile material, whether
    any portion of the fissile material is outside the confinement
    system; and

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} any portion of the contents of the package is
    outside the containment system.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Preliminary report

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} If any of the conditions exist, the person must
  immediately make a preliminary report to the Commission and to the
  consignor.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Contents of preliminary report

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} The preliminary report must include information on
  how and where the condition was discovered and on any action that the
  person has taken or proposes to take with respect to it.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Full report

  [(6)]{.lawlabel} Within 21 days after the condition has been
  discovered the consignor and the person who made the preliminary
  report must file a full report with the Commission that includes the
  following information:

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the date, time and location of the discovery of the
    condition;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the names of the persons involved;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} the details of the packaging and packages;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} the probable cause;

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} the effects on the environment, the health and
    safety of persons, and national or international security that have
    resulted or may result;

  - [(f)]{.lawlabel} the doses of radiation that any person has received
    or is likely to have received; and

  - [(g)]{.lawlabel} the actions taken to remedy the condition and to
    prevent its recurrence.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Undeliverable consignments

**[[41]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-41 .sectionLabel}** If a consignment cannot
be delivered to the consignee, the carrier must

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} notify the consignor, the consignee and the
  Commission; and

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} keep the consignment in an area to which access is
  controlled by the carrier until it can be delivered to the consignor
  or the consignee.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Records to be kept and retained

- **[[42]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-42 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Every
  person who packs radioactive material in a Type IP-2, Type IP-3 or
  Type A package must keep a record documenting the following
  information concerning the package:

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the technical specifications of its design;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the type, quantity and physical state of the
    radioactive material that it is designed to contain;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} any document that demonstrates that the package
    meets the requirements of these Regulations and the management
    system; and

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} instructions for packing, transport, receiving,
    maintenance and unpacking.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Period of retention

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Every person who is required to keep a record must
  retain it for a period ending two years after the day on which the
  packing occurs.
