## [Short Title]{.HTitleText1} {#h-977459 .Part}

**[[1]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-1 .sectionLabel}** These Regulations may be
cited as the [Canada Oil and Gas Geophysical Operations
Regulations](/eng/regulations/SOR-96-117).

## [Interpretation]{.HTitleText1} {#h-977461 .Part}

**[[2]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-2 .sectionLabel}** In these Regulations,

[[Act]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[Act]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means the [Canada Oil and Gas Operations
    Act](/eng/acts/O-7); ([Loi]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

[[complement]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[complement]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}, in respect of a vessel or
    platform from which an offshore geophysical operation is conducted,
    means all persons on the vessel or platform whose primary duties
    relate to the operation of the vessel or
    platform; ([équipage]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

[[conservation officer]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[conservation officer]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means the person
    appointed as such pursuant to section 53 of the Act; ([agent du
    contrôle de l'exploitation]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

[[explosive]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[explosive]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} has the same meaning as in section
    2 of the [Explosives
    Act](/eng/acts/E-17); ([explosif]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

[[geophysical crew]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[geophysical crew]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means all persons engaged
    in a geophysical operation, but does not include any member of the
    complement; ([équipe d'étude géophysique]{.DefinedTermLink
    lang="fr"})

[[geophysical operation]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[geophysical operation]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means the measurement
    or investigation, by indirect methods, of the subsurface of the
    earth for the purpose of locating oil or gas or of determining the
    nature of the seabed and subsurface conditions at a proposed
    drilling site or of a proposed pipeline route, and includes a
    seismic survey, resistivity survey, gravimetric survey, magnetic
    survey, electrical survey and geochemical survey and any work
    preparatory to that measurement or investigation, such as field
    tests of energy sources, calibration of instruments and cable
    ballasting, but does not include a velocity survey or a vertical
    seismic survey that is not a walkaway vertical seismic
    survey; ([étude géophysique]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

[[geophysical operation authorization]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[geophysical operation authorization]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means an
    authorization issued pursuant to paragraph 5(1)(b) of the Act to
    conduct a geophysical operation; ([autorisation d'étude
    géophysique]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

[[gravimetric survey]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[gravimetric survey]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means a geophysical
    operation that measures the properties of the earth's gravitational
    field; ([étude gravimétrique]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

[[interest]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[interest]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} has the same meaning as in section
    2 of the [Canada Petroleum Resources
    Act](/eng/acts/C-8.5); ([titre]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

[[magnetic survey]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[magnetic survey]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means a geophysical
    operation that measures the properties of the earth's magnetic
    field; ([étude magnétique]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

[[non-exclusive survey]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[non-exclusive survey]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means a geophysical
    operation that is conducted to acquire data for the purpose of sale,
    in whole or in part, to the public; ([étude non
    exclusive]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

[[offshore geophysical operation]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[offshore geophysical operation]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means a
    geophysical operation that is not an onshore geophysical
    operation; ([étude géophysique extracôtière]{.DefinedTermLink
    lang="fr"})

[[onshore geophysical operation]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[onshore geophysical operation]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means a
    geophysical operation that is conducted on or over land not normally
    submerged or on or over ice; ([étude géophysique sur
    terre]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

[[operator]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[operator]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means a person who holds a
    geophysical operation authorization; ([exploitant]{.DefinedTermLink
    lang="fr"})

[[participant]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[participant]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means a person who is a party to
    an agreement pursuant to which a participation survey is
    conducted; ([participant]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

[[participation survey]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[participation survey]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means a geophysical
    operation that is conducted by an operator pursuant to an agreement
    between the operator and one or more participants to acquire data
    that are to be shared among the participants; ([étude en
    participation]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

[[seismic energy source]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[seismic energy source]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means an energy source
    that is used to generate acoustic waves in a seismic
    survey; ([source d'énergie sismique]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

[[seismic survey]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[seismic survey]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means a geophysical operation
    that uses a seismic energy source to generate acoustic waves that
    propagate through the earth, are reflected from or refracted along
    subsurface layers of the earth, and are subsequently
    recorded; ([étude sismique]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

[[shotpoint]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[shotpoint]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means the surface location of a
    seismic energy source. ([point de tir]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

## [PART I]{.HLabel1}[General]{.HTitleText1} {#h-977482 .Part}

### [Geophysical Operation Authorization]{.HTitleText2} {#h-977483 .Subheading}

**[[3]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-3 .sectionLabel}** Any person may apply for a
geophysical operation authorization by submitting to the Chief
Conservation Officer three copies of a completed application form.

- **[[4]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-4 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Subject
  to section 5, an application in respect of an offshore geophysical
  operation shall be submitted not less than

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} 30 days before the planned commencement date of the
    operation, if chemical explosives are not the proposed seismic
    energy source; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} 90 days before the planned commencement date of the
    operation, if chemical explosives are the proposed seismic energy
    source.

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} Subject to section 5, an application in respect of an
  onshore geophysical operation shall be submitted not less than 30 days
  before the planned commencement date of the operation.

<!-- -->

- **[[5]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-5 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} An
  application to extend the duration for which a geophysical operation
  has been authorized shall be submitted at least 15 days before the end
  of the period being extended or, where the commencement date is being
  modified, the planned new commencement date.

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} An application in respect of any modification of a
  geophysical operation that has been authorized, other than a
  modification of its duration, shall be submitted at least 15 days
  before the commencement of the geophysical operation or, where the
  geophysical operation has commenced, the start of the geophysical
  operation as modified.

**[[6]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-6 .sectionLabel}** When a geophysical
operation is commenced, terminated or cancelled by an operator, the
operator shall forthwith notify the Chief Conservation Officer in
writing of the date of commencement, termination or cancellation.

**[[7]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-7 .sectionLabel}** Every operator shall post a
copy of the geophysical operation authorization in a conspicuous
location in the vessel, platform or aircraft from which the geophysical
operation is conducted or, in the case of an onshore geophysical
operation, at the field location of that operation.

### [Damage to Property]{.HTitleText2} {#h-977495 .Subheading}

**[[8]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-8 .sectionLabel}** Every operator shall take
all reasonable safeguards against damage to property as a result of a
geophysical operation.

### [Fire]{.HTitleText2} {#h-977497 .Subheading}

**[[9]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-9 .sectionLabel}** Where a fire occurs as a
result of a geophysical operation, the operator shall take all safe and
reasonable measures to control and extinguish the fire and to minimize
any danger to persons, property or the environment that results or may
reasonably be expected to result from the fire.

### [Refuse]{.HTitleText2} {#h-977499 .Subheading}

**[[10]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-10 .sectionLabel}** Subject to any other
applicable law, every operator shall ensure that all refuse produced as
a result of a geophysical operation is handled in the following manner:

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} all fuel, oil, oily material or lubricants are
  collected in a closed system that is designed for that purpose;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} all oil or oily material that is not burned at the
  field location where the operation is conducted and all
  non-combustible material is transported in a suitable container to,
  and disposed of at, a suitable waste disposal facility on land; and

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} where combustible material is burned on a vessel or
  platform, precautions are taken to ensure that the fire does not
  endanger any person or the safety of the vessel or platform.

## [PART II]{.HLabel1}[Offshore Geophysical Operations]{.HTitleText1} {#h-977504 .Part}

### [Air Gun System]{.HTitleText2} {#h-977505 .Subheading}

**[[11]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-11 .sectionLabel}** Where an operator who is
conducting an offshore geophysical operation uses or intends to use an
air gun as a seismic energy source, the operator shall ensure that

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} all air gun components are maintained in good
  operating condition and are kept free from dirt, oil and excess
  grease;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} during the operation, the air vessels, air manifolds,
  air lines, electrical lines and the compressor of the air gun system
  are regularly inspected for signs of abrasion and wear, and that the
  compressor, where defective, and any defective air vessels are
  promptly repaired or replaced and any defective manifolds or lines are
  promptly replaced;

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} all fittings, valves, hoses, electrical lines, pipes
  or other components used for an air gun comply with the manufacturer's
  specifications for that air gun;

- [(d)]{.lawlabel} where there is air pressure in the air gun, the
  pressure is maintained as low as is practicable but sufficiently high
  to ensure that the air gun remains seated and that there is no danger
  of accidental firing;

- [(e)]{.lawlabel} no maintenance of the air gun is carried out until

  - [(i)]{.lawlabel} the air pressure in the air gun and the air line
    connected to the air gun has been completely bled off, and

  - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} the shuttle of the air gun can be moved freely by
    use of a wooden safety tool to confirm that the air gun has been
    completely depressurized; and

- [(f)]{.lawlabel} where more than one air gun is used as a seismic
  energy source, a procedure is established and followed for the
  connection of each air gun to its air line and pressure control valve.

### [Air Gun Testing]{.HTitleText2} {#h-977515 .Subheading}

- **[[12]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-12 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Where
  an air gun is test-fired on the deck of a vessel or platform during an
  offshore geophysical operation, the operator shall ensure that the
  person who is responsible for the operation and maintenance of the air
  gun is present during the test.

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} Where a test referred to in subsection (1) is carried
  out, the responsible person referred to in that subsection shall
  ensure that

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} before the test-firing, a siren is sounded to alert
    all persons aboard the vessel or platform of an impending air gun
    test-firing operation, in time to allow evacuation of an area within
    an 8 m radius of the test-firing site;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} not more than one air gun is test-fired at one
    time;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} before the test-firing, an inspection is done to
    ensure that the area within an 8 m radius of the test-firing site is
    clear of unauthorized persons;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} all pipes and hoses connected to the air gun that
    are subject to high pressure are secured or equipped with safety
    chains to prevent whipping of the pipes or hoses when air pressure
    is injected into them;

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} the air pressure in the air gun is below 500 psi;
    and

  - [(f)]{.lawlabel} the person in charge of the vessel or platform is
    advised that the test is being carried out.

- [(3)]{.lawlabel} During an offshore geophysical operation, no air gun
  shall be test-fired while the air gun is in the water if there are
  divers within 1,500 m of the air gun.

- [(4)]{.lawlabel} During an offshore geophysical operation, no air gun
  shall be test-fired on a vessel or platform without the approval of
  the Chief Safety Officer.

### [Gas Exploders]{.HTitleText2} {#h-977527 .Subheading}

**[[13]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-13 .sectionLabel}** Where an operator who is
conducting an offshore geophysical operation uses or intends to use a
gas exploder as a seismic energy source, the operator shall ensure that

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} no person smokes, welds or brazes in any area that is
  in close proximity to any gas cylinders or inflammable liquid tanks;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} gas storage areas are properly ventilated;

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} all valves and fittings used on a gas cylinder are
  approved by the manufacturer of the cylinder for use on the cylinder;

- [(d)]{.lawlabel} all equipment used for handling explosives is
  approved by the manufacturer of the equipment for the handling of
  explosives;

- [(e)]{.lawlabel} every gas cylinder and inflammable liquid tank is
  stored in an area set aside for that purpose and signs warning of the
  hazard of explosion are posted in conspicuous locations in that area;

- [(f)]{.lawlabel} every propane or butane cylinder is stored at the
  greatest possible distance from any oxygen cylinder or inflammable
  liquid tank; and

- [(g)]{.lawlabel} every gas cylinder is protected from overheating.

### [Electrical Seismic Energy Sources]{.HTitleText2} {#h-977536 .Subheading}

**[[14]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-14 .sectionLabel}** Where an operator who is
conducting an offshore geophysical operation uses or intends to use an
electrical seismic energy source, the operator shall ensure that

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} the charging and discharging circuits of the
  electrical seismic energy source are equipped with circuit breakers;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} the electrical cables of the electrical seismic
  energy source are protected from damage and are adequately insulated
  and grounded to prevent current leakage and electrical shock; and

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} the electrical seismic energy source, when tested, is
  fully immersed in water.

### [Helicopter Support]{.HTitleText2} {#h-977541 .Subheading}

**[[15]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-15 .sectionLabel}** Where a helicopter is
used in an offshore geophysical operation, the operator shall ensure
that

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} the helicopter deck on the vessel or platform from
  which the operation is conducted is designed, constructed and operated
  in accordance with the Guidelines Respecting Helicopter Facilities on
  Ships, TP 4414, published in December 1986 by the Canadian Coast
  Guard, as amended from time to time; and

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} an immersion suit that complies with the Canadian
  General Standards Board Standard CAN/CGSB-65.17-M88, Helicopter
  Passenger Transportation Suit System, published in January 1988, as
  amended from time to time, is worn by every member of the geophysical
  crew who is on a flight to or from the vessel or platform from which
  the operation is conducted.

## [PART III]{.HLabel1}[Onshore Geophysical Operations]{.HTitleText1} {#h-977545 .Part}

### [Survey Monuments]{.HTitleText2} {#h-977546 .Subheading}

**[[16]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-16 .sectionLabel}** Every operator who is
conducting an onshore geophysical operation shall

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} determine the location of survey monuments in the
  vicinity of the field location of the operation and along any access
  roads or trails;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} before the movement of any equipment, ensure that all
  survey monuments are clearly flagged; and

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} ensure that no work that is related to the operation
  is conducted within 2 m of a survey monument.

### [Seismic Energy Sources]{.HTitleText2} {#h-977551 .Subheading}

- **[[17]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-17 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Every
  operator who is conducting an onshore geophysical operation shall,
  when determining the location for a seismic energy source, ensure that
  the seismic energy source

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} is located so that, when the energy source is
    activated, no damage is caused to any wells, mines, pipelines,
    buried utilities, buildings or dams;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} is placed at least 2 m from any driveway, gateway
    or buried telephone or other communication line;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} where the energy source is a charge comprised of
    explosives, is placed

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} where the quantity of explosives being used for
      the charge is set out in column I of an item of Schedule I, at
      least the distance set out in column II of that item from any oil
      or gas well or the centre line of any oil or gas pipeline, and

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} where the quantity of explosives being used for
      the charge is set out in column I of an item of Schedule I, at
      least twice the distance set out in column II of that item from
      any dam, residence, area of public congregation or water well; and

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} where the energy source is other than a charge
    referred to in paragraph (c), is placed at least

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} 100 m from any dam,

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} 15 m from any oil or gas well or the centre line
      of any oil or gas pipeline,

    - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} 50 m from any residence, structure with a
      concrete base or area of public congregation, and

    - [(iv)]{.lawlabel} 100 m from any water well if the energy source
      is vibroseis, or 50 m from such a well if the energy source is not
      vibroseis.

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} An operator shall not allow more than 500 kg of
  explosives to be detonated in any shot hole or array of shot holes.

- [(3)]{.lawlabel} Every operator who is conducting an onshore
  geophysical operation shall ensure that

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} magazines that contain a quantity of explosives set
    out in column I of an item of Schedule II are located at least

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} the distance set out in column II of that item
      from any highway or road accessible to the public, any railway,
      airfield, bank of a navigable or recreational waterway, park or
      other recreational area, or the work area for the operation, and

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} twice the distance set out in column II of that
      item from any building or storage area for inflammable substances
      in bulk; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} magazines are located or protected so that they
    will not be damaged by accidental impact.

### [Preparation of a Charge]{.HTitleText2} {#h-977570 .Subheading}

- **[[18]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-18 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Every
  operator who is conducting an onshore geophysical operation shall,
  when charges are being prepared, ensure that

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} no tools other than tools made of bronze or another
    non-sparking material are used to cut or pierce a cartridge;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} there is no stripping of cartridges;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} priming is done only at the blasting site and all
    explosives, other than the charge to be loaded into the shot hole,
    are kept inside a magazine until the primed cartridge is loaded into
    the shot hole;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} no detonating cord is capped and no cartridge is
    primed in any place where explosives are stored; and

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} the detonating cord is handled in a manner that
    prevents bending or pinching of the cord.

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} Every operator who is conducting an onshore
  geophysical operation shall ensure that all the electric detonators
  used in a circuit are of the same design and made by the same
  manufacturer.

### [Drilling Shot Holes for Charges]{.HTitleText2} {#h-977579 .Subheading}

- **[[19]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-19 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Every
  operator who is conducting an onshore geophysical operation shall,
  when a shot hole is being drilled for a charge, ensure that

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} no drilling is done within 5 m of a shot hole that
    contains a charge, whether or not the charge has been detonated; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the shot hole is of sufficient size to allow the
    insertion of a charge into position in the shot hole without the use
    of undue force.

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} Every operator who is conducting an onshore
  geophysical operation shall, when a shot hole is being drilled in an
  area that is prone to deposits of shallow gas, ensure that

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the drilling rig is positioned, with respect to the
    wind, so that gas encountered during drilling will not accumulate in
    the vicinity of the rig;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the drilling rig is free from heat sources that
    might ignite any gas that has accumulated in the vicinity of the
    rig; and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} the engine is equipped with air intake shut-off
    valves that can be activated by the driller.

- [(3)]{.lawlabel} Every operator who is conducting an onshore
  geophysical operation shall, when gas is encountered during drilling,
  and where it can be done safely, allowing sufficient time to permit
  evacuation, ensure that the air intake shut-off valves on the engine
  are placed in the off position.

### [Loading Charges into Shot Holes]{.HTitleText2} {#h-977589 .Subheading}

- **[[20]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-20 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Every
  operator who is conducting an onshore geophysical operation shall,
  when charges are being loaded into a shot hole, ensure that

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} detonator lead wires are unravelled or unwound
    slowly when a charge is being lowered into a shot hole and are not
    unravelled or unwound by being thrown or dragged along the ground;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} damaged lead wires and damaged connecting wires are
    not used in blasting circuits;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} every member of the geophysical crew who is engaged
    in the handling of explosives or involved in the blasting operation
    is warned of the potential build-up of static electricity on the
    member's clothing or in the atmosphere as a result of drifting sand
    or snow and of possible accidental firing of detonators if detonator
    lead wires are thrown to the ground;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} loading poles and pole extension fittings for them
    are made of non-sparking, anti-static material;

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} undue force is not used to insert a charge into
    position in a shot hole; and

  - [(f)]{.lawlabel} any device that is used to decrease the buoyancy of
    a charge or to anchor a charge in a shot hole is made of
    non-sparking material.

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} Every operator who is conducting an onshore
  geophysical operation shall, once a charge is loaded into a shot hole,
  ensure that

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the detonator lead wires remain shunted at all
    times except during circuit testing;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} detonation of the charge occurs within 30 days
    after the day on which the charge is loaded into the shot hole, or
    any longer period approved by a conservation officer; and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} in inhabited areas or areas where there is the
    possibility that the detonator lead wires or detonating cord of the
    charge may be tampered with, a temporary plug is placed in the shot
    hole and the ground in the vicinity of the shot hole is levelled.

- [(3)]{.lawlabel} Every operator who is conducting an onshore
  geophysical operation shall ensure that

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} when blasting in the vicinity of buildings,
    railways, roads or inhabited areas, the charge used is no larger
    than required;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} when blasting within 50 m of an overhead power
    line, the shooter who is responsible for the blasting uses

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} detonating cord as a downline to the charge, and

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} a short detonator lead wire to initiate the
      detonating cord if the total detonator lead wire length is less
      than the distance from the overhead power line to the nearest
      point on the ground at the blasting site;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} when blasting in the area of a commercial
    electromagnetic transmitter with power in an amount that is set out
    in column I of an item of Schedule III, a charge is not loaded into
    a shot hole, primed or detonated unless the shot hole is located at
    least the distance set out in column II of that item from the base
    of the transmitter mast; and

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} the circuit of every electric detonator is tested
    with a blaster's galvanometer immediately after the charge is loaded
    into a shot hole and, if the test indicates that the circuit is
    open, no attempt is made to remove the charge and a fresh primed
    cartridge is inserted into the shot hole.

- [(4)]{.lawlabel} Every operator who is conducting an onshore
  geophysical operation shall ensure that charges are not loaded into a
  shot hole during an electrical storm or when an electrical storm is
  imminent.

### [Flagging Charged Shot Holes]{.HTitleText2} {#h-977610 .Subheading}

**[[21]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-21 .sectionLabel}** Every operator who is
conducting an onshore geophysical operation shall ensure that all shot
holes that contain a charge are clearly flagged.

### [Firing Charges]{.HTitleText2} {#h-977612 .Subheading}

**[[22]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-22 .sectionLabel}** Every operator who is
conducting an onshore geophysical operation shall ensure that

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} detonator lead wires remain shunted at all times
  except when the charges are ready to be fired and during circuit
  testing after loading;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} all blasting equipment at the blasting site is under
  the direct supervision and control of the shooter;

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} when conducting a blasting operation in the vicinity
  of buildings, railways, roads or inhabited areas, the shooter

  - [(i)]{.lawlabel} takes adequate precautions to prevent any damage to
    property, and

  - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} places warning signs or barricades or uses
    flagpersons to ensure that no persons other than those engaged in
    the blasting operation remain in the area made dangerous by the
    blasting operation;

- [(d)]{.lawlabel} when blasting operations are being carried out, the
  shooter takes adequate precautions to ensure that no charge is fired
  until all persons in the vicinity of the charge are protected by
  suitable cover from falling rocks, flying debris, mud and any other
  material disturbed or displaced as a result of the detonation or are
  at a safe distance from the charge;

- [(e)]{.lawlabel} detonators that are used near the surface of the
  ground are covered in such a manner that any fragments of metal and
  debris resulting from the detonation are confined;

- [(f)]{.lawlabel} a radio transmitter is not used at or near a blasting
  site when there are detonators outside of a magazine and above ground;

- [(g)]{.lawlabel} all blasting equipment is maintained in good working
  condition;

- [(h)]{.lawlabel} repair work to blasting equipment or to the firing
  cable is not conducted while the firing cable is wired to charges;

- [(i)]{.lawlabel} the blasting equipment is disconnected from the
  blasting circuit and the ends of the lead wires connected to the
  charge are twisted together

  - [(i)]{.lawlabel} immediately after firing, if the charge fails to
    detonate, and

  - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} before any member of the geophysical crew inspects
    any shot hole that contains or may contain explosives;

- [(j)]{.lawlabel} every detonator remains disconnected from the firing
  cable until the next charge or the next series or pattern of charges
  is ready to be fired and until the shotpoint is clear of all persons;

- [(k)]{.lawlabel} all shot holes containing a charge are fired before
  the termination of the operation;

- [(l)]{.lawlabel} blasting operations are not carried out during an
  electrical storm or when an electrical storm is imminent;

- [(m)]{.lawlabel} anti-static detonators are used where possible for
  all blasting operations; and

- [(n)]{.lawlabel} all electric detonator lead wires and other refuse
  from the blasting operation are retrieved from the field location of
  the operation.

### [Detonating Cords]{.HTitleText2} {#h-977632 .Subheading}

- **[[23]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-23 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Every
  operator who is conducting an onshore geophysical operation shall
  ensure that, when a detonating cord is used in water,

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the end of the cord is sealed;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the entire length of cord is submerged before the
    firing cable is connected to the blasting equipment; and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} the charge is detonated as soon as possible after
    loading.

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} Every operator who is conducting an onshore
  geophysical operation shall ensure that, when a detonating cord is
  used on damp ground, the charge is detonated as soon as possible after
  loading.

- [(3)]{.lawlabel} Every operator who is conducting an onshore
  geophysical operation shall ensure that no vehicle is driven over a
  detonating cord.

### [Misfired Charges]{.HTitleText2} {#h-977640 .Subheading}

- **[[24]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-24 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Every
  operator who is conducting an onshore geophysical operation shall, in
  respect of any charge that has not detonated as a result of misfire,
  ensure that

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} no attempt is made to remove the charge from the
    shot hole; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} an attempt is made to detonate the charge promptly
    by means of a fresh primer or by inserting and detonating another
    charge in that shot hole.

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} When a charge fails to detonate after an attempt is
  made under paragraph (1)(b), the operator shall ensure that the charge
  and the lead wires that are connected to it are buried in the shot
  hole and that the shot hole is plugged in accordance with section 25.

- [(3)]{.lawlabel} Every operator who is conducting an onshore
  geophysical operation shall, on completion of the operation, report in
  writing to a conservation officer the location of all charges that
  failed to detonate.

### [Plugging Shot Holes]{.HTitleText2} {#h-977647 .Subheading}

- **[[25]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-25 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Every
  operator who is conducting an onshore geophysical operation shall,
  after a charge has detonated, ensure that the shot hole is plugged by

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} filling the shot hole with drilling mud or cuttings
    to the point where the plug required by paragraph (b) is to be
    inserted;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} inserting a shot hole plug of a type approved by
    the Chief Conservation Officer to a depth of at least 30 cm below
    the surface;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} filling the shot hole above the plug with drilling
    mud or cuttings and tamping the contents into the shot hole; and

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} spreading any remaining drilling mud or cuttings
    over the ground in the vicinity of the shot hole.

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} Where, during an onshore geophysical operation, water
  or gas comes to the surface of a shot hole, the operator shall

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} in the case of water, immediately attempt to plug
    the shot hole to confine the water; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} in the case of gas, immediately evacuate the site
    until the gas has dissipated.

- [(3)]{.lawlabel} Every operator who, during an onshore geophysical
  operation, disturbs a shot hole from a previous geophysical operation
  shall ensure that the shot hole is plugged in accordance with
  subsection (1).

### [Walkaway Vertical Seismic and Resistivity Surveys]{.HTitleText2} {#h-977658 .Subheading}

**[[26]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-26 .sectionLabel}** Every operator who is
conducting an onshore geophysical operation shall ensure that

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} in the case of a walkaway vertical seismic survey,
  all shot holes in an area where drilling operations for a well are
  being conducted are clearly marked; and

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} in the case of a resistivity survey, all electrodes
  are clearly flagged or cordoned off to prevent accidental human
  contact with the electrodes.

### [Archaeological Sites]{.HTitleText2} {#h-977662 .Subheading}

- **[[27]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-27 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Where
  an archaeological site or a burial ground is discovered during an
  onshore geophysical operation, the operator shall so inform a
  conservation officer and suspend the operation in the immediate area
  of the discovery until permitted by the conservation officer to resume
  the operation in that area.

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} A conservation officer shall permit the resumption of
  a geophysical operation that was suspended under subsection (1) if the
  conservation officer, after consultation with the Minister of
  Communications, is satisfied that the operation will not disturb the
  archaeological site or the burial ground and will not affect the
  archaeological or other special characteristics or the nature of the
  site or ground.

## [PART IV]{.HLabel1}[Occupational Safety and Health]{.HTitleText1} {#h-977666 .Part}

### [Radio Communication]{.HTitleText2} {#h-977667 .Subheading}

**[[28]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-28 .sectionLabel}** Every operator shall
ensure that radio communication is maintained

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} in the case of an onshore geophysical operation, with
  all vehicles in the vicinity of the operation, to the extent possible;
  and

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} in the case of an offshore geophysical operation,
  with all vessels and platforms in the vicinity of the operation and
  with a shore-based station.

### [Safe Working Practices]{.HTitleText2} {#h-977671 .Subheading}

**[[29]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-29 .sectionLabel}** Every operator shall
ensure that all equipment and materials that are used during a
geophysical operation are handled, operated and maintained in accordance
with the manufacturers' specifications.

**[[30]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-30 .sectionLabel}** Every operator who is
conducting an offshore geophysical operation shall ensure that every
member of the geophysical crew

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} wears a suitable personal flotation device at all
  times when the member is working on deck;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} is equipped with a safety belt and a safety line
  whenever the member is positioned or working near the cable reel or
  working on the back deck during periods when there is any possibility
  of the member falling or being thrown or swept overboard;

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} does not work alone on the back deck; and

- [(d)]{.lawlabel} wears high visibility clothing.

**[[31]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-31 .sectionLabel}** Every operator who is
conducting a geophysical operation shall ensure that an evacuation route
is set up from each work station and that the route is accessible to
every member of the geophysical crew who is working at that station.

**[[32]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-32 .sectionLabel}** Every operator who is
conducting an onshore geophysical operation shall ensure that

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} every member of the geophysical crew wears high
  visibility clothing when working at the field location of the
  operation; and

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} every vehicle used in the geophysical operation is
  equipped with at least one portable fire extinguisher with a 5B
  rating.

### [No Smoking]{.HTitleText2} {#h-977682 .Subheading}

- **[[33]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-33 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} No
  person shall smoke near a marine recording cable or in any area where
  inflammable materials or explosives are being used or stored in the
  course of any geophysical operation.

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} Every operator shall post, near the cable and in each
  area referred to in subsection (1), a sign prohibiting smoking.

### [Hours of Work]{.HTitleText2} {#h-977686 .Subheading}

- **[[34]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-34
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Subject to subsection (2), every
  operator shall ensure that no member of the geophysical crew is
  required to work

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} a shift in excess of 12 consecutive hours; or

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} two successive shifts the combined total of which
    exceeds 12 hours unless that member has had at least 6 consecutive
    hours of rest between those shifts.

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} Subsection (1) does not apply to any member of the
  geophysical crew who is required to work in the case of an emergency.

### [Training of Geophysical Crew]{.HTitleText2} {#h-977692 .Subheading}

- **[[35]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-35 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Every
  operator shall ensure that every member of the geophysical crew

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} is familiar with the safety equipment that the
    member may use, and with the safety procedures that the member may
    have to carry out during the operation;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} undergoes the instruction, training and drills
    necessary to enable the member to cope with both normal operations
    and emergency situations; and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} is familiar with the Safety Manual for Geophysical
    Field Operations, 6th edition, 1986, published by the International
    Association of Geophysical Contractors, as amended from time to
    time.

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} Every operator who is conducting an offshore
  geophysical operation shall ensure that every member of the
  geophysical crew has successfully completed

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} a survival course approved by the Chief Safety
    Officer; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} a helicopter underwater escape course approved by
    the Chief Safety Officer, where regular changes of geophysical crew
    by helicopter are planned.

- [(3)]{.lawlabel} The Chief Safety Officer shall approve

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} a course referred to in paragraph (2)(a) if the
    Chief Safety Officer is satisfied that the course will provide an
    adequate level of knowledge of the hazards and emergencies that are
    likely to be encountered on a vessel or platform that is engaged in
    a geophysical operation and of techniques for surviving those
    hazards and emergencies; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} a course referred to in paragraph (2)(b) if the
    Chief Safety Officer is satisfied that the course will provide
    adequate training in the methods of escaping from a helicopter that
    is underwater.

- [(4)]{.lawlabel} Every operator shall ensure that only those members
  of the geophysical crew who are trained in the operation and
  maintenance of the seismic energy source and the components of seismic
  energy systems will be responsible for their handling and maintenance.

### [Access to Oil and Gas Occupational Safety and Health Regulations]{.HTitleText2} {#h-977705 .Subheading}

**[[36]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-36 .sectionLabel}** Every operator shall keep
a copy of the [Oil and Gas Occupational Safety and Health
Regulations](/eng/regulations/SOR-87-612) at the field location of each
geophysical operation in a place that is accessible to the geophysical
crew.

## [PART V]{.HLabel1}[Reporting Requirements]{.HTitleText1} {#h-977707 .Part}

### [Status Report]{.HTitleText2} {#h-977708 .Subheading}

**[[37]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-37 .sectionLabel}** Every operator shall
submit to the Chief Conservation Officer, at the commencement and
termination of the geophysical operation and once a week during the
operation, in a manner and form approved by the Chief Conservation
Officer, a report on the progress of the operation that includes

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} the number assigned to the operation that is the
  subject of the geophysical operation authorization;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} the identification of the lines on which the data are
  collected;

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} the quantity of data collected per line;

- [(d)]{.lawlabel} the location and status of any vessels and platforms
  from which the operation is conducted;

- [(e)]{.lawlabel} any unusual weather conditions or other incidents
  that cause downtime; and

- [(f)]{.lawlabel} the location of any shot hole referred to in
  subsection 25(2).

### [Final Report]{.HTitleText2} {#h-977716 .Subheading}

- **[[38]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-38
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Subject to subsection (3), within 12
  months after the date of termination of a geophysical operation, every
  operator shall submit to the Chief Conservation Officer a report that
  includes

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} a title page that indicates the number that is
    assigned to the operation that is the subject of the geophysical
    operation authorization, the report title, the type of operation
    conducted, the location of the operation, the duration of operations
    at that field location, the names of the contractors, the operator,
    the interest owners, if any, as defined in section 2 of the [Canada
    Petroleum Resources Act](/eng/acts/C-8.5), and the author, and the
    date of the report;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} a table of contents;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} an introduction or abstract;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} location maps that show the boundaries of the area
    that is subject to each interest covered by the operation and the
    identification number of each such interest;

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} a summary of significant dates, the number of
    members of the complement, if applicable, the number of members of
    the geophysical crew, the type and number of each type of equipment
    used, the production data, the total distance surveyed, the downtime
    per day, and the number of kilometres of data recorded per day;

  - [(f)]{.lawlabel} a summary of weather, sea, ice and topographic
    conditions and their effect on the operation;

  - [(g)]{.lawlabel} a general description of the operation including
    the instrument type, the accuracy of the navigation, positioning and
    survey systems, the parameters for the energy source and recording
    system and the field configuration of the source lines and the
    receiver lines;

  - [(h)]{.lawlabel} a detailed description of the geophysical data
    processing method including the processing sequence and the
    processing parameters for seismic, magnetic, gravimetric and other
    geophysical surveys;

  - [(i)]{.lawlabel} shotpoint maps, track plots, flight lines with
    numbered fiducial points, gravity station maps and, for seabed
    surveys, location maps for core holes, grab samples and seabed
    photographs;

  - [(j)]{.lawlabel} a fully processed, migrated seismic section for
    each seismic line recorded and, in the case of a 3-D survey, each
    line generated from the 3-D data set;

  - [(k)]{.lawlabel} a high-resolution section for each line recorded in
    a well-site seabed survey or a pipeline route survey;

  - [(l)]{.lawlabel} a series of gravity and magnetic profiles across
    all gravimetric and magnetic surveys for which interpretative maps
    have not been made;

  - [(m)]{.lawlabel} shotpoint location data;

  - [(n)]{.lawlabel} bathymetric and topographic maps that are compiled
    from the data collected;

  - [(o)]{.lawlabel} interpretative maps that are appropriate to the
    data collected including

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} structure and isopach maps, time structure and
      time interval maps, velocity and residual velocity maps, and
      seismic amplitude and character change maps,

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} final Bouguer gravity maps and any residual or
      other processed gravity maps, and

    - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} final total magnetic intensity contour maps and
      any residual, gradient or other processed magnetic maps;

  - [(p)]{.lawlabel} synthetic seismograms and seismic modelling studies
    that use synthetic seismograms, vertical seismic profiles at wells
    that were used in the interpretation of the operation data,
    amplitude versus offset studies, and seismic inversion sections, if
    any; and

  - [(q)]{.lawlabel} the interpretation of maps and seismic sections
    including

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} geological and geophysical correlations,

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} where applicable, correlations between gravity,
      magnetic and seismic data,

    - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} in the case of seabed surveys, the geophysical
      correlation of shallow seismic data with data from cores and
      geotechnical boreholes,

    - [(iv)]{.lawlabel} details of corrections or adjustments that were
      applied to the data during processing or compilation, and

    - [(v)]{.lawlabel} the operator's velocity information that was used
      in a time-to-depth conversion.

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} An operator shall incorporate in a map submitted
  pursuant to paragraph (1)(o) any previous data collected by the
  operator that are related to the area covered by the map and that are
  of a type similar to the data from which the map was produced.

- [(3)]{.lawlabel} An operator who has conducted a non-exclusive survey
  need not, in the report required by subsection (1), provide the
  information and materials described in paragraphs (1)(n) to (q) in
  respect of data that are available for purchase by the public.

- [(4)]{.lawlabel} Where an operator who has conducted a non-exclusive
  survey ceases to make available for purchase by the public any data
  from that survey that were so available, the operator shall, within 12
  months after the date on which the operator ceased to make the data
  available, submit to the Chief Conservation Officer a supplementary
  report that contains the information and materials described in
  paragraphs (1)(n) to (q) in respect of the data, unless the Chief
  Conservation Officer has received a report pursuant to subsection (5)
  that includes such information and materials.

- [(5)]{.lawlabel} Every purchaser of geophysical data that arise from a
  geophysical operation in an area that is subject to an interest, where
  the costs of the purchase of the data are credited against deposit or
  rental requirements of the interest, and every participant shall
  submit to the Chief Conservation Officer a report that contains all of
  the information and materials described in paragraphs (1)(n) to (q)
  that have been prepared by or for that purchaser or participant.

- [(6)]{.lawlabel} Where a purchaser of geophysical data that arise from
  a geophysical operation in an area that is subject to an interest has
  reprocessed the data and the costs of the reprocessing are credited
  against deposit or rental requirements of the interest, the purchaser
  shall submit to the Chief Conservation Officer a report that contains
  the information and materials described in paragraphs (1)(a), (h), (j)
  to (l) and (o) to (q) that have been prepared in respect of the
  reprocessed data by or for the purchaser.

- [(7)]{.lawlabel} The reports required by subsections (5) and (6) shall
  be submitted

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} in the case of a participant, within 12 months
    after the date of termination of the geophysical operation; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} in the case of a purchaser, by the time the costs
    referred to in subsection (5) or (6) are credited.

- [(8)]{.lawlabel} A person who has submitted a report referred to in
  this section shall, in respect of data that pertain to the location of
  shotpoints or stations, immediately notify the Chief Conservation
  Officer of any errors, omissions or corrections identified in or made
  to the data subsequent to the submission of the report.

- [(9)]{.lawlabel} A report referred to in this section shall be
  submitted in the form, manner and quantity approved by the Chief
  Conservation Officer.

### [Retention of Data]{.HTitleText2} {#h-977754 .Subheading}

- **[[39]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-39 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Every
  operator shall, after completion of a geophysical operation, retain in
  Canada the following information and materials:

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} seismic field data in digital format and a
    description of the data format, together with all supporting
    information;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} fully processed, migrated seismic data in digital
    format;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} in the case of a magnetic survey, the final digital
    field data, field analog monitors, diurnal charts, altitude
    profiles, and all other supporting information;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} in the case of a gravimetric survey, the location,
    elevation, final digital field data, and gravity profiles;

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} in the case of seabed investigations at well-sites,
    all sidescan sonar records and mosaics, fathometer records,
    sub-bottom profile records, grab samples, cores, and seabed
    photographs; and

  - [(f)]{.lawlabel} all other observations or readings that were
    obtained during the field operation.

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} No person shall destroy or discard any information or
  material referred to in subsection (1) after the period referred to in
  subsection (4) unless the person has given the Chief Conservation
  Officer not less than 60 days' notice of that intention and, if so
  requested within the notice period, has given the Chief Conservation
  Officer the information or material or a copy thereof.

- [(3)]{.lawlabel} The Chief Conservation Officer may require an
  operator to supply the information and materials referred to in
  subsection (1), in a form approved by the Chief Conservation Officer.

- [(4)]{.lawlabel} Subject to subsection (6), no person shall destroy,
  discard or remove from Canada any of the information or material
  referred to in subsection (1) within 15 years after the completion of
  the geophysical operation without the written approval of the Chief
  Conservation Officer.

- [(5)]{.lawlabel} Where fewer than 15 years have elapsed since the
  completion of the geophysical operation, the Chief Conservation
  Officer shall approve the destruction, discarding or removal from
  Canada of any of the information or material referred to in
  subsection (1) if the Chief Conservation Officer is satisfied that the
  information or material is not of any significant use or value.

- [(6)]{.lawlabel} Information or material referred to in subsection (1)
  may be removed from Canada without the approval of the Chief
  Conservation Officer for the purpose of being processed in a foreign
  country, provided that the information or material is returned to
  Canada as soon as the processing is complete.

- [(7)]{.lawlabel} Every operator shall retain in Canada on reproducible
  film the most recent fully processed, migrated seismic sections of the
  geophysical operation and shall not destroy that film or remove it
  from Canada without the written approval of the Chief Conservation
  Officer.

- [(8)]{.lawlabel} The Chief Conservation Officer shall approve the
  destruction or removal from Canada of the most recent fully processed,
  migrated seismic sections on reproducible film if the Chief
  Conservation Officer is satisfied that a copy of the film has been
  retained in Canada or the film is not of any significant use or value
  in Canada.

## [PART VI]{.HLabel1}[ accidents]{.HTitleText1} {#h-977770 .Part}

### [Reports]{.HTitleText2} {#h-977771 .Subheading}

**[[40]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-40 .sectionLabel}** Every operator shall
inform the Chief Conservation Officer and the Chief Safety Officer
immediately, by the most rapid and practical means, of any serious
accident or incident that occurs during a geophysical operation and that
causes injury to or loss of life of any person, or damage to property,
or that constitutes a threat to the environment.

### [Investigation]{.HTitleText2} {#h-977773 .Subheading}

**[[41]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-41 .sectionLabel}** The Chief Conservation
Officer and Chief Safety Officer may investigate any accident or
incident that occurs during a geophysical operation and that

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} involves the death of or injury to any person;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} causes significant damage to or failure of
  geophysical equipment; or

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} results in pollution or other damage to the
  environment.
