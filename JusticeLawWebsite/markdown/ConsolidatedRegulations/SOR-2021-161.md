## [Interpretation]{.HTitleText1} {#h-1302645 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Definitions

**[[1]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-1 .sectionLabel}** The following definitions
apply in these Regulations.

[[Act]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[Act]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means the [Pay Equity
    Act](/eng/acts/P-4.2). ([Loi]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

[[band]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[band]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} has the same meaning as in subsection
    49(2) of the Act. ([bande]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

[[frozen]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[frozen]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}, in respect of compensation
    associated with a job class, means that the compensation includes

    - [(a)]{.lawlabel} salary at a rate that is continued in force under
      section 107 of the [Federal Public Sector Labour Relations
      Act](/eng/acts/P-33.3);

    - [(b)]{.lawlabel} salary at a rate set out in a collective
      agreement applicable to a bargaining unit for which a strike may
      be declared or authorized without contravening subsection 194(1)
      of that Act or for which the conditions in paragraphs 89(1)(a)
      to (d) of the [Canada Labour Code](/eng/acts/L-2) are met; or

    - [(c)]{.lawlabel} salary at a rate that

      - [(i)]{.lawlabel} under section 56 of the [Federal Public Sector
        Labour Relations Act](/eng/acts/P-33.3), cannot be altered
        except under a collective agreement or with the consent of the
        Federal Public Sector Labour Relations and Employment Board,

      - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} under subsection 24(4) of the [Canada Labour
        Code](/eng/acts/L-2), cannot be altered except pursuant to a
        collective agreement or with the consent of the Canada
        Industrial Relations Board, or

      - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} under paragraph 50(b) of the [Canada Labour
        Code](/eng/acts/L-2), cannot be altered without the consent of
        the bargaining agent. ([gelée]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}References to employers

**[[2]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-2 .sectionLabel}** If a group of employers is
recognized by the Pay Equity Commissioner as a single employer under
section 106 of the Act, every reference in sections 10, 14 and 15,
subsection 16(1), sections 18 to 20 and 22, subsections 23(1) to (6) and
sections 24 to 26, 28 to 31, 38.1, 39 and 41 to 46.1 to an employer is,
in respect of the group of employers, to be read as a reference to that
group of employers unless the context otherwise requires.

## [Postings --- General Rules]{.HTitleText1} {#h-1302659 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Format and place

**[[3]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-3 .sectionLabel}** A document that is required
under the Act to be posted must be posted in printed or electronic form
and in such a way as to be readily available to all employees to whom
the document relates. A document in printed format must be posted in a
conspicuous place.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Accessibility

**[[4]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-4 .sectionLabel}** If an employee to whom a
document that is required to be posted under the Act relates has a
[[disability]{.dfn}]{.DefinitionRef} as defined in section 2 of the
[Accessible Canada Act](/eng/acts/A-0.6), that document must be posted
in a form that is accessible to that employee.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Information to be provided

**[[5]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-5 .sectionLabel}** An employer that posts in
electronic form a document that it is required to post under the Act
must provide the employees to whom the document relates with any
information necessary to enable them to access the document.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Date of posting

**[[6]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-6 .sectionLabel}** An employer that makes any
posting under the Act must indicate in the posting the date on which the
posting is made.

## [Pay Equity Plan]{.HTitleText1} {#h-1302668 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Notice --- employer's obligation

- **[[7]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-7 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} An
  employer that is required to post a notice under subsection 14(1)
  or (2) of the Act must do so within 60 days after the day on which it
  becomes subject to the Act.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Duration of posting

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The employer must keep the notice posted until it
  posts the final version of the pay equity plan in accordance with
  subsection 55(1) or paragraph 57(2)(b) or 94(1)(b) of the Act, until
  it posts a notice under subsection 15(1) or (2) of the Act or until it
  becomes required under subsection 30(6) of the Act to establish more
  than one pay equity plan.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Notice --- group of employers' obligation

- **[[8]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-8 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} An
  employer that is in a group of employers and that is required to post
  a notice under subsection 15(1) or (2) of the Act must do so

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} within 60 days after the day on which the Pay
    Equity Commissioner recognizes the group as a single employer under
    section 106 of the Act if the group of employers becomes subject to
    the Act, for the purposes of subsection 55(1) and paragraphs
    61(1)(b) and 89(2)(b) of the Act, less than 60 days after the day on
    which they are recognized; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} by the date on which the group of employers becomes
    subject to the Act, for the purposes referred to in paragraph (a),
    if that date is 60 days or more after the day on which the Pay
    Equity Commissioner recognizes the group as a single employer under
    section 106 of the Act.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Duration of posting

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The employer must keep the notice posted until each
  employer in the group of employers posts the final version of the pay
  equity plan in accordance with subsection 55(1) or paragraph 57(2)(b)
  of the Act, until the employer posts a notice under subsection 14(1)
  or (2) of the Act or until the group of employers becomes required
  under subsection 30(6) of the Act to establish more than one pay
  equity plan.

## [Process for Establishment of Pay Equity Plan]{.HTitleText1} {#h-1302684 .Part}

### [Frozen Compensation]{.HTitleText2} {#h-1302685 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Prohibited comparison --- frozen
compensation

**[[10]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-10 .sectionLabel}** In carrying out the
comparison of compensation under sections 47 to 50 of the Act, an
employer --- or, if a pay equity committee has been established, that
committee --- must ensure that no comparison is made between frozen
compensation associated with a predominantly female or predominantly
male job class and compensation that is not frozen and that is
associated with a predominantly female or predominantly male job class
in which unionized employees occupy positions, unless the salary rate
that is used to determine salary in the calculation of the frozen
compensation associated with a job class is

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} the salary rate obtained by adding, to the highest
  salary rate in the range of salary rates for positions in the job
  class, the product obtained by multiplying

  - [(i)]{.lawlabel} the salary at the highest rate in the range of
    salary rates for positions in the job class

  by

  - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} the average percentage by which, since the
    compensation associated with the job class became frozen, the salary
    for positions in job classes that contain positions occupied by
    unionized employees in respect of whom the pay equity plan applies
    and who are governed by a collective agreement that is not expired
    has increased; or

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} if a pay equity committee has been established, the
  salary rate determined using a method other than the one set out in
  paragraph (a) that minimizes, to the extent possible, the differences
  in compensation that result only from the compensation associated with
  a job class being frozen.

### [Factors]{.HTitleText2} {#h-1302693 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Calculation --- equal average method

- **[[11]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-11 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  factor referred to in paragraph 49(1)(d) of the Act and the factor
  referred to in paragraph 28(d) are determined by the formula

  ::: {#1302697 .Subsection}
  ((A × B) − C) ÷ D

  where

  [A]{.dfn}
  :   is the number of predominantly female job classes within the band;

  [B]{.dfn}
  :   is,
      - [(a)]{.lawlabel} if there is more than one predominantly male
        job class within the band, the average compensation associated
        with the predominantly male job classes within the band,

      - [(b)]{.lawlabel} if there is only one predominantly male job
        class within the band, the compensation associated with that job
        class, or

      - [(c)]{.lawlabel} if there are no predominantly male job classes
        within the band, the compensation calculated under paragraph
        49(1)(b) of the Act or paragraph 28(b), as the case may be;

  [C]{.dfn}
  :   is the sum of the compensation associated with the predominantly
      female job classes within the band; and

  [D]{.dfn}
  :   is the sum of the differences, for each predominantly female job
      class within the band whose compensation is less than the value
      determined for B, between the value of B and the compensation
      associated with the job class.
  :::

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Clarification

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} In the calculation of the factor referred to in
  paragraph 28(d), references in subsection (1) to a predominantly male
  job class are to be read as references to a predominantly male job
  class chosen or created, as the case may be, under subsection 19(1).

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Calculation --- equal line method

- **[[12]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-12 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} With
  respect to a predominantly female job class, the factor referred to in
  paragraph 50(1)(c) of the Act and the factor referred to in paragraph
  29(1)(c) are determined by the formula

  ::: {#1302711 .Subsection}
  ((A × B) ÷ C) + (D -- (E × B))

  where

  [A]{.dfn}
  :   is determined by the formula
      ::: {#1302714 .NestedFormula}
      F ÷ G

      where

      [F]{.dfn}
      :   is the absolute value of the difference between the
          compensation associated with the predominantly female job
          class and the compensation associated with a predominantly
          male job class, were such a job class located on the male
          regression line, in which the value of the work performed is
          equal to that of the predominantly female job class, and

      [G]{.dfn}
      :   is the compensation associated with such a predominantly male
          job class;
      :::

  [B]{.dfn}
  :   is determined by the formula
      ::: {#1302719 .NestedFormula}
      ((H − I) − (J × K)) ÷ (L − (M × K))

      where

      [H]{.dfn}
      :   is the sum of the products obtained by multiplying, for each
          predominantly female job class, the value of work performed in
          the job class by the compensation associated with a
          predominantly male job class, were such a job class located on
          the male regression line, in which the value of the work
          performed is equal to that of the predominantly female job
          class,

      [I]{.dfn}
      :   is the sum of the products obtained by multiplying, for each
          predominantly female job class, the value of work performed in
          the job class by the compensation associated with that job
          class,

      [J]{.dfn}
      :   is determined by the formula
          ::: {#1302724 .NestedFormula}
          (P -- Q) ÷ R

          where

          [P]{.dfn}
          :   is the sum of the compensation associated with
              predominantly male job classes, were such job classes
              located on the male regression line, in which the value of
              the work performed is equal to that of the predominantly
              female job classes,

          [Q]{.dfn}
          :   is the sum of the compensation associated with the
              predominantly female job classes, and

          [R]{.dfn}
          :   is the sum of the absolute values of the differences, for
              each predominantly female job class that is located below
              the male regression line, between the compensation
              associated with the job class and the compensation
              associated with a predominantly male job class, were such
              a job class located on the male regression line, in which
              the value of the work performed is equal to that of the
              predominantly female job class;
          :::

      [K]{.dfn}
      :   is the sum of the products obtained by multiplying, for each
          predominantly female job class that is located below the male
          regression line, the value of the work performed in the job
          class by the absolute value of the difference between the
          compensation associated with the job class and the
          compensation associated with a predominantly male job class,
          were such a job class located on the male regression line, in
          which the value of the work performed is equal to that of the
          predominantly female job class,

      [L]{.dfn}
      :   is the sum of the products obtained by multiplying, for each
          predominantly female job class that is located below the male
          regression line, the value of the work performed in that job
          class by the quotient calculated for the job class using the
          formula set out in A in this subsection,

      [M]{.dfn}
      :   is determined by the formula
          ::: {#1302732 .NestedFormula}
          (N ÷ O)

          where

          [N]{.dfn}
          :   is the sum of the quotients calculated using the formula
              set out in A in this subsection, for each predominantly
              female job class that is located below the male regression
              line, and

          [O]{.dfn}
          :   is the sum of the absolute values of the differences
              between the compensation associated with each
              predominantly female job class that is located below the
              male regression line and the compensation associated with
              a predominantly male job class, were such a job class
              located on the male regression line, in which the value of
              the work performed is equal to that of the predominantly
              female job class;
          :::
      :::

  [C]{.dfn}
  :   is an amount equal to the difference referred to in paragraph
      50(1)(c) of the Act or paragraph 29(1)(c), as the case may be;

  [D]{.dfn}
  :   is the value determined for J in this subsection; and

  [E]{.dfn}
  :   is the value determined for M in this subsection.

### [Rules if Regression Lines Cross]{.HTitleText2} {#h-1302743 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Choice of method

**[[14]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-14 .sectionLabel}** For the purposes of
subsection 50(2) of the Act, the following rules apply:

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} an employer --- or, if a pay equity committee has
  been established, that committee --- must apply the rules set out in
  paragraphs 50(1)(b) to (d) of the Act, without taking into account
  subparagraph 50(1)(b)(i); and

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} if the application of the rules in paragraph (a) does
  not cause the regression lines to coincide without reducing the
  compensation associated with any job class, the employer or pay equity
  committee, as the case may be, must instead compare the compensation
  using

  - [(i)]{.lawlabel} the equal average method set out in section 49 of
    the Act,

  - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} the segmented line method set out in section 15,
    or

  - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} the sum of differences method set out in section
    16.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Segmented line method

**[[15]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-15 .sectionLabel}** An employer or pay equity
committee, as the case may be, that uses the segmented line method must
apply the following rules:

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} the employer or pay equity committee must divide the
  predominantly female job classes and the predominantly male job
  classes into the following two segments:

  - [(i)]{.lawlabel} one segment that includes the job classes in which
    the value of work performed is less than the value at which the
    regression lines established under paragraph 50(1)(a) of the Act
    intersect, and

  - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} one segment that includes the job classes in which
    the value of work performed is equal to or greater than the value at
    which the regression lines established under that paragraph
    intersect;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} for each segment, the employer or pay equity
  committee must establish a female regression line for the
  predominantly female job classes in the segment and a male regression
  line for the predominantly male job classes in the segment;

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} in a segment in which the female regression line is
  entirely below the male regression line, the employer or pay equity
  committee must apply the rules set out in paragraphs 50(1)(b) to (d)
  of the Act, and if the application of those rules does not cause the
  male and female regression lines to coincide without reducing the
  compensation associated with any job class, the employer or committee
  must use the equal average method set out in section 49 of the Act or
  the sum of differences method set out in section 16 to compare the
  compensation associated with all predominantly female job classes and
  predominantly male job classes; and

- [(d)]{.lawlabel} in a segment in which the female regression line
  crosses the male regression line, the employer or committee must apply
  the rules set out in paragraphs 50(1)(b) to (d) of the Act, without
  taking into account subparagraph 50(1)(b)(i), and if the application
  of those rules does not cause the male and female regression lines to
  coincide without reducing the compensation associated with any job
  class, the employer or committee must use the equal average method set
  out in section 49 of the Act or the sum of differences method set out
  in section 16 to compare the compensation associated with all
  predominantly female job classes and predominantly male job classes.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Sum of differences method

- **[[16]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-16 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  employer or pay equity committee, as the case may be, that uses the
  sum of differences method must multiply, for each predominantly female
  job class that is located below the male regression line established
  under paragraph 50(1)(a) of the Act, the factor calculated in
  accordance with subsection (2) by the absolute value of the difference
  between the compensation associated with the predominantly female job
  class and the compensation associated with a predominantly male job
  class, were such a job class located on the male regression line, in
  which the value of the work performed is equal to that of the
  predominantly female job class.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Factor

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} For the purposes of subsection (1), the factor is the
  result of the formula

  ::: {#1302764 .Subsection}
  (A − B) ÷ C

  where

  [A]{.dfn}
  :   is the sum of the compensation associated with predominantly male
      job classes, were such job classes located on the male regression
      line, in which the value of the work performed is equal to that of
      the predominantly female job classes;

  [B]{.dfn}
  :   is the sum of the compensation associated with the predominantly
      female job classes or the value determined for A, whichever is
      less; and

  [C]{.dfn}
  :   is the sum of the absolute values of the differences, for each
      predominantly female job class that is located below the male
      regression line, between the compensation associated with the job
      class and the compensation associated with a predominantly male
      job class, were such a job class located on the male regression
      line, in which the value of the work performed is equal to that of
      the predominantly female job class.
  :::

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Increase in compensation

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} The increase in compensation associated with a
  predominantly female job class located below the male regression line
  is the product calculated in accordance with subsection (1) in respect
  of that job class.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Clarification

**[[17]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-17 .sectionLabel}** The segmented line method
set out in section 15 and the sum of differences method set out in
section 16 are to be applied without regard either to the number of
employees or to the number of positions in a job class.

### [Obligations --- No Predominantly Male Job Classes]{.HTitleText2} {#h-1302773 .Subheading}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Application

**[[18]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-18 .sectionLabel}** Sections 19 to 29 apply
with respect to an employer --- or, if a pay equity committee has been
established, to that committee --- for the determination of differences
in compensation for the purposes of section 60 of the Act, if the
employer or committee, as the case may be, has determined that there are
no predominantly male job classes.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Choice of method

- **[[19]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-19 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} An
  employer --- or, if a pay equity committee has been established, that
  committee --- must use, to determine differences in compensation for
  the purposes of section 60 of the Act

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} at least three predominantly male job classes of
    its choice from another employer that meets the conditions set out
    in subsection (2); or

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} three fictional predominantly male job classes,
    each of which is created by the employer or committee based on a
    different typical job class set out in Schedule 1.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Other employer

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} For the purposes of paragraph (1)(a), the other
  employer must meet the following conditions:

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} it must have at least three job classes that it ---
    or, if a pay equity committee has been established by the other
    employer, that committee --- has determined under section 35 of the
    Act to be predominantly male and for which the compensation has been
    calculated in accordance with sections 44 to 46 of the Act; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} it must agree to provide the employer --- or, if a
    pay equity committee has been established by that employer, that
    committee --- that has chosen to use those job classes with the data
    in its possession that is necessary to determine the value of work
    performed in those job classes and, for each job class chosen, with
    the salary at the highest rate in the range of salary rates for
    positions in the job class.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Criteria

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} An employer --- or, if a pay equity committee has
  been established, that committee --- choosing job classes provided by
  another employer under paragraph (1)(a) must, to the extent possible,
  ensure that those job classes

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} come from an employer with characteristics similar
    to its own --- or, in the case of a pay equity committee choosing
    job classes, those of the relevant employer --- such as the
    following:

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} the two employers are part of the same industry,

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} they operate in regions where the cost of living
      is similar,

    - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} they have a similar number of employees,

    - [(iv)]{.lawlabel} they have a similar proportion of unionized
      employees, and

    - [(v)]{.lawlabel} they have similar compensation practices; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} are representative of the range of values of work
    performed in the job classes of that other employer, as determined
    by the other employer or the pay equity committee established by
    that employer, as the case may be, under section 41 of the Act.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Determination of value of work

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Exclusions from compensation

**[[24]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-24 .sectionLabel}** An employer --- or, if a
pay equity committee has been established, that committee --- may
exclude from the calculation of compensation, with respect to each job
class in respect of which compensation is required to be calculated, any
form of compensation that is equally available, and provided without
discrimination on the basis of gender, in respect of all of those job
classes.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Differences in compensation excluded

**[[25]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-25 .sectionLabel}** An employer --- or, if a
pay equity committee has been established, that committee --- must
exclude from the calculation of compensation associated with a
predominantly female job class any differences in compensation that
either increase or decrease compensation in any or all positions in that
job class as compared with the compensation that would otherwise be
associated with the position, if the differences are based on any one or
more of the following factors and those factors have been designed and
are applied so as not to discriminate on the basis of gender:

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} the existence of a system of compensation that is
  based on seniority or length of service;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} the practice of temporarily maintaining an employee's
  compensation following their reclassification or demotion to a
  position that has a lower rate of compensation until the rate of
  compensation for the position is equivalent to or greater than the
  rate of compensation payable to the employee immediately before the
  reclassification or demotion;

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} a shortage of skilled workers that causes an employer
  to temporarily increase compensation due to its difficulty in
  recruiting or retaining employees with the requisite skills for
  positions in a job class;

- [(d)]{.lawlabel} the geographic area in which an employee works;

- [(e)]{.lawlabel} the fact that an employee is in an employee
  development or training program and receives compensation at a rate
  different than that of an employee doing the same work in a position
  outside the program;

- [(f)]{.lawlabel} the non-receipt of compensation --- in the form of
  benefits that have a monetary value --- due to the temporary, casual
  or seasonal nature of a position;

- [(g)]{.lawlabel} the existence of a merit-based compensation plan that
  is based on a system of formal performance ratings and that has been
  brought to the attention of the employees; or

- [(h)]{.lawlabel} the provision of compensation for extra-duty
  services, including compensation for overtime, shift work, being on
  call, being called back to work and working or travelling on a day
  that is not a working day.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Comparison of compensation

**[[26]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-26 .sectionLabel}** An employer --- or, if a
pay equity committee has been established, that committee --- that has
calculated under section 23 the compensation associated with each job
class must, using the compensation so calculated, compare, in accordance
with sections 27 to 29, the compensation associated with the
predominantly female job classes with the compensation associated with
the predominantly male job classes chosen or created under subsection
19(1), for the purpose of determining whether there is any difference in
compensation between those job classes.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Compensation comparison methods

**[[27]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-27 .sectionLabel}** The comparison of
compensation must be made in accordance with the equal average method
set out in section 28 or the equal line method set out in section 29.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Equal average method

**[[28]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-28 .sectionLabel}** An employer or pay equity
committee, as the case may be, that uses the equal average method of
comparison of compensation must apply the following rules:

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} the average compensation associated with the
  predominantly female job classes within a band --- or, if there is
  only one such job class within a band, the compensation associated
  with that job class --- is to be compared to

  - [(i)]{.lawlabel} if there is more than one predominantly male job
    class chosen or created under subsection 19(1) within the band, the
    average compensation associated with those predominantly male job
    classes within the band,

  - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} if there is only one predominantly male job class
    chosen or created under subsection 19(1) within the band, the
    compensation associated with that job class, or

  - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} if there are no predominantly male job classes
    chosen or created under subsection 19(1) within the band, the
    compensation calculated under paragraph (b);

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} the compensation for the purpose of subparagraph
  (a)(iii) is

  - [(i)]{.lawlabel} the amount determined by the formula

    ::: {#1302856 .Subparagraph}
    (A × B) ÷ C

    where

    [A]{.dfn}
    :   is the average compensation associated with the predominantly
        male job classes chosen or created under subsection 19(1) ---
        or, if there is only one such job class, the compensation
        associated with that job class --- that are within the band that
        is closest to the band within which the predominantly female job
        class or classes are located,

    [B]{.dfn}
    :   is the average value of the work performed in the predominantly
        female job classes within the band or, if there is only one such
        job class, the value of the work performed in that job class,
        and

    [C]{.dfn}
    :   is the average value of the work performed in the predominantly
        male job classes within the band referred to in the description
        of A or, if there is only one such job class, the value of the
        work performed in that job class, or
    :::

  - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} despite subparagraph (i), if there is at least one
    predominantly male job class chosen or created under subsection
    19(1) within each of two bands that are equidistant from the band
    within which the predominantly female job class or classes are
    located and there is no other band containing at least one
    predominantly male job class that is closer to that band, the amount
    determined by the formula

    ::: {#1302862 .Subparagraph}
    (A + B) ÷ 2

    where

    [A]{.dfn}
    :   is the average compensation associated with the predominantly
        male job classes chosen or created under subsection 19(1) within
        one of the two bands or, if there is only one such job class,
        the compensation associated with that job class, and

    [B]{.dfn}
    :   is the average compensation associated with the predominantly
        male job classes chosen or created under subsection 19(1) within
        the other band or, if there is only one such job class, the
        compensation associated with that job class;
    :::

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} the compensation associated with a predominantly
  female job class within a band is to be increased only if

  - [(i)]{.lawlabel} that compensation is lower than the compensation or
    average compensation referred to in subparagraph (a)(i), (ii) or
    (iii), as the case may be, and

  - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} the average compensation associated with the
    predominantly female job classes within the band --- or, if there is
    only one such job class, the compensation associated with that job
    class --- is lower than the compensation or average compensation
    referred to in subparagraph (a)(i), (ii) or (iii), as the case may
    be;

- [(d)]{.lawlabel} if the compensation associated with a predominantly
  female job class within a band is to be increased, the increase is to
  be determined by multiplying the factor calculated in accordance with
  section 11 by an amount equal to the difference between the
  compensation associated with the job class and the compensation or
  average compensation referred to in subparagraph (a)(i), (ii) or
  (iii), as the case may be; and

- [(e)]{.lawlabel} an increase in compensation associated with the
  predominantly female job class or classes within a band is to be made
  in such a way that, after the increase, the average compensation
  associated with the predominantly female job classes within the band
  --- or, if there is only one such job class, the compensation
  associated with that job class --- is equal to the compensation or
  average compensation referred to in subparagraph (a)(i), (ii) or
  (iii), as the case may be.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Equal line method

- **[[29]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-29 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} An
  employer or pay equity committee, as the case may be, that uses the
  equal line method of comparison of compensation must apply the
  following rules:

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} a female regression line must be established for
    the predominantly female job classes and a male regression line must
    be established for the predominantly male job classes chosen or
    created under subsection 19(1);

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the compensation associated with a predominantly
    female job class is to be increased only if

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} the female regression line is entirely below the
      male regression line, and

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} the predominantly female job class is located
      below the male regression line;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} if the compensation associated with a predominantly
    female job class is to be increased, the increase is to be
    determined by multiplying the factor calculated in accordance with
    section 12 by an amount equal to the difference between the
    compensation associated with the predominantly female job class and
    the compensation associated with a predominantly male job class
    chosen or created under subsection 19(1), were such a job class
    located on the male regression line, in which the value of the work
    performed is equal to that of the predominantly female job class;
    and

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} an increase in compensation associated with the
    predominantly female job classes is to be made in such a way that,
    after the increase, the female regression line coincides with the
    male regression line.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Crossed regression lines

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Despite paragraphs (1)(b) to (d), if the female
  regression line crosses the male regression line, an employer or pay
  equity committee, as the case may be, must apply the equal average
  method set out in section 28 for the comparison of compensation.

**[[40]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-40 .sectionLabel}** For the purposes of

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} all changes that are likely to have an impact on the
  identification of job classes in accordance with sections 32 to 34 of
  the Act, other than changes that are likely to result in

  - [(i)]{.lawlabel} the creation or elimination of a job class that is
    predominantly female or predominantly male, or

  - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} the merger of a predominantly female or
    predominantly male job class with another job class;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} all changes that are likely to impact the
  determination, in accordance with sections 35 to 38 of the Act, of
  whether a job class is predominantly female or predominantly male ---
  or neither --- other than

  - [(i)]{.lawlabel} the change of a predominantly male job class to a
    predominantly female job class,

  - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} the change of a predominantly female job class to
    a predominantly male job class,

  - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} the change of a job class that was neither
    predominantly female nor predominantly male to a job class that is
    either predominantly female or predominantly male, and

  - [(iv)]{.lawlabel} the change of a predominantly female or
    predominantly male job class to a job class that is neither
    predominantly male nor predominantly female;

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} in the case of a job class in the core public
  administration, all changes that are likely to have an impact on the
  value of work performed in the job class as that value is determined
  in accordance with sections 41 to 43 of the Act, other than any
  changes in the skill required to perform the work, in the effort
  required to perform the work, in the responsibility required in the

  performed

  - [(i)]{.lawlabel} that are significant,

  - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} that impact a substantial portion of the positions

  - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} whose impact on the job class is not temporary;
    and

- [(d)]{.lawlabel} any change in the compensation associated with a
  predominantly female or predominantly male job class if, on a

  predominantly male job classes.
