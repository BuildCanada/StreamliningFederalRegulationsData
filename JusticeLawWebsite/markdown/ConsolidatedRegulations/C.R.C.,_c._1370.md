## [Prescription of Classes of Radiation Emitting Devices and Standards]{.HTitleText1} {#h-506325 .Part}

- **[[3]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-3 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  classes of radiation emitting devices described in Schedule I are
  prescribed as classes of radiation emitting devices for the purposes
  of the Act.

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} The standards set out in Schedule II for prescribed
  classes of radiation emitting devices are prescribed as standards
  regulating the design, construction or functioning of those prescribed
  classes of radiation emitting devices and their components.

## [Importation]{.HTitleText1} {#h-506329 .Part}

**[[3.1]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-3.1 .sectionLabel}** Any person who imports
for sale or lease a device that is a [[medical

## [Detention of Seized Devices]{.HTitleText1} {#h-506333 .Part}

- **[[4]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-4 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} An
  inspector who seizes a device or a component of a device pursuant to
  subsection 10(1) of the Act may

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} detain the device or component for the time that is
    necessary to determine whether the device or component complies with
    the requirements set out in these Regulations;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} keep or store the device or component in the
    building or place where it was seized or remove it to any other
    place for testing to ascertain whether or not the component or
    device complies with the standards prescribed therefor; or

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} release the device or component unconditionally or
    on the condition that

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} it be destroyed forthwith or retained by the
      owner until it is destroyed, or

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} within a period fixed by the inspector, it be
      returned

      - [(A)]{.lawlabel} to the country from which it was exported, or

      - [(B)]{.lawlabel} to the manufacturer, distributor or importer
        thereof and be made to comply with the standards prescribed
        therefor.

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} An inspector who seizes a device or a component of a
  device under subsection 10(1) of the Act shall sign and keep a written
  record of the seizure that sets out

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the place, date and hour of the seizure;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the name of the person who owns the device or
    component or on whose premises the device or component was seized;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} the name of the manufacturer, the model number and
    the date and place of manufacture of the device or component;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} the place, if any, to which the device or component
    has been removed; and

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} particulars of the manner in which the device or
    component has been dealt with after seizure.

- [(3)]{.lawlabel} The person who owns a device or a component of a
  device seized pursuant to subsection 10(1) of the Act or on whose
  premises the device or component was seized or an employee or an agent
  of such person shall sign the record kept pursuant to subsection (2)
  and shall receive a copy thereof.

- [(4)]{.lawlabel} Where a device or a component of a device has been
  released pursuant to paragraph (1)(c) on a condition and the condition
  has not been complied with, the inspector may again seize and detain
  the device or component.

## [SCHEDULE I]{.scheduleLabel}[(Section 3)]{.OriginatingRef} {#h-506358 .scheduleLabel}
:::

- **1** Television receivers, including video monitors and video display
  systems, being electronic appliances designed to display a picture or
  alphanumeric information, or both, after receiving signals through
  electromagnetic waves, cable or other means of transmission and
  including the cabinet or case of such appliances.

- **2** Dental X-ray equipment, being radiation emitting devices that
  are designed primarily for the examination of dental and maxillofacial
  structures in living humans and that have an X-ray generating tube
  designed to be used outside the mouth.

- **3** Microwave ovens, being appliances or sets of components that are
  designed to supply microwave energy to material within a cavity.

- **4** A baggage inspection X-ray device, being an X-ray generating
  appliance designed primarily for the examination of carry-on baggage,
  or the examination of parcels, mail or similar items, including the
  X-ray generator, the X-ray detector and display and control systems.

- **5** A demonstration-type gas discharge device, being a device that

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} contains an electronic device in which glow
    discharges or X-rays or both may be produced by the acceleration of
    electrons and ions; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} is designed to demonstrate the production,
    properties or effects of glow discharges or X-rays, or the flow of
    electrons or ions.

- **6** Photofluorographic X-ray equipment being X-ray generating
  appliances designed primarily for the examination of the human chest
  and the recording photographically in reduced size of the image
  produced on a fluorescent screen.

- **7** A laser scanner, being a device that uses scanned laser
  radiation within the wavelength range of 400 to 1400 nanometres to
  decipher or generate codes represented by drawn or printed geometrical
  patterns.

- **8** A demonstration laser, being a device that consists of or
  incorporates a laser and that is primarily intended to be used for
  demonstrating the principles of optics in educational institutions.

- **9** Low Energy Electron Microscopes being electron-optical devices
  with an operating energy of 500 kilo-electron volts (keV) or less in
  which a beam of electrons, focused by means of electron lenses, is
  used to produce an enlarged image of a minute object on a fluorescent
  screen, photographic plate or any other detector-display system,
  including both the transmission and scanning types of devices.

- **10** High intensity mercury vapour discharge lamps, being lamps
  incorporating a high-pressure arc discharge tube with a fill
  consisting primarily of mercury, whether such lamps are described as
  mercury vapour lamps, metal halide lamps, self-ballasted lamps or
  otherwise when sold, but not including tungsten filament
  self-ballasted lamps.

- **11** Tanning equipment as defined in section 1 of Part XI of
  Schedule II.

- **12** Diagnostic X-ray equipment, being radiation emitting devices
  that use X-rays and are designed for the examination of the human
  body. The equipment does not include dental X-ray equipment,
  photofluorographic X-ray equipment, radiation therapy simulators and
  computer-assisted tomographic equipment.

- **13** Ultrasound therapy devices, being devices designed to generate
  and emit ultrasonic power at acoustic frequencies above 20 kHz for use
  in physical therapy.

- **14** Analytical X-ray equipment, being X-ray generating devices that
  contain an X-ray tube and that use X-radiation to determine the
  elemental composition, or examine the microstructure, of material.

- **15** Cabinet X-ray equipment, being X-ray generating devices, not
  including analytical X-ray equipment or baggage inspection X-ray
  devices, that have the X-ray tube permanently installed in a cabinet
  and are designed primarily for the examination of material, part or
  all of which is placed within the cabinet.

### [PART I]{.HLabel1}[Television Receivers]{.HTitleText1} {#part-itelevision-receivers .SchedHeadL1}

#### [Interpretation]{.HTitleText2} {#interpretation .SchedHeadL2}

- **1** In this Part,

  [[maximum test voltage]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[maximum test voltage]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means

      - [(a)]{.lawlabel} 127 volts, if the television receiver is
        designed to operate from a 110-120 volt power source, or

      - [(b)]{.lawlabel} 110 per cent of the maximum voltage from which
        the television receiver is designed to operate, if the
        television receiver is designed to operate otherwise than from a
        110-120 volt power source; ([tension maximale
        d'essai]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

  [[service control]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[service control]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means a control that is
      installed on a television receiver by the manufacturer thereof for
      the purpose of adjustment and that, under normal usage, is not
      accessible to the user of the receiver; ([commande
      interne]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

  [[user control]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[user control]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means a control that is
      provided on or external to a television receiver by the
      manufacturer thereof for the purpose of adjustment and that for a
      fully assembled television receiver under normal usage, is
      accessible to the user. ([commande externe]{.DefinedTermLink
      lang="fr"})

#### [Standards of Design and Construction]{.HTitleText2} {#standards-of-design-and-construction .SchedHeadL2}

- - **2** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Every television receiver shall be designed
    and constructed in such a way that

    - [(a)]{.lawlabel} under normal conditions of use, and

    - [(b)]{.lawlabel} on failure or malfunction of any one component or
      subsequent failure or malfunction of other components caused by
      that failure or malfunction,

    it functions in accordance with the standards of functioning
    described in section 3 for as long as the receiver has its original
    components or has replacement components recommended by the
    manufacturer.

  - [(2)]{.lawlabel} Without limiting the generality of subsection (1),
    the components of a receiver that emit X-rays shall have sufficient
    shielding to enable the receiver to comply with the standards of
    functioning set out in section 3.

  - [(3)]{.lawlabel} Where the shielding required by subsection (2)
    necessitates the use of individual shields, such shields shall
    either

    - [(a)]{.lawlabel} be non-removable, or interlocked; or

    - [(b)]{.lawlabel} bear a radiation warning sign that is permanently
      affixed and clearly visible under the conditions of servicing, and
      cautions against operation of the receiver when the shield is
      removed.

  - [(4)]{.lawlabel} Subject to subsection (6), every television
    receiver shall bear on the rear external surface of its cabinet or
    case, a permanent label that sets out, with respect to the receiver

    - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the name and address of the manufacturer;

    - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the model number;

    - [(c)]{.lawlabel} the city and country of manufacture or a code by
      which that city and country are identified and the key to which is
      supplied by the manufacturer to the Minister before the sale of
      the receiver;

    - [(d)]{.lawlabel} the month and year of manufacture, without
      abbreviation, with the year shown as a four-digit number;

    - [(e)]{.lawlabel} the brand name of the receiver;

    - [(f)]{.lawlabel} the chassis family designation; and

    - [(g)]{.lawlabel} the serial number.

  - [(5)]{.lawlabel} The permanent label required by subsection (4)
    shall be clearly visible at any time.

  - [(6)]{.lawlabel} Where a television receiver is manufactured for
    sale under the trade name of a person who is not the manufacturer of
    the receiver, the trade name and the address of that person may, for
    the purposes of paragraph (4)(a), be substituted for the name and
    address of the manufacturer if the name and address of the
    manufacturer are permanently affixed to the inside of the cabinet or
    case of the receiver and are clearly visible under conditions of
    servicing.

  - [(7) and (8)]{.lawlabel} [\[Revoked, SOR/94-40, s. 1\]]{.Repealed}

#### [Standards of Functioning]{.HTitleText2} {#standards-of-functioning .SchedHeadL2}

- **3** Every television receiver shall function in such a way that

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} when the receiver

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} is fully assembled,

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} is used with any supply voltage up to the
      maximum test voltage,

    - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} is used with any settings of the user controls
      and service controls, and

    - [(iv)]{.lawlabel} displays a synchronous raster covering at least
      60 per cent of the viewable screen area,

    the emission of ionizing radiation therefrom is such that the
    exposure rate of X-rays, when averaged over a period of five
    minutes, to an object having a 10 square centimetre cross section
    and centred at five centimetres from any accessible external surface
    of the television receiver does not exceed 0.5 milliroentgen per
    hour; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} when the receiver, without its cabinet or case, or
    any part of the chassis, cabinet or case,

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} is used with any supply voltage up to the maximum
      test voltage,

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} is used with any settings of the user controls
      and service controls, and

    - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} displays a synchronous raster covering at least
      60 per cent of the viewable screen area,

    the emission of ionizing radiation therefrom is such that the
    exposure rate of X-rays, when averaged over a period of five
    minutes, to an object having a 10 square centimetre cross section
    and centred at 10 centimetres from the surface of any component of
    the receiver does not exceed 2.5 milliroentgen per hour.

### [PART II]{.HLabel1}[Dental X-Ray Equipment]{.HTitleText1} {#part-iidental-x-ray-equipment .SchedHeadL1}

#### [Interpretation]{.HTitleText2} {#interpretation-1 .SchedHeadL2}

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Definitions

  **1** The following definitions apply in this Part.

  [[aluminum]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[aluminum]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means aluminum that has a degree
      of purity of 99.9% or higher and a density of
      2.70 g/cm^3^. ([aluminium]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

  [[aluminum equivalent]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[aluminum equivalent]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means the attenuation
      equivalent of an object, expressed in thickness of
      aluminum. ([équivalent en aluminium]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

  [[coefficient of variation]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[coefficient of variation]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means the ratio
      of the estimated standard deviation to the mean value of a series
      of measurements, determined by the formula

      ::: {#506432 .Definition}
      <figure>
      <div class="imageGroupcenter">
      <img src="/images/CRC1370img/CRC1370_e020.jpg"
      alt="The coefficient of variation, determined by the formula “C equals S divided by X bar, which is also equal to one divided by X bar multiplied by, open square bracket, sum from i equals one to n, open parentheses, X sub i minus X bar, close parentheses, squared, divided by n minus one, close square bracket, to the power of one half”." />
      </div>
      </figure>

      where

      [C]{.dfn}
      :   is the coefficient of variation;

      [S]{.dfn}
      :   is the estimated standard deviation;

      [[X]{.Overbar}]{.dfn}
      :   is the mean value of the measurements;

      [X~i~]{.dfn}
      :   is the value of the i^th^ measurement; and

      [n]{.dfn}
      :   is the number of measurements. ([coefficient de
          variation]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})
      :::

  [[dental volumetric reconstruction]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[dental volumetric reconstruction]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means
      reconstruction of the three-dimensional attenuation distribution
      of all or part of the irradiated volume from a series of
      two-dimensional projections that are produced by an X-ray beam on
      an X-ray image receptor moving around the head of a
      patient. ([reconstruction dentaire volumétrique]{.DefinedTermLink
      lang="fr"})

  [[deviation]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[deviation]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}, in respect of any parameter of
      dental X-ray equipment, means the percentage error between the
      measured value and the value indicated either on the equipment or
      in the accompanying information. ([variation]{.DefinedTermLink
      lang="fr"})

  [[digital X-ray image receptor]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[digital X-ray image receptor]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means an
      X-ray image receptor that has a conversion method which is
      electrically powered. The conversion method may include
      single-step direct conversion or multi-step indirect
      conversion. ([récepteur numérique d'image
      radiologique]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

  [[effective image reception area]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[effective image reception area]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means the
      part of the image reception area that is configured to receive an
      X-ray pattern that can be processed for display or
      storage. ([surface réceptrice de l'image
      efficace]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

  [[extra-oral]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[extra-oral]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}, in relation to dental
      radiography, means that the X-ray image receptor is located
      outside the oral cavity. ([extra-oral]{.DefinedTermLink
      lang="fr"})

  [[hand-held]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[hand-held]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}, in respect of transportable
      dental X-ray equipment, means that the equipment, once it is
      placed into service, is held in or supported by the
      hand. ([portatif]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

  [[intra-oral]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[intra-oral]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}, in relation to dental
      radiography, means that all or part of the X-ray image receptor is
      located inside the oral cavity. ([intra-oral]{.DefinedTermLink
      lang="fr"})

  [[irradiation time]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[irradiation time]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means the duration of an
      irradiation, measured as the interval between the instant when the
      air kerma rate rises for the first time to a value of 50% of the
      peak value and the instant when it drops for the last time below
      50% of the peak value. ([temps d'irradiation]{.DefinedTermLink
      lang="fr"})

  [[lead equivalent]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[lead equivalent]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means the attenuation
      equivalent of an object, expressed in thickness of
      lead. ([équivalent en plomb]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

  [[loading factor]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[loading factor]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means a factor the value of
      which influences the X-ray tube load. It includes the following:

      - [(a)]{.lawlabel} in the case of an X-ray beam that is produced
        by the discharge of the capacitor through an X-ray tube, the
        X-ray tube voltage and the amount of capacitor charge;

      - [(b)]{.lawlabel} in the case of a field emission device in which
        the emission of electrons from the cathode is due solely to the
        action of an electric field, the X-ray tube voltage and the
        number of pulses; and

      - [(c)]{.lawlabel} in any other case, the X-ray tube voltage and
        either

        - [(i)]{.lawlabel} the X-ray tube current and irradiation time,
          or

        - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} the current time product. ([paramètre de
          charge]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

  [[mode of operation]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[mode of operation]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means the technical
      state that is defined by a configuration of several predetermined
      loading factors and other settings for radiography or radioscopy,
      selectable simultaneously by the operation of a single
      control. ([mode de fonctionnement]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

  [[rectification type]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[rectification type]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means the process by
      which the X-ray generator of dental X-ray equipment converts high
      voltage to X-ray tube voltage. ([type de
      redressement]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

  [[transportable]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[transportable]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}, in relation to dental X-ray
      equipment, means that the equipment is constructed so that, once
      it is placed into service, it is capable of being moved from one
      place to another. ([transportable]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

  [[X-ray field]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[X-ray field]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means the area on a surface
      that is intersected by a radiation beam whose boundary is
      determined by the points where the air kerma drops to 25% of the
      air kerma at the centre of the area. ([champ de rayonnement
      X]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

  [[X-ray image receptor]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[X-ray image receptor]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means a device that
      converts incident X-rays into a visible image or into a form that
      can be made into a visible image. ([récepteur d'image
      radiologique]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

#### [Information and Labelling]{.HTitleText2} {#information-and-labelling .SchedHeadL2}

##### [Information]{.HTitleText3} {#information .SchedHeadL3}

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}General requirements

  **2** The manufacturer, distributor and importer must ensure that all
  of the following information accompanies each piece of dental X-ray
  equipment:

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the manufacturer's name and civic address, and its
    postal address if different;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the model designation of the equipment;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} the installation instructions;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} any radiological safety procedures and additional
    precautions that are necessary because of any unique features of the
    equipment;

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} instructions for use that include

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} a description of the influence of the main
      settings or selections that are available to the operator on the
      radiation dose to the patient,

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} if protection of the operator is affected by
      distance from the equipment, information on the impact of distance
      on the radiation dose, and

    - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} all information necessary to minimize the
      operator's exposure to radiation;

  - [(f)]{.lawlabel} maintenance instructions;

  - [(g)]{.lawlabel} procedures for quality control testing to be
    performed on the equipment, including how often the tests are to be
    performed and the acceptance criteria;

  - [(h)]{.lawlabel} for each X-ray tube assembly,

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} the nominal focal spot sizes,

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} the cooling curves for the anode and for the
      X-ray tube housing,

    - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} the X-ray tube rating charts, and

    - [(iv)]{.lawlabel} the focal spot position;

  - [(i)]{.lawlabel} the duty cycles, its rectification type and its

  - [(j)]{.lawlabel} the nominal line voltage, the maximum line current

    equipment at the maximum line current;

  - [(k)]{.lawlabel} the loading factors that constitute the maximum
    line current condition for the X-ray generator;

  - [(l)]{.lawlabel} the recommended loading factors for each patient
    size;

  - [(m)]{.lawlabel} when combinations of loading factors are indicated

  - [(n)]{.lawlabel} if the equipment can operate in automatic exposure

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} the accuracy limits of the automatic exposure

    - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} the reproducibility of the air kerma relative

  - [(p)]{.lawlabel} if the equipment is battery-powered, the minimum

  - [(s)]{.lawlabel} for transportable equipment, recommendations for

#### [Construction and Functioning Standards]{.HTitleText2} {#construction-and-functioning-standards .SchedHeadL2}

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Control panel

  **9** The control panel of dental X-ray equipment must have all of the
  following features:

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} a visual indicator that warns the operator when one
    further actuation of a control will initiate the emission of X-rays;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} a visual indicator that warns the operator when
    X-rays are being emitted and that is clearly visible during the
    emission;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} if the equipment can emit X-rays while not in
    automatic exposure control mode, controls and visual indicators that
    enable the operator to select the loading factors or mode of
    operation before initiation of the irradiation;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} if more than one X-ray source assembly is
    controlled by the same control panel, a visual indicator that shows,
    before initiation of the irradiation, which of the X-ray source
    assemblies is connected and ready to emit X-rays;

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} if the equipment allows for the selection of added
    filters by remote control or an automatic system, a visual indicator
    that shows which filter has been selected;

  - [(f)]{.lawlabel} if the equipment is battery-powered, a visual
    indicator that shows whether the battery is adequately charged for
    the proper operation of the equipment;

  - [(g)]{.lawlabel} for extra-oral equipment, an indicator that
    displays the dose area product; and

  - [(h)]{.lawlabel} for extra-oral equipment, an indicator that
    displays the air kerma at the entrance of the X-ray image receptor,
    unless the air kerma is already provided in the accompanying
    information required by section 4.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Irradiation switch --- functions

  - **10** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Every irradiation switch of dental X-ray
    equipment must enable all of the following functions:

    - [(a)]{.lawlabel} to initiate and to end an irradiation;

    - [(b)]{.lawlabel} to permit the emission of X-rays only when the
      operator exerts continuous pressure on the switch;

    - [(c)]{.lawlabel} to operate in a way that it is not possible to
      initiate an irradiation without first releasing the switch by
      which the previous irradiation was initiated; and

    - [(d)]{.lawlabel} except in the case of hand-held intra-oral
      equipment, to permit the operator to stand at least 2 m from the
      X-ray source assembly when the X-ray tube is energized.

  - [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Hand-held intra-oral equipment

    [(2)]{.lawlabel} Hand-held intra-oral equipment must also be
    equipped with a second irradiation switch, whether attached or
    remote, that gives the operator the option to stand at least 2 m
    from the X-ray source assembly when the equipment is supported on a
    stand.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Protected area

  **11** Dental X-ray equipment, except transportable equipment, must
  have a means to permit initiation of an irradiation by the operator
  from an area that is protected from radiation by structural shielding
  or distance.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Audible signal

  **12** Dental X-ray equipment must emit a signal, clearly audible to
  the operator, that indicates the end of an irradiation.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Line voltage fluctuations

  **13** Dental X-ray equipment must have a means appropriate to its
  rectification type to compensate for variations in X-ray tube voltage
  that are caused by line voltage fluctuations.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}X-ray tube

  **14** The X-ray tube of dental X-ray equipment must be securely
  affixed to and aligned within the X-ray tube housing.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}X-ray source assembly

  **15** The X-ray source assembly of dental X-ray equipment must
  maintain its required position or trajectory without drift or
  vibration during operation.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Multiple X-ray source assemblies

  **16** If more than one X-ray source assembly of dental X-ray
  equipment is controlled by the same control panel, the equipment must
  have a visual indicator on or near each X-ray tube housing that shows
  which of the X-ray source assemblies has been selected.

  - [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Total filtration --- intra-oral
    equipment

    [(2)]{.lawlabel} Despite paragraph (1)(b), if intra-oral dental
    X-ray equipment operates at a maximum nominal X-ray tube voltage of
    70 kV or less, it may have a total filtration of at least 1.5 mm
    aluminum equivalent.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Automatic exposure control

  **18** Dental X-ray equipment that has an automatic exposure control
  must have the following features:

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} a means to automatically end the irradiation when,

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} in the case of extra-oral equipment, either

      - [(A)]{.lawlabel} the product of the X-ray tube voltage, X-ray
        tube current and irradiation time exceeds 64 kJ per irradiation,
        or

      - [(B)]{.lawlabel} the current time product exceeds 640 mAs per
        irradiation, and

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} in the case of intra-oral equipment, either

      - [(A)]{.lawlabel} the product of the X-ray tube voltage, X-ray
        tube current and irradiation time exceeds 3.2 kJ per
        irradiation, or

      - [(B)]{.lawlabel} the current time product exceeds 32 mAs per
        irradiation;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} a visual indicator or audible signal to warn the
    operator that the irradiation has ended because one of the limits
    set out in paragraph (a) has been reached; and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} a reset control that must be activated manually
    once one of the limits set out in paragraph (a) has been reached and
    before another irradiation under automatic exposure control can be
    initiated.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Minimum distance --- focal spot to skin

  **19** The minimum distance between the focal spot and the skin must
  be

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} 15 cm, in the case of extra-oral equipment; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} 20 cm, in the case of intra-oral equipment.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Maximum deviation --- focal spot to
  image receptor

  **20** If the distance between the focal spot and the X-ray image
  receptor of extra-oral dental X-ray equipment is adjustable, the
  equipment must have a visual indicator that shows the distance that is
  selected and that has a maximum deviation of 5%.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Beam-limiting device --- extra-oral
  equipment

  - **21** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Extra-oral dental X-ray equipment must have
    a beam-limiting device that meets the following requirements:

    - [(a)]{.lawlabel} when used for dental volumetric reconstruction
      with a circular image reception area, the device must operate so
      that

      - [(i)]{.lawlabel} the X-ray field does not exceed the boundary of
        the effective image reception area by more than 2 cm, measured
        along any diameter, and

      - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} at least 90% of the area of the X-ray field
        overlaps the effective image reception area;

    - [(b)]{.lawlabel} when used for dental volumetric reconstruction
      with a rectangular image reception area, the device must operate
      so that

      - [(i)]{.lawlabel} along each of the two axes of the image
        reception area, the edges of the X-ray field do not exceed the
        corresponding edges of the effective image reception area by
        more than 2 cm or 3% of the indicated distance between the focal
        spot and the image receptor, whichever is larger, when the image
        reception plane is perpendicular to the X-ray beam axis, and

      - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} the sum of the discrepancies on both axes does
        not exceed 3 cm or 4% of the indicated distance between the
        focal spot and the X-ray image receptor, whichever is larger;

    - [(c)]{.lawlabel} when used for projection radiography, the device
      must operate so that the X-ray field does not exceed the boundary
      of the effective image reception area; and

    - [(d)]{.lawlabel} when used for narrow beam scanning radiography,
      the device must operate so that

      - [(i)]{.lawlabel} along the axis of the image reception area that
        is parallel to the direction of the scanning, the X-ray field
        does not exceed the boundary of the effective image reception
        area by more than 1 mm on each side, and

      - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} along the axis of the image reception area
        that is perpendicular to the direction of the scanning, the
        X-ray field does not exceed boundary of the effective image
        reception area.

  - [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Beam-limiting device --- intra-oral
    equipment

    [(2)]{.lawlabel} Intra-oral dental X-ray equipment must have a
    beam-limiting device that meets the following requirements:

    - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the device limits the X-ray field to a circle of
      not more than 6 cm diameter, measured at the point at which the
      beam exits the equipment;

    - [(b)]{.lawlabel} if the device has an optional means to limit the
      exit field to a rectangular shape, it must operate so that the
      rectangle fits inside that circle;

    - [(c)]{.lawlabel} if the device has a rectangular exit field, it
      must operate so that the exit field can rotate with respect to the
      X-ray beam axis; and

    - [(d)]{.lawlabel} in the case of equipment that has an integrated
      direct digital X-ray image receptor, the device has a means to
      limit the X-ray field to a rectangular shape that does not exceed
      the boundary of the effective image reception area by more than
      1 cm in the diagonal.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Incremental settings

  **22** When irradiation times are 0.08 s or greater, the range of
  settings for the X-ray tube current, irradiation time or current time
  product of dental X-ray equipment must be in increments that are not
  greater than the decimal multiples and submultiples of 1.00, 1.25,
  1.60, 2.00, 2.50, 3.20, 4.00, 5.00, 6.30 and 8.00.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Modes of operation

  **23** Dental X-ray equipment that has more than one mode of operation
  must be constructed in accordance with the following requirements:

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} in the case of extra-oral equipment that does not
    have an integrated direct digital X-ray image receptor, the step
    size of the adjustment between adjacent settings of the current time
    product must not be greater than 1.6 mAs; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} in the case of intra-oral equipment,

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} the step size of the adjustment between adjacent
      settings of the current time product must not be greater than
      1.6 mAs, and

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} the ratio of the maximum to minimum current time
      product settings must be at least 4:1.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Current time product

  **24** For each selectable X-ray tube voltage of intra-oral dental
  X-ray equipment, the ratio of the maximum to minimum current time
  product settings must be at least 16:1.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Primary shielding

  **25** Extra-oral dental X-ray equipment must have primary protective
  shielding that completely overlaps the X-ray field and that provides
  the following minimum attenuation:

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} 0.5 mm lead equivalent, in the case of a nominal
    X-ray tube voltage of 90 kV or less; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} 2 mm lead equivalent, in the case of a nominal
    X-ray tube voltage of more than 90 kV.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Attenuation equivalent

  **26** All parts of extra-oral dental X-ray equipment that are located
  in the path of the X-ray beam between the patient and the X-ray image
  receptor must, when taken together, have an attenuation of not more
  than 1.2 mm aluminum equivalent, as determined at the highest X-ray
  tube voltage of the equipment.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Hand-held intra-oral equipment

  **27** Hand-held intra-oral dental X-ray equipment must have all of
  the following features:

    constructed to include the following safety features:

    - [(a)]{.lawlabel} for each microwave power source, a device or

      - [(i)]{.lawlabel} the door cannot be opened until the microwave

      - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} the microwave power generating component

    - [(h)]{.lawlabel} components and shields constructed and positioned
      so that adjustments to the service controls and user controls to

      that results from radiation within an infinitesimally small
      wavelength range, expressed in watts per square metre per
      nanometre (W/m^2^/nm). ([éclairement énergétique
      spectral]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

  [[spectral transmittance]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[spectral transmittance]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means the ratio of
      the spectral irradiance that is transmitted through protective
      eyewear to the spectral irradiance that is incident and normal to
      the surface of the eyewear. ([transmittance
      spectrale]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

  [[tanning equipment]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[tanning equipment]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means a device that

      - [(a)]{.lawlabel} can be equipped with one or more ultraviolet
        lamps; and

      - [(b)]{.lawlabel} induces skin tanning or other cosmetic effects.

      It does not include any such device that is used in the production
      of therapeutic effects for medical purposes. ([appareil de
      bronzage]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

  [[timer]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[timer]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means a device that is capable of
      ending the emission of ultraviolet radiation from tanning
      equipment after a preset period. ([minuterie]{.DefinedTermLink
      lang="fr"})

  [[ultraviolet lamp]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[ultraviolet lamp]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means a device that
      produces ultraviolet radiation in the wavelength range from 200 nm
      to 400 nm and is used in tanning equipment. ([lampe à rayonnements
      ultraviolets]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

  [[wavelength]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[wavelength]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means a wavelength as measured
      in air. ([longueur d'onde]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

- **9** Every piece of tanning equipment must have the following safety

- **11** Every ultraviolet lamp that is used in tanning equipment must
  be constructed so that it cannot be inserted and operated in a
  single-contact medium screw lampholder or a double-contact medium

- **12** Every piece of tanning equipment must be accompanied by a
  number of sets of protective eyewear at least equal to the maximum
  number of persons who may, at the same time, be exposed to ultraviolet

#### [Functioning Standards]{.HTitleText2} {#functioning-standards .SchedHeadL2}

- **13** Every piece of tanning equipment, whether it has its original
  components or replacement components recommended by the manufacturer,
  must, under the conditions of use specified by the manufacturer, meet
  the functioning standards set out in this Part.

- **14** Every ultraviolet lamp that is used in tanning equipment must
  function so that, at any distance and in any direction from the
  radiation source, the irradiance within the wavelength range from 200
  nm to less than 260 nm does not exceed 0.003 of the irradiance within
  the wavelength range from 260 nm to 320 nm.

- **15** Every replacement ultraviolet lamp must function so that the
  maximum exposure time remains within 10% of the maximum exposure time
  originally recommended by the manufacturer.

- **16** The timer must

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} be adjustable to preset times and have a maximum
    timer setting not greater than the maximum exposure time recommended
    by the manufacturer;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} have a margin of error not greater than 10% of the
    maximum timer setting; and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} not automatically reset when the tanning equipment
    emissions have been ended by the timer.

- **17** Protective eyewear must have a spectral transmittance that is

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} not more than 0.001 over the wavelength range from
    200 nm to 320 nm;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} not more than 0.01 over the wavelength range from
    320 nm to 400 nm; and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} sufficient over wavelengths greater than 400 nm to
    enable the user to read the labels and use the control specified in
    paragraph 9(a).

##### [Radiographic Equipment]{.HTitleText3} {#radiographic-equipment .SchedHeadL3}

- **10** Radiographic equipment that is equipped with an automatic
  exposure control must have

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} if the operating X-ray tube voltage is 50 kV or
    more, a minimum irradiation time capability that does not exceed

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} in the case of a field emission device that
      operates in pulse mode, the time equivalent to two pulses, or

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} in the case of any other radiographic equipment,
      the greater of 1/60 s or the time required to deliver a current
      time product of 5 mAs;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} a means to automatically terminate the irradiation
    when

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} if the operating X-ray tube voltage is less than
      50 kV, the current time product exceeds 1,200 mAs per irradiation,
      or

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} if the operating X-ray tube voltage is 50 kV or
      more,

      - [(A)]{.lawlabel} the current time product exceeds 600 mAs per
        irradiation, or

      - [(B)]{.lawlabel} the product of the X-ray tube voltage, X-ray
        tube current and irradiation time exceeds 60 kVAs per
        irradiation; and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} when an irradiation under automatic exposure
    control terminates because the limits specified in paragraph (b)
    have been reached,

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} a visual indicator or audible signal that warns
      the operator of the termination, and

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} a reset control that must be activated manually
      before another irradiation under automatic exposure control can be
      made.

- - **11** [(1)]{.lawlabel} General purpose radiographic equipment must
    have

    - [(a)]{.lawlabel} a beam limiting device that

      - [(i)]{.lawlabel} permits stepless adjustment of the size of the
        X-ray field, and

      - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} when it is set at the smallest aperture and at
        a focal spot to image receptor distance of 1 m, has an X-ray
        field of 5 cm by 5 cm, or less;

    - [(b)]{.lawlabel} an X-ray field indicator that uses light to
      visually define the X-ray field so that the limits of the X-ray
      field are visible under the ambient lighting conditions in an
      X-ray room; and

    - [(c)]{.lawlabel} a means by which the operator may

      - [(i)]{.lawlabel} determine when the X-ray beam axis is
        perpendicular to the image receptor plane,

      - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} determine the focal spot to image receptor
        distance to within 2% of that distance, and

      - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} align the centre of the X-ray field with the
        centre of the image reception area to within 2% of the focal
        spot to image receptor distance.

  - [(2)]{.lawlabel} The X-ray field indicator referred to in paragraph
    (1)(b) must

    - [(a)]{.lawlabel} illuminate on average at a minimum of 100 lx when
      measured from the lesser of

      - [(i)]{.lawlabel} the distance of 1 m from the X-ray source, or

      - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} the maximum focal spot to image receptor
        distance;

    - [(b)]{.lawlabel} be circumscribed by the beam limiting device;

    - [(c)]{.lawlabel} have as its perimeter the locus of points at
      which the illumination is one fourth of the maximum illumination
      in the area; and

    - [(d)]{.lawlabel} when the X-ray beam axis is perpendicular to the
      image receptor plane, visually define the X-ray field within the
      following specifications, namely,

      - [(i)]{.lawlabel} the separation between the perimeter of the
        visually defined field and that of the X-ray field does not
        exceed 2% of the focal spot to image receptor distance, and

      - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} the dimensions of the X-ray field are
        indicated and are accurate to within 2% of the focal spot to
        image receptor distance.

- - **12** [(1)]{.lawlabel} General purpose radiographic equipment that
    has a positive beam limiting system must

    - [(a)]{.lawlabel} permit stepless adjustment of the size of the
      X-ray field;

    - [(b)]{.lawlabel} when it is set at the smallest aperture and at a
      focal spot to image receptor distance of 1 m, have an X-ray field
      of 5 cm by 5 cm, or less;

    - [(c)]{.lawlabel} permit adjustment of the size of the X-ray field
      to dimensions that are smaller than those of the image reception
      area;

    - [(d)]{.lawlabel} under the conditions of operation specified in
      subsection (2),

      - [(i)]{.lawlabel} automatically adjust the dimensions of the
        X-ray field to the dimensions of the image reception area, or to
        a selected portion of that area, within 5 s after insertion of
        the image receptor, or

      - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} prevent the emission of X-rays until the beam
        limiting device is manually adjusted so that

        - [(A)]{.lawlabel} the dimensions of the X-ray field do not
          exceed those of the image reception area, or the selected
          portion of that area, by more than 3% of the focal spot to
          image receptor distance, and

        - [(B)]{.lawlabel} the sum of the absolute values of the
          differences in the dimensions of the X-ray field and the image
          reception area, or the selected portion of that area, does not
          exceed 4% of the focal spot to image receptor distance; and

    - [(e)]{.lawlabel} automatically revert to one of the requirements
      set out in paragraph (d) on any change to

      - [(i)]{.lawlabel} the dimensions of the image reception area, or

      - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} the focal spot to image receptor distance, if
        the change would result in failure to meet the requirements of
        paragraph (d).

  - [(2)]{.lawlabel} For the purposes of paragraph (1)(d), the
    conditions of operation are as follows:

    - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the image receptor is inserted into a permanently
      mounted cassette holder;

    - [(b)]{.lawlabel} neither the length nor the width of the image
      reception area exceeds 50 cm;

    - [(c)]{.lawlabel} the X-ray beam axis is within 3° of the
      perpendicular to the image receptor plane;

    - [(d)]{.lawlabel} the X-ray beam axis is within 3° of

      - [(i)]{.lawlabel} the horizontal plane, when the focal spot to
        image receptor distance is 90 cm or more but not more than 205
        cm, or

      - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} the vertical plane, when the focal spot to
        image receptor distance is 90 cm or more but not more than 130
        cm; and

    - [(e)]{.lawlabel} neither tomography nor stereoscopic radiography
      is being performed.

##### [Radioscopic Equipment]{.HTitleText3} {#radioscopic-equipment .SchedHeadL3}

- **18** Radioscopic equipment must

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} have an X-ray image intensifier that includes
    protective shielding that

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} for any focal spot to image receptor distance,
      intercepts the entire cross section of the X-ray beam,

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} intercepts the X-ray beam and scattered
      radiation from the image intensifier that would otherwise reach
      the operator,

    - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} prevents the radioscopic X-ray tube from
      emitting X-rays unless the protective shielding is in place to
      intercept the X-ray beam, and

    - [(iv)]{.lawlabel} sufficiently attenuates the X-rays transmitted
      through or scattered from the entrance window of the radioscopic
      imaging assembly to meet the requirements of section 32;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} for mobile radioscopic equipment, have an X-ray
    image intensifier that is an integral part of the equipment or is
    interlocked in such a manner that its removal prevents X-rays from
    being produced;

    intensity for the purposes of subsection (1) shall produce a result

      ultrasonic power; and

    - [(b)]{.lawlabel} an ultrasound detector of dimensions less than
      one wavelength in water to measure the pulse repetition rate, the
      pulse duration and the effective radiating area.

  - [(3)]{.lawlabel} The power indicator referred to in paragraph
    2(4)(b) shall show on the scale of the ultrasonic power control or

    ±20 per cent when the output is greater than 10 per cent of the
    maximum ultrasonic power.

  - [(4)]{.lawlabel} The timer referred to in paragraph 2(4)(d) shall be

    to within 10 per cent for settings from five to 10 minutes, and to
    within one minute for settings greater than 10 minutes.

  - [(5)]{.lawlabel} The ultrasonic power output shall remain constant
    within ±20 per cent of its initial value during one hour of
    continuous operation, at maximum output and at rated supply line
    voltage, in water at a temperature of 22°C ± 3°C.

  - [(6)]{.lawlabel} The actual ultrasonic frequency of an ultrasound
    therapy device shall not differ more than ±5% from the ultrasonic
    frequency of the device that is stated on the external surface of
    the housing of the device pursuant to subparagraph 2(3)(a)(vi).

  - [(7)]{.lawlabel} The effective radiating area shall be kept within
    ±20 per cent of the rated value given by the manufacturer.

      a key before X-rays can be produced and the removal of the key to
      terminate production of X-rays,

    - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} a warning light that indicates when the power
      is "ON",

    - [(iv)]{.lawlabel} a warning light, in addition to the warning
      light referred to in subparagraph (iii), that

      - [(A)]{.lawlabel} indicates when X-rays are being generated,

      - [(B)]{.lawlabel} where the device is designed to operate with a
        pulsed X-ray beam, is activated for at least one-half second,
        and

      - [(C)]{.lawlabel} unless there is a warning light or other
        indicator referred to in subparagraph (v) incorporated on the
        control panel, is connected and interlocked in such a manner
        that X-rays cannot be produced if the warning light
        malfunctions, and

    - [(v)]{.lawlabel} unless the warning light referred to in
      subparagraph (iv) is connected and interlocked in the manner
      described in clause (iv)(C), a warning light or other indicator
      that duplicates the functions of the warning light referred to in
      subparagraph (iv) whose function is not affected if the warning
      light referred to in that subparagraph malfunctions;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} shielded doors or panels over all access openings
    designed for insertion or removal of any material to be examined
    except where the design of the device prevents the insertion of any
    part of the human body into the primary X-ray beam;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} safety features connecting and interlocking each of
    the doors and panels referred to in paragraph (b) with at least two
    independent interlocks to prevent the generation of X-rays if any
    door or panel is open;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} interlocks on all doors and panels, other than
    those doors and panels referred to in paragraph (b), that allow
    access to areas inside the cabinet where the exposure rate to X-rays
    may exceed 0.5 milliroentgen per hour to prevent the generation of
    X-rays if any door or panel is opened or removed;

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} subject to section 8, one or more controls that
    require separate operator action to initiate each X-ray exposure;
    and

  - [(f)]{.lawlabel} a beam limiting device that

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} ensures that the primary X-ray beam is aligned
      with the X-ray detector, and

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} restricts the size of the primary X-ray beam, at
      the plane of the X-ray detector, so that it does not exceed the
      maximum size of the detector.

- **8** Paragraph 7(e) does not apply to cabinet X-ray equipment that

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} contains a conveyor or other automatic feed system
    for insertion and removal of material to be examined where the X-ray
    exposure or sequence of X-ray exposures is initiated automatically
    by means of a photocell or other material sensing device; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} is designed and constructed to include the
    following additional safety features:

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} where the device requires the continuous presence
      of an operator during any examination of material, a control or
      switch of a type that

      - [(A)]{.lawlabel} requires continuous pressure by the operator to
        maintain automatic operation of the device, and

      - [(B)]{.lawlabel} when released, terminates the X-ray exposure or
        sequence of exposures and stops the conveyor or other automatic
        feed system,

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} where the device is controlled by a central
      processing unit and does not require the continuous presence of an
      operator during any examination of material, provision in the
      circuitry of the device for the connection of

      - [(A)]{.lawlabel} a remote warning light to indicate when X-rays
        are being generated, and

      - [(B)]{.lawlabel} a control or switch to stop the conveyor or
        other automatic feed system and terminate the X-ray exposure or
        sequence of X-ray exposures from a location other than the
        control panel, and

    - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} a conveyor or other automatic feed system of
      sufficient length to prevent the insertion of any part of the
      human body into any area of the device where the exposure to
      X-rays exceeds 0.5 milliroentgen per hour.

- **9** Cabinet X-ray equipment that is designed to permit the entry of
  a person shall be designed and constructed to include the following
  additional safety features:

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} a switch or control within the cabinet that, when
    activated,

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} prevents X-rays from being generated, and

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} cannot be reset, by-passed or overriden from the
      control panel;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} controls for initiating X-ray generation that are
    external to the cabinet and that make it impossible for the
    initiation of X-ray generation from within the cabinet;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} where any optical or image-monitoring equipment is
    used within the cabinet and fluoroscopy alignment is required, a
    control device external to the cabinet designed to remotely focus
    the device;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} an audible and visible warning signal within the
    cabinet that

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} is activated for at least ten seconds immediately
      prior to initiation of X-ray generation after the closing of any
      door that is designed to permit human access into the cabinet, and

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} is connected in such a way that failure or
      malfunction of any single component in the device does not cause a
      simultaneous failure of both the audible and visible warning
      signal; and

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} a warning light within the cabinet that, when
    X-rays are being produced,

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} remains on continuously, or

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} where the X-ray generation period is one-half
      second or less, remains on for at least one-half second.

#### [Standards of Functioning]{.HTitleText2} {#standards-of-functioning-10 .SchedHeadL2}

- **10** Cabinet X-ray equipment, when fully assembled for use, shall
  function in such a way that, under all possible operating conditions
  of X-ray generation and at the maximum possible material handling rate
  specified by the manufacturer, the exposure rate from leakage
  radiation, averaged over a time period that is not less than five
  minutes, does not exceed 0.5 milliroentgen per hour at a distance of
  five centimetres from

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} any accessible external surface of the device,
    including the shielded doors or panels referred to in paragraph
    7(b), and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} where the design of the device prevents the
    insertion of any part of the human body into the primary X-ray beam,
    an imaginary plane surface that is drawn to close those access
    openings referred to in paragraph 7(b),

  when averaged over a detection area of ten square centimetres.

#### [Warning Sign Specifications]{.HTitleText2} {#warning-sign-specifications-4 .SchedHeadL2}

- **11** The X-radiation warning sign referred to in section 4 is a sign
  that

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} is shown in two contrasting colours;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} is clearly visible and identifiable from a distance
    of one metre;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} has no outer dimensions less than two centimetres;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} bears the words "CAUTION, X-RAYS" and "[ATTENTION,
    RAYONS X]{.otherLang lang="fr"}"; and

    ### [PART VII]{.HLabel1}[Laser Products]{.HTitleText1} {#part-viilaser-products .SchedHeadL1}

    #### [Interpretation]{.HTitleText2} {#interpretation-15 .SchedHeadL2}

    - [1]{.lawlabel} The following definitions apply in this Part.

      [[IEC 60825-1]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

      :   [[IEC 60825-1]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means the International
          Electrotechnical Commission standard IEC 60825-1, Edition 3.0,
          2014-05, entitled Safety of laser products -- Part 1:
          Equipment classification and requirements. ([CEI
          60825-1]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

      [[IEC 62471]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

      :   [[IEC 62471]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means the International
          Electrotechnical Commission standard IEC 62471, Edition 1.0,
          2006-07, entitled Photobiological safety of lamps and lamp
          systems. ([CEI 62471]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

      [[IEC 62471-5]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

      :   [[IEC 62471-5]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means the International
          Electrotechnical Commission standard IEC 62471-5, Edition 1.0,
          2015-06, entitled Photobiological safety of lamps and lamp
          systems -- Part 5: Image projectors. ([CEI
          62471-5]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

    - [2]{.lawlabel} For the purposes of this Part

      - [(a)]{.lawlabel} words and expressions used in the standards
        incorporated by reference have the same meanings as in the Act;

      - [(b)]{.lawlabel} a reference to "plaque" in the French version
        of IEC 60825-1 is to be read as a reference to
        [[étiquette]{.dfn}]{.DefinitionRef} as defined in section 2 of
        the French version of the Act; and

      - [(c)]{.lawlabel} a reference to "rayonnement" in the French
        versions of IEC 60825-1, IEC 62471 and IEC 62471-5 is to be read
        as a reference to [radiation]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"} as
        defined in section 2 of the French version of the Act.

    #### [Standards of Design, Construction and Functioning]{.HTitleText2} {#standards-of-design-construction-and-functioning .SchedHeadL2}

    - - [3]{.lawlabel} [(1)]{.lawlabel} Except as otherwise provided in
        this Part, a laser product must be designed and constructed, and
        must function, in accordance with the applicable requirements
        set out in IEC 60825-1, without regard to any other standard
        incorporated by reference to which it refers. 

      - [(2)]{.lawlabel} The following are exempted from subsection (1):

        - [(a)]{.lawlabel} a laser product that is a component or a
          repair part as set out in clause 1 of IEC 60825-1;

        - [(b)]{.lawlabel} a laser product that is a [[medical
          device]{.dfn}]{.DefinitionRef} as defined in section 1 of the
          [Medical Devices Regulations](/eng/regulations/SOR-98-282) and
          that belongs to Class II, III or IV in accordance with the
          classification rules set out in Schedule 1 to those
          Regulations.

      - ###### Exception {#exception .MarginalNote}

        [(3)]{.lawlabel} A laser product referred to in clauses 4 and 5
        of IEC 60825-1 with an emission level that does not exceed the
        accessible emission limit for Class 1 under all conditions of
        operation, maintenance, service and failure is exempt from
        clause 6 of IEC 60825-1 and sections 6 to 8 of this Part.

    - - [4]{.lawlabel} [(1)]{.lawlabel} Except as otherwise provided in
        this Part, a laser product that is designed to function as a
        conventional lamp and that satisfies the criteria referred to in
        subclause 4.4 of IEC 60825-1 may have its emissions evaluated in
        accordance with the applicable requirements set out in IEC 62471
        or IEC 62471-5.

      - [(2)]{.lawlabel} A laser product referred to in subsection (1)
        must respect the requirements of that subsection or the
        requirements of subsection 3(1).

    - [5]{.lawlabel} The following provisions of IEC 60825-1 do not
      apply to a laser product:

      - [(a)]{.lawlabel} those relating to the training of personnel
        referred to in subclause 6.1;

      - [(b)]{.lawlabel} those relating to the protection against
        non-optical hazards referred to in subclause 6.15.1; and

      - [(c)]{.lawlabel} those relating to the additional requirements
        for specific laser products referred to in clause 9.

    #### [Information and Labelling]{.HTitleText2} {#information-and-labelling-3 .SchedHeadL2}

    ##### [General]{.HTitleText3} {#general-2 .SchedHeadL3}

    - - [6]{.lawlabel} [(1)]{.lawlabel} The information referred to in
        sections 7 and 8 must be provided in both official languages.

      - ###### Exceptions {#exceptions .MarginalNote}

        [(2)]{.lawlabel} The following laser products are exempt from
        sections 7 and 8:

        - [(a)]{.lawlabel} a laser product that is a component or a
          repair part, as set out in clause 1 of IEC 60825-1, and

        - [(b)]{.lawlabel} a laser product that is a [[medical
          device]{.dfn}]{.DefinitionRef} as defined in section 1 of the
          [Medical Devices Regulations](/eng/regulations/SOR-98-282) and
          that belongs to one of Class II, III or IV in accordance with
          the classification rules set out in Schedule 1 to those
          Regulations.

    ##### [Information]{.HTitleText3} {#information-3 .SchedHeadL3}

    - - [7]{.lawlabel} [(1)]{.lawlabel} Except as otherwise provided in
        this Part, the manufacturer, distributor and importer of a laser
        product must ensure that the product is accompanied by the
        information described in subclause 8.1 of IEC 60825-1 or, if
        applicable, the information described in subclauses 6.6 and 6.7
        of IEC 62471-5.

      - [(2)]{.lawlabel} The information described in items 8.1a),
        c), f) and i) of IEC 60825-1 is required for a laser product
        with an "accessible emission", as defined in subclause 3.2 of
        that standard, during operation, maintenance or service, that is
        greater than that of a Class 3R laser product, as determined in
        accordance with item 5.3d) of that standard.

      - [(3)]{.lawlabel} The requirements for additional information
        under subclause 8.2 of IEC 60825-1 do not apply to a laser
        product.

    ##### [Labelling]{.HTitleText3} {#labelling-3 .SchedHeadL3}

    - - [8]{.lawlabel} [(1)]{.lawlabel} A laser product must bear labels
        that meet the requirements of clause 7 of IEC 60825-1 or, if
        applicable, subclause 6.5 of IEC 62471-5.

      - [(2)]{.lawlabel} A laser product designed to function as a
        conventional lamp must be labelled in accordance with the
        requirements of subclause 4.4 of IEC 60825-1 and, if applicable,
        subclauses 6.5 and 6.7 of IEC 62471-5.

      - [(3)]{.lawlabel} In addition to the information referred to in
        subclause 4.4 and clause 7 of IEC 60825-1, the labels of a laser
        product, including one designed to function as a conventional
        lamp, must contain

        - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the manufacturer's name and the civic address
          of its principal place of business;

        - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the model number of the product;

        - [(c)]{.lawlabel} the date of manufacture of the product; and

        - [(d)]{.lawlabel} if the product is imported for sale or lease,
          the distributor's name and the civic address of its principal
          place of business in Canada.

      - [(4)]{.lawlabel} Despite section 6, the information referred to
        in paragraphs (3)(a) to (d) must be shown in at least one
        official language.
