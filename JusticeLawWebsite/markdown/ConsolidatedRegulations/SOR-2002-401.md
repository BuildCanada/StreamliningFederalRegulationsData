## [Legal Capacity]{.HTitleText1} {#h-697607 .Part}

**[[2]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-2 .sectionLabel}** UNESCO has the legal
capacities of a body corporate.

## [Privileges and Immunities of UNESCO]{.HTitleText1} {#h-697609 .Part}

### [Convention on Privileges and Immunities]{.HTitleText2} {#h-697610 .Subheading}

**[[3]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-3 .sectionLabel}** UNESCO has the privileges
and immunities set out in Articles II and III of the Convention on
Privileges and Immunities to the extent that they are specified in
sections 4 to 10.

### [Property, Funds and Assets]{.HTitleText2} {#h-697612 .Subheading}

- **[[4]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-4 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} UNESCO,
  its property and assets, wherever located and by whomsoever held,
  shall enjoy immunity from every form of legal process except in so far
  as in any particular case UNESCO has expressly waived its immunity. No
  waiver of immunity extends to any measure of execution except with
  UNESCO's express consent.

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} The property and assets of UNESCO, wherever located
  and by whomsoever held, are immune from search, requisition,
  confiscation, expropriation and any other form of interference,
  whether by executive, administrative, judicial or legislative action.

**[[5]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-5 .sectionLabel}** Without being restricted by
financial controls, regulations or moratoria of any kind, UNESCO may

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} hold funds, gold or currency of any kind and operate
  accounts in any currency;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} transfer its funds, gold or currency from Canada to
  another country, to Canada from another county or within Canada; and

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} convert any currency held by it into any other
  currency.

**[[6]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-6 .sectionLabel}** The premises of UNESCO are
inviolable, subject to the application of fire or safety regulations.

**[[7]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-7 .sectionLabel}** The archives of UNESCO, and
in general all documents belonging to it or held by it, are inviolable,
wherever located.

- **[[8]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-8 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} UNESCO,
  its assets, income and other property are

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} exempt from all direct taxes other than taxes that
    are, in fact, no more than charges for public utility services;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} exempt from customs duties and prohibitions and
    restrictions on imports and exports in respect of articles imported
    or exported by UNESCO for its official use so long as those articles
    are not sold in Canada except under conditions agreed with the
    Government of Canada; and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} exempt from customs duties and prohibitions and
    restrictions on imports and exports in respect of its publications.

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} UNESCO shall not, as a general rule, claim exemption
  from excise duties and from taxes on the sale of movable and immovable
  property which form part of the price to be paid. Nevertheless, when
  UNESCO is making important purchases for official use of property on
  which such duties and taxes have been charged or are chargeable, the
  Government of Canada shall, whenever possible, make appropriate
  administrative arrangements for the remission or return of the amount
  of duty or tax.

### [Communications]{.HTitleText2} {#h-697628 .Subheading}

- **[[9]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-9 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} UNESCO
  shall enjoy for its official communications treatment not less
  favourable than that accorded by the Government of Canada to any other
  Government, including the latter's diplomatic mission, in the matter
  of priorities, rates and taxes on mails, cables, telephone and other
  communications, and press rates for information to the press and
  radio.

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} No censorship shall be applied to the official
  correspondence and other official communications of UNESCO.

**[[10]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-10 .sectionLabel}** UNESCO has the right to
use codes and to dispatch and receive its correspondence by courier or
in bags, which have the same immunities and privileges as diplomatic
couriers and bags.

## [Representatives of Foreign States]{.HTitleText1} {#h-697633 .Part}

- **[[11]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-11 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} In
  this section, [[representatives of a member]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}
  means representatives of a foreign state that is a member of UNESCO
  and includes all delegates, deputy delegates, advisers, technical
  experts and secretaries of delegations of the foreign state.

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} The representatives of a member have the privileges
  and immunities set out in Article IV of the Convention on Privileges
  and Immunities to the extent that they are specified in this section.

- [(3)]{.lawlabel} Representatives of a member to the principal and
  subsidiary organs of UNESCO and to conferences convened by UNESCO or
  by the Institute on UNESCO's behalf shall, while exercising their
  functions and during their journey to and from the place of meeting in
  Canada, enjoy the following privileges and immunities:

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} immunity from personal arrest or detention and from
    seizure of their personal baggage, and, in respect of words spoken
    or written and all acts done by them in their capacity as
    representatives of a member, immunity from legal process of every
    kind;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} inviolability for all papers and documents;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} the right to use codes and to receive papers or
    correspondence by courier or in sealed bags;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} exemption in respect of themselves and their
    spouses from immigration restrictions, alien registration or
    national service obligations while they visit or pass through Canada
    in the exercise of their functions;

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} the same facilities in respect of currency or
    exchange restrictions as are accorded to representatives of foreign
    governments on temporary official missions;

  - [(f)]{.lawlabel} the same immunities and facilities in respect of
    their personal baggage as are accorded to diplomatic envoys; and

  - [(g)]{.lawlabel} such other privileges, immunities and facilities
    not inconsistent with the foregoing as diplomatic envoys enjoy,
    except that they have no right to claim exemption from customs
    duties on goods imported (otherwise than as part of their personal
    baggage) or from excise duties or sales taxes.

- [(4)]{.lawlabel} In order to secure, for the representatives of a
  member to the principal and subsidiary organs of UNESCO and to
  conferences convened by UNESCO or the Institute in UNESCO's name,
  complete freedom of speech and independence in the discharge of their
  duties, the immunity from legal process in respect of words spoken or
  written and all acts done by them in discharging their duties
  continues to be accorded, notwithstanding that the persons concerned
  are no longer representatives of a member.

- [(5)]{.lawlabel} Where the incidence of any form of taxation depends
  upon residence, periods during which the representatives of a member
  to the principal and subsidiary organs of UNESCO and to conferences
  convened by UNESCO or by the Institute in UNESCO's name are present in
  Canada for the discharge of their duties are not considered as periods
  of residence.

- [(6)]{.lawlabel} Privileges and immunities are accorded to the
  representatives of a member not for the personal benefit of the
  individuals themselves, but in order to safeguard the independent
  exercise of their functions in connection with UNESCO.

- [(7)]{.lawlabel} A foreign state not only has the right but is under a
  duty to waive the immunity of its representative in any case where in
  the opinion of the foreign state the immunity would impede the course
  of justice, and it can be waived without prejudice to the purpose for
  which the immunity is accorded.

- [(8)]{.lawlabel} Subsections (3) to (5) do not apply in respect of a
  Canadian citizen or a permanent resident.

## [Senior Officials]{.HTitleText1} {#h-697650 .Part}

- **[[12]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-12 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  following senior officials, if they are not Canadian citizens or
  permanent residents, are designated under paragraph 5(1)(f) of the
  Act:

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the Director-General; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} any other official of UNESCO who holds a position
    equivalent to the position of Deputy Secretary-General of the United
    Nations.

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} Designated senior officials have the privileges and
  immunities comparable to the privileges and immunities accorded to
  diplomatic agents under the Vienna Convention.

- [(3)]{.lawlabel} Privileges and immunities are granted to designated
  senior officials in the interests of UNESCO and not for the personal
  benefit of the individuals themselves.

- [(4)]{.lawlabel} The Director-General has the right and the duty to
  waive the immunity granted to a designated senior official in any case
  where, in the opinion of the Director-General, the immunity would
  impede the course of justice and can be waived without prejudice to
  the interests of UNESCO. In the case of the Director-General, the
  General Conference of UNESCO has the right to waive immunity.

## [Other Officials]{.HTitleText1} {#h-697658 .Part}

- **[[13]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-13 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  following officials are designated under paragraph 5(1)(g) of the Act:

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the Director-General and any other official of
    UNESCO who holds a position equivalent to the position of Deputy
    Secretary-General of the United Nations, if they are Canadian
    citizens or permanent residents;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} all other officials of UNESCO who are not
    designated under subsection 12(1).

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} Designated officials have the privileges and
  immunities set out in section 18 of Article V of the Convention on
  Privileges and Immunities to the extent that they are specified in
  this section.

- [(3)]{.lawlabel} Designated officials shall

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} be immune from legal process in respect of words
    spoken or written and all acts performed by them in their official
    capacity;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} if they are not Canadian citizens or permanent
    residents, be exempt from taxation on the salaries and emoluments
    paid to them by UNESCO;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} be immune, together with their spouses and
    relatives dependent on them, from immigration restrictions and alien
    registration;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} be immune from national service obligations;

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} be given, together with their spouses and relatives
    dependent on them, the same repatriation facilities in time of
    international crisis as diplomatic envoys;

  - [(f)]{.lawlabel} be accorded the same privileges in respect of
    exchange facilities as are accorded to the officials of comparable
    ranks forming part of diplomatic missions to the Government of
    Canada; and

  - [(g)]{.lawlabel} have the right to import free of duty their
    furniture and effects at the time of first taking up their post in
    Canada.

- [(4)]{.lawlabel} Privileges and immunities are granted to designated
  officials in the interests of UNESCO and not for the personal benefit
  of the individuals themselves.

- [(5)]{.lawlabel} The Director-General has the right and the duty to
  waive the immunity of any designated official in any case where, in
  the opinion of the Director-General, the immunity would impede the
  course of justice and can be waived without prejudice to the interests
  of UNESCO.

## [Experts]{.HTitleText1} {#h-697674 .Part}

- **[[14]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-14
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Experts who perform missions for
  UNESCO are designated under paragraph 5(1)(h) of the Act and have the
  privileges and immunities set out in Article VI of the Convention on
  Privileges and Immunities to the extent that they are specified in
  this section.

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} Designated experts performing missions for UNESCO
  shall be accorded such privileges and immunities as are necessary for
  the independent exercise of their functions during the period of their
  missions, including the time spent on journeys in connection with
  their missions. In particular they shall be accorded

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} immunity from personal arrest or detention and from
    seizure of their personal baggage;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} in respect of words spoken or written and acts done
    by them in the course of the performance of their mission, immunity
    from legal process of every kind;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} inviolability for all papers and documents;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} for the purpose of their communications with
    UNESCO, the right to use codes and to receive papers or
    correspondence by courier or in sealed bags;

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} the same facilities in respect of currency or
    exchange restrictions as are accorded to representatives of foreign
    governments on temporary official missions; and

  - [(f)]{.lawlabel} the same immunities and facilities in respect of
    their personal baggage as are accorded to diplomatic envoys.

- [(3)]{.lawlabel} The immunity from legal process described in
  paragraph (2)(b) continues to be enjoyed notwithstanding that the
  persons concerned are no longer employed on missions for UNESCO.

- [(4)]{.lawlabel} Privileges and immunities are granted to designated
  experts in the interests of UNESCO and not for the personal benefit of
  the individuals themselves.

- [(5)]{.lawlabel} The Director-General has the right and the duty to
  waive the immunity of any designated expert in any case where, in the
  opinion of the Director-General, the immunity would impede the course
  of justice and can be waived without prejudice to the interests of
  UNESCO.

## [Coming into Force]{.HTitleText1} {#h-697687 .Part}

**[[15]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-15 .sectionLabel}** This Order comes into
force on the day on which it is registered.
