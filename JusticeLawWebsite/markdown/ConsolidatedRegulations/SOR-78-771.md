## Charlo Airport Zoning Regulations {#charlo-airport-zoning-regulations .Title-of-Act}

[SOR]{.abbr title="Statutory Orders and Regulations"}/78-771
:::

[AERONAUTICS ACT](/eng/acts/A-2)

Registration 1978-10-13

Zoning Regulations Respecting Charlo Airport

P.C. 1978-3100 1978-10-12
::::

::::::::: section
::: {.section .order}
His Excellency the Governor General in Council, on the recommendation of
the Minister of Transport, pursuant to section 6 of the [Aeronautics
Act](/eng/acts/A-2), is pleased hereby to approve the annexed [Zoning
Regulations respecting Charlo Airport](/eng/regulations/SOR-78-771),
made by the Minister of Transport, on the 10th day of October, 1978.
:::

## [Short Title]{.HTitleText1} {#h-864161 .Part}

**[[1]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-1 .sectionLabel}** These Regulations may be
cited as the [Charlo Airport Zoning
Regulations](/eng/regulations/SOR-78-771).

## [Interpretation]{.HTitleText1} {#h-864163 .Part}

**[[2]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-2 .sectionLabel}** In these Regulations,

[[airport]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[airport]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means the Charlo Airport, in the
    County of Restigouche, in the Province of New Brunswick;
    ([aéroport]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

[[airport reference point]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[airport reference point]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means the point
    determined in the manner set out in Part I of the schedule; ([point
    de repère de l'aéroport]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

[[approach surface]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[approach surface]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means an imaginary inclined
    plane extending upward and outward from each end of a strip along
    and at right angles to the projected centre line thereof, which
    approach surface is more particularly described in Part III of the
    schedule; ([surface d'approche]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

[[Minister]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[Minister]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means the Minister of Transport;
    ([ministre]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

[[outer surface]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[outer surface]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means an imaginary surface
    located above and in the immediate vicinity of the airport, which
    outer surface is more particularly described in Part IV of the
    schedule; ([surface extérieure]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

[[strip]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[strip]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means the rectangular portion of the
    landing area of the airport including the runway prepared for the
    take-off and landing of aircraft in a particular direction, which
    strip is more particularly described in Part V of the schedule;
    ([bande]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

[[transitional surface]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[transitional surface]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means an imaginary
    inclined plane extending upward and outward from the lateral limits
    of a strip and its approach surfaces, which transitional surface is
    more particularly described in Part VI of the schedule. ([surface de
    transition]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

**[[3]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-3 .sectionLabel}** For the purposes of these
Regulations, the airport reference point is deemed to be 114 feet above
sea level.

## [Application]{.HTitleText1} {#h-864173 .Part}

**[[4]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-4 .sectionLabel}** These Regulations apply to
all lands and lands under water, including public road allowances
adjacent to or in the vicinity of the airport, the outer limits of which
lands are described in Part II of the schedule, other than such lands as
from time to time form part of the airport.

## [General]{.HTitleText1} {#h-864175 .Part}

**[[5]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-5 .sectionLabel}** No person shall erect or
construct on any land, or land under water, to which these Regulations
apply, any building, structure or object, or any addition to any
existing building, structure or object, the highest point of which will
exceed in elevation at the location of that point any of the surfaces
hereinafter set out that project immediately over and above the surface
of the land at that location, namely,

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} the approach surfaces;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} the outer surface; and

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} the transitional surfaces.

## [Natural Growth]{.HTitleText1} {#h-864180 .Part}

**[[6]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-6 .sectionLabel}** Where an object of natural
growth on any land to which these Regulations apply exceeds in elevation
any of the surfaces set out in paragraphs 5(a) to (c), the Minister may
make a direction that the owner or occupier of the land on which that
object is growing remove the excessive growth thereof.

::: HistoricalNote
- SOR/79-895, s. 1
:::

## [Disposal of Waste]{.HTitleText1} {#h-864184 .Part}

**[[7]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-7 .sectionLabel}** No owner or occupier of any
land or land under water to which these Regulations apply shall permit
such land or any part thereof to be used for the disposal or
accumulation thereon of any waste, material or substance edible by or
attractive to birds.

::: HistoricalNote
- SOR/79-895, s. 1
:::

## [SCHEDULE]{.scheduleLabel}[(ss. 2 and 4)]{.OriginatingRef} {#h-864189 .scheduleLabel}
:::

### [PART I]{.HLabel1}[Description of Airport Reference Point Charlo Airport, County of Restigouche, New Brunswick]{.HTitleText1} {#part-idescription-of-airport-reference-point-charlo-airport-county-of-restigouche-new-brunswick .SchedHeadL1}

Being a point measured five hundred (500) feet perpendicular and
northerly from a centreline point three thousand (3,000) feet westerly
from the easterly end of the runway and having co-ordinate values of
N. 1,544,317.27 feet and E. 1,041,507.02 feet, with reference to the New
Brunswick Horizontal Control System.

### [PART II]{.HLabel1}[Description of the Outer Limits of Lands]{.HTitleText1} {#part-iidescription-of-the-outer-limits-of-lands .SchedHeadL1}

A circular area having a radius of thirteen thousand (13,000) feet from
the airport reference point as shown on Department of Transport
Plans S-424B, C, D and E, dated September 2, 1977.

### [PART III]{.HLabel1}[Description of the Approach Surface]{.HTitleText1} {#part-iiidescription-of-the-approach-surface .SchedHeadL1}

Being an imaginary surface abutting each end of the strip consisting of
an inclined plane having a ratio of one and sixty-six hundredths (1.66)
feet measured vertically to one hundred (100) feet measured horizontally
rising to an imaginary horizontal line drawn parallel to and ten
thousand (10,000) feet measured horizontally from, each end of the
strip, thence the approach surface shall rise at a ratio of two (2) feet
measured vertically to one hundred (100) feet measured horizontally to
an imaginary horizontal line drawn parallel to, and fifty thousand
(50,000) feet measured horizontally from, each end of the strip and
located at a height of nine hundred and sixty-six (966) feet above the
elevation at each end of the strip, the outer ends of each surface being
eight thousand (8,000) feet on each side of the projected centreline as
shown on Department of Transport Plans S-424A, B, C, D, E and F dated
September 2, 1977.

### [PART IV]{.HLabel1}[Description of the Outer Surface]{.HTitleText1} {#part-ivdescription-of-the-outer-surface .SchedHeadL1}

Being an imaginary surface consisting of

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} a common plane established at a constant elevation of
  one hundred and fifty (150) feet above the assigned elevation of the
  airport reference point, and

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} where the common plane described in paragraph (a) is
  less than thirty (30) feet above the surface of the ground, an
  imaginary surface located thirty (30) feet above the surface of the
  ground,

which outer surface is shown on Department of Transport Plans S-424B, C,
D and E, dated September 2, 1977.

### [PART V]{.HLabel1}[Description of the Strip]{.HTitleText1} {#part-vdescription-of-the-strip .SchedHeadL1}

The strip is one thousand (1,000) feet in width, five hundred (500) feet
being on each side of the centreline of the runway, and six thousand
four hundred (6,400) feet in length as shown on Department of Transport
Plan S-424C dated September 2, 1977.

### [PART VI]{.HLabel1}[Description of Each Transitional Surface]{.HTitleText1} {#part-videscription-of-each-transitional-surface .SchedHeadL1}

Being an imaginary surface consisting of an inclined plane rising at a
ratio of one (1) foot measured vertically to seven (7) feet measured
horizontally at right angles to the centre line and centre line produced
of the strip, and extending upward and outward from the lateral limits
of the strip and its approach surfaces to an intersection with the outer
surface as shown on Department of Transport Plan S-424C dated
September 2, 1977.
