## Vessel Fire Safety Regulations {#vessel-fire-safety-regulations .Title-of-Act}

[SOR]{.abbr title="Statutory Orders and Regulations"}/2017-14
:::

[CANADA SHIPPING ACT, 2001](/eng/acts/C-10.15)

Registration 2017-02-03

Vessel Fire Safety Regulations

P.C. 2017-84 2017-02-03
::::

:::::::::::: section
::: {.section .order}
Whereas Part 1 of the proposed Regulations sets out standards that are
additional or complementary to the standards set out in the
International Convention for the Safety of Life at Sea, 1974 and the
Protocol of 1988 relating to the Convention, and the Governor in Council
is satisfied that those additional or complementary standards meet the
objectives of the Convention and Protocol;

Therefore, His Excellency the Governor General in Council, on the
recommendation of the Minister of Transport, pursuant to paragraph
35(1)(d) and subsection 120(1) of the [Canada Shipping Act,
2001](/eng/acts/C-10.15)[[Footnote
]{.wb-invisible}^a^](#fn_a){#fn_a-ID0EDABA}, makes the annexed [Vessel
Fire Safety Regulations](/eng/regulations/SOR-2017-14).

### [Application]{.HTitleText2} {#h-839787 .Subheading}

- **[[101]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-101 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} This
  Part applies in respect of Canadian vessels everywhere that are

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} passenger vessels that are Safety Convention
    vessels;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} passenger vessels that are not Safety Convention
    vessels and are

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} 24 m or more in length, other than vessels
      referred to in paragraph (2)(a), or

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} more than 15 gross tonnage but of less than 24 m
      in length that are carrying more than 36 berthed passengers;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} cargo vessels of 500 gross tonnage or more; or

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} vessels of more than 15 gross tonnage that carry
    dangerous goods, other than in limited quantities, and that are
    referred to in regulation 19.2.2 of Chapter II-2 of SOLAS or have
    cargo spaces referred to in that regulation.

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} This Part, other than the requirements with respect
  to structural fire protection, applies in respect of Canadian vessels
  everywhere that are

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} passenger vessels of 24 m or more in length that

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} are not Safety Convention vessels,

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} are carrying no berthed passengers and fewer
      than 100 unberthed passengers, and

    - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} are engaged on a sheltered waters voyage or a
      near coastal voyage, Class 2, limited; or

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} cargo vessels of 24 m or more in length but of less
    than 500 gross tonnage, other than vessels that carry dangerous
    goods, other than in limited quantities, and that are referred to in
    regulation 19.2.2 of Chapter II-2 of SOLAS or have cargo spaces
    referred to in that regulation.

- [(3)]{.lawlabel} This Part does not apply in respect of

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} pleasure craft;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} fishing vessels;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} high-speed craft;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} vessels that do not have mechanical means of
    propulsion;

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} wooden vessels of primitive build;

  - [(f)]{.lawlabel} vessels that are capable of engaging in the
    drilling for, or the production, conservation or processing of, oil
    or gas; or

  - [(g)]{.lawlabel} nuclear vessels.

### [Compliance]{.HTitleText2} {#h-839810 .Subheading}

- **[[102]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-102
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Except as otherwise provided in this
  Part, a vessel's authorized representative must ensure that the
  requirements of Chapter II-2 of SOLAS --- other than those of
  regulations 1.1 and 1.3 to 1.6 and of Part E --- and the requirements

  vessel.

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} Except as otherwise provided in this Part, a vessel's
  master must ensure that the requirements of regulation 7.8.1 and Part
  E of Chapter II-2 of SOLAS and of sections 117, 118, 151 and 152 are
  met in respect of the vessel.

- [(3)]{.lawlabel} For the purposes of subsection (1), in respect of
  vessels that are not Safety Convention vessels,

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the construction requirements set out in Chapter
    II-2 of SOLAS for vessels carrying more than 36 passengers apply in
    respect of vessels referred to in paragraph 101(1)(b) that

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} are carrying more than 36 berthed passengers,

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} are carrying more than 50 passengers, of which
      more than 12 but not more than 36 are berthed passengers, or

    - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} are carrying more than 100 passengers and are
      engaged on a voyage that is not a sheltered waters voyage or a
      near coastal voyage, Class 2, limited; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the construction requirements set out in Chapter
    II-2 of SOLAS for vessels carrying not more than 36 passengers apply
    in respect of vessels referred to in subparagraph 101(1)(b)(i) that

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} are carrying not more than 50 passengers, of
      which not more than 36 are berthed passengers,

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} are carrying not more than 100 passengers, of
      which not more than 12 are berthed passengers, and are engaged on
      a voyage that is not a sheltered waters voyage or a near coastal
      voyage, Class 2, limited, or

    - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} are carrying more than 100 passengers, of which
      not more than 12 are berthed passengers, and are engaged on a
      voyage that is a sheltered waters voyage or a near coastal voyage,

- [(4)]{.lawlabel} Despite subsections (1) and (2), the requirements for
  tankers that are set out in Chapter II-2 of SOLAS --- other than those

  ensure that any requirements of that regulation that are in addition
  to or different from the requirements referred to in those subsections

  that equipment be fitted not later than July 1, 2005 does not apply
  before

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} if the tanker is a Safety Convention vessel, the
    day on which it is first registered as a Canadian vessel or the day
    on which this section comes into force, whichever is later;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} if the tanker is not a Safety Convention vessel,
    the day on which it is first registered as a Canadian vessel or the
    first anniversary of the day on which this section comes into force,
    whichever is later; and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} if the tanker is transferred from the register of
    another state to the Register for the first time on the day on which
    this section comes into force, the day on which it is transferred to
    the Register.

### [Limitations on Grandfathering]{.HTitleText2} {#h-839833 .Subheading}

- **[[106]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-106
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Except in respect of the
  requirements referred to in subsection (3), sections 103 and 104 do
  not apply in respect of

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} parts of a vessel that, beginning on the day on
    which this section comes into force, undergo repairs, alterations or
    modifications that

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} substantially alter the vessel's dimensions or
      its passenger accommodation spaces, or

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} substantially increase the vessel's service life
      or the life of the vessel's outfitting;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} systems and equipment that are related to the parts
    of a vessel described in paragraph (a);

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} parts of a vessel that, beginning on the day on
    which this section comes into force, are replaced; or

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} systems and equipment that, beginning on the day on
    which this section comes into force, are replaced.

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} Sections 103 and 104 do not apply if the vessel's
  intended service changes in such a manner that any of the requirements
  with respect to structural fire protection and fire safety systems and
  equipment that would have been required under the Act to be met are no
  longer met.

- [(3)]{.lawlabel} Sections 103 and 104 apply only until the first
  anniversary of the day on which this section comes into force in
  respect of the requirements of regulations 13.3.4.2 to 13.3.4.5 and
  13.4.3 and part E --- except regulations 16.3.2.2 and 16.3.2.3 --- of
  Chapter II-2 of SOLAS.

- [(4)]{.lawlabel} Sections 103 and 104 do not apply in respect of

## [PART 2]{.HLabel1}[Structural Fire Protection --- Alternatives for Certain Vessels]{.HTitleText1} {#h-840103 .Part}

### [Interpretation]{.HTitleText2} {#h-840104 .Subheading}

**[[200]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-200 .sectionLabel}** The following
definitions apply in this Part.

[[A-60 class fire rating]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[A-60 class fire rating]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means the thermal and
    integrity requirements specified for "A-60" class divisions.
    ([indice de résistance au feu de type A-60]{.DefinedTermLink
    lang="fr"})

[["F" class divisions]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [["F" class divisions]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means divisions that are
    formed by bulkheads, decks, ceilings or linings and that meet the
    fire-test requirements for "F" class divisions set out in Annex 1 to
    the FTP Code. ([cloisonnements du type « F »]{.DefinedTermLink
    lang="fr"})

### [Application]{.HTitleText2} {#h-840108 .Subheading}

- **[[201]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-201 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} This
  Part applies in respect of Canadian vessels everywhere that are

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} passengers vessels of 24 m or more in length that

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} are not Safety Convention vessels,

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} are carrying no berthed passengers and fewer
      than 100 unberthed passengers, and

    - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} are engaged on a sheltered waters voyage or a
      near coastal voyage, Class 2, limited; or

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} cargo vessels of 24 m or more in length but of less
    than 500 gross tonnage.

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} This Part does not apply in respect of

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} vessels that carry dangerous goods, other than in
    limited quantities, and that are referred to in regulation 19.2.2 of
    Chapter II-2 of SOLAS or have cargo spaces referred to in that
    regulation;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} pleasure craft;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} fishing vessels;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} high-speed craft;

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} vessels that do not have mechanical means of
    propulsion;

  - [(f)]{.lawlabel} wooden vessels of primitive build;

  - [(g)]{.lawlabel} vessels that are capable of engaging in the
    drilling for, or the production, conservation or processing of, oil
    or gas; or

  - [(h)]{.lawlabel} nuclear vessels.

behind ceilings, panelling or linings in accommodation spaces, service
spaces or control stations must be divided by close-fitting draft stops
spaced not more than 7 m apart.

#### [Insulating Materials]{.HTitleText3} {#h-840201 .Subheading}

- **[[215]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-215
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Insulating materials must be
  non-combustible, except in a cargo space, mail room, baggage room, or
  refrigerated compartment of a service space. However, vapour barriers
  and adhesives used with insulating materials, as well as the
  insulating materials used for pipe fittings for cold service systems,
  need not be of non-combustible materials if the non-combustible
  materials are the minimum quantity feasible and their exposed surfaces
  have low flame spread characteristics.

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} The exposed surface of insulating materials fitted on
  the internal boundaries of machinery spaces of category A must be
  impervious to oil and oil vapours.

**[[216]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-216 .sectionLabel}** If organic foam is used
in a cargo space or a refrigerated compartment of a service space,

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} the exposed surfaces of the foam must be

  - [(i)]{.lawlabel} sealed with an intumescent coating that meets the
    flame spread, smoke and toxicity requirements set out in Annex 1 to
    the FTP Code, and

  - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} covered with a steel-sheet protective facing;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} in the case of organic foam used in a cargo space or
  a refrigerated compartment of a service space on a vessel of steel
  construction, the boundaries to the space must be of steel
  construction; and

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} in the case of organic foam used in a refrigerated
  compartment of a service space on a vessel of aluminum construction,
  the boundaries to the space must be of steel or aluminum construction.

#### [Ventilation Systems]{.HTitleText3} {#h-840211 .Subheading}

##### [Stopping and Closing]{.HTitleText4} {#h-840212 .Subheading}

- **[[217]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-217
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Means must be provided for closing
  the main inlets and outlets of every ventilation system from a
  position outside the space served by the system. The means of closing
  must

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} be readily accessible;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} be prominently and permanently marked as being the
    main inlet or outlet to the space; and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} indicate whether the inlets and outlets are open or
    closed.

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} Power ventilation of accommodation spaces, service
  spaces, cargo spaces, control stations and machinery spaces must be
  capable of being stopped from a readily accessible position outside
  the space or station served. Access to this position must not be
  easily cut off in the event of a fire in the space served.

- [(3)]{.lawlabel} Means of control must be provided for stopping
  ventilation fans. The means must

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} if they are provided for stopping the ventilation
    serving machinery spaces, be grouped so as to be operable from two
    positions, one of which is outside those spaces; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} if they are provided for stopping the ventilation
    of the machinery spaces, be entirely separate from the means
    provided for stopping the ventilation of other spaces.

- [(4)]{.lawlabel} The means of control that are required by
  subsection (3) to be located outside the machinery spaces must be
  arranged so that a source of power to stop the fans and close the main
  openings is provided

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} from each individual motor control circuit; or

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} from a dedicated source of power feeding an
    individual motor, or a group of motors connected to a motor control
    centre for a particular space, without any shunt-trip arrangements.

- [(5)]{.lawlabel} A stop circuit of the means of control referred to in
  subsection (4) that is activated manually must be manually reset.

- [(6)]{.lawlabel} Means must be provided for closing, from a safe
  position, the annular spaces around funnels.

##### [Ventilation Openings]{.HTitleText4} {#h-840227 .Subheading}

- **[[218]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-218 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A
  ventilation opening must not be installed in or under a stairway
  enclosure door.

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} Any ventilation opening in a door in a corridor
  bulkhead must be in the lower half of the door and must be fitted with
  a grille made of non-combustible material.

- [(3)]{.lawlabel} The total net area of a ventilation opening in or
  under a door in a corridor bulkhead must not be more than 0.05 m^2^.

##### [Ventilation Ducts]{.HTitleText4} {#h-840232 .Subheading}

- **[[219]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-219 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A
  ventilation duct that serves a machinery space of category A or a
  galley must not pass through an accommodation space, service space or
  control station unless

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the ventilation duct is made of steel that is
    supported, stiffened and of one of the following thicknesses:

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} 3 mm, if the width or diameter of the duct is not
      more than 300 mm,

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} 5 mm, if the width or diameter of the duct is
      760 mm or more, or

    - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} the thickness obtained by linear interpolation
      between the thicknesses set out in subparagraphs (i) and (ii), if
      the width or diameter of the duct is more than 300 mm but less
      than 760 mm; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the ventilation duct

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} is insulated to an A-60 class fire rating
      throughout the accommodation space, service space or control
      station, or

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} meets the following requirements:

      - [(A)]{.lawlabel} it is fitted, at each of the space's or
        station's divisions that is penetrated by the duct, with a
        fail-safe automatic-closing fire damper that can be operated
        from both sides of the division, and

      - [(B)]{.lawlabel} it is insulated to an A-60 class fire rating
        from the machinery space or galley to a point at least 5 m
        beyond each fire damper.

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} A ventilation duct that serves an accommodation
  space, service space or control station must not pass through a
  machinery space of category A or a galley unless

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the ventilation duct meets the requirements of
    paragraph (1)(a); and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the ventilation duct

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} is insulated to an A-60 class fire rating
      throughout the machinery space or galley, or

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} meets the following requirements:

      - [(A)]{.lawlabel} it is fitted, at each of the space's or
        station's divisions that is penetrated by the duct, with a
        fail-safe automatic-closing fire damper that can be operated
        from both sides of the division, and

      - [(B)]{.lawlabel} it is insulated to an A-60 class fire rating
        from the accommodation space, service space or control station
        to a point at least 5 m beyond each fire damper.

##### [Storerooms that Contain Highly Flammable Materials]{.HTitleText4} {#h-840251 .Subheading}

- **[[220]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-220 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A
  storeroom that contains highly flammable materials must be provided
  with a ventilation system that is separate from the ventilation
  systems serving other spaces.

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} The ventilation system must ventilate high and low
  levels of the storeroom. Every inlet or outlet of the system must be
  positioned in an area free from flammable gases.

- [(3)]{.lawlabel} A wire mesh guard that is corrosion-resistant and
  arrests sparks must be fitted over the opening of the inlet or outlet.

##### [Independent Ventilation Systems]{.HTitleText4} {#h-840256 .Subheading}

**[[221]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-221 .sectionLabel}** A ventilation system
that serves a machinery space, galley, stairway enclosure, cargo space
or special category space must not serve another space and must be
independent of every other ventilation system.

##### [Non-combustible Materials --- Trunks and Ducts]{.HTitleText4} {#h-840258 .Subheading}

- **[[222]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-222 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  trunks and ducts of ventilation systems must be constructed of
  non-combustible materials.

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} If a trunk or duct serves spaces on both sides of an
  "A" class division, fire dampers must be fitted so as to prevent the
  spread of fire and smoke between compartments. Manual fire dampers
  must be operable from both sides of the division.

- [(3)]{.lawlabel} If a trunk or duct has a free cross-sectional area of
  more than 0.02 m^2^ and passes through an "A" class division, the
  trunk or duct must be fitted with an automatic-closing fire damper.

- [(4)]{.lawlabel} If a trunk or duct serves compartments situated on
  only one side of an "A" class division, the opening in the division
  must be lined with a steel sheet sleeve unless the trunk or duct
  passing through the division is of steel in the vicinity of the
  passage and unless the portion of the trunk or duct in that vicinity

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} has sleeves that are at least 3 mm thick and 900 mm
    long and is insulated with fire insulation that has at least the
    same fire integrity as the division, if the trunk or duct has a free
    cross-sectional area of more than 0.02 m^2^; or

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} is fitted with automatic fire dampers that can be
    operated manually, if the trunk or duct has a free cross-sectional
    area of more than 0.085 m^2^.

- [(5)]{.lawlabel} A sleeve referred to in paragraph (4)(a) that passes
  through a bulkhead must be of the same length on either side of the
  bulkhead.

- [(6)]{.lawlabel} Paragraph (4)(b) does not apply if the trunk or duct
  passes through a space surrounded by "A" class divisions without
  serving that space and the trunk or duct has the same fire integrity
  as the divisions through which it passes.

#### [Electric Radiators]{.HTitleText3} {#h-840268 .Subheading}

- **[[223]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-223
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Every electric radiator must be
  fixed in position.

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} An electric radiator must not be fitted with a
  heating element exposed to such an extent that clothing, curtains or
  other similar materials could be scorched or set on fire by heat from
  the element.

#### [Exposed Surfaces]{.HTitleText3} {#h-840272 .Subheading}

- **[[224]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-224 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  exposed surfaces within accommodation spaces, service spaces, control

  surfaces behind bulkheads, ceilings, panelling or linings in
  accommodation spaces, service spaces or control stations, must meet

  to the FTP Code.

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} The exposed surfaces of glass-reinforced plastic
  within accommodation spaces, service spaces, control stations,
  machinery spaces of category A or other machinery spaces of similar
  fire risk to machinery spaces of category A must

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} be coated with a final layer of fire retardant

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} be coated with a fire retardant coating; or

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} be protected by non-combustible materials that meet
    the requirements for non-combustible materials set out in Annex 1 to
    the FTP Code.

- [(3)]{.lawlabel} The paints, varnishes and other finishes used on any
  exposed interior surface must be a fire retardant coating.

#### [Primary Deck Coverings]{.HTitleText3} {#h-840280 .Subheading}

**[[225]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-225 .sectionLabel}** The primary deck
coverings within accommodation spaces, service spaces or control

#### [Plastic Piping]{.HTitleText3} {#h-840282 .Subheading}

**[[226]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-226 .sectionLabel}** Plastic piping fitted

#### [Piping Penetrating Decks and Bulkheads]{.HTitleText3} {#h-840284 .Subheading}

**[[227]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-227 .sectionLabel}** The piping penetrating
an "A" class division or "B" class division in an accommodation space,

temperature that the division is able to withstand.

#### [Materials for Overboard Scuppers, Sanitary Discharges or Other Outlets]{.HTitleText3} {#h-840286 .Subheading}

- **[[228]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-228 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} On a
  vessel whose hull is constructed of combustible materials, materials
  that are readily rendered ineffective by heat must not be used for
  overboard scuppers, sanitary discharges or other outlets that are
  close to the waterline if failure of the materials in the event of
  fire would give rise to danger of flooding.

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} On a vessel whose hull is constructed of
  non-combustible materials, materials that are used for overboard
  scuppers, sanitary discharges or other outlets that are close to the

#### [Remote Means To Stop Pumps]{.HTitleText3} {#h-840290 .Subheading}

- **[[229]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-229
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Remote means must be provided to

  remote means to stop the pumps and with a visual indicator of whether
  the pumps are running.

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} The remote means must be arranged so that a source of

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} from each individual motor control circuit; or

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} from a dedicated source of power feeding an

    centre for a particular space, without any shunt-trip arrangements.

- [(3)]{.lawlabel} A stop circuit of the remote means that is activated

#### [Highly Flammable Liquids or Liquefied Gases]{.HTitleText3} {#h-840297 .Subheading}

- **[[230]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-230 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} If
  highly flammable liquids or liquefied gases are in a space, the space

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} must be directly accessible from an open deck and
    must not be otherwise accessible; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} must be provided with

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} if the deck area of the space is less than

      - [(B)]{.lawlabel} a fire extinguisher that has a

        extinguisher referred to in clause (A).

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} The pressure-adjusting devices and relief valves on
  cylinders that contain highly flammable liquids or liquefied gases
  must exhaust within the space where the cylinders are located. The
  space must be gastight if it adjoins another enclosed space.

- [(8)]{.lawlabel} The width, number and continuity of the means of

### [Application]{.HTitleText2} {#h-840362 .Subheading}

- **[[301]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-301 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} This
  Part applies in respect of Canadian vessels everywhere that are not
  Safety Convention vessels and that are

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} vessels of more than 15 gross tonnage but of less
    than 24 m in length that are not passenger-carrying vessels;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} passenger-carrying vessels of more than 15 gross
    tonnage but of less than 24 m in length that, if applicable, are
    carrying not more than 36 berthed passengers; or

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} passenger-carrying vessels of not more than 15
    gross tonnage that are carrying more than 12 passengers.

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} This Part does not apply in respect of

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} vessels that carry dangerous goods, other than in
    limited quantities, and that are referred to in regulation 19.2.2 of
    Chapter II-2 of SOLAS or have cargo spaces referred to in that
    regulation;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} pleasure craft;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} fishing vessels;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} high-speed craft;

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} vessels that do not have mechanical means of
    propulsion;

  - [(f)]{.lawlabel} wooden vessels of primitive build;

  - [(g)]{.lawlabel} vessels that are capable of engaging in the
    drilling for, or the production, conservation or processing of, oil
    or gas;

  - [(h)]{.lawlabel} nuclear vessels; or

  - [(i)]{.lawlabel} vessels to which the Special-purpose Vessels
    Regulations apply.

  section 318 or 319 of the [Canada Shipping Act](/eng/acts/S-9),
  R.S.C., 1985, c. S-9, includes any predecessor enactment relating to
  the same subject matter.

### [Limitations on Grandfathering]{.HTitleText2} {#h-840384 .Subheading}

- **[[304]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-304
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Section 303 does not apply in
  respect of

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} parts of a vessel that, beginning on the day on
    which this section comes into force, undergo repairs, alterations or
    modifications that

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} substantially alter the vessel's dimensions or

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} substantially increase the vessel's service life
      or the life of the vessel's outfitting;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} systems and equipment that are related to the parts
    of a vessel described in paragraph (a);

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} parts of a vessel that, beginning on the day on
    which this section comes into force, are replaced; or

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} systems and equipment that, beginning on the day on
    which this section comes into force, are replaced.

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} Section 303 does not apply if the vessel's intended
  service changes in such a manner that any of the requirements that
  were required to be met for the issuance of the certificate are no
  longer met.

passenger-carrying vessel must

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} carry not more than 100 passengers or not more than
  12 berthed passengers; and

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} engage only on sheltered waters voyages and near
  coastal voyages, Class 2, limited.

### [Amount and Storage of Certain Flammable Liquids]{.HTitleText2} {#h-840398 .Subheading}

carried as cargo or being used as fuel or lubricant in a vessel's system
--- must

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} be kept to a minimum and in no case exceed 30 kg; and

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} be stored in a locker that meets the requirements of
  subsection 319(3).

#### [Additional Requirements for Portable Fire Extinguishers]{.HTitleText3} {#h-840452 .Subheading}

- **[[311]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-311
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Every portable fire extinguisher
  must be kept fully charged, and must be replaced according to its
  manufacturer's specifications, if applicable.

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} A portable fire extinguisher that is required by this
  Part to be carried on a vessel must be mounted with a clamp bracket
  that holds the fire extinguisher securely in place but provides quick
  and positive release of the fire extinguisher for immediate use.

- [(3)]{.lawlabel} If a portable fire extinguisher is intended to be
  carried and operated by hand, it must not weigh more than 23 kg.

- [(4)]{.lawlabel} Every portable fire extinguisher must be stored where
  its operation will not be affected by icing or cold temperature.

- [(5)]{.lawlabel} Every portable fire extinguisher must be marked with
  a number at least 13 mm high, and its storage location must be marked
  with a corresponding number at least 13 mm high. However, if only one
  type and size of portable fire extinguisher is carried, the numbering
  may be omitted.

#### [Fire Buckets]{.HTitleText3} {#h-840459 .Subheading}

**[[312]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-312 .sectionLabel}** Every fire bucket must
have a capacity of 10 L or more, be made of metal with a round bottom
and a hole in the centre, be painted red and be fitted with a line of
sufficient length to enable the bucket to be filled from the surrounding
body of water from any deck.

### [Means of Escape]{.HTitleText2} {#h-840461 .Subheading}

#### [Exits]{.HTitleText3} {#h-840462 .Subheading}

- **[[313]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-313
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Every accommodation space,
  wheelhouse, machinery space, or other space accessible to passengers
  or where the crew is normally employed, must have a primary exit and
  an emergency exit. However, an emergency exit is not required if the
  space is too small to have both a primary exit and an emergency exit.

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} The primary exit and the emergency exit must

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} be unobstructed, be readily accessible and lead as
    directly as possible to an open deck;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} subject to paragraph 315(1)(e), have a clear
    opening that has a width and length of at least 560 mm or that
    provides a means of exit that is equivalent;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} be operable from both sides;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} be capable of being retained in an open position
    while being used as part of an escape route; and

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} be so located that a single incident occurring
    inside or outside the space would be unlikely to prevent the use of
    the primary exit and the emergency exit at the same time.

- [(3)]{.lawlabel} Despite paragraph (2)(e), the primary exit and the
  emergency exit from the wheelhouse must, if feasible, be located on
  opposite sides of the vessel.

- [(4)]{.lawlabel} In each public space on a passenger-carrying vessel,
  the primary exit and the emergency exit must be identified by a
  photoluminescent or illuminated sign that contains a green pictogram
  and a white or lightly tinted graphical symbol and that has been
  certified by a product certification body as meeting the applicable
  specifications set out in standard ISO 3864-1, entitled Graphical
  symbols --- Safety colours and safety signs --- Part 1: Design
  principles for safety signs and safety markings, and standard ISO
  7010, entitled Graphical symbols --- Safety colours and safety signs
  --- Registered safety signs, published by the International
  Organization for Standardization.

- [(5)]{.lawlabel} If a sign required by subsection (4) is not visible
  from an area in the public space, a white, or lightly tinted, and
  green photoluminescent or illuminated sign with an arrow pointing the
  way to the exit to which the sign relates must be in a readily visible
  location in the space and must be certified by a product certification
  body as meeting the applicable specifications set out in the standards
  referred to in subsection (4).

#### [Escape Routes]{.HTitleText3} {#h-840474 .Subheading}

- **[[314]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-314 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} This
  section applies to the escape routes from an accommodation space,
  service space, wheelhouse or machinery space, or from any other space
  accessible to passengers or where the crew is normally employed.

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} Every stairway, corridor, door and ladder must be
  arranged to provide a ready means of escape to the muster and
  embarkation stations. Every door must be constructed to open outwards
  and be hinged on the forward side.

- [(3)]{.lawlabel} If the location of an exit in a space is such that
  egress from the space would be difficult without an aid such as a
  handhold or ladder, an appropriate aid must be permanently fitted.

- [(4)]{.lawlabel} Every handhold or ladder that permits access to or
  from a deck must, if feasible, extend sufficiently above the level of
  the deck to allow safe and rapid access.

- [(5)]{.lawlabel} Every ladder or stairway from a space other than a
  machinery space must be constructed of non-combustible material or be
  coated with a fire retardant coating, and must be equipped with
  anti-skid rungs or stairs.

- [(6)]{.lawlabel} Every ladder or stairway from a machinery space must
  be constructed of non-combustible material and be equipped with
  anti-skid rungs or stairs.

- [(7)]{.lawlabel} An intumescent coating must not be used on a stairway
  or ladder.

- [(8)]{.lawlabel} Every stairway of more than 1 m in height must be
  equipped with handrails or handholds on both sides and must maintain a
  clear width of 760 mm.

- [(9)]{.lawlabel} On a vessel that is not a passenger-carrying vessel,
  every portable ladder must be

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} stowed in a readily accessible and clearly
    identified location that is as close as feasible to where it is
    meant to be used; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} designed so that it can be securely fixed in place
    without the use of tools.

- [(10)]{.lawlabel} On a passenger-carrying vessel,

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} vertical ladders and deck scuttles must not be
    provided except

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} in areas occupied only by crew members, and

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} in areas where it is not feasible to install a
      stairway; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} portable ladders must not be provided except in
    areas occupied only by crew members.

#### [Arrangement of Stairways and Inclined Ladders on Passenger-carrying Vessels]{.HTitleText3} {#h-840492 .Subheading}

- **[[315]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-315 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} On a
  passenger-carrying vessel, the stairways and inclined ladders must be
  arranged so that

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the sum of the riser height and tread depth on each
    stair is not less than 430 mm and not more than 460 mm;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} except in the case of exterior stairways and
    inclined ladders without risers, a stair that has a tread depth of
    less than 260 mm must have a nosing of not less than 25 mm or other
    means of increasing the depth of the tread;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} handrails are fitted on both sides of every flight
    of stairs, and have a height measured vertically above the tread of
    not less than 840 mm and not more than 910 mm;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} at the top and bottom of every flight of stairs
    there is a clear landing with a surface area that is at least equal
    to the square of the actual tread width of the adjacent stair or, if
    that is not feasible, the largest surface area that is feasible;

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} the clear width of every door opening to a stairway
    or inclined ladder is at least equal to the width of the stair
    closest to the opening;

  - [(f)]{.lawlabel} every stairway or inclined ladder has a minimum
    width of 760 mm or, if the stairway or inclined ladder is intended
    for use by more than 60 persons, of 760 mm plus 10 mm for each
    person in addition to the first 60; and

  - [(g)]{.lawlabel} the maximum angle of inclination from the
    horizontal of every stairway or inclined ladder is

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} 45° or, in the case of a stairway or inclined
      ladder intended only for the use of the crew, 55°, or

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} the minimum angle that is feasible, if the
      maximum angle required by subparagraph (i) is not feasible.

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} For the purposes of paragraphs (1)(e) and (f), if the
  distance between handrails is less than the width of the treads, the
  width of the stair and the width of the stairway or inclined ladder
  must be measured between the handrails.

### [Structural Fire Protection]{.HTitleText2} {#h-840505 .Subheading}

#### [Insulation, Fire Retardant Coatings and Fire Retardant Resins]{.HTitleText3} {#h-840506 .Subheading}

- **[[316]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-316 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  exposed surface of insulating materials fitted on the internal
  boundaries of machinery spaces must be impervious to oil and oil
  vapours.

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} All thermal insulation and acoustic insulation,
  including pipe and ventilation lagging, must be non-combustible.

- [(3)]{.lawlabel} Polyurethane foam or other organic foam insulation
  must not be used unless it is

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} used to insulate a cargo space or a cold room or
    similar galley service space, sealed with a fire retardant coating
    of the intumescent type and covered with a steel-sheet protective
    facing; or

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} used as a flotation material that is installed in a
    dedicated and enclosed space and protected from fire risks.

<!-- -->

- **[[317]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-317 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} This
  section applies in respect of the insulation, fire retardant coating
  and fire retardant resin required by this Part.

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} The insulation, fire retardant coating or fire
  retardant resin used on a deck or bulkhead must

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} cover the entire surface of the deck or bulkhead;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} be carried past the penetration, intersection or
    terminal point of the deck or bulkhead for a distance of at least
    380 mm;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} be installed on the side on which the fire risk is
    higher, unless it is not possible to do so; and

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} if the insulation, coating or resin is vulnerable
    to damage, be protected to prevent any loss of its fire-resistant
    properties.

- [(3)]{.lawlabel} The insulation installed on a deck or bulkhead must
  be held in place by closely spaced studs and clips or by another
  method that will

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} hold the insulation in place for at least 60
    minutes when it is exposed to fire; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} hold the insulation in place despite the vibration
    and motion of the vessel and the normal wear.

- [(4)]{.lawlabel} If insulation is installed on the stiffener side of a
  deck or bulkhead, at least 50% of the thickness of the insulation must
  be installed on top of the stiffener.

- [(5)]{.lawlabel} Fire retardant coating and fire retardant resin must
  be applied, and the surfaces to which they are to be applied must be
  prepared, in accordance with the specifications of its manufacturer.

#### [Cooking and Heating Appliances]{.HTitleText3} {#h-840525 .Subheading}

- **[[318]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-318 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} On a
  passenger-carrying vessel, the boundaries of a galley that contains
  cooking appliances must consist of "F" class divisions or "B-15" class
  divisions, or be insulated with 30-minute fire rated insulation, if

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the vessel has sleeping accommodations; or

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} meals are cooked in the galley with an appliance,
    other than a microwave, that has a heating surface that can exceed
    120°C.

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} Every cooking or heating appliance must be installed
  in accordance with the appliance manufacturer's recommendations, if
  any, with regard to clearances and with regard to materials, including
  insulation, installed in way of the appliance. If the space does not
  allow the minimum clearance, if any, recommended by the manufacturer,

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} 30-minute fire rated insulation must be installed
    to the surfaces in way of the appliance;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} all surfaces around the appliance must be
    non-combustible; and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} the appliance must be installed so that the
    temperature of every surface adjacent to the appliance will not,
    when the appliance is operated, exceed 90°C or, if the surface is
    readily accessible, 60°C.

- [(3)]{.lawlabel} If life saving equipment, a muster station or an
  embarkation station is located on the deck above a space that contains
  a cooking or heating appliance, or on the other side of a bulkhead
  delimiting that space,

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} in the case of a steel or aluminum vessel, the deck

### [Fire Detection, Alarms and Communications]{.HTitleText2} {#h-840631 .Subheading}

#### [Automatic Fire Detection and Alarm Systems]{.HTitleText3} {#h-840632 .Subheading}

- **[[331]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-331 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} An
  automatic fire detection and alarm system must be installed in order
  to detect the presence and location of a fire in an accommodation
  space, machinery space and service space.

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} The system must indicate the presence of the fire by
  an audio signal given at one or more points on the vessel so as to
  come rapidly to the notice of the vessel's master and crew. In an
  occupied machinery space with high ambient noise level, the system
  must also indicate the presence of the fire by flashing red lights or
  beacons of sufficient intensity and number to alert the occupants of
  the space.

- [(3)]{.lawlabel} The system must be designed so that

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} power supplies and electric circuits necessary for
    the operation of the system are monitored for losses of power and
    fault conditions;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} a loss of power or a fault condition initiates a
    visual and audible fault signal at the control panel that is
    distinct from a fire signal;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} there are at least two sources of power supply for
    the system, one of which is an emergency source;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} visual and audible alarm signals at the control
    panel indicate when the normal supply has failed and the system is
    operating on the emergency source of power;

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} the power supply for the system is provided by
    separate feeders reserved solely for that purpose;

  - [(f)]{.lawlabel} the smoke and heat detectors for the system are
    grouped into sections and the activation of any detector initiates a
    visual and audible fire signal at the control panel;

  - [(g)]{.lawlabel} if the vessel has a public address system, the
    audio signal to indicate the presence of a fire is automatically
    interrupted during communication from the system; and

  - [(h)]{.lawlabel} the control panel is located at the main control
    position.

- [(4)]{.lawlabel} The system must be installed so that

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} there is at least one smoke detector in each cabin,
    corridor, escape route or stairway, and in each service space not
    containing cooking appliances;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} there is at least one heat detector in each public
    room or machinery space, and in each service space containing
    cooking appliances;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} the smoke detectors and heat detectors are located
    for optimum performance, and the surface coverage of each detector
    does not exceed its manufacturer's specifications; and

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} the wiring that forms part of the system does not
    enter any galley or machinery space, or any other enclosed space of
    high fire risk, except to the extent that the wiring is necessary to
    provide for fire detection or alarm in the space or to connect to
    the appropriate power supply in the space.

- [(5)]{.lawlabel} The smoke and heat detectors must be certified by a
  product certification body or be of a type approved by a recognized
  organization.

- [(6)]{.lawlabel} The heat detectors must be

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} dual-action rate-of-rise and fixed-temperature; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} set at a temperature appropriate for the protected
    space but in no case more than 78°C.

  the minimum diameter required by subsection 334(1) for fire mains on
  the vessel.

- [(4)]{.lawlabel} On a vessel that carries deck cargo, the fire piping
  and fire hydrants must be arranged to avoid risk of damage by deck
  cargo.

- [(5)]{.lawlabel} The maximum pressure at a fire hydrant must not
  exceed the pressure at which a fire hose can be effectively controlled
  by one crew member.

- [(6)]{.lawlabel} Every fire hydrant must be installed so that

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} a fire hose can be easily connected to it;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} it is limited to a position from the horizontal to
    the vertical pointing downward, so that the fire hose will lead
    horizontally or downward in order to minimize the possibility of
    kinking; and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} there is sufficient clearance below it to
    accommodate the radius of bend of the fire hose under pressure and
    to allow deployment of the hose in every direction.

- [(7)]{.lawlabel} The fire piping must be installed with a gradient
  that allows drainage under all normal operating conditions. Drain
  valves must be provided where necessary for effective drainage.

- [(8)]{.lawlabel} The fire piping and fire hydrants must be installed
  so as to avoid the possibility of freezing.

<!-- -->

- **[[336]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-336 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  fire piping and the valves and fittings of the water firefighting
  system must be made of galvanized steel or another material of
  equivalent mechanical strength and equivalent corrosion and
  fire-resistance.

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} The joints in the fire piping must be connected in a
  manner that prevents leakage and must meet the pressure requirements
  of the fire piping system. Flanged or screwed connections, or other
  connections that are at least as reliable as flanged or screwed
  connections, must be used.

<!-- -->

- **[[337]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-337
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Every fire hydrant must be fitted
  with a valve so that any fire hose attached to the hydrant can be
  detached while the fire pumps are in operation.

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} Every valve fitted to fire piping must be designed to
  open with a counter-clockwise rotation of the valve's handle.

- [(3)]{.lawlabel} The tools and accessories that are necessary to use a
  fire hydrant or fire hose must be located in close proximity to the
  hydrant or hose.

<!-- -->

- **[[338]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-338 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A
  flexible hose must not be used as part of the fire piping unless the
  hose

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} is necessary to reduce the effect of vibration;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} has a length that is not more than six times the
    diameter of the rigid pipe to which it is attached;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} is clearly visible at all times;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} is oil-resistant;

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} is certified by a product certification body or a
    testing laboratory as meeting

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} the requirements of standard SAE J1942, entitled
      Hose and Hose Assemblies for Marine Applications, for type VW, or

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} the requirements of another standard that are
      equivalent to the requirements referred to in subparagraph (i);

  - [(f)]{.lawlabel} can withstand collapsing due to suction; and

  - [(g)]{.lawlabel} is secured at each end with a corrosion-resistant
    and galvanically compatible fitting that consists of

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} a swaged sleeve,

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} a sleeve and threaded insert, or

    - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} two metallic hose clamps of a type that is not
      dependent on spring tension for compressive force and that has a
      nominal bandwidth of at least 12 mm.

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} A metallic hose clamp may be used only with a
  flexible hose that is designed for clamps. The hose clamp must be

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} fastened over the flexible hose and over the hose
    fitting, the spud or the rigid pipe; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} installed at least 12 mm from the end of the
    flexible hose.

### [Fixed Gas Fire-extinguishing Systems]{.HTitleText2} {#h-840727 .Subheading}

#### [General]{.HTitleText3} {#h-840728 .Subheading}

- **[[339]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-339
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Subject to subsection (6), every
  machinery space must be served by

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} a fixed gas fire-extinguishing system; or

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} a fixed aerosol fire-extinguishing system.

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} The fixed fire-extinguishing system must

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} be an engineered system certified for marine use by
    a product certification body or be of a type approved by a
    recognized organization, and be installed and maintained by the
    manufacturer, or a person authorized by the manufacturer, in
    accordance with the certification or type approval; or

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} be a pre-engineered system certified for marine use
    by a product certification body or be of a type approved by a
    recognized organization, and be installed and maintained in
    accordance with its design limitations and the manufacturer's
    instructions.

- [(3)]{.lawlabel} Subsection (2) does not apply in respect of the
  design of pressure containers for a fixed fire-extinguishing system.
  Every pressure container for a fixed fire-extinguishing system must
  bear a mark indicating that it

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} meets the applicable requirements for transport by
    road or ship that are set out in section 5.10 of the [Transportation
    of Dangerous Goods Regulations](/eng/regulations/SOR-2001-286);

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} meets the applicable requirements for transport by
    road or ship that are set out in Title 49, subpart C of part 178 of
    the Code of Federal Regulations of the United States; or

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} meets the applicable requirements for transportable
    pressure equipment that are set out in Directive 2010/35/EU of the
    European Parliament and of the Council and has undergone a
    conformity assessment procedure in accordance with that Directive by
    a notified body designated by a member state of the European Union.

- [(4)]{.lawlabel} When a fixed fire-extinguishing system is activated,
  a complete charge must be released simultaneously.

- [(5)]{.lawlabel} On a wooden or composite vessel,

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} a fixed gas or aerosol fire-extinguishing system
    must have two independent complete charges of gas or aerosol. The
    quantity of gas or aerosol for each charge must meet the
    requirements of subsection 345(2), 346(2) or 347(2), as the case may
    be, respecting the quantity of gas or aerosol for the system; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} a fixed aerosol fire-extinguishing system must be
    certified by a product certification body or be of a type approved
    by a recognized organization for deep-seated fires in Class A
    materials.

- [(6)]{.lawlabel} A machinery space is not required to be served by a
  fixed fire-extinguishing system if

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} in addition to the portable fire extinguishers
    required by subsection 309(1), the space is provided with a portable
    gas fire extinguisher that

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} does not weigh more than 23 kg, and

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} meets the requirements of subsections 345(2)
      and (3) or subsections 346(2) and (5), as the case may be;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the space is provided with a readily accessible
    port that permits the additional portable gas fire extinguisher to
    be discharged directly into the space without the need to open the
    primary access to the space, and that is

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} labelled to clearly indicate its firefighting
      purpose and how to use it,

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} capable of accommodating the discharge nozzle of
      the extinguisher, and

    - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} arranged so that the extinguisher may be
      discharged in accordance with the manufacturer's instructions; and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} the additional portable gas fire extinguisher is
    located outside the space and close to the port.

- [(7)]{.lawlabel} The following definitions apply in this section.

  [[engineered system]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[engineered system]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means, in respect of a
      fixed fire-extinguishing system, a system that requires
      calculations and a design that are specific to the vessel in which
      it is fitted and whose purpose is to determine the flow rates,
      nozzle pressures, pipe size, area or volume protected by each
      nozzle, the quantity of extinguishing agent and the number and
      types of nozzles and their placement. ([système sur
      mesure]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

  [[pre-engineered system]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[pre-engineered system]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means, in respect of
      a fixed fire-extinguishing system, a system that

      - [(a)]{.lawlabel} does not require calculations, or a design,
        that are specific to the vessel in which it is fitted; and

      - [(b)]{.lawlabel} is specifically limited as to the type of space
        it can protect and the size of that space. ([système
        précalculé]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

#### [Components]{.HTitleText3} {#h-840758 .Subheading}

- **[[340]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-340 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  piping, valves and fittings of a fixed fire-extinguishing system must
  be made of galvanized steel or another material of equivalent
  corrosion and fire-resistance, and must be securely connected to one
  another and securely supported.

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} The components of the fixed fire-extinguishing system
  must be resistant to or protected from mechanical, chemical or other
  damage that could render them inoperative.

- [(3)]{.lawlabel} The relief valves of the fixed fire-extinguishing
  system must be safely vented.

#### [Controls and Alarms]{.HTitleText3} {#h-840763 .Subheading}

- **[[341]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-341
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Every fixed gas fire-extinguishing
  system that serves a machinery space must have a means of control that

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} can be operated manually from a position that is
    outside the space and that is not likely to be cut off by a fire in
    the space;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} requires at least two steps to activate the system;
    and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} is readily accessible and simple to operate.

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} Means must be provided in the wheelhouse, or in a
  location readily accessible from the position where the means of
  control is located, to

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} stop the ventilation fans that serve the machinery
    space; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} shut off the source of power or fuel for any
    machinery or equipment in the machinery space that could contribute
    to sustaining a fire or create any other unsafe condition in the
    case of fire.

- [(3)]{.lawlabel} If the machinery space has a gross volume of more
  than 57 m^3^ or is normally occupied, the fixed fire-extinguishing
  system must not have an automatic means to release the extinguishing
  agent.

- [(4)]{.lawlabel} Unless the machinery space is too small for a crew
  member to enter it, the fixed fire-extinguishing system must have an
  alarm to warn of any impending release of the extinguishing agent. The
  alarm must

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} be separate from any other alarm;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} have a sound that is distinct from the sound of any
    other signal or alarm in the space;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} have a sound level of more than 85 dB;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} if the space has an ambient noise level that
    exceeds the sound level of the alarm, have flashing red lights or
    beacons of sufficient intensity and number to alert the occupants of
    the space; and

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} be set off automatically when the system is
    activated, and stay on for at least 20 seconds, or any longer period
    that is necessary to allow the occupants of the space to escape,
    before the agent is released.

- [(5)]{.lawlabel} If the fixed fire-extinguishing system has an
  automatic means to release the extinguishing agent,

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} means must be provided in the wheelhouse to warn
    the occupants of the impending release of the extinguishing agent;
    and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the means required by subsection (2) must be
    automatic.

#### [Escape of Extinguishing Agent]{.HTitleText3} {#h-840782 .Subheading}

- **[[342]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-342
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Every machinery space must be
  capable of keeping the quantity of the extinguishing agent required by
  subsection 345(2), 346(2) or 347(2), as the case may be, within the
  space for at least 15 minutes.

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} The openings that can admit air to, or allow the
  extinguishing agent to escape from, a machinery space must be capable
  of being closed from outside the space. The means of closing must meet
  the requirements of subsection 329(4).

- [(3)]{.lawlabel} The openings that are used to vent a machinery space
  must be vented to outside the vessel and not to a location in the
  vicinity of a muster station.

- [(4)]{.lawlabel} If the release of the extinguishing agent into a
  machinery space could cause over- or under-pressurization that would
  affect the integrity of the space, measures must be in place to
  protect the integrity of the space.

- [(4)]{.lawlabel} Clear instructions for the safe operation of a fixed
  fire-extinguishing system must be kept near the means of control for
  the system.

- [(5)]{.lawlabel} The procedure to follow in case of a fire in a
  machinery space must be posted at each fire control station and must
  include instructions for

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} stopping the ventilation fans that serve the space;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} shutting off the source of power or fuel for any
    machinery or equipment in the space that could contribute to
    sustaining a fire or create any other unsafe condition in the case
    of fire;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} closing the openings to the space;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} ensuring that nobody is in the space; and

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} venting the space after the release of the
    extinguishing agent into the space, using an opening vented to
    outside the vessel and not to a location in the vicinity of a muster
    station or to any location where passengers or crew are present.

#### [Containers]{.HTitleText3} {#h-840812 .Subheading}

- **[[344]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-344 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A
  container that is used in a fixed fire-extinguishing system must be
  kept in a location where it is not exposed to severe weather
  conditions and where it is protected from mechanical, chemical or
  other damage.

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} Means must be provided to indicate whether the
  container has been discharged.

- [(3)]{.lawlabel} Means must be provided for the crew to safely check
  the quantity of extinguishing agent and the pressure in the container.

- [(4)]{.lawlabel} If the fixed fire-extinguishing system serves a
  machinery space, the container must be kept in a location that is

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} readily accessible from outside the machinery space
    and, if feasible, from an open deck;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} safely vented to outside the vessel; and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} outside the accommodation spaces and the machinery
    space.

- [(5)]{.lawlabel} Despite paragraph (4)(c), unless the container
  contains carbon dioxide, the container may be kept in the machinery
  space if the space has a gross volume of not more than 57 m^3^ and is
  not normally occupied.

- [(6)]{.lawlabel} If the container is connected to a common manifold, a
  non-return valve must be installed to allow the container to be
  disconnected

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} without affecting the use of the other containers
    connected to the common manifold; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} in a manner that prevents any discharge at the
    point of disconnection when the fixed fire-extinguishing system is
    activated.

#### [Fixed Carbon Dioxide Fire-extinguishing Systems]{.HTitleText3} {#h-840825 .Subheading}

- **[[345]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-345 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} For
  the purposes of subsection 339(2), in the case of a fixed carbon
  dioxide fire-extinguishing system, "certified for marine use by a
  product certification body" is to be read as "certified for marine use
  by a product certification body in accordance with the standard NFPA
  12, entitled Standard on Carbon Dioxide Extinguishing Systems,".

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} The quantity of carbon dioxide in a fixed carbon
  dioxide fire-extinguishing system that serves a machinery space must
  be sufficient to achieve, at a specific volume of 0.56 m^3^ per
  kilogram, a volume of free gas equal to

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} 60% of the gross volume of the space, if that gross
    volume is not more than 14 m^3^;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} 40% of the gross volume of the space, if that gross
    volume is more than 136 m^3^; and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} the percentage obtained by linear interpolation
    between the percentages set out in paragraphs (a) and (b), if the
    gross volume of the space is more than 14 m^3^ but not more than
    136 m^3^.

- [(3)]{.lawlabel} The fixed fire-extinguishing system must release a
  sufficient quantity of carbon dioxide to allow 85% of the quantity
  required by subsection (2) to be reached in 120 seconds or less in the
  machinery space.

#### [Other Fixed Gas Fire-extinguishing Systems]{.HTitleText3} {#h-840833 .Subheading}

- **[[346]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-346 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} For
  the purposes of subsection 339(2), in the case of a fixed gas
  fire-extinguishing system that uses a gas other than carbon dioxide,
  "certified for marine use by a product certification body" is to be
  read as "certified for marine use by a product certification body in
  accordance with the standard NFPA 2001, entitled Standard on Clean
  Agent Fire Extinguishing Systems,".

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} The quantity of gas in a fixed gas fire-extinguishing
  system that uses a gas other than carbon dioxide and that serves a
  machinery space must be sufficient to protect the space. The required
  quantity of gas must be calculated using the minimum expected ambient
  temperature in the space, the minimum design concentration of the gas
  and the net volume of the space.

- [(3)]{.lawlabel} The minimum design concentration of the gas is the
  greater of

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the concentration that is 30% above the minimum
    extinguishing concentration of the gas, when that concentration is
    determined by a cup burner test, and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the extinguishing concentration of the gas, when
    that concentration is determined by full-scale testing.

- [(4)]{.lawlabel} The net volume of the machinery space is its gross
  volume, including the volume of the bilge, the volume of the casing
  and the volume of free air contained in compressed air receivers that
  can be released into the space in the event of a fire, minus the
  volume of objects in the space.

- [(5)]{.lawlabel} If the fixed fire-extinguishing system uses
  halocarbon as the extinguishing agent, the system must release a
  sufficient quantity of the agent to allow 95% of the minimum design
  concentration of the agent to be reached in 10 seconds or less in the
  machinery space. If the system uses an inert gas as the extinguishing
  agent, the system must release a sufficient quantity of the agent to
  allow 85% of the minimum design concentration of the agent to be
  reached in 120 seconds or less in the space.

#### [Fixed Aerosol Fire-extinguishing Systems]{.HTitleText3} {#h-840842 .Subheading}

- **[[347]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-347 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} For
  the purposes of subsection 339(2), in the case of a fixed aerosol
  fire-extinguishing system,

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} "certified for marine use by a product
    certification body" is to be read as "certified for marine use by a
    product certification body in accordance with the standard NFPA
    2010, entitled Standard for Fixed Aerosol Fire Extinguishing
    Systems,"; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} for the purpose of maintenance of the generator, a
    machinery space is to be considered a severe environment.

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} The quantity of aerosol in a fixed aerosol
  fire-extinguishing system that serves a machinery space must be
  sufficient to protect the space. The required quantity of aerosol must
  be calculated using the minimum expected ambient temperature in the
  space, the design application density of the aerosol, the net volume
  of the space and, if the system uses a condensed aerosol, the
  efficiency of the system's generator.

- [(3)]{.lawlabel} The design application density of the aerosol must be
  at least 30% above the extinguishing application density, when the
  extinguishing application density of the aerosol is determined by
  full-scale testing.

- [(4)]{.lawlabel} The net volume of the machinery space is its gross
  volume, including the volume of the bilge, the volume of the casing,
  and the volume of free air contained in compressed air receivers that
  can be released into the space in the event of a fire, minus the
  volume of objects in the space.

- [(5)]{.lawlabel} The fixed fire-extinguishing system must release a
  sufficient quantity of aerosol to allow the design application density
  of the aerosol to be reached in 120 seconds or less in the machinery
  space.
