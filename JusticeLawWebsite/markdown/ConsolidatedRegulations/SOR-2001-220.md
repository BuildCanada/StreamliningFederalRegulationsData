## Nunavut Archaeological and Palaeontological Sites Regulations {#nunavut-archaeological-and-palaeontological-sites-regulations .Title-of-Act}

[SOR]{.abbr title="Statutory Orders and Regulations"}/2001-220
:::

[NUNAVUT ACT](/eng/acts/N-28.6)

Registration 2001-06-14

Nunavut Archaeological and Palaeontological Sites Regulations

P.C. 2001-1111  2001-06-14
::::

:::::::::::: section
::: {.section .order}
Her Excellency the Governor General in Council, on the recommendation of
the Minister of Indian Affairs and Northern Development, pursuant to
subsection 51(1) of the [Nunavut Act](/eng/acts/N-28.6)[[Footnote
]{.wb-invisible}^a^](#fn_SOR-2001-220_e_hq_5644){#fn_SOR-2001-220_e_hq_5644-ID0EDBAA},
hereby makes the annexed [Nunavut Archaeological and Palaeontological
Sites Regulations](/eng/regulations/SOR-2001-220).

## [Interpretation]{.HTitleText1} {#h-669997 .Part}

**[[1]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-1 .sectionLabel}** The following definitions
apply in these Regulations.

[[archaeological artifact]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[archaeological artifact]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means any tangible
    evidence of human activity that is more than 50 years old and in
    respect of which an unbroken chain of possession or regular pattern
    of usage cannot be demonstrated, and includes a Denesuline
    archaeological specimen referred to in section 40.4.9 of the Nunavut
    Land Claims Agreement. ([artefact archéologique]{.DefinedTermLink
    lang="fr"})

[[archaeological site]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[archaeological site]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means a site where an
    archaeological artifact is found. ([lieu
    archéologique]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

[[Class 1 permit]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[Class 1 permit]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means a permit that entitles
    the permittee to survey and document the characteristics of an
    archaeological or palaeontological site in a manner that does not
    alter or otherwise disturb the site. ([permis de classe
    1]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

[[Class 2 permit]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[Class 2 permit]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means a permit that entitles
    the permittee to

    - [(a)]{.lawlabel} survey and document the characteristics of an
      archaeological or palaeontological site;

    - [(b)]{.lawlabel} excavate an archaeological or palaeontological
      site;

    - [(c)]{.lawlabel} remove archaeological artifacts from an
      archaeological site or remove fossils from a palaeontological
      site; or

    - [(d)]{.lawlabel} otherwise alter or disturb an archaeological or
      palaeontological site. ([permis de classe 2]{.DefinedTermLink
      lang="fr"})

[[designated agency]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[designated agency]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} has the same meaning as in
    section 33.1.1 of the Nunavut Land Claims Agreement. ([organisme
    désigné]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

[[fossil]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[fossil]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means the hardened or preserved
    remains of or the impression of previously living organisms and
    vegetation and includes

    - [(a)]{.lawlabel} natural casts;

    - [(b)]{.lawlabel} preserved tracks, coprolites and plant remains;
      and

    - [(c)]{.lawlabel} the preserved shells and exoskeletons of
      invertebrates and the preserved eggs, teeth and bones of
      vertebrates. ([fossile]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

[[Inuit-owned lands]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[Inuit-owned lands]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} has the same meaning as in
    section 1.1.1 of the Nunavut Land Claims Agreement. ([terres
    inuit]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

[[Nunavut Land Claims Agreement]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[Nunavut Land Claims Agreement]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means the
    Agreement between the Inuit of the Nunavut Settlement Area and Her
    Majesty the Queen in right of Canada, signed on May 25, 1993.
    ([Accord sur les revendications territoriales du
    Nunavut]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

[[palaeontological site]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[palaeontological site]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means a site where a
    fossil is found. ([lieu paléontologique]{.DefinedTermLink
    lang="fr"})

::: HistoricalNote
- SOR/2009-149, s. 1

## [Application]{.HTitleText1} {#h-670017 .Part}

**[[2]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-2 .sectionLabel}** These Regulations apply to
all lands and waters in Nunavut other than

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} those within the boundaries of a park, as defined in
  the [Canada National Parks Act](/eng/acts/N-14.01); and

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} any lands set apart as a national historic site of
  Canada under section 42 of that Act.

## [Protection of Artifacts and Fossils]{.HTitleText1} {#h-670021 .Part}

- **[[3]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-3 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Subject
  to subsection (2), no person shall possess or sell

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} an archaeological artifact that was removed from an
    archaeological site on or after June 15, 2001; or

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} a fossil that was removed from an palaeontological
    site on or after June 15, 2001.

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} The prohibition on possession in subsection (1) does
  not apply to

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} a person or organization receiving an
    archaeological artifact or fossil pursuant to section 15 or 16;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} a person or organization possessing an
    archaeological artifact or fossil under the terms of an agreement
    with a person or organization referred to in paragraph (a); or

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} the holder of a Class 2 permit, during the term of
    the permit and for a period of three months after the expiration of
    the permit.

## [Protection of Archaeological Sites]{.HTitleText1} {#h-670030 .Part}

**[[4]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-4 .sectionLabel}** No person shall search for
archaeological sites or archaeological artifacts, or survey an
archaeological site, without a Class 1 or Class 2 permit.

- **[[5]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-5 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} No
  person shall excavate, alter or otherwise disturb an archaeological
  site, or remove an archaeological artifact from an archaeological
  site, without a Class 2 permit.

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} No person, other than a person engaged in a search
  and rescue operation, shall dive, or approach with an underwater
  submersible, to within 30 m of an archaeological artifact without a
  Class 2 permit.

- [(3)]{.lawlabel} Subsection (1) shall not be interpreted to prohibit
  the establishment of an outpost camp on an archaeological site in
  accordance with section 7.6.3 of the Nunavut Land Claims Agreement.

## [Protection of Palaeontological Sites]{.HTitleText1} {#h-670036 .Part}

**[[6]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-6 .sectionLabel}** No person shall search for
palaeontological sites or fossils, or survey a palaeontological site,
without a Class 1 or Class 2 permit.

**[[7]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-7 .sectionLabel}** No person shall excavate,
alter or otherwise disturb a palaeontological site, or remove a fossil
from a palaeontological site, without a Class 2 permit.

## [Issuance of Permits]{.HTitleText1} {#h-670039 .Part}

- **[[8]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-8 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A person
  may apply for a Class 1 permit by submitting an application, in
  writing, to the designated agency, setting out

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the name and qualifications of the applicant and of
    all persons who will be working on the proposed project;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} a description of the project, including

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} a statement as to whether the project relates to
      archaeological or palaeontological sites, and

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} a map and geographic coordinates of the project
      area; and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} the objectives of the project.

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} Subject to section 10, within 90 days after receipt
  of an application made under subsection (1) or after any longer period
  required to ascertain that the requirements of paragraphs (a) and (b)
  have been met, the designated agency shall issue a Class 1 permit for
  the proposed project if

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the applicant has demonstrated the expertise in
    archaeology or palaeontology necessary to conduct the project and
    complete the report required under subsection 14(2); and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the applicant has complied with all conditions
    precedent to obtaining such a permit set out in the Nunavut Land
    Claims Agreement.

- **[[9]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-9 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A person
  may apply for a Class 2 permit by submitting an application, in
  writing, to the designated agency, setting out

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the name and qualifications of the applicant;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the name and qualifications of all persons who will
    be working on the proposed project, if known;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} a description of the project, including

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} a statement as to whether the project relates to
      archaeological or palaeontological sites, and

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} a map and geographic coordinates of the project
      area;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} the objectives of the project;

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} plans for the conservation of any archaeological
    artifacts or fossils proposed to be collected under the permit;

  - [(f)]{.lawlabel} where the project relates to an archaeological
    site, a description of arrangements made for acceptance,

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} by a curation repository designated by the Inuit
      Heritage Trust under section 33.7.6 of the Nunavut Land Claims
      Agreement, of any archaeological artifacts proposed to be
      collected under the permit on Inuit-owned lands, or

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} by a curation repository designated by the
      designated agency under section 33.7.7 of the Nunavut Land Claims
      Agreement, of any archaeological artifacts proposed to be
      collected under the permit on any other lands;

  - [(g)]{.lawlabel} where the project relates to a palaeontological
    site, a description of arrangements made with the minister of the
    government of Nunavut responsible for culture and heritage for
    acceptance of any fossils proposed to be collected under the permit;

  - [(h)]{.lawlabel} a copy of the budget of the project, including
    funds allocated for the preservation of archaeological artifacts or
    fossils, and a confirmation of the project funding; and

  - [(i)]{.lawlabel} a description of the manner in which the
    archaeological or palaeontological site will be restored.

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} Subject to section 10, within 90 days after receipt
  of an application made under subsection (1) or after any longer period
  required to ascertain that the requirements of paragraphs (a) to (c)
  have been met, the designated agency shall issue a Class 2 permit for
  the proposed project if

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the applicant has demonstrated the expertise in
    archaeology or palaeontology necessary to conduct the project;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the scientific and cultural benefits of the project
    outweigh the adverse impact of the project on the archaeological or
    palaeontological site; and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} the applicant has complied with all conditions
    precedent to obtaining such a permit set out in the Nunavut Land
    Claims Agreement.

::: HistoricalNote
- SOR/2009-149, s. 3

- **[[10]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-10
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Subject to subsections (2) and (3),
  an applicant who has contravened these Regulations or the conditions
  of any previous permit or other authorization for the search for, or
  excavation of, archaeological or palaeontological sites that was
  issued in any country is not entitled to be issued a permit under
  section 8 or 9, if the contravention has not been remedied.

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} An applicant who is alleged to have contravened the
  conditions of any previous permit or other authorization referred to
  in subsection (1) and who has not remedied the contravention, may
  provide the designated agency with a statement explaining the
  circumstances of the alleged contravention including any other
  relevant information that the applicant considers appropriate.

- [(3)]{.lawlabel} The designated agency shall consider the applicant's
  statement in respect of an alleged contravention and shall decide
  whether the applicant should be issued a Class 1 or Class 2 permit
  despite the alleged contravention of the conditions of any previous
  permit or other authorization referred to in subsection (1) that has
  not been remedied.

## [Assignment]{.HTitleText1} {#h-670079 .Part}

**[[11]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-11 .sectionLabel}** A permit shall not be
assigned.

## [Expiration]{.HTitleText1} {#h-670081 .Part}

**[[12]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-12 .sectionLabel}** A permit expires on
December 31 of the year for which it was issued.

## [Site Restoration]{.HTitleText1} {#h-670083 .Part}

**[[13]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-13 .sectionLabel}** A person who excavates an
archaeological or palaeontological site shall, on completion of the
excavation, restore the site, in so far as is practicable, to its
original state.

## [Reports]{.HTitleText1} {#h-670085 .Part}

- **[[14]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-14 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} On or
  before March 31 of the year following the year for which a permit was
  issued, the holder of a Class 1 permit shall provide a copy of the
  report referred to in subsection (2), and the holder of a Class 2
  permit shall provide a copy of the report referred to in subsection
  (3), to each of

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} where the permit is in respect of an archaeological
    site,

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} the Inuit Heritage Trust;

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} the minister of the government of Nunavut
      responsible for culture and heritage; and

    - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} the Canadian Museum of Civilization; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} where the permit is in respect of a
    palaeontological site, the minister of the government of Nunavut
    responsible for culture and heritage.

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} A report of work done under a Class 1 permit shall
  set out the name of the permittee, the date of the report and the
  permit number and shall include, for each archaeological or
  palaeontological site visited, a description of the work undertaken,
  including

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} a description of the site;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} National Topographic Series maps, on a scale of
    1:50,000 or 1:250,000, showing the location of the site;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} a detailed plan of the site; and

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} representative photographs of the site.

- [(3)]{.lawlabel} A report of work done under a Class 2 permit shall
  set out the name of the permittee, the date of the report and the
  permit number and shall include, for each archaeological or
  palaeontological site visited,

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} a description of the work undertaken, including

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} a description of the site,

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} National Topographic Series maps, on a scale of
      1:50,000 or 1:250,000, showing the location of the site,

    - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} detailed plans of the site and each excavation
      unit on the site,

    - [(iv)]{.lawlabel} a vertical scale drawing of the stratigraphy of
      each excavation unit,

    - [(v)]{.lawlabel} representative photographs of the site, taken
      before and during excavation and after completion of restoration
      of the site,

    - [(vi)]{.lawlabel} a description of any subsurface testing, and

    - [(vii)]{.lawlabel} measurements of the depths at which all
      archaeological artifacts or fossils were found and their
      horizontal provenience;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} a description of the methods used in data
    acquisition, recording and analysis, including those used in field,
    archival and laboratory investigations;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} a description of any archaeological artifact or
    fossil conservation treatments and the name of the conservator;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} a description of any environmental factors and
    recent history relating to the site;

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} an assessment of the current physical status of the
    site and any present or potential factors that could alter that
    status; and

  - [(f)]{.lawlabel} an interpretation of the significance of the site
    based on a summary examination of the findings resulting from the
    work undertaken.

## [Repository]{.HTitleText1} {#h-670112 .Part}

- **[[15]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-15
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Subject to subsection (2), all
  archaeological artifacts collected by a permittee shall be submitted,
  on or before March 31 of the year following the year for which the
  permit was issued,

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} where the artifacts were collected on Inuit-owned
    lands, to a curation repository designated by the Inuit Heritage
    Trust under section 33.7.6 of the Nunavut Land Claims Agreement; or

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} where the artifacts were collected on any other
    lands, to a curation repository designated by the designated agency
    under section 33.7.7 of the Nunavut Land Claims Agreement.

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} Any Denesuline archaeological specimens collected by
  a permittee shall be submitted to the designated agency on or before
  March 31 of the year following the year for which the permit was
  issued.

**[[16]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-16 .sectionLabel}** All fossils collected by
a permittee shall be submitted, on or before March 31 of the year
following the year for which the permit was issued, to the minister of
the government of Nunavut responsible for culture and heritage.

## [Coming into Force]{.HTitleText1} {#h-670119 .Part}

**[[17]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-17 .sectionLabel}** These Regulations come
into force on June 15, 2001.
