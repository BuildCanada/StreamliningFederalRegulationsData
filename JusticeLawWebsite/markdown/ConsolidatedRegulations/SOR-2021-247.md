## [PART 1]{.HLabel1}[General]{.HTitleText1} {#h-1329269 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Definitions

- **[[1]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-1 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  following definitions apply in these Regulations.

  [[accommodations area]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[accommodations area]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means the area of a
      marine installation or structure that contains the sleeping
      quarters, dining areas, food preparation areas, general recreation
      areas, office areas and medical rooms, and includes all washrooms
      in that area. ([aire d'habitation]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

  [[Act]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[Act]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means the [Canada--Newfoundland and
      Labrador Atlantic Accord Implementation
      Act](/eng/acts/C-7.5). ([Loi]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

  [[advanced first aid certificate]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[advanced first aid certificate]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means a
      certificate evidencing the holder's successful completion of a
      training program whose curriculum conforms to the curriculum for
      advanced first aid set out in CSA Group standard Z1210, First aid
      training for the workplace -- Curriculum and quality management
      for training agencies, or, in the case of marine crew on a vessel,
      whose curriculum conforms to Chapter 4 of Department of Transport
      publication TP 13008, Training Standards for Marine First Aid and
      Marine Medical Care. ([certificat en secourisme
      avancé]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

  [[ANSI]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[ANSI]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means the American National Standards
      Institute. ([ANSI]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

  [[ASME]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[ASME]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means the American Society of
      Mechanical Engineers. ([ASME]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

  [[biological exposure index]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[biological exposure index]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means the
      biological exposure index established for a substance or agent by
      the American Conference of Governmental Industrial Hygienists in
      its publication TLVs and BEIs: Based on the Documentation of the
      Threshold Limit Values for Chemical Substances and Physical Agents
      & Biological Exposure Indices. ([indice biologique
      d'exposition]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

  [[competent person]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[competent person]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means a person who, in
      respect of a task,

      - [(a)]{.lawlabel} has the knowledge, training and experience
        necessary to do the task in a manner that protects the health
        and safety of all persons at the workplace; and

      - [(b)]{.lawlabel} has knowledge of the provisions of the Act,
        these Regulations and the occupational health and safety program
        that apply to the task and of the potential or actual danger
        that the task poses to the health or safety of
        persons. ([personne compétente]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

  [[confined space]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[confined space]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means an enclosed or
      partially enclosed space that

      - [(a)]{.lawlabel} is not designed or intended for human occupancy
        except on a temporary basis for the purpose of performing a
        specific task;

      - [(b)]{.lawlabel} is or may become hazardous to a person in it,
        including by reason of its design, construction, location or
        atmosphere or the materials or substances it contains, without
        regard to any protection that may be afforded to the person
        through the use of personal protective equipment or additional
        ventilation; and

      - [(c)]{.lawlabel} has restricted means of access and egress, or
        an internal configuration, that could make first aid,
        evacuation, rescue or other emergency response services
        difficult to provide. ([espace clos]{.DefinedTermLink
        lang="fr"})

  [[de-energized]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[de-energized]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}, in respect of any equipment,
      machine, device or system, or any component of one of those
      things, means that it is disconnected from all energy sources and
      void of any residual or stored energy. ([hors
      tension]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

  [[dive project]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[dive project]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means any work or activity
      for which an authorization to dive has been issued. ([projet de
      plongée]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

  [[electrical equipment]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[electrical equipment]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means equipment that
      uses electricity or that is used for the generation or
      distribution of electricity. ([équipement
      électrique]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

  [[energized]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[energized]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}, in respect of an electrical
      conductor, a circuit part or electrical equipment, means that it
      is a source of voltage or is electrically connected to a source of
      voltage. ([Version anglaise seulement]{.DefinitionOtherLangOnly})

  [[energy]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[energy]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} includes electrical, mechanical,
      hydraulic, pneumatic, chemical, radiant, thermal and gravitational
      energy. ([énergie]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

  [[energy-isolating device]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[energy-isolating device]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means a device
      that physically prevents the transmission or release of energy or
      a substance that is a source of energy, including

      - [(a)]{.lawlabel} a manually operated electrical circuit breaker;

      - [(b)]{.lawlabel} a disconnect switch;

      - [(c)]{.lawlabel} a manually operated switch by means of which
        the conductors of a circuit can be disconnected from all
        ungrounded supply conductors;

      - [(d)]{.lawlabel} a valve; or

      - [(e)]{.lawlabel} a blind, blank or blocking seal. ([dispositif
        d'isolation des sources d'énergie]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

  [[environmental conditions]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[environmental conditions]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means
      meteorological, oceanographical and other natural conditions,
      including ice conditions, that may affect operations at a
      workplace. ([conditions environnementales]{.DefinedTermLink
      lang="fr"})

  [[first aider]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[first aider]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means a person who holds a
      valid standard first aid certificate or advanced first aid
      certificate or who meets the requirements referred to in
      subsection 33(1) but is not a
      medic. ([secouriste]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

  [[hazard information]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[hazard information]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}, in respect of a
      hazardous substance, means information respecting the health and
      physical hazards posed by the substance and respecting its proper
      and safe storage, handling, use and disposal. ([renseignements sur
      les risques]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

  [[hazardous product]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[hazardous product]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} has the same meaning as
      in section 2 of the [Hazardous Products
      Act](/eng/acts/H-3). ([produit dangereux]{.DefinedTermLink
      lang="fr"})

  [[high-pressure washing]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[high-pressure washing]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means the use of
      water or another liquid delivered from a pump at a pressure
      exceeding 10 MPa, with or without the addition of solid particles,
      to remove unwanted matter from a surface. ([lavage sous haute
      pression]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

  [[hot work]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[hot work]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means any work or activity, other
      than the use of explosives, that involves the use of or is likely
      to produce fire, sparks or another source of ignition. ([travail à
      chaud]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

  [[IMO Resolution MSC.81(70)]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[IMO Resolution MSC.81(70)]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means the annex

      lang="fr"})

  [[lockout]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[lockout]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means the securing, in accordance

      system. ([cadenassage]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

  [[lockout device]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[lockout device]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means a device that

  [[LSA Code]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[LSA Code]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means the annex to International

  [[materials handling equipment]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[materials handling equipment]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means

      manutention]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

  [[medic]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[medic]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means a person designated under

      lang="fr"})

  [[mobile equipment]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[mobile equipment]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means wheeled or tracked

      mobile]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

  [[occupational health and safety program]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[occupational health and safety
      program]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means the occupational health and

  [[piping system]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[piping system]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means an assembly of pipes,

  [[professional engineer]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

## [PART 2]{.HLabel1}[Occupational Health and Safety Management and Oversight]{.HTitleText1} {#h-1329344 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Occupational health and safety policy

**[[4]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-4 .sectionLabel}** The occupational health and
safety policy referred to in section 205.011 of the Act must contain

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} the commitment of the operator to cooperate with any
  committee or coordinator, as the case may be, with regard to health
  and safety; and

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} an overview of the duties of all persons under Part
  III.1 of the Act.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Occupational health and safety management
system

- **[[5]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-5 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  occupational health and safety management system referred to in
  section 205.015 of the Act must set out procedures for

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} setting goals for the improvement of workplace
    health and safety, identifying specific targets against which the
    attainment of those goals is to be measured and reviewing those
    goals and targets at least annually;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} ensuring that employees

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} are competent persons in respect of all tasks to
      be carried out by them,

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} are kept aware of activities and occurrences
      that may have an impact on their health or safety at the
      workplace, and

    - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} are supervised to ensure that they perform
      their duties safely;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} ensuring that the most recent versions of all
    documents associated with the system are readily available to all
    persons at the workplace;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} collecting, managing and analyzing data with
    respect to occupational health and safety, including hazards,
    occupational disease, accidents, incidents and other hazardous
    occurrences, and identifying trends in that data; and

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} keeping training and competency records in respect
    of employees, including workplace committee members.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Auditing

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The audit referred to in paragraph 205.015(2)(g) of
  the Act must be carried out at as soon as practicable after each of
  the following occurrences and, in any event, at least once every three
  years:

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} any change of circumstances that may affect the
    health and safety of persons at the workplace;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the provision by a health and safety officer to the
    operator of a report under subsection 205.074(1) of the Act
    indicating non-compliance with Part III.1 of the Act; and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} the making by a health and safety officer of an
    order under section 205.092 or 205.093 of the Act in relation to the
    workplace.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Improvements

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} The operator must implement any improvements
  identified during the audit referred to in paragraph 205.015(2)(g) of
  the Act as soon as practicable.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Occupational health and safety program

- **[[6]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-6 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Every
  occupational health and safety program must

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} set out procedures for ensuring that all employees
    at the workplace comply with the program and with Part III.1 of the
    Act and all regulations made under that Part;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} set out procedures for ensuring that employees are
    competent persons in respect of all tasks to be carried out by them
    and are kept aware of activities and occurrences that may have an
    impact on their health or safety at the workplace;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} set out procedures for keeping training and
    competency records in respect of employees;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} set out procedures for keeping records necessary
    for the auditing of the program;

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} set out procedures and schedules that conform to
    paragraph 205.019(1)(p) of the Act for carrying out inspections for
    the purpose of hazard identification;

  - [(f)]{.lawlabel} prioritize the implementation of hazard control
    measures in the following order:

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} measures that involve the elimination of hazards,

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} measures that involve the selection of less
      hazardous means of carrying out work and activities,

    - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} measures that involve the use of engineering
      controls to reduce the risks posed by hazards,

    - [(iv)]{.lawlabel} measures that involve the use of administrative
      controls to reduce the risks posed by hazards, and

    - [(v)]{.lawlabel} measures that involve protection from the effects
      of hazards;

  - [(g)]{.lawlabel} identify the persons responsible for implementing
    hazard control measures, including after an occupational disease,
    accident, incident or other hazardous occurrence;

  - [(h)]{.lawlabel} set out procedures for

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} the reporting of hazards by persons at the
      workplace to the employer, and

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} the reporting, by the employer to a committee or
      to the coordinator, of hazards, occupational diseases, accidents,
      incidents, other hazardous occurrences and failures to comply with
      the provisions of Part III.1 of the Act, the regulations made
      under that Part or the occupational health and safety requirements
      of any authorization issued in relation to the workplace;

  - [(i)]{.lawlabel} set out procedures for the prompt investigation of
    occupational diseases, accidents, incidents and other hazardous
    occurrences to determine their root cause and identify any actions
    that are necessary to prevent their reoccurrence; and

  - [(j)]{.lawlabel} set out procedures for implementing corrective and
    preventive measures following an occupational disease, accident,
    incident or other hazardous occurrence and verifying the
    effectiveness of those measures.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Auditing

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The audit referred to in paragraph 205.02(2)(h) of
  the Act must be carried out at as soon as practicable after any of the
  following occurrences and, in any event, at least once every three
  years:

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} any change of circumstances that may affect the
    health and safety of persons at the workplace;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} any change made by the operator to its management
    system;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} the provision by a health and safety officer to the
    employer of a report under subsection 205.074(2) of the Act
    indicating non-compliance with Part III.1 of the Act; and

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} the making by a health and safety officer of an
    order under section 205.092 or 205.093 of the Act in relation to the
    workplace.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Improvements

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} The employer must implement any improvements
  identified during the audit referred to in paragraph 205.02(2)(h) of
  the Act as soon as practicable.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Workplace committee

- **[[7]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-7 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A
  workplace committee that establishes rules of procedure must include
  among them

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the quorum required for committee meetings;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the manner in which the committee will address
    complaints or concerns of employees, work refusals, occupational
    diseases, accidents, incidents and other hazardous occurrences that
    are reported to it;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} a rule whereby a complaint or concern raised with
    any committee member is to be considered a complaint or concern
    raised with the committee as a whole; and

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} the time and manner in which complaints and
    concerns are to be responded to and recommendations made under
    paragraph 205.043(5)(d) of the Act.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Minutes

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Every workplace committee must, for the purpose of
  paragraph 205.043(4)(d) of the Act, provide a copy of the minutes of
  its committee meetings to any employee on request.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Record keeping

**[[8]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-8 .sectionLabel}** All records that are
required under the Act to be kept must be maintained in a manner that
ensures their accessibility.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Posting of documents

- **[[9]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-9 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  period for which an operator or employer, as the case may be, must
  ensure that a document is posted under paragraph 205.097(4)(a) of the
  Act is at least 45 days.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Appeal

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} If a decision or order is appealed under subsection
  205.1(1) of the Act, the operator or employer, as the case may be,
  must ensure that all related documents referred to in paragraphs
  205.097(1)(a) to (d) of the Act remain posted until the 45th day after
  the day on which the decision or order is revoked, confirmed or varied
  under subsection 205.1(6) of the Act.

## [PART 3]{.HLabel1}[Reporting and Investigation]{.HTitleText1} {#h-1329411 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Report to supervisor or employer

**[[10]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-10 .sectionLabel}** An employee who becomes
aware of an occupational disease or an accident, incident or other
hazardous occurrence at the workplace must, without delay, report it to
their supervisor or their employer, orally or in writing.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Report to employer with control

**[[11]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-11 .sectionLabel}** A supervisor to whom or
employer to which --- if that employer does not have control over the
workplace --- an occupational disease, accident, incident or other
hazardous occurrence is reported under section 10 must, without delay,
report it to the employer with control over the workplace, orally or in
writing.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Employer obligations

**[[12]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-12 .sectionLabel}** An employer that becomes
aware of an occupational disease or an accident, incident or other
hazardous occurrence at a workplace under its control must, without
delay,

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} take all measures necessary to ensure the health and
  safety of all persons at the workplace; and

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} provide to the operator, in writing, a brief
  description of the occupational disease, accident, incident or other
  hazardous occurrence, including the name of any affected persons and,
  if applicable, the date on which and the time and location at which it
  occurred.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Notification of Chief Safety Officer

**[[13]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-13 .sectionLabel}** An operator that is
required under subsection 205.017(1) of the Act to notify the Chief
Safety Officer of an occupational disease, accident, incident or other
hazardous occurrence must do so in writing.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Investigation

- **[[14]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-14 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} An
  operator that is required, under subsection 205.017(2) of the Act, to
  investigate an occupational disease, accident, incident or other
  hazardous occurrence must obtain, within 14 days after the day on
  which it becomes known to the operator, a report, prepared by a
  competent person and accompanied by supporting documentation, that
  sets out, in respect of the disease, accident, incident or other
  occurrence and to a level of detail that is proportional to its actual
  or potential severity,

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} in the case of an accident, incident or other
    hazardous occurrence, the date on which and the time and location at
    which it occurred;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the name of the affected employee, if any;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} a description of it and of any resulting symptoms
    or injury;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} a description of the treatment provided, if any;

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} its causal factors and root causes;

  - [(f)]{.lawlabel} other information relevant to its nature or impact;
    and

  - [(g)]{.lawlabel} corrective and preventive measures that could be
    taken to prevent a similar situation from reoccurring.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Report

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The operator must submit a copy of the report and
  supporting documentation without delay to

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the workplace committee or the coordinator, as the
    case may be; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the Chief Safety Officer.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Material change

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} If the operator becomes aware of new information that
  may result in a material change to the report, the operator must
  obtain an updated report and supporting documentation and submit a
  copy without delay to the persons referred to in paragraphs (2)(a) and
  (b).

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Records

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} For the purpose of subsection 205.017(2) of the Act,

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the records that every operator must keep include
    all reports obtained under subsection (1) or (3) and their
    supporting documentation; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the period for which each record must be retained
    is

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} 40 years from the day on which it is made, if it
      relates to an occupational disease or exposure or potential
      exposure to a hazardous substance in excess of the threshold limit
      value or biological exposure index for that substance,

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} 10 years from the day on which it is made, if it
      relates to an incident other than the exposure or potential
      exposure referred to in subparagraph (i), or

    - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} five years from the day on which it is made, if
      it relates to any other accident or hazardous occurrence.

## [PART 4]{.HLabel1}[Training --- General]{.HTitleText1} {#h-1329445 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Provision of general training

**[[15]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-15 .sectionLabel}** The training that every
employer must provide to each of its employees includes,

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} before the employee is first transported to a
  workplace and then as necessary to ensure the training remains valid
  for the duration of the employee's employment at the workplace,

  - [(i)]{.lawlabel} an offshore survival training program appropriate
    to the workplace location and to the means of transportation to be
    used to transport the employee to and from the workplace,

  - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} training on the legislation applicable to
    occupational health and safety, including the rights of employees
    and the duties of operators, employers, supervisors and employees,
    and

  - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} training on hydrogen sulfide safety, if hydrogen
    sulfide may be present at the workplace; and

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} without delay on the employee's arrival at a
  workplace at which they have not been present in the previous six
  months and before they perform any work there,

  - [(i)]{.lawlabel} an orientation to the hazards and emergency
    procedures at the workplace,

  - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} training in respect of any emergency duties that
    may be assigned to them at that workplace, and

  - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} if the workplace is a marine installation or
    structure that is equipped with lifeboats, practice in boarding a
    lifeboat and securing themselves on a seat.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Competent person

**[[16]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-16 .sectionLabel}** Every employer must
ensure that all instruction and training that it is required to provide
under the Act is developed by and, if applicable, delivered by a
competent person.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Records

**[[17]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-17 .sectionLabel}** Every employer must
retain records of all instruction and training provided under the Act
for

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} at least five years after the day on which the person
  to whom the instruction or training is provided ceases to be employed
  at any of the employer's workplaces; or

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} if the person to whom the instruction or training is
  provided is not an employee of the employer, at least five years after
  the instruction or training is provided.

## [PART 5]{.HLabel1}[Emergency Response and Preparedness]{.HTitleText1} {#h-1329462 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Emergency response plan

- **[[18]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-18 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Every
  employer must, for each workplace under its control that is a marine
  installation or structure and having regard to the risk assessment
  carried out by it for the purpose of the occupational health and
  safety program, develop, implement and maintain a written emergency
  response plan in preparation for any reasonably foreseeable emergency
  that might compromise the health and safety of persons at that
  workplace or at any other workplace under its control that is a
  workboat or dive site associated with the marine installation or
  structure.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Contents of plan

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The emergency response plan must

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} indicate the maximum number of persons who can
    safely occupy the workplace;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} indicate the minimum number of persons needed at
    the workplace to be able to maintain safe operations in the event of
    an emergency;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} set out procedures for ensuring that the personnel
    on board list, which sets out the total number of persons at the
    workplace each day and the name, position, employer and, if
    applicable, cabin number of each, is kept up to date;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} set out the name and contact information of the
    operator, if the operator is not the employer with control over the
    workplace;

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} provide for the establishment of emergency response
    teams;

  - [(f)]{.lawlabel} set out the name, position and contact information,
    including the usual location, of each person responsible for
    overseeing the emergency response teams and the implementation of
    emergency response procedures, as well as the name, position and
    contact information of those persons' delegates;

  - [(g)]{.lawlabel} set out the duties of employees, including members
    of the emergency response teams, and the procedures to be followed
    by all persons during an emergency;

  - [(h)]{.lawlabel} indicate the muster station or other location where
    each employee is required to report during an emergency;

  - [(i)]{.lawlabel} identify the system to be used for counting
    employees at each muster station and determining which employees, if
    any, are missing;

  - [(j)]{.lawlabel} include a description of all emergency alarm
    signals that may be used, including how the order to abandon is to
    be given;

  - [(k)]{.lawlabel} set out contact information for obtaining a means
    of transportation to be used to evacuate the workplace;

  - [(l)]{.lawlabel} identify and set out contact information for all
    emergency response entities --- and other entities operating nearby
    --- that could render assistance in the event of an emergency;

  - [(m)]{.lawlabel} include verified drawings of the layout of the
    workplace that clearly identify the person who verified them,
    indicate the scale of the drawings and show

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} the location of all exits, fire escapes,
      stairways, elevators, corridors and other exit routes,

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} the location of all muster stations, temporary
      refuge areas, evacuation stations and other locations where
      lifeboats and life rafts are stored,

    - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} the location, quantity and type of all
      equipment that may be used or worn in implementing emergency
      response procedures,

    - [(iv)]{.lawlabel} the location of manual emergency shutdown and
      activation devices for all safety critical systems,

    - [(v)]{.lawlabel} the location, quantity and type of all emergency
      communications equipment,

    - [(vi)]{.lawlabel} the location of all first aid stations, medical
      rooms and casualty clearing areas, and

    - [(vii)]{.lawlabel} the location of all designated hazardous
      substance storage areas; and

  - [(n)]{.lawlabel} identify all resources necessary for the plan's
    implementation.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Availability of plan

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} The employer must ensure that a copy of the emergency
  response plan is made readily available to all employees at the
  workplace.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Multiple employers

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} If an employer has employees at a workplace not under
  its control, it must ensure that those employees comply with

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the duties and procedures set out in the emergency
    response plan developed by the employer with control over the
    workplace; or

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} emergency duties and procedures that the employer
    has ensured are aligned with those referred to in paragraph (a).

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Posting of information

**[[19]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-19 .sectionLabel}** Every employer must
ensure that the following items are posted in the specified locations,
separately from the emergency response plan, at each workplace under its
control that is a marine installation or structure:

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} a station bill containing the information referred to
  in subsection 7(1) of the [Fire and Boat Drills
  Regulations](/eng/regulations/SOR-2010-83) as well as a description of
  any additional alarm signals, the membership of all emergency response
  teams and the location of all evacuation stations

  - [(i)]{.lawlabel} in conspicuous places on every deck, and

  - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} on the bridge, if the workplace is a vessel, or at
    the location where the installation manager referred to in section
    193.2 of the Act is expected to be during an emergency, if the
    workplace is not a vessel;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} the personnel on board list referred to in paragraph
  18(2)(c), at the applicable location referred to in subparagraph
  (a)(ii); and

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} a drawing identifying all emergency escape routes
  from the location at which it is posted

  - [(i)]{.lawlabel} at conspicuous locations around the workplace, and

  - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} in every person's sleeping quarters.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Instruction and training

**[[20]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-20 .sectionLabel}** The instruction and
training that every employer must provide to each of its employees
includes

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} training in the procedures to be followed by the
  employee in the event of an emergency; and

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} instruction on the location of any emergency and fire
  protection equipment that the employee may be reasonably expected to
  use and training in the use of that equipment.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Means of evacuation

**[[21]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-21 .sectionLabel}** Every employer must
ensure, with respect to each workplace under its control that is a
marine installation or structure, that

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} all muster stations, escape routes, exits, stairways
  and any other means of evacuation are maintained in serviceable
  condition and, to the extent feasible, are accessible and ready for
  use at all times;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} all exits to the exterior, muster stations and
  evacuation stations are clearly identified by illuminated signs or
  otherwise clearly visible in all conditions; and

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} all escape routes are clearly identified with
  light-reflecting or illuminated markings.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Emergency equipment

- **[[22]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-22 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Every
  employer must ensure that the location of all equipment to be used or
  worn in implementing emergency response procedures at each workplace
  under its control is clearly identified with light-reflecting or
  illuminated signs.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Grab bags

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Every employer must provide, in all sleeping quarters
  at a workplace under its control, a readily available grab bag for
  each person assigned to the sleeping quarters containing a smoke hood,
  heat-resistant gloves and a portable light source to enable the person
  to reach muster stations, temporary refuge areas and evacuation
  stations in conditions of fire, intense heat or smoke.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Emergency escape breathing devices or
  respirators

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} Every employer must ensure that the emergency escape
  breathing devices or respirators that it provides in accordance with
  paragraph 46(a) are provided in appropriate quantities and at
  appropriate locations at the workplace to facilitate escape, having
  regard to

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the maximum number of persons who may be at the
    workplace;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} how those persons are generally distributed among
    various areas at the workplace; and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} the configuration of the workplace and the
    potential for a person's ability to move within it to be impeded by
    hazards arising from the situation that requires escape or from the
    escape itself.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Immersion suits

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} Every employer must ensure that the immersion suits
  that it provides in accordance with paragraph 46(b) are provided in
  appropriate quantities and sizes and at appropriate locations at the
  workplace to facilitate abandonment, having regard to

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the maximum number of persons who may be at the
    workplace;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} how those persons are generally distributed among
    various areas at the workplace;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} those persons' sizes; and

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} the configuration of the workplace and the
    potential for a person's ability to move within it to be impeded by
    hazards arising from the situation that requires abandonment or from
    the abandonment itself.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Minimum number required

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} Despite subsection (4), the employer must provide the
  following minimum number of immersion suits:

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} in the case of a workplace that is a marine
    installation or structure used for drilling or production or as a
    living accommodation,

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} if it is normally attended, two immersion suits
      for each person at the workplace, including one in the person's
      sleeping quarters, and

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} if it is normally unattended, one immersion suit
      for each person at the workplace;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} in the case of a workplace that is a marine
    installation or structure used for construction, diving or
    geotechnical or seismic work, one immersion suit for each person at
    the workplace, plus two additional suits in each of the bridge and
    the engine control room; and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} in the case of any other workplace, one immersion
    suit for each person at the workplace.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Emergency alert system

**[[23]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-23 .sectionLabel}** Every employer must
ensure that each workplace under its control that is a marine
installation or structure is equipped with a public address and alarm
system that is audible or visible, as the case may be, in all areas of
the workplace where a person may be present and is to be used to warn
persons if

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} the workplace has to be evacuated;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} a fire is detected;

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} there is a malfunction of a mechanical ventilation
  system provided for an area where toxic or combustible gases may
  accumulate to hazardous levels;

- [(d)]{.lawlabel} there is a person overboard; or

- [(e)]{.lawlabel} there is any other threat to the health or safety of
  persons at the workplace.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Emergency power source

**[[24]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-24 .sectionLabel}** Every employer must
ensure that each workplace under its control that is a marine
installation or structure is equipped with an emergency power source
that is sufficient to operate the following to the degree necessary to
allow for safe occupancy of or egress from the workplace in the case of
a failure of the main power system:

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} the public address and alarm system;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} the emergency lighting system;

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} internal and external communications systems; and

- [(d)]{.lawlabel} light and sound signals marking the location of the
  workplace.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Emergency descent control

- **[[25]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-25 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Every
  employer must provide, on each derrick or other elevated part of a
  workplace under its control that is a marine installation or
  structure, if there is only one usual means of escape from that
  location, a device that would allow a person to descend from the
  location by another means at a controlled speed in an emergency.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Loss of power

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The device must be capable of being operated despite
  the loss of the main source of power.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Instructions

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} The employer must ensure that written instructions
  for operating the device are kept in a conspicuous place near the
  location where the device is stored.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Fire and explosion

- **[[26]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-26 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Fire
  and explosion are prescribed risks for the purpose of paragraph
  205.02(2)(a) of the Act and every employer must ensure that each
  workplace under its control is designed, constructed, arranged and
  maintained to minimize those risks.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Hazardous areas

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The occupational health and safety program in respect
  of a workplace that is a marine installation or structure must
  identify

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} all areas at the workplace, as classified according
    to a comprehensive and documented classification system, in which
    flammable, explosive or combustible substances are or are likely to
    be present in sufficient quantities and for sufficient periods of
    time to require special precautions to be taken in the selection,
    installation or use of machinery and electrical equipment to prevent
    a fire or explosion; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the precautions applicable to those areas.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Signage

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} The employer with control over the workplace must
  ensure that signs are posted in conspicuous places at each of the
  areas referred to in subsection (2), identifying them as areas in
  which there is a risk of fire or explosion.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Prohibition

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} The employer must ensure that no person uses an open
  flame or other source of ignition in an area referred to in
  subsection (2) unless they are carrying out hot work in accordance
  with Part 26.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Temporary or portable heating equipment

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} Every employer must ensure that any temporary or
  portable heating equipment that is used at a workplace under its
  control is located, protected and used in a manner that prevents the
  equipment from being overturned or damaged and any combustible
  materials in the vicinity from igniting.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Firefighting equipment

**[[27]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-27 .sectionLabel}** Every employer must equip
each workplace under its control with the firefighting equipment that is
appropriate for that type of workplace and all classes of fire that may
occur there.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Fire team equipment

- **[[28]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-28 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  personal protective equipment that every employer with control over a
  workplace that is a marine installation or structure must provide to
  each of its employees, and any other individual at the workplace, who
  is tasked with fighting fires includes

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} a self-contained breathing apparatus with two full
    spare cylinders that

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} is selected and maintained in accordance with CSA
      Group standard Z94.4, Selection, use, and care of respirators,

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} conforms to the design and performance
      requirements in National Fire Protection Association Standard NFPA
      1981, Standard on Open-Circuit Self-Contained Breathing Apparatus
      (SCBA) for Emergency Services, and

    - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} is equipped with a personal distress alarm
      device;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} life safety ropes, belts and harnesses that conform
    to the design and performance requirements in National Fire
    Protection Association Standard NFPA 1983, Standard on Life Safety
    Rope and Equipment for Emergency Services, with the provisions of
    that standard pertaining to flame resistance being read as
    mandatory; and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} personal protective clothing --- including boots,
    gloves, helmet and visor, coat and trousers --- that conforms to the
    design and performance requirements in National Fire Protection
    Association Standard NFPA 1971, Standard on Protective Ensembles for
    Structural Fire Fighting and Proximity Fire Fighting.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Other equipment

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The employer must also provide,

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} to each employee referred to in subsection (1),

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} a portable electric safety lamp that can be
      easily attached to the employee's clothing and will operate safely
      in anticipated conditions for at least three hours, and

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} an axe with an insulated handle and carrying
      belt; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} to the fire team as a whole, at least two two-way
    portable radiotelephone apparatus that are designed not to produce
    any spark or other source of ignition.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Alternative equipment

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} Despite subsection (1) and paragraph (2)(a), if a
  workplace is a ship used for construction or diving or for
  geotechnical or seismic work, the employer may instead provide
  fire-fighter's outfits that conform to the International Maritime
  Organization's International Code for Fire Safety Systems.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Quantity

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} The number of sets of equipment referred to in
  subsections (1) and (2) or fire-fighter's outfits referred to in
  subsection (3), as the case may be, that the employer must provide at
  the workplace --- and their sizing, if applicable --- is to be
  determined having regard to the risk assessment carried out by the
  employer for the purpose of the occupational health and safety
  program.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Minimums

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} Despite subsection (4), the number of sets of
  equipment or outfits, as the case may be, that the employer must
  provide is at least

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} four, if the workplace is a ship used for
    construction or diving or for geotechnical or seismic work; or

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} ten, in any other case.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Equipment accessibility

  [(6)]{.lawlabel} The employer must ensure that the equipment provided
  in accordance with this section is kept ready for use and stored in a
  place that is easily accessible, with at least two sets of equipment
  or two outfits, as the case may be, being easily accessible from the
  helicopter deck, if any, of the marine installation or structure.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Falls into ocean

**[[29]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-29 .sectionLabel}** The risk of a person at a
workplace falling into the ocean is a prescribed risk for the purpose of
paragraph 205.02(2)(a) of the Act and the employer with control over
that workplace must

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} provide appropriate life-saving appliances and ensure
  they are held in readiness;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} ensure that a competent person is readily available
  at all times to operate the life-saving appliances; and

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} ensure that a fast rescue boat that meets the
  requirements of Chapter V of the LSA Code is provided --- or available
  from a standby vessel that is no more than 500 m away --- and held in
  readiness.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Emergency drills and exercises

- **[[30]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-30 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Every
  employer must establish, for each workplace under its control that is
  a marine installation or structure and having regard to the risk
  assessment carried out by it for the purpose of the occupational
  health and safety program, a plan that describes the emergency drills
  and exercises that must be conducted at the workplace in relation to
  various scenarios and sets out the frequency with which they must be
  conducted.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Minimum frequency

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Despite subsection (1), the employer must ensure that

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} a drill to practise mustering is conducted at least
    once a week;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} a fire drill is conducted at least once a month;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} a drill to practise escape to the location of
    lifeboats or life rafts in preparation for abandonment of the
    workplace is conducted at least once a month;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} if the workplace is equipped with lifeboats,

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} each employee participates, at least once every
      six months, in a drill that requires them to board a lifeboat
      while wearing an immersion suit and to secure themselves on a
      seat, and

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} if feasible, a lifeboat launching drill is
      conducted annually to test the integrity and operation of the
      lifeboats and launching equipment; and

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} all drills and exercises are repeated as soon as
    practicable after any significant change to the emergency plan or to
    the work or activities carried out at the workplace with respect to
    which an authorization has been issued.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Alternative to launching drill

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} If compliance with subparagraph (2)(d)(ii) is not
  feasible, the employer must ensure that additional inspections and
  testing of all components that would otherwise be tested by the
  launching drill are carried out in consultation with the lifeboat
  manufacturer and with the prior approval of the Chief Safety Officer.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Equitable scheduling

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} The employer must schedule drills and exercises to
  ensure the equitable participation of all employees, regardless of
  their shift or rotation.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Visitors

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} The employer must ensure that any person visiting the
  workplace who has not participated in the emergency drills or
  exercises is accompanied throughout the visit by someone who has done
  so.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Records

  [(6)]{.lawlabel} The employer must keep a record of all emergency
  drills and exercises conducted that contains

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the date on which and the time at which the drill
    or exercise was conducted;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} a description of the drill or exercise scenario;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} a list of all persons who participated in the drill
    or exercise;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} the length of time taken to complete the drill or
    exercise, including the length of time to achieve a full muster; and

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} observations regarding the execution of the drill
    or exercise and opportunities for improvement.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Record retention

  [(7)]{.lawlabel} The employer must retain the records referred to in
  subsection (6) for at least three years after the day on which the
  drill or exercise is carried out.

## [PART 6]{.HLabel1}[First Aid and Medical Care]{.HTitleText1} {#h-1329628 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Operator's obligations

**[[31]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-31 .sectionLabel}** Every operator must
ensure that

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} a physician who has specialized knowledge in the
  treatment of illnesses and injuries that may arise at the workplace is
  readily available at all times to provide medical advice, remotely
  from a location in Newfoundland and Labrador, to any medic or first
  aider at the workplace and to be transported to the workplace, if
  necessary, to provide medical care, unless the workplace has a medic
  who is a physician with that knowledge;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} an emergency medical evacuation service is available
  at all times for transporting an injured or ill person from the
  workplace to a hospital onshore using a means of transportation that

  - [(i)]{.lawlabel} is equipped with appropriate first aid and medical
    supplies,

  - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} is capable of accommodating and securing an
    occupied stretcher, and

  - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} has one or more competent persons available on
    board to provide first aid or medical care to the injured or ill
    person during transportation; and

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} persons at the workplace have a means of quickly
  summoning the emergency medical evacuation service.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Employer obligations

- **[[32]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-32 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Every
  employer with control over a workplace must

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} when assessing the risk of illness or injury at the
    workplace for the purpose of the occupational health and safety
    program, consult with a medic, if one is required at the workplace,
    and take into account

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} the location of the workplace and the expected
      delay in obtaining emergency medical services,

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} the layout of the workplace, and

    - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} environmental factors, including thermal
      considerations;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} develop, in consultation with a medic, if one is
    required at the workplace --- and with a specialized dive physician,
    if a dive project is to be carried out from the workplace --- a
    written medical emergency response plan that addresses all
    reasonably foreseeable emergencies at the workplace and takes into
    account the location of the workplace, the time of year at which the
    work is to be carried out, the expected number of persons at the
    workplace during normal operations and the workplace's maximum
    capacity;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} determine, in consultation with a medic, if one is
    required at the workplace --- and with a specialized dive physician,
    if a dive project is to be carried out from the workplace --- the

    medication and facilities needed to respond to all reasonably

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} establish and make readily available to all persons

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} keep a diagram indicating the location of all first

  - [(f)]{.lawlabel} keep an up-to-date list of telephone numbers for

  - [(g)]{.lawlabel} keep a list of all medics and first aiders who are

  - [(h)]{.lawlabel} keep a list of all medics who are present at the

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} whose location is clearly identified by conspicuous

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} that is supervised by a medic or, if no medic is

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} that allows for optimum ease of access to persons

    - [(iv)]{.lawlabel} a handwashing facility supplied with running hot

    - [(v)]{.lawlabel} a hand-held shower head that can easily reach the

    - [(vi)]{.lawlabel} a storage cupboard and counter,

    - [(vii)]{.lawlabel} a separate cubicle or curtained-off area with a

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Medics

- **[[33]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-33 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} An
  employer may designate a person as a medic if that person

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} has experience with helicopter or fixed-wing
    aircraft evacuation for medical purposes;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} holds an advanced cardiac life support certificate
    or basic cardiac life support instructor's certificate issued by an
    entity that bases its training on International Liaison Committee on
    Resuscitation guidelines; and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} meets one of the following requirements:

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} they hold a licence to practise medicine in
      Canada and have at least two years' clinical experience in
      intensive care or emergency practice,

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} they hold a registered nursing certificate
      recognized by a provincial regulatory body and have at least two
      years' clinical experience in intensive care or emergency
      practice, or

    - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} they hold an advanced care paramedic
      certificate or critical care paramedic certificate issued by a
      college in Canada and have at least three years' experience as an
      advanced life support provider.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Designation in writing

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The designation under subsection (1) must be made in
  writing.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}No other duties

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} The employer must not assign to the medic any other
  duties that will interfere with the prompt and adequate provision of
  first aid and medical care.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Responsibility

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} When providing first aid or medical care to an
  injured or ill person, a medic

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} must not be overruled by anyone other than the
    physician referred to in paragraph 31(a); and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} must follow any directions given by the physician
    referred to in paragraph 31(a).

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}First aiders

- **[[34]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-34 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Every
  employer must allow any first aider --- and any other employee that
  the first aider needs for assistance --- to provide prompt and
  adequate first aid to an injured or ill person and ensure that they
  have adequate time to do so, with no loss of pay or benefits.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Responsibility

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} When providing first aid to an injured or ill person,
  a first aider

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} must not be overruled by anyone other than a
    physician, a medic or, if they hold a standard first aid
    certificate, a first aider with an advanced first aid certificate;
    and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} must remain in charge of the person's care until
    the first aid is complete or the person is under the care of a
    physician, a medic or, if they hold a standard first aid
    certificate, a first aider with an advanced first aid certificate.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Treatment records

- **[[35]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-35 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Every
  first aider or medic who provides care to an injured or ill person or
  from whom treatment is sought must make and sign a record containing
  the following information:

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the full name of the injured or ill person;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} a brief description of the injury or illness ---
    and of the occurrence that gave rise to it, if any --- including, as
    applicable, the date on which and the time and location at which the
    injury or occurrence occurred or the date on which and the time at
    which symptoms of the illness were first experienced;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} a brief description of any treatment provided by
    the first aider or medic, including the date on which and time at
    which it was provided; and

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} a brief description of any arrangements made for
    the treatment or transportation of the injured or ill person.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Retention

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The employer with control over the workplace at which
  the record is made must retain it, from the day on which the injury or
  illness is first documented, for

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} 40 years, in the case of treatment for an
    occupational disease or exposure to a hazardous substance;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} 10 years, in the case of treatment for an injury
    resulting from an incident, other than exposure to a hazardous
    substance, or for a [[musculoskeletal
    injury]{.dfn}]{.DefinitionRef}, as defined in subsection 41(3); and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} five years, in any other case.

## [PART 7]{.HLabel1}[Employee Well-being]{.HTitleText1} {#h-1329732 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Occupational health and safety program

**[[36]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-36 .sectionLabel}** Every occupational health
and safety program must set out measures for promoting mental health and
healthy lifestyles and must address substance abuse, the effects on
mental health of working in a remote location and the management of
mental illness.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Impairment

- **[[37]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-37
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Impairment, including as a result of
  fatigue, stress, injury, illness, another physical or psychological
  condition, alcohol or drugs, is a prescribed risk for the purpose of
  paragraph 205.02(2)(a) of the Act and every occupational health and
  safety program must

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} set out the roles and duties of all workplace
    parties in identifying and preventing the consequences of impairment
    on the health and safety of employees;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} identify factors that may contribute to impairment
    or its causes, such as work and workplace conditions, work
    scheduling and task type and length;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} require that the factors referred to in
    paragraph (b) be regularly monitored at the workplace and taken into
    account, in conjunction with incident reports, employee complaints,
    workplace committee reports and records of excess work hours kept
    under paragraph (e) or subsection 39(3), to identify any potential
    impairment;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} require the development, maintenance and
    implementation of an appropriate work shift design that allows
    adequate rest periods;

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} require records to be kept of hours worked by an
    employee beyond their usual shift or rotation; and

  - [(f)]{.lawlabel} require that the risk of fatigue be taken into
    account in developing all workplace procedures.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Working while impaired

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Every employer must ensure that no employee at a
  workplace under its control is permitted to work if their ability to
  do so is impaired in a manner that is likely to be hazardous to their
  health or safety or that of any other person at the workplace.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Investigation of incidents

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} Impairment must be considered as a potential causal
  factor in the investigation of all incidents at the workplace.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Fatigue training

**[[38]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-38 .sectionLabel}** The instruction and
training that every employer must provide to its employees includes
instruction and training on the factors that contribute to fatigue,
procedures for identifying and reporting fatigue and the role and duties
of employees in managing fatigue.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Rest periods

- **[[39]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-39 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Every
  employer must ensure that no employee works at a workplace under its
  control unless they have been provided with a period of at least 11
  consecutive hours of rest in the previous 24 hours.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Exception

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} An employer may, in extenuating circumstances, allow
  an employee to work without having had that rest period if the
  employer has assessed the risk associated with the employee working
  the extra hours and determined, in consultation with the employee,
  that the work can be carried out without increased risk to their
  health or safety.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Documentation

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} If an employer allows an employee to work without
  having had that rest period, the employer must ensure that a
  description of the work, the name of the employee, the hours worked,
  the reason for the exception and the result of the risk assessment
  referred to in subsection (2) are recorded.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Non-application in emergency

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} Subsection (1) does not apply in the event of an
  emergency at the workplace that may be hazardous to the health or
  safety of employees.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Alternative

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} Despite subsections (1) and (2), the employer with
  control over a workplace for which an authorization has been issued
  for a period of less than six months may alternatively comply, in
  respect of the marine crew, with the daily hours of work and minimum
  rest requirements outlined in the International Convention on
  Standards of Training, Certification and Watchkeeping for Seafarers
  (STCW), 1978.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Thermal stress

**[[40]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-40 .sectionLabel}** Thermal stress is a
prescribed risk for the purpose of paragraph 205.02(2)(a) of the Act and
every employer must ensure, in respect of all persons at each workplace
under its control who may be exposed to heat or cold, that

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} their exposure is kept below the applicable threshold
  limit value or action limit established by the American Conference of
  Governmental Industrial Hygienists in its publication TLVs and BEIs:
  Based on the Documentation of the Threshold Limit Values for Chemical
  Substances and Physical Agents & Biological Exposure Indices, as the
  case may be;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} they are informed of the risk and advised of measures
  to be taken to minimize their exposure to it;

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} they are regularly monitored for signs of thermal
  stress;

- [(d)]{.lawlabel} they are provided with clothing and equipment that
  offers protection against thermal stress;

- [(e)]{.lawlabel} screens or shelters are provided to protect them
  against the elements, if applicable;

- [(f)]{.lawlabel} measures are taken to acclimatize them to
  temperatures at the workplace;

- [(g)]{.lawlabel} hot or cold beverages, as the case may be, are made
  available to them; and

- [(h)]{.lawlabel} work schedules, including rest periods, are
  established having regard to thermal stress.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Musculoskeletal injury

- **[[41]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-41
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Musculoskeletal injury is a
  prescribed risk for the purpose of paragraph 205.02(2)(a) of the Act
  and the procedures referred to in that paragraph must include an
  assessment, in consultation with the following persons, of the extent
  to which that risk is associated with each type of work carried out at
  the workplace:

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} a representative sample of employees who are
    required to carry out that type of work; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} employees who have signs or symptoms of
    musculoskeletal injury.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Hazard control measures

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The employer must ensure that interim hazard control
  measures are implemented without delay after the risks of
  musculoskeletal injury are assessed and permanent measures, determined
  with regard to the parameters established by the American Conference
  of Governmental Industrial Hygienists in its publication TLVs and
  BEIs: Based on the Documentation of the Threshold Limit Values for
  Chemical Substances and Physical Agents & Biological Exposure Indices,
  are implemented as soon as practicable.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Definition of [[musculoskeletal
  injury]{.dfn}]{.DefinitionRef}

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} In this section, [[musculoskeletal
  injury]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means an injury to or disorder of the
  muscles, tendons, ligaments, joints, nerves, blood vessels or related
  soft tissue, including a sprain, strain or inflammation.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Workplace violence and harassment

- **[[42]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-42
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Workplace violence and harassment is
  a prescribed risk for the purpose of paragraph 205.02(2)(a) of the Act
  and every employer must develop and post at a place accessible to all
  employees a policy setting out the employer's commitment to

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} provide a safe, healthy and violence and
    harassment-free workplace;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} dedicate sufficient attention, resources and time
    to address factors that contribute to workplace violence and
    harassment;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} communicate to its employees information in its
    possession about the factors referred to in paragraph (b); and

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} assist employees who have been exposed to workplace
    violence and harassment.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Occupational health and safety program

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Every occupational health and safety program must

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} require that the assessment of the risk of violence
    and harassment at the workplace take into account the nature of the
    work carried out at the workplace, the conditions under which that
    work is carried out and previous experiences at the workplace and
    other similar workplaces; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} include procedures for

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} summoning immediate assistance in response to
      violence that poses an immediate risk of physical injury,

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} reporting incidents of workplace violence and
      harassment to the employer or a supervisor, and

    - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} investigating and addressing reports of
      workplace violence and harassment.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Training

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} The training that every employer must provide to each
  of its employees includes training on the factors that contribute to
  workplace violence and harassment.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Definition of [[workplace violence and
  harassment]{.dfn}]{.DefinitionRef}

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} In this section, [[workplace violence and
  harassment]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means any action, conduct or comment,
  including of a sexual nature, that can reasonably be expected to cause
  offence, humiliation or other physical or psychological injury or
  illness to an employee.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Disruptive behaviour

**[[43]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-43 .sectionLabel}** Every employer must
instruct all employees at each workplace under its control to refrain
from engaging in disruptive behaviour at the workplace that may be
hazardous to themselves or any other person.

## [PART 8]{.HLabel1}[Personal Protective Equipment]{.HTitleText1} {#h-1329800 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Employee obligations

- **[[44]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-44 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  personal protective equipment that every employee must use or wear for
  the purpose of paragraph 205.027(b) of the Act includes, in respect of
  any hazard to which they are exposed, all personal protective
  equipment that the employer or operator provides to them for the
  purpose of preventing or reducing injury from that hazard.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Compatibility with clothing

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Every employee must ensure that any clothing worn by
  them does not interfere with the proper functioning of any personal
  protective equipment used or worn by them.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Requirements

**[[45]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-45 .sectionLabel}** Every employer must
ensure that all personal protective equipment that it provides to its
employees, or to other individuals at a workplace under its control,

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} is designed to effectively protect the user or wearer
  from the hazard for which it is provided;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} is selected having regard to any other hazards in the
  work area in which it is intended to be used or worn;

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} does not create a hazard when used or worn for the
  purpose for which it is provided;

- [(d)]{.lawlabel} is compatible with all other personal protective
  equipment that the employer provides to be used or worn at the same
  time, so that one item of equipment does not make another item
  ineffective; and

- [(e)]{.lawlabel} is maintained in good working order and in a clean
  and sanitary condition.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Prescribed equipment

**[[46]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-46 .sectionLabel}** The personal protective
equipment that every employer must provide to its employees and other
individuals at a workplace under its control includes

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} if the workplace is a marine installation or
  structure,

  - [(i)]{.lawlabel} emergency escape breathing devices that conform to
    the International Maritime Organization's International Code for
    Fire Safety Systems and that, if they are to be used for escape from
    an atmosphere that is immediately dangerous to life and health,

    - [(A)]{.lawlabel} have a rated service time in excess of the
      anticipated time needed to reach the nearest temporary safe refuge
      or muster station, and

    - [(B)]{.lawlabel} if they are multifunctional self-contained
      breathing apparatuses or airline respirators, have an auxiliary
      self-contained air supply with a rated service time in excess of
      the anticipated time needed to allow for escape by way of the
      planned escape route and, in any event, of not less than 15
      minutes, or

  - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} respirators for the purpose of escape that are
    selected in accordance with CSA Group Standard Z94.4, Selection,
    use, and care of respirators;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} if the workplace is a marine installation or
  structure, immersion suits that

  - [(i)]{.lawlabel} conform to

    - [(A)]{.lawlabel} Chapter II of the LSA Code and IMO Resolution
      MSC.81(70), with the provisions of that Resolution being read as
      mandatory, or

    - [(B)]{.lawlabel} Underwriters Laboratories standard ANSI/CAN/UL
      15027-2, Standard for Immersion Suits -- Part 2: Abandonment
      Suits, Requirements Including Safety, and

  - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} are appropriate for all expected environmental
    conditions in the vicinity of the workplace, all situations that may
    require emergency evacuation and the time it would take for rescue
    operations to reach the area and complete a rescue;

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} if the workplace is a workboat, an anti-exposure suit
  for each employee or individual that

  - [(i)]{.lawlabel} conforms to Chapter II of the LSA Code and IMO
    Resolution MSC.81(70), with the provisions of that Resolution being
    read as mandatory, and

  - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} is appropriate for all expected environmental
    conditions in the vicinity of the workplace;

- [(d)]{.lawlabel} if the employee or individual is in the vicinity of
  moving equipment or loads, personal protective clothing that conforms
  to CSA Group standard Z96, High-visibility safety apparel, other than
  the provisions of that standard that pertain to marking, and that is
  selected in accordance with that standard's annex on selection, which
  is to be read as mandatory;

- [(e)]{.lawlabel} if the employee or individual may be exposed to a
  risk of head injury, protective headwear that conforms to CSA Group
  standard Z94.1, Industrial protective headwear --- Performance,
  selection, care, and use, other than the provisions of that standard
  that pertain to marking;

- [(f)]{.lawlabel} if the employee or individual may be exposed to a
  risk of injury to the eyes, face, ears or front of the neck, eye or
  face protectors that conform to CSA Group standard Z94.3, Eye and face
  protectors, other than the provisions of that standard that pertain to
  marking, and that are compatible with any corrective lenses worn by
  the employee or individual;

- [(g)]{.lawlabel} if the employee or individual may be exposed to a
  risk of foot injury or electric shock through footwear, protective
  footwear that conforms to

  - [(i)]{.lawlabel} CSA Group standard Z195, Protective footwear, other
    than the provisions of that standard that pertain to marking,

  - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} ASTM International standard F2413, Standard
    Specification for Performance Requirements for Protective (Safety)
    Toe Cap Footwear, other than the provisions of that standard that
    pertain to marking, or

  - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} International Organization for Standardization
    standard ISO 20345, Personal protective equipment --- Safety
    footwear, other than the provisions of that standard that pertain to
    marking;

- [(h)]{.lawlabel} if the employee or individual may be exposed to noise
  levels exceeding the threshold limit value for sound, other than while
  diving, personal protective equipment that conforms to and is selected
  and maintained in accordance with CSA Group standard Z94.2, Hearing
  protection devices - Performance, selection, care, and use;

- [(i)]{.lawlabel} if the employee or individual may be exposed to a
  hazard from a type of gas that can be monitored with a personal gas
  monitoring device, a device of that type that is explosion-proof and
  has been calibrated in accordance with the manufacturer's
  instructions;

- [(j)]{.lawlabel} if the employee or individual may be exposed to fire
  or radiated heat from fire, personal protective clothing that conforms
  to the design and performance requirements set out in Canadian General
  Standards Board standard CAN/CGSB 155.20, Workwear for protection
  against hydrocarbon flash fire and optionally steam and hot fluids, or
  in Chapter 7 of National Fire Protection Association standard NFPA
  2112, Standard on Flame-Resistant Clothing for Protection of
  Industrial Personnel Against Short-Duration Thermal Exposures from
  Fire;

- [(k)]{.lawlabel} if the employee or individual may be exposed to
  respiratory hazards, respiratory protective equipment that is

  - [(i)]{.lawlabel} selected and maintained in accordance with CSA
    Group standard Z94.4, Selection, use, and care of respirators, and

  - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} in the case of a pressure-demand self-contained
    breathing apparatus that is to be used in atmospheres that are
    immediately dangerous to life and health, equipped with an audible
    alarm that sounds when the air supply has diminished to 33% of its
    capacity;

- [(l)]{.lawlabel} if the employee or individual may be exposed to a
  risk of injury to or through the skin, an effective shield, screen,
  cream, lotion or body covering; and

- [(m)]{.lawlabel} if the employee or individual is exposed to a risk of
  falling into the water,

  - [(i)]{.lawlabel} a life jacket that is appropriate for all expected
    environmental conditions and conforms to Chapter II of the LSA Code
    and IMO Resolution MSC.81(70), with the provisions of that
    Resolution being read as mandatory,

  - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} a personal flotation device that is appropriate
    for all expected environmental conditions and

    - [(A)]{.lawlabel} has been approved by the Minister of Transport,
      the Canadian Coast Guard or the United States Coast Guard,

    - [(B)]{.lawlabel} is appropriate for the weight of the person who
      will wear it,

    - [(C)]{.lawlabel} has sufficient buoyancy to keep the person's head
      afloat, and

    - [(D)]{.lawlabel} is capable of being inflated manually, regardless
      of whether it is also equipped with automated inflation
      technology, or

  - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} a fall-arrest system as described in paragraph
    109(1)(d).

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Respiratory protective equipment

- **[[47]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-47 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Every
  employer must ensure that any respiratory protective equipment that
  they provide to employees or other individuals at a workplace under
  its control is used in accordance with CSA Group standard Z94.4,
  Selection, use, and care of respirators.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Air supply

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The employer must ensure that any respiratory
  protective equipment that supplies air is used only if

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} that air conforms to either CSA Group standard
    Z180.1, Compressed breathing air and systems or European Committee
    for Standardization (CEN) standard EN 12021, Respiratory equipment
    --- Compressed gases for breathing apparatus; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the system that supplies the air is tested,
    operated and maintained in accordance with CSA Group standard
    Z180.1, Compressed breathing air and systems.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Personal gas monitoring device

**[[48]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-48 .sectionLabel}** Every employer must
ensure that each personal gas monitoring device used at a workplace
under its control is bump tested before each use.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Records

**[[49]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-49 .sectionLabel}** Despite subsection 87(2),
every employer must retain the records referred to in paragraph 87(1)(f)
in respect of all personal protection equipment that they provide for as
long as the equipment is in service.

## [PART 10]{.HLabel1}[Work Permits]{.HTitleText1} {#h-1329906 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Contents

- **[[53]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-53 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A work
  permit that is required by these Regulations must be issued, in either
  paper or electronic form, by a competent person designated by the
  employer with control over the workplace at which the activity to
  which the work permit relates is carried out, must be approved by a
  second competent person designated by that employer and must set out

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the name of the person who issued it and the person
    who approved it;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the name of each person to whom it is issued;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} the periods during which the permit is valid;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} the activity to which the permit relates, the
    location at which the activity is to be carried out and any
    restrictions to which it is subject;

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} any circumstances under which the activity is to be
    carried out that may have an effect on the health and safety risks
    associated with it, including

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} environmental conditions,

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} impediments to the proper use of any equipment
      or other thing, and

    - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} other activities being carried out in the area,
      with reference to any permit or certificate associated with those
      activities;

  - [(f)]{.lawlabel} work procedures --- including those that apply to a
    specific space, task, material, type of equipment or system --- that
    are developed having regard to the circumstances referred to in
    paragraph (e) and are to be followed to minimize the health and
    safety risks associated with the activity, including

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} any equipment, machine, device or system that
      must be locked out,

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} any tests that must be performed before, during
      and after the activity,

    - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} the particulars of any tags or signs to be
      used,

    - [(iv)]{.lawlabel} any protective equipment to be used,

    - [(v)]{.lawlabel} the procedures to be followed in the case of an
      emergency or any other change in the conditions in which the
      activity is carried out, the persons involved or the equipment
      being used, and

    - [(vi)]{.lawlabel} procedures for addressing any impediment to the
      proper use of any equipment or other thing;

  - [(g)]{.lawlabel} any other engineering and administrative control
    measures in relation to the activity that are necessary for the
    health and safety of persons at the workplace;

  - [(h)]{.lawlabel} the identification number of any lock used in a
    lockout referred to in subparagraph (f)(i);

  - [(i)]{.lawlabel} the results of any tests referred to in
    subparagraph (f)(ii), the date on which and time at which they were
    performed and the signature of the person who performed them; and

  - [(j)]{.lawlabel} any other information or documentation that is
    necessary to ensure that all persons involved in the activity are
    informed of the health and safety risks associated with it.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Signatures

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The work permit must be signed by the person who
  issued it, the person who approved it and every person involved in the
  activity to which it relates, to certify that they have read and
  understood its contents.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Occupational health and safety program

**[[54]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-54 .sectionLabel}** Every occupational health
and safety program must address the issuance and use of work permits,
including

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} activities that require a work permit;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} the work permit issuance process, including roles and
  responsibilities in obtaining or issuing a work permit, having regard
  to the nature of the activity to which the permit relates;

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} methods of assessing hazards;

- [(d)]{.lawlabel} methods of communicating information about work
  permits to affected employees;

- [(e)]{.lawlabel} the instruction and training to be given to employees
  with respect to work permits; and

- [(f)]{.lawlabel} record-keeping requirements in relation to work
  permits.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Employer obligations

- **[[55]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-55 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Every
  employer must ensure that

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} every activity that requires a work permit and is
    carried out at a workplace under its control is carried out in
    accordance with a work permit; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} every work permit issued at a workplace under its
    control is made readily available to employees for the duration of
    the activity to which it relates.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Retention of copy

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Every employer must retain a copy of each work permit
  issued at a workplace under its control for at least three years after
  the day on which the activity to which it relates is completed.

## [PART 11]{.HLabel1}[Facilities]{.HTitleText1} {#h-1329946 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Application

**[[56]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-56 .sectionLabel}** This Part applies in
respect of a workplace that is a marine installation or structure.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Accommodations area

- **[[57]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-57 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Every
  employer must ensure that the accommodations area at each workplace
  under its control

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} is constructed in a manner that allows it to be
    easily cleaned and disinfected;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} is constructed so that sleeping quarters are not
    exposed to sound levels in excess of 70 dB;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} is equipped with adequate water and sewage systems;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} is equipped with adequate heating, air-conditioning
    and ventilation systems that ensure that

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} its thermal conditions conform to ANSI/American
      Society of Heating, Refrigerating and Air-Conditioning Engineers
      (ASHRAE) standard 55, Thermal Environmental Conditions for Human
      Occupancy, and

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} its ventilation rate conforms to ANSI/ASHRAE
      standard 62.1, Ventilation for Acceptable Indoor Air Quality; and

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} is maintained in a clean and sanitary condition and
    in good repair.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Storage of equipment

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The employer must ensure that no equipment is stored
  in an accommodations area unless the equipment

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} is intended to be used in the accommodations area;
    and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} is stored in a closet that is provided for that
    purpose and fitted with a door.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Washrooms

- **[[58]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-58 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Every
  employer must make available a sufficient number of washrooms for use
  by persons of all gender identities at each workplace under its
  control, in locations conveniently accessible from all work areas.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Multiple toilets

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} If there are multiple toilets within a washroom, the
  employer must ensure that

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} each toilet is partitioned in a separate stall with
    a solid, properly closing door and fastener to ensure privacy; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} if the washroom is for use by persons of more than
    one gender identity, the partitions and doors extend from floor to
    ceiling.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Requirements

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} The employer must ensure that all washrooms

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} contain handwashing facilities as described in
    subsection 60(2);

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} are, on their floors and the lower 15 cm of their
    walls and partitions, watertight, except for drains, and impervious
    to moisture;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} are adequately heated;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} are adequately ventilated;

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} are maintained in a clean and sanitary condition
    and in good repair;

  - [(f)]{.lawlabel} are provided with a sufficient supply of toilet
    paper; and

  - [(g)]{.lawlabel} are provided with a waste receptacle with a lid.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Portable toilet units

- **[[59]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-59 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} If the
  number of washrooms at a workplace is not sufficient to accommodate
  the number of persons at that workplace during its commissioning or
  decommissioning, the employer with control over the workplace may
  satisfy its obligations under subsection 58(1) by supplementing the
  available washrooms with portable toilet units.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Requirements

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The employer must ensure that all portable toilet
  units

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} are supplied with

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} soap in a dispenser, clean water and disposable
      towels, or

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} hand sanitizer;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} are emptied and serviced at regular intervals in
    accordance with good hygiene practice; and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} satisfy the requirements set out in paragraphs
    58(3)(d) to (g).

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Handwashing facilities

- **[[60]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-60 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Every
  employer must make available a sufficient number of handwashing
  facilities for use by persons at each workplace under its control, in
  locations conveniently accessible from all work areas.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Requirements

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The employer must ensure that all handwashing
  facilities

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} have a supply of either clean hot and cold or clean
    warm water;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} are supplied with soap in a dispenser;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} are supplied with individual clean and sanitary
    towels or another suitable means of drying hands; and

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} are maintained in a clean and sanitary condition
    and in good repair.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Showers

- **[[61]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-61 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Every
  employer must make available a sufficient number of showers for use by
  persons at each workplace under its control.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Requirements

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The employer must ensure that all showers

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} are designed for use by one person at a time, with
    walls, partitions or curtains in place as necessary to ensure
    privacy;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} have floors and walls that are watertight, except
    for drains, and impervious to moisture;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} have a supply of clean hot and cold water;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} are supplied with individual clean and sanitary
    towels; and

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} are maintained in a clean and sanitary condition
    and in good repair.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Changing facilities

**[[62]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-62 .sectionLabel}** Every employer must
provide, at each workplace under its control, a changing facility that

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} is located adjacent to a washroom;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} is of sufficient size to allow employees to change in
  and out of work clothing;

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} has, for each employee at the workplace who is on
  rotation, a locker with sufficient capacity to store the employee's
  personal clothing while they are working and their work clothing and
  equipment while they are not working;

- [(d)]{.lawlabel} has sufficient capacity to allow for storage of
  personal protective equipment belonging to off-rotation employees, if
  there is insufficient storage available to those employees in their
  sleeping quarters; and

- [(e)]{.lawlabel} contains a means of drying wet clothing.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Sleeping quarters

- **[[63]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-63 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Every
  employer must ensure that the sleeping quarters at each workplace
  under its control contain, for each person assigned to those quarters,

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} a standalone bed or bunk

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} that has inner dimensions of at least 1.98 m by
      80 cm,

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} that is not part of a unit that is more than
      double-tiered,

    - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} whose bottom is at least 30 cm off the floor,
      if it is a standalone bed or the lower bunk in a double-tiered
      unit, or approximately midway between the bottom of the lower bunk
      and the ceiling, if it is the upper bunk in a double-tiered unit,

    - [(iv)]{.lawlabel} that is equipped with an access ladder and a
      suitable barrier to protect against falls, if it is the upper bunk
      in a double-tiered unit,

    - [(v)]{.lawlabel} that can be easily cleaned and disinfected, and

    - [(vi)]{.lawlabel} that is supplied with clean and sanitary
      bedding;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} a storage area fitted with a locking device to hold
    the employee's personal belongings; and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} a reading lamp.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Private room and washroom

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Every employer must, to the extent feasible, assign
  each person at a workplace under its control their own sleeping
  quarters with direct access to their own washroom containing a shower.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Alternative

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} If compliance with subsection (2) is not feasible,
  the employer must

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} assign no more than two persons to sleep in the
    same sleeping quarters at the same time, unless a greater number is
    approved in advance by the Chief Safety Officer on a short-term
    basis; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} if the workplace is a marine installation or
    structure used for drilling or production or as a living
    accommodation, ensure that all persons have direct access from their
    sleeping quarters to a washroom containing a shower and that no more
    than two sleeping quarters have direct access to the same washroom.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Dining area

**[[64]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-64 .sectionLabel}** Every employer must
ensure the provision, at each workplace under its control, of a dining
area that is

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} of sufficient size to allow individual seating and
  table space for each employee expected to be using the area at one
  time;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} separated from any place containing a hazardous
  substance that may contaminate food, dishes or utensils;

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} provided with waste receptacles; and

- [(d)]{.lawlabel} maintained in a clean and sanitary condition and in
  good repair.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Smoking areas

- **[[65]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-65 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} It is
  prohibited to smoke or use a vaping device at a workplace other than
  in an area designated for that purpose by the employer with control
  over the workplace.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Designation of areas

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} An employer must select any area that it designates
  as an area in which smoking or the use of a vaping device is permitted
  having regard to

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the area's proximity to fire and explosion hazards;
    and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the need to prevent exposure of other persons at
    the workplace to emissions --- both directly from the product and as
    exhaled by the user --- from smoking or the use of a vaping device.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Prohibition in vicinity of drilling or
  production

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} It is prohibited to smoke or use a vaping device ---
  even within a designated area --- on the deck of a marine installation
  or structure if drilling or production activities are being carried
  out in the vicinity.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Indoor areas

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} The employer must ensure, with respect to any indoor
  area that it designates as an area in which smoking or the use of a
  vaping device is permitted, that

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the designated area is maintained under negative
    pressure with respect to the adjacent area;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the designated area is separated from the adjacent
    area by solid walls, floors and ceilings and solid doors equipped
    with an automatic closing mechanism; and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} air transfer into the designated area is maintained
    at a rate of at least 24 L/s per occupant, regardless of whether the
    doors are open or closed, and air is not recirculated.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Signage

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} Every employer must ensure that signage is posted
  outside each entrance to an area in which smoking or the use of a
  vaping device is permitted, indicating

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} that persons entering the area may be exposed to
    emissions from smoking or the use of a vaping device; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the area's maximum occupancy level, as determined
    with regard to its air transfer rate, if the area is indoors,.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Designation removed

  [(6)]{.lawlabel} If an employer removes the designation of an area as
  an area in which smoking or the use of a vaping device is permitted,
  it must ensure that the signage referred to in paragraph (5)(a)
  remains posted outside each entrance to the area until the area
  contains no residual contaminants from the smoking or vaping activity.

## [PART 12]{.HLabel1}[Sanitation and Housekeeping]{.HTitleText1} {#h-1330059 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Waste material

**[[66]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-66 .sectionLabel}** The risks associated with
the accumulation of and exposure to waste material, including garbage,
recyclable refuse, food waste and debris, are prescribed risks for the
purpose of paragraph 205.02(2)(a) of the Act and every employer must
ensure, at each workplace under its control, that

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} waste material is collected, handled, segregated and
  removed in a safe and hygienic manner;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} waste receptacles and other facilities for disposing
  of and storing waste material are provided to prevent its hazardous
  accumulation; and

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} all waste receptacles that are provided in dining and
  food preparation areas or that are intended to hold waste material
  that could give rise to a hazard, including waste material that is
  flammable or combustible, are

  - [(i)]{.lawlabel} made of fire-rated material,

  - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} leakproof,

  - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} fitted with a tight-fitting lid, and

  - [(iv)]{.lawlabel} maintained in good working order and in a clean
    and sanitary condition.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Pests

- **[[67]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-67 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  risks associated with the presence of pests are prescribed risks for
  the purpose of paragraph 205.02(2)(a) of the Act and every employer
  must ensure that the enclosed parts of each workplace under its
  control are constructed, equipped and maintained in a manner that
  prevents, to the extent feasible, the entry of pests.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Elimination of pests

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} If pests have entered an enclosed part of the
  workplace, the employer must immediately take all steps necessary to
  eliminate the pests and prevent their re-entry.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Records

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} The occupational health and safety program must
  provide for the keeping of pest control inspection and pesticide
  application records.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Cleanliness and orderliness

**[[68]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-68 .sectionLabel}** The hazard control
measures set out in every occupational health and safety program must
include procedures for

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} maintaining the workplace in a clean and orderly
  state;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} ensuring that all surfaces at the workplace on which
  a person may stand are kept free of slipping and tripping hazards; and

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} ensuring that all cleaning of the workplace is
  carried out in a manner that does not allow dust or any other
  substance that may be harmful to employees' health or safety to
  contaminate the air.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Storage

**[[69]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-69 .sectionLabel}** Every employer must
ensure that all things at each workplace under its control are stored or
placed in a manner that does not a present a hazard to the health or
safety of any person, including by

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} impeding the safe movement of persons, equipment or
  things through corridors, entrances or exits;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} impeding access to or the use of firefighting, first
  aid or other emergency equipment;

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} interfering with the operation of fixed fire
  protection equipment;

- [(d)]{.lawlabel} impeding access to electrical panels, equipment
  control panels or emergency disconnect switches or devices;

- [(e)]{.lawlabel} obstructing ventilation or illumination;

- [(f)]{.lawlabel} exceeding the maximum load-carrying capacity of the
  thing on which they are stored or placed; or

- [(g)]{.lawlabel} being stacked in a manner that makes them unstable.

## [PART 13]{.HLabel1}[Food and Potable Water]{.HTitleText1} {#h-1330090 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Food safety

**[[70]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-70 .sectionLabel}** The risks arising from
the consumption of unsafe food are, in respect of every workplace at
which food is served, prescribed risks for the purpose of paragraph
205.02(2)(a) of the Act and the employer with control over the workplace
must ensure that

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} all preparation, storage, handling or serving of food
  is done in accordance with the Codex Alimentarius Commission's
  "Recommended International Code of Practice: General Principles of
  Food Hygiene", as set out in its publication Food Hygiene (Basic
  Texts), with the recommendations in the Code being read as mandatory;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} foods that require refrigeration to prevent them from
  becoming hazardous to health are maintained at a temperature of 4°C or
  lower;

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} foods that require freezing to prevent them from
  becoming hazardous to health are maintained at a temperature of -18°C
  or lower; and

- [(d)]{.lawlabel} temperature logs are maintained for hot and cold
  holding units, including refrigerators and freezers.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Potable water

- **[[71]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-71 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Every
  employer must provide, to all persons at each workplace under its
  control, potable water for drinking and food preparation and must
  ensure that clean and sanitary cups are provided for drinking water
  that is not provided from a drinking fountain.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Occupational health and safety program

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The risks associated with the consumption of
  non-potable water are prescribed risks for the purpose of paragraph
  205.02(2)(a) of the Act and every occupational health and safety
  program must

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} provide for on-site oversight by a competent person
    of the daily operation of the system by which potable water is
    provided;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} require notification of the workplace committee or
    coordinator, as the case may be, of any samples taken from that
    system that fail to meet the requirements for potable water;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} set out procedures for addressing any failures of
    the system to provide water that meets the requirements for potable
    water; and

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} address the keeping of records relating to the
    system, its oversight and its performance.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Definition of [[potable
  water]{.dfn}]{.DefinitionRef}

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} In this section, [[potable
  water]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means water that conforms to the
  Department of Health's Guidelines for Canadian Drinking Water Quality.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Emergency lighting

## [PART 15]{.HLabel1}[Sound Levels]{.HTitleText1} {#h-1330130 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Unimpeded communication

**[[76]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-76 .sectionLabel}** Every employer must
ensure that sound levels at each workplace under its control do not
impede communication during normal or emergency operations.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Noise

- **[[77]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-77
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Excessive noise is a prescribed risk
  for the purpose of paragraph 205.02(2)(a) of the Act and every
  employer must ensure, with respect to each workplace under its
  control, other than an underwater area, that

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} noise surveys are carried out in accordance with
    CSA Group standard Z107.56, Measurement of noise exposure;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} to the extent feasible, the threshold limit value
    for sound is not exceeded; and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} if it is not feasible to reduce sound levels at the
    workplace to within the threshold limit value,

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} signs are posted at the entrances to or on the
      periphery of areas where employees may be exposed to excessive
      sound levels that clearly indicate the presence of a noise hazard
      and identify the personal protective equipment that is required in
      that area, and

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} every employee at the workplace undergoes an
      audiometric test every two years, or more frequently as
      recommended by an audiologist or occupational physician.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Survey results

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The employer must retain the results of all noise
  surveys for at least 10 years after the day on which they were carried
  out.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Instruction and training

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} The instruction and training that every employer must
  provide to its employees includes instruction and training on the
  risks posed by excessive noise.

## [PART 16]{.HLabel1}[Ventilation]{.HTitleText1} {#h-1330145 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Air quality

- **[[78]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-78 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Poor
  air quality is a prescribed risk for the purpose of paragraph
  205.02(2)(a) of the Act and every employer must ensure that all
  contaminants in the air at each workplace under its control are kept
  below the applicable threshold limit values, including --- if the
  workplace is a marine installation or structure --- through the
  installation, use, maintenance and testing of appropriate ventilation
  systems and other engineering controls.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Local exhaust ventilation

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The ventilation systems must, if feasible, include
  local exhaust ventilation systems where necessary to prevent
  contaminants from entering an employee's breathing zone while the
  employee is working.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Ventilation system

**[[79]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-79 .sectionLabel}** Every employer must
ensure, with respect to any ventilation system installed at a workplace
under its control, that

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} it is equipped with a device that provides a warning
  if the system is not working properly;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} all contaminants that it removes are exhausted clear
  of the area from which they are drawn and prevented from entering any
  work area or accommodations area;

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} it and any humidification equipment that forms a part
  of it

  - [(i)]{.lawlabel} are constructed and maintained in a manner that
    minimizes the growth and dissemination through the system of
    micro-organisms, insects and mites, and

  - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} if feasible, are readily accessible for cleaning
    and inspection; and

- [(d)]{.lawlabel} unless it is installed in an accommodations area, its
  minimum ventilation rate conforms to American Conference of
  Governmental Industrial Hygienists standard Industrial Ventilation: A
  Manual of Recommended Practice for Design.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Internal combustion engine

**[[80]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-80 .sectionLabel}** If mobile equipment
powered by an internal combustion engine is operated indoors or in an
enclosed work area, the employer with control over the workplace at
which it is operated must ensure that the engine is maintained in a
manner that ensures conformity with the requirements of American
Conference of Governmental Industrial Hygienists standard Industrial
Ventilation: A Manual of Recommended Practice for Design relating to
vehicle exhaust ventilation.

## [PART 17]{.HLabel1}[Structural Safety]{.HTitleText1} {#h-1330161 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Movement within workplace

**[[81]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-81 .sectionLabel}** Every employer must
ensure, to the extent feasible, that all persons at each workplace under
its control that is a marine installation or structure are able to move
around the workplace, including through corridors, without bending,
sidling or tripping and must ensure that any changes in floor elevation
and ceiling height that pose a risk of injury and cannot be eliminated
are clearly marked.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Doors

**[[82]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-82 .sectionLabel}** Every employer must
ensure, at each workplace under its control that is a marine
installation or structure, that

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} any swinging door that opens onto a stairway does so
  over a floor or landing that extends under the full swing of the door;
  and

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} the use of any double-action swinging door that does
  not permit persons approaching from one side of the door to be aware
  of persons on the other side is restricted to a single direction.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Guard-rails

**[[83]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-83 .sectionLabel}** Any guard-rail that is
required under these Regulations must

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} include

  - [(i)]{.lawlabel} a horizontal rail, cable or chain positioned not
    less than 90 cm and not more than 1.1 m above the working surface,

  - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} unless the guard-rail is located at the top of a
    fixed ladder, one or more additional horizontal rails, cables or
    chains positioned below the one referred to in subparagraph (i) so
    that the distance between the working surface and the nearest rail,
    cable or chain or between any two adjacent rails, cables or chains
    does not exceed half the distance between the working surface and
    the rail, cable or chain referred to in subparagraph (i), and

  - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} vertical supports spaced not more than 3 m apart
    at their centres;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} be capable of withstanding the greater of

  - [(i)]{.lawlabel} the maximum load that is likely to be imposed on
    it, and

  - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} a static load of not less than 890 N applied in
    any direction at any point on the rail, cable or chain referred to
    in subparagraph (a)(i); and

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} be capable of withstanding the effects of fire.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Wall and floor openings and open edges

**[[84]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-84 .sectionLabel}** Every employer must
ensure that, in any area on a marine installation or structure under its
control to which a person might have access,

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} every opening in a wall, partition or bulkhead that
  measures at least 75 cm high and 30 cm wide and from which there is a
  drop of more than 1.2 m, or that otherwise poses a hazard to any
  person, and every opening in a floor, platform or deck whose smallest
  dimension measures at least 30 cm is

  - [(i)]{.lawlabel} protected by a guard-rail, or

  - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} covered with material that is

    - [(A)]{.lawlabel} securely fastened to a supporting structural
      member of the marine installation or structure, and

    - [(B)]{.lawlabel} capable of withstanding all loads that are likely
      to be imposed on it; and

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} every other open edge from which there is a drop of
  more than 1.2 m, other than on a helicopter deck, is protected by a
  guard-rail.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Open-top enclosures

- **[[85]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-85 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Every
  employer must ensure, at each workplace under its control, that, if an
  employee has access to the top of a bin, hopper, tank, vat, pit or
  similar enclosure with an opening at the top that is large enough for
  a person to fit through,

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the enclosure's opening is covered with a grating,
    screen or other covering; or

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} there is a walkway over or adjacent to the opening
    that is not less than 50 cm wide and is fitted with guard-rails.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Support capability

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The grating, screen, covering or walkway must be
  capable of supporting the greater of

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the maximum load that is likely to be imposed on
    it, and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} a live load of 6 kN.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Access to inside

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} If an employee is required to access the inside of an
  open-top enclosure from its top, the employer must ensure, if
  feasible, that there is a fixed ladder on the inside wall of the
  enclosure that permits the employee to safely enter and exit.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Structural openings

**[[86]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-86 .sectionLabel}** Every employer must
ensure, before any opening is made in the structure of a marine
installation or structure under its control, including in any floor or
wall, that the locations of all pipes, cable and conduits in the area
where the opening is to be made are clearly marked.

## [PART 18]{.HLabel1}[Equipment, Machines and Devices]{.HTitleText1} {#h-1330199 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Requirements

- **[[87]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-87 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Every
  operator and employer must ensure, with respect to any equipment,
  machine or device that that operator or employer provides for use at a
  workplace, including any part of or accessory used with one of those
  things, that

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} only a competent person installs, assembles, uses,
    handles, stores, adjusts, modifies, maintains, repairs, inspects,
    tests, cleans or dismantles it;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the activities referred to in paragraph (a) are
    carried out in accordance with its manufacturer's instructions and,
    if they are carried out outdoors, having regard to existing
    environmental conditions;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} the manufacturer's instructions respecting its
    operation and maintenance are made readily available to any person
    carrying out an activity referred to in paragraph (a);

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} adequate space is provided around it to allow the
    activities referred to in paragraph (a) to be carried out safely;

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} it is subject to

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} a brief visual inspection before each use by the
      person using it, and

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} a thorough safety inspection at least once each
      year if

      - [(A)]{.lawlabel} its purpose is to preserve or protect life,

      - [(B)]{.lawlabel} its use would, in the absence of any hazard
        control measures, pose a risk to the health or safety of persons
        at the workplace, or

      - [(C)]{.lawlabel} it is subject to degradation over time that
        could affect its safety;

  - [(f)]{.lawlabel} any person who maintains, repairs, modifies, tests
    or inspects it --- other than by carrying out a brief visual
    inspection --- makes and signs a record that clearly identifies the
    equipment, machine or device, describes the activity carried out and
    provides the person's name, the date of the activity and, if
    applicable, the person's observations regarding the safety of the
    equipment, machine or device;

  - [(g)]{.lawlabel} no person uses it in a manner that may compromise
    the health or safety of a person at the workplace, including by

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} maintaining, repairing or cleaning any powered
      equipment, machine or device while it is operational, or

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} operating any equipment, machine or device that
      is equipped with a guard while the guard is not in its proper
      position; and

  - [(h)]{.lawlabel} no person intentionally tampers or interferes with
    it such that the health and safety of any person at the workplace
    could be compromised, including, unless done in accordance with
    these Regulations, by impairing or rendering inoperative a safety
    device or system that is used with it.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Records

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The operator or employer that provides the equipment,
  machine or device must

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} retain the records referred to in paragraph (1)(f),
    as well as a record setting out the date that they acquired the
    equipment, machine or device, until the day that is five years after
    the day on which the equipment, machine or device is taken out of
    service at the workplace; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} ensure that those records are made readily
    available to any person who uses, inspects, tests, maintains,
    repairs or modifies the equipment, machine or device.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Exception --- maintenance, repair or
  cleaning

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} Despite subparagraph (1)(g)(i), a person is permitted
  to maintain, repair or clean a powered piece of equipment, machine or
  device while it is operational if

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} its continued operation is essential to the
    maintenance, repair or cleaning; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} if feasible, the energy source for any of its parts
    whose operation is not essential is controlled in accordance with
    Part 27 or those parts are equipped with guards.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Exception --- use without guard

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} Despite subparagraph (1)(g)(ii), a person is
  permitted to operate any equipment, machine or device without its
  guard in the proper position if necessary to

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} permit the release of any part of a person that is
    trapped in the equipment, machine or device; or

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} test, maintain, repair or clean the equipment,
    machine or device if

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} its energy source is, if feasible, controlled in
      accordance with Part 27, and

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} the person who performs the work does not leave
      the equipment, machine or device until the guard has been replaced
      and verified to be functioning properly.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Alternative procedures

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} Every employer must establish --- and must instruct
  all employees to follow --- procedures for minimizing the risk of
  injury if equipment, machines or devices at a workplace under the
  employer's control must be maintained, repaired, cleaned or tested
  while operational and without a guard in place and it is not feasible
  to control their energy source as described in paragraph (3)(b) or
  subparagraph (4)(b)(i).

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Removal from service

**[[88]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-88 .sectionLabel}** Every employer must
ensure that any equipment, machine or device at a workplace under its
control that it has reason to doubt is safe for use is taken out of
service and identified in a manner that ensures it is not inadvertently
returned to service until a competent person determines it to be safe
for use.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Hair, clothing and accessories

**[[89]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-89 .sectionLabel}** Every employer must
ensure that all persons at each workplace under its control not wear
long hair, loose-fitting clothing, dangling accessories, jewellery or
other similar items unless those items are tied, covered or otherwise
secured as necessary to prevent them from coming into contact with
equipment or machines or from otherwise presenting a risk to health or
safety.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Pedestrian passage

**[[90]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-90 .sectionLabel}** Every employer must
ensure, at each workplace under its control, that a path for pedestrian
use is clearly identified with floor markings or physical means through
any area in which mobile equipment or other equipment that presents a
risk of injury to persons passing through is being used.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Standards

- **[[91]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-91 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Every
  employer must ensure, in respect of each workplace under its control,
  that

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} all equipment and machines conform to and are used
    in accordance with all applicable provisions of CSA Group standard
    Z432, Safeguarding of machinery;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the starting and stopping controls of all equipment
    and machines are located within easy reach of the person operating
    the equipment or machine;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} all access doors on equipment or machines that
    expose moving parts when opened are, if feasible, equipped with
    interlocks that

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} prevent the access door from opening while the
      moving parts are in motion, or

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} immediately disconnect the power from the
      driving mechanism when the door is opened, causing the moving
      parts to stop and preventing them from restarting until the door
      is closed;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} all controls on equipment, machines and devices are

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} clearly marked with their functions in a manner
      and location that ensures the markings remain visible to the
      person operating the equipment, machine or device, and

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} designed, positioned or shielded to prevent
      inadvertent activation;

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} all insulated equipment and devices are protected
    against damage to their insulating material;

  - [(f)]{.lawlabel} all portable hand-held motor-operated electric
    tools conform to CSA Group standard C22.2 No. 60745, Hand-Held
    Motor-Operated Electric Tools -- Safety, or CSA Group standard C22.2
    No. 62841, Electric motor-operated hand-held tools, transportable
    tools and lawn and garden machinery -- Safety, as applicable;

  - [(g)]{.lawlabel} all powder-actuated fastening tools, fasteners and
    powder loads conform to and are used in accordance with
    ANSI/American Society of Safety Professionals (ASSP) standard A10.3,
    Safety Requirements for Powder-Actuated Fastening Systems, except
    with respect to the required eye protection, which must instead
    conform to paragraph 46(f);

  - [(h)]{.lawlabel} all power presses conform to and are used in
    accordance with CSA Group standard Z142, Code for power press
    operation: Health, safety, and safeguarding requirements;

  - [(i)]{.lawlabel} all electric tools that plug into an electrical
    receptacle are grounded, unless they

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} have a protective system of double insulation, or

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} are used in a location where reliable grounding
      cannot be obtained and are supplied from a double-insulated
      portable ground fault circuit interrupter of the class A type that
      conforms to CSA Group standard C22.2 No. 144, Ground Fault Circuit
      Interrupters, on a 125-volt or 15-, 20- or 30-ampere circuit;

  - [(j)]{.lawlabel} all equipment, machines and devices that are a
    potential source of ignition are, if they are used in an area
    referred to in subsection 26(2), rated by their manufacturer as
    appropriate for use in such an area and used only with control
    measures in place to minimize the risk of fire or explosion;

  - [(k)]{.lawlabel} all hoses that contain a substance under pressure
    and that are connected to equipment or to a machine or device are
    equipped with restraining devices as necessary to prevent the hoses'
    hazardous movement, including in the event of accidental
    disconnection;

  - [(l)]{.lawlabel} all abrasive blasting or high-pressure washing
    machines have operating controls that

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} are located near the nozzle,

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} are hand-operated, and

    - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} require continuous pressure by the person
      operating them to permit the flow of material;

  - [(m)]{.lawlabel} all abrasive wheels are

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} inspected by a competent person and determined to
      be free from defects, cracks or other problems before being
      installed,

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} mounted between flanges,

    - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} used only on machines that are equipped with
      machine guards, including, in the case of a bench grinder, a wheel
      guard and a work rest or other device that, without making contact
      with the abrasive wheel, prevents the work piece from jamming
      between the abrasive wheel and the wheel guard, and

    - [(iv)]{.lawlabel} used only on machines whose number of
      revolutions per minute does not exceed the maximum speed rating of
      the abrasive wheel;

  - [(n)]{.lawlabel} all equipment and machines whose operation may
    cause the ejection of material that may pose a hazard to a person
    are, if feasible, equipped with a means of safely containing the
    material;

  - [(o)]{.lawlabel} all equipment and machines that have exposed
    moving, electrically charged or hot parts or that process, transport
    or handle material that constitutes a hazard are, if feasible,
    equipped with

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} a guard that is not readily removable without the
      use of tools and that physically prevents persons from coming into
      contact with the parts or material or from being exposed to the
      hazard they present, or

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} if rendering the equipment or machine
      inoperative would minimize the hazard, a device that renders the
      equipment or machine inoperative if a person or their clothing
      comes into contact with or comes too close to a part of the
      equipment or machine that is likely to cause injury;

  - [(p)]{.lawlabel} all wire rope in tension, other than on a crane or
    hoist, is protected by a guard, if feasible; and

  - [(q)]{.lawlabel} if the workplace is a marine installation or
    structure, all temporary or portable heating equipment that is used
    in an enclosed area

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} provides complete combustion of the fuel used in
      it or is equipped with an exhaust system that discharges the
      products of combustion outside the enclosed area, and

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} is used only while carbon monoxide levels in the
      enclosed area are being continuously monitored.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Alternative safeguards

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} If it is not feasible for equipment or a machine to
  be equipped as described in paragraph (1)(c), (n) or (o), or for wire
  rope in tension to be protected as described in paragraph (1)(p), the
  employer must ensure that another guard, safety device or awareness
  barrier is put in place to protect against the hazard.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Fuelling

- **[[92]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-92 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Every
  employer must ensure that no equipment or machine at a workplace under
  its control is fuelled, and no fuel is transferred between containers,

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} in the following locations:

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} a place where the vapours from the fuel are not
      readily dissipated, or

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} the hold of a vessel or any other enclosed space
      at the workplace; or

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} in the following circumstances:

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} subject to subparagraph (ii), while there is any
      source of ignition in the vicinity that presents a risk of fire or
      explosion, or

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} in the case of equipment, while the equipment's
      engine is running, unless it is designed to be fuelled in that
      manner.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Exception

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Despite subparagraph (1)(a)(ii), equipment may be
  fuelled in the hold of a vessel or another enclosed space if

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} an employee who has a suitable fire extinguisher
    ready for use is in the hold or space;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} no one other than the employee referred to in
    paragraph (a) and those employees engaged in the fuelling are in the
    hold or space;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} the fuelling is carried out by transferring fuel
    directly into the equipment's fuel tank or, in the case of liquefied
    gas, by replacing spent cylinders;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} no more fuel than is necessary to fill the
    equipment's fuel tank --- or, in the case of liquefied gas, no more
    than the number of cylinders in need of replacement --- is taken
    into the hold or space; and

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} atmospheric gas levels in the hold or space are
    continuously monitored.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Procedures

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} Every employer must develop procedures to be followed
  respecting the fuelling of equipment to protect the health and safety
  of employees.

## [PART 20]{.HLabel1}[Ladders, Stairs and Ramps]{.HTitleText1} {#h-1330312 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Application

**[[95]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-95 .sectionLabel}** This Part applies in
respect of a workplace that is a marine installation or structure.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Ship's ladder

**[[96]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-96 .sectionLabel}** For the purposes of this
Part, any reference to stairs includes a permanently installed
structure, commonly known as a ship's ladder, that has a steep pitch,
rigid treads supported by rigid side rails and a handrail on each side.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Requirement to install

**[[97]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-97 .sectionLabel}** If an employee in the
course of routine work is required to move between levels that are more
than 45 cm apart, the employer with control over the workplace must
ensure that a fixed ladder, fixed stairs or a fixed ramp is installed
between the levels.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Stairs, ramps and fixed ladders

- **[[98]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-98 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Every
  employer must ensure that all stairs, ramps and fixed ladders that are
  installed at each workplace under its control, as well as all cages,
  landings and platforms used with the fixed ladders, are designed and
  maintained to support any load that is likely to be imposed on them
  and to safely accommodate all persons who are likely to use them and
  all equipment that is likely to pass over them.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Hazard protection

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} If stairs, a ramp or a fixed ladder end in direct
  proximity to anything that would pose a risk of injury to a person
  were they to inadvertently come into contact with it, the employer
  must ensure that a barricade is installed to protect persons using the
  stairs, ramp or ladder from that hazard.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Temporary stairs

**[[99]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-99 .sectionLabel}** Every employer must
ensure that all temporary stairs installed at a workplace under its
control are securely fastened in place and have

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} uniform steps in the same flight;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} a slope of not more than 1.2 to 1; and

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} a hand rail not less than 90 cm and not more than
  1.1 m above the stair level

  - [(i)]{.lawlabel} on at least one side, and on every open side, if
    the stairs are not more than 1.12 m wide, or

  - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} on both sides, if the stairs are more than 1.12 m
    wide.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Ramps

**[[100]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-100 .sectionLabel}** Every employer must
ensure that every ramp installed at a workplace under its control is

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} securely fastened in place;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} braced if necessary to ensure its stability; and

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} equipped with cleats or surfaced in a manner that
  provides a safe footing for users.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Fixed ladders

- **[[101]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-101
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Every employer must ensure that any
  fixed ladder installed at a workplace under its control, other than
  one installed as part of a scaffold,

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} is installed with its underside angled not less
    than 75 degrees and not more than 90 degrees from the ground;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} is securely held in place at the top and bottom and
    at intervals of not more than 3 m;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} has rungs that are at least 15 cm from the wall, at
    uniformly spaced intervals of not more than 30 cm, and do not impede
    any employee from safely exiting the ladder onto a platform or
    landing;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} has side rails that extend not less than 90 cm
    above each landing or platform;

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} is not coated with material that may hide flaws
    affecting its integrity;

  - [(f)]{.lawlabel} if it is more than 6 m in length, is fitted, if
    feasible, with a protective cage for the portion of its length that
    is more than 2.5 m above its bottom; and

  - [(g)]{.lawlabel} if it is more than 9 m in length, is equipped with
    landings or platforms, at intervals of no more than 6 m, that are

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} at least 0.36 m^2^ in area, and

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} fitted at their outer edges with a guard-rail
      and toe board.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Use

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} While ascending or descending a fixed ladder at a
  workplace, every employee must

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} face the ladder;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} maintain a three-point contact with the ladder; and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} carry any tools, equipment or materials in a pouch
    or holster or in another secure manner.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Prohibition

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} An employee must not use a metal or wire-bound fixed
  ladder if there is a risk of it coming into contact with an energized
  electrical conductor or circuit part or with energized electrical
  equipment.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Portable ladders

- **[[102]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-102
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Every employer must ensure that any
  portable ladder used at a workplace under its control

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} conforms to CSA Group standard Z11, Portable
    ladders;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} has a minimum load rating of 113.4 kg;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} if used, other than as part of a scaffold system,
    on a marine installation or structure that is used for the drilling
    for or production of petroleum products, is made of a
    non-combustible material; and

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} is not coated with material that may hide flaws
    affecting its integrity.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Use

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} An employee who uses a portable ladder at a workplace
  must do so in accordance with CSA Group standard Z11, Portable
  ladders, and must ensure that, while the ladder is in use,

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} it is placed on a firm and stable footing and
    positioned so that it is not necessary to use the underside;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} it is secured in such a manner that it cannot be
    dislodged accidentally from its position; and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} unless it is a self-supporting ladder, the upper
    portion of its side rails rests on a bearing surface capable of
    safely withstanding the load imposed on it.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Prohibitions

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} An employee must not

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} position a portable ladder in an elevator shaft or
    hoistway when that space is being used for hoisting;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} position a portable ladder near moving equipment
    that is under a load;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} use a portable ladder in a manner that may
    compromise its stability or the stability of any person on it; or

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} use a metal or wire-bound portable ladder if there
    is a risk of it coming into contact with an energized electrical
    conductor or circuit part or with energized electrical equipment.

## [PART 21]{.HLabel1}[Scaffolding and Platforms]{.HTitleText1} {#h-1330373 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Definition of [[elevating work
platform]{.dfn}]{.DefinitionRef}

**[[103]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-103 .sectionLabel}** In this Part,
[[elevating work platform]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means a type of integral
chassis aerial platform that has an adjustable position platform that is
supported from ground level by an articulating or telescoping boom or by
a vertically oriented, telescoping or elevating mast.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Use --- general

- **[[104]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-104
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Every employer must ensure that no
  employee uses a scaffold, suspended work platform or elevating work
  platform at a workplace under the employer's control unless

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the employer has authorized its use;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the employee has been trained and instructed in its
    safe and proper use; and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} it has been inspected and certified by a competent
    person as being fit for the use to which the employee intends to put
    it.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Hazardous conditions

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The employer must ensure that no employee uses a
  scaffold, suspended work platform or elevating work platform in
  environmental conditions that are likely to increase the risk to the
  health or safety of the employee unless its use in those conditions is
  necessary to remove a hazard or rescue a person.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Prevention of contact

**[[105]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-105 .sectionLabel}** The employer must
ensure that, if there is a risk of a person or equipment coming into
contact with a scaffold, suspended work platform or elevating work
platform in a manner that would pose a hazard, a barricade is installed
or, if that is not feasible, another means of preventing the contact is
provided.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Scaffolds

- **[[106]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-106
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Every employer must ensure, with
  respect to any scaffold used at a workplace under its control, that

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} its configuration at the workplace is designed by a
    competent person;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} it is erected, used, inspected, dismantled and
    stored in accordance with CSA Group standard CAN/CSA-Z797, Code of
    practice for access scaffold;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} if used to support a temporary floor or subjected
    to loads that could cause it to overturn, it is erected and used in
    accordance with written instructions approved by a professional
    engineer;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} either it is capable of supporting at least four
    times the load that is likely to be imposed on it or it has been
    approved by a professional engineer and consists of components that
    have been manufactured in accordance with a quality management
    system;

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} its footings and supports are capable of supporting
    all static and dynamic loads that are likely to be imposed on them;

  - [(f)]{.lawlabel} to the extent feasible, it uses only manufactured
    platforms;

  - [(g)]{.lawlabel} its platforms or planks are adequately secured and
    installed in a manner that avoids gaps and overlapping;

  - [(h)]{.lawlabel} all of its wooden components are treated with a
    transparent fire retardant coating and are stored and maintained so
    that their integrity and fire retardant properties are preserved;

  - [(i)]{.lawlabel} all of its components are compatible with each
    other;

  - [(j)]{.lawlabel} if it is a continuous run scaffold or a double-pole
    tube and coupler scaffold, it has internal horizontal cross-bracing
    installed in the bay immediately adjacent to and at the level of a
    building tie, unless equivalent bracing is achieved using
    manufactured scaffold planks secured by end hooks to provide a fully
    decked work platform at that level; and

  - [(k)]{.lawlabel} any vertical ladder more than 9 m in length that is
    used with it has a landing or platform at least every 6 m.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Ladder jack scaffold

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Every employer must ensure that no ladder jack
  scaffold is used at a workplace under its control.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Elevating work platforms

**[[107]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-107 .sectionLabel}** Every employer must
ensure, with respect to any elevating work platform at a workplace under
its control, that

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} its rated capacity is marked on it in a location that
  is clearly visible to any person using it;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} it is equipped with controls of a continuous pressure
  type that return to the neutral or stop position when released;

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} it is equipped with an emergency stop device that is
  red in colour and located within easy reach of the person operating
  it;

- [(d)]{.lawlabel} if its lifting mechanism creates a shear hazard to
  employees, that mechanism is adequately guarded or identified with
  signs, decals or similar markings warning of the hazard; and

- [(e)]{.lawlabel} if it is self-propelled or mobile, it is used only
  with the approval of the Chief Safety Officer.

## [PART 22]{.HLabel1}[Fall Protection and Rope Access]{.HTitleText1} {#h-1330409 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Risk of falling

**[[108]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-108 .sectionLabel}** The risk of a person
falling from any of the following locations is a prescribed risk for the
purpose of paragraph 205.02(2)(a) of the Act:

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} 3 m or more above the nearest safe surface or above
  water;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} any distance above a surface or thing that could
  cause injury or illness to the person; or

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} a portable ladder, if

  - [(i)]{.lawlabel} there are high wind or wave conditions,

  - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} the person is performing a task that does not
    permit them to maintain their centre of gravity between the ladder's
    side rails,

  - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} the person is performing a task that is not a
    light duty task, or

  - [(iv)]{.lawlabel} the person is performing a task for more than a
    short duration at any one location.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Means of protection

- **[[109]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-109
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Every employer must ensure that
  whichever of the following means of fall protection is most
  appropriate in the circumstances is provided whenever a person at a
  workplace under its control is in a location referred to in section
  108:

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} a guard-rail;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} temporary flooring;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} a travel restraint system that conforms to and has
    been selected in accordance with --- and every component of which
    conforms to and has been selected in accordance with --- all
    applicable CSA Group standards in the Z259 series;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} a fall-arrest system that conforms to and has been
    selected in accordance with --- and every component of which
    conforms to and has been selected in accordance with --- all
    applicable CSA Group standards in the Z259 series; or

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} a safety net that, unless it is to be used in or
    around the helicopter landing deck area of a marine installation or
    structure, conforms to ANSI/American Society of Safety Professionals
    (ASSP) standard A10.11, Safety Requirements for Personnel Nets and
    has been installed, inspected and tested in accordance with that
    standard.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Occupational health and safety program

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Every occupational health and safety program must

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} set out factors, including efficacy and
    feasibility, to be considered by the employer in determining the
    most appropriate means of fall protection for the purpose of
    subsection (1);

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} address the assembly, maintenance, inspection, use
    and disassembly, as the case may be, of all means of fall protection
    provided and their components, including by establishing a schedule
    for their inspection; and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} if fall-arrest systems are to be provided at the
    workplace, address the risks associated with the potential for swing
    as a result of anchorage placement when a fall-arrest system is
    being used.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Fall-arrest system required

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} Despite subsection (1) and paragraph (2)(a), the
  employer must ensure that a fall-arrest system described in paragraph
  (1)(d) is provided to every person

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} who is on a fixed ladder more than 6 m in length;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} who is on an [[elevating work
    platform]{.dfn}]{.DefinitionRef}, as defined in section 103; or

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} who uses a work-positioning system.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Use

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} The employer must ensure that any means of protection
  referred to in paragraphs (1)(c) to (e) that it provides is used in
  accordance with the standards referred to in those paragraphs and, in
  the case of a fall-arrest system provided to a person referred to in
  paragraph (3)(b), is secured to an anchorage point that is approved by
  the platform's manufacturer or a professional engineer.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Safety net

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} The employer must ensure that any safety net provided

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} is positioned as close as feasible to, and in any
    case no further than 4.6 m below, the area from which there is a
    risk of a fall;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} extends at least 2.4 m beyond that area on all
    sides or, if the area is a gangway, at least 1.8 m beyond both
    sides;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} is positioned and maintained so that its maximum
    deflection does not permit any portion of a person who falls into it
    to come into contact with any other surface;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} is kept free of debris, obstructions or intervening
    objects that could be struck by a person falling into it; and

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} is, if connected to another safety net, connected
    using splice joints that are at least as strong as the weakest of
    the nets.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Components

  [(6)]{.lawlabel} The employer must ensure that

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} all anchorages used in a means of fall protection
    are capable of withstanding the following forces in any direction in
    which the force may be applied:

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} 22 kN, in the case of non-engineered anchorages,
      and

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} twice the maximum arresting force anticipated,
      in the case of engineered anchorages;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} any self-retracting device used in a means of fall
    protection is

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} anchored above the user's head, unless the
      manufacturer's instructions allow for a different anchorage
      location, and

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} used in a manner that

      - [(A)]{.lawlabel} minimizes the hazards of swinging, and

      - [(B)]{.lawlabel} limits the distance that a user who fell would
        drop to less than 1.2 m; and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} any lanyard used in a fall-arrest system is
    equipped with an energy absorber, unless

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} the lifeline used is self-retracting, or

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} the fall-arrest system is designed by a
      competent person to

      - [(A)]{.lawlabel} limit the free fall to less than 1.2 m and the
        arresting force to less than 4 kN, and

      - [(B)]{.lawlabel} prevent the user from coming into contact with
        any unsafe surface.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Definition of [[rope
  access]{.dfn}]{.DefinitionRef}

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} In this section, [[rope access]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Work permit

**[[111]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-111 .sectionLabel}** A work permit is
required for any activity at a workplace that requires the use of a
fall-arrest system or travel restraint system.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Instruction and training

- **[[112]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-112 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  instruction and training that every employer must provide to its
  employees and other individuals at a workplace under its control who
  are involved in activities requiring the use of a fall-arrest system
  or travel restraint system includes

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} an overview of the provisions of all applicable
    health and safety legislation and standards that relate to fall
    protection, including those relating to the roles and
    responsibilities of workplace parties;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} training on the identification of fall hazards;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} an overview of the hierarchy of controls that may
    be used to minimize the risk of falling and of injury from a fall;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} training on the different means of fall protection
    and the most suitable application of each;

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} instruction on selecting all relevant components of
    the fall-arrest system or travel restraint system, including
    connecting hardware;

  - [(f)]{.lawlabel} instruction on assessing and selecting specific
    anchors for use with the fall-arrest system or travel restraint
    system;

  - [(g)]{.lawlabel} training on the effects on the human body of a fall
    --- including free fall and swing fall --- and fall-arrest, which
    must address maximum arresting force and the purpose of energy
    absorbers;

  - [(h)]{.lawlabel} instruction and training on the use, storage,
    maintenance and inspection --- including pre-use inspection --- of
    fall-arrest systems, travel restraint systems and their components,
    including practice in inspecting, fitting, adjusting and connecting
    the systems and components; and

  - [(i)]{.lawlabel} instruction and training on emergency response
    procedures to be used if a fall occurs, including practice in
    carrying them out.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Timing

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The instruction and training must be provided

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} before the work that requires the use of the
    fall-arrest system or travel restraint system begins; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} at least once every three years.

## [PART 23]{.HLabel1}[Falling Objects]{.HTitleText1} {#h-1330499 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Risk of injury

- **[[113]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-113 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  risk of injury from falling objects and material at the workplace,
  whether they are falling over or from a height, is a prescribed risk
  for the purpose of paragraph 205.02(2)(a) of the Act.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Toe board or panel

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Subject to subsection (3), every employer must
  ensure, at each workplace under its control, that wherever there is a
  risk of objects or material falling from a raised work area onto a
  person below, a toe board or other solid or mesh panel that extends
  from the floor of the raised area to a sufficient height to prevent
  the objects or material from falling from the raised area is
  installed.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Alternative measures

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} If the installation of a toe board or panel is not
  feasible, the employer must ensure that

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the objects or material are fastened to something
    in a manner that would, if they were to fall, prevent them from
    reaching a person below;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} a safety net is positioned below the raised area to
    catch the objects or material; or

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} the area below and adjacent to the raised area is
    barricaded so that no person may enter it while work is underway.

## [PART 24]{.HLabel1}[Materials Handling]{.HTitleText1} {#h-1330510 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Definitions

**[[114]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-114 .sectionLabel}** The following
definitions apply in this Part.

[[personnel transfer]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[personnel transfer]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means the transfer by
    crane of persons between a vessel and marine installation or
    structure, between vessels or between marine installations or
    structures. ([transfert du personnel]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

[[signaller]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[signaller]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means a person who directs, by
    means of visual or auditory signals, the safe movement and operation
    of materials handling equipment. ([signaleur]{.DefinedTermLink
    lang="fr"})

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Lifting risks

- **[[115]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-115 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  risks associated with the use of materials handling equipment,
  including to lift persons or things, are prescribed risks for the
  purpose of paragraph 205.02(2)(a) of the Act and the occupational
  health and safety program in respect of a workplace at which materials
  handling equipment is to be used for lifting must

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} identify the types of lifts that are expected to be
    performed at the workplace;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} set out criteria for classifying lifts by risk
    level, including criteria relating to the type of lift, its
    complexity, its physical elements, the expertise of those involved
    in carrying it out and the environmental conditions in which it is
    carried out;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} set out procedures for the preparation and
    performance of lifts by type and risk level, including

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} communication requirements among all persons
      involved in the lifts,

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} in the case of lifts of persons, the required
      use of personal protective equipment by the person being lifted,
      and

    - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} in the case of lifts of persons over the water,
      the availability of fast rescue boats;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} identify any operational limits on lifting
    operations and any environmental conditions, such as wind, sea state
    and temperature, that may affect those operations, including by
    reducing the load that the materials handling equipment is able to
    safely handle or support;

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} set out procedures for the maintenance, inspection,
    testing, repair and replacement of lifting equipment, fixed pad eyes
    and loose lifting gear; and

  - [(f)]{.lawlabel} set out procedures for communicating the provisions
    of the program that relate to lifting operations to all persons who
    are involved in carrying them out and all other persons in the
    vicinity who may be affected by them.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Personnel transfer risk

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} For the purpose of paragraph (1)(b), a personnel
  transfer must not be classified as a low risk lift.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Work permit

**[[116]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-116 .sectionLabel}** A work permit is
required for all lifts carried out at a workplace using materials
handling equipment, except those classified under the occupational
health and safety program as low risk.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Prohibitions

**[[117]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-117 .sectionLabel}** It is prohibited for

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} any employee to use or attempt to use materials
  handling equipment at a workplace if they have reason to doubt they
  can do so safely; or

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} any signaller to direct any movement of materials
  handling equipment that would pose a risk to the health or safety of
  any person.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Hazardous conditions

**[[118]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-118 .sectionLabel}** The employer must
ensure that no person uses materials handling equipment at a workplace
under its control in conditions in which that use presents a risk to the
health or safety of any person unless necessary to prevent a greater
risk to the health or safety of any person.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Manual handling

**[[119]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-119 .sectionLabel}** Every employer must
ensure that, if the manual handling of any thing may be hazardous to the
health or safety of an employee, including because of its weight, size,
shape or toxicity, that thing is, to the extent feasible, handled only
using materials handling equipment.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Rated capacity

- **[[120]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-120
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Every employer must ensure that a
  competent person who is independent of the operator and employer
  inspects and proof tests all materials handling equipment that is to
  be used at a workplace under the employer's control if

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the equipment is to be used at the workplace for
    the first time;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} repairs or modifications have been made to the
    equipment's load-carrying components;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} the equipment has been in contact with an electric
    arc or current; or

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} there is any other reason to doubt that the rated
    capacity of the equipment that was most recently certified under
    subsection (2) or the limitations that were most recently indicated
    under that subsection continue to be accurate, including as a result
    of damage sustained by the equipment or modifications made to it.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Certification

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The employer must ensure that the competent person,
  on the basis of the inspection and proof test, certifies in writing
  the rated capacity of the equipment and indicates in writing any
  limitations that must be imposed on its use having regard to
  environmental conditions.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Materials handling equipment

- **[[121]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-121
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Every employer must ensure, with
  respect to all materials handling equipment used at a workplace under
  its control, that

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the equipment is, to the extent feasible,

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} designed and constructed to prevent the failure
      of any of its parts, and

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} equipped with safety devices that will ensure
      that any such failure does not result in a loss of control of the
      equipment or its load or in any other hazardous situation;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the equipment is marked in a manner that identifies
    its manufacturer and model;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} the equipment's rated capacity --- or, in the case
    of equipment that can be operated in a range of positions or
    configurations, the greatest of its rated capacities --- is clearly
    marked on a permanent part of it, in a position where the mark can
    be easily read;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} if the equipment can be operated in a range of
    positions or configurations, a chart indicating the rated capacities
    across that range is posted within view of the person operating it;

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} the equipment's use in existing environmental
    conditions, including wind, sea state and temperature, is
    continually assessed by the person operating it, having regard to
    the limitations indicated under subsection 120(2), to determine
    whether those conditions have reduced the load that the equipment is
    able to safely handle or support to below its rated capacity and, if
    so, the extent to which the load has been reduced;

  - [(f)]{.lawlabel} the equipment is operated in accordance with its
    rated capacity or, if applicable, its reduced capacity as determined
    under paragraph (e);

  - [(g)]{.lawlabel} any braking, steering and other control systems
    with which the equipment is equipped can safely control and stop the
    load's movement;

  - [(h)]{.lawlabel} if the equipment is used for lifting, moving or
    positioning persons, it is equipped with at least two independent
    braking systems and a fail-safe control system;

  - [(i)]{.lawlabel} if the equipment is used for lifting, moving or
    positioning persons, it has --- before its first use and after any
    repair or modification --- been certified by a competent person who
    is independent of the operator and employer as being safe for that
    use, including in conjunction with any other equipment or device
    with which it is used;

  - [(j)]{.lawlabel} if the equipment is powered, it is equipped with

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} an audible signalling device within easy reach of
      the person operating the equipment, and

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} an emergency stopping device that, when engaged,
      will immediately shut down and isolate the equipment and that is
      within easy reach of the person operating it as well as at any
      other location from which it is reasonably foreseeable that a
      person may need to stop the equipment;

  - [(k)]{.lawlabel} if operation of the equipment could result in a
    fire, it is equipped, in a location that is readily accessible to
    the person operating it, with firefighting equipment that is
    appropriate to all fire hazards that may arise;

  - [(l)]{.lawlabel} the equipment is designed and constructed, to the
    extent feasible, so that any vibration, jolting or other uneven
    movement of it will not cause injury to any person or impair the
    ability of the person operating the equipment to control it;

  - [(m)]{.lawlabel} any glass in the equipment's doors, windows and
    other parts is of a type that will not shatter into dangerous pieces
    on impact;

  - [(n)]{.lawlabel} if the equipment is regularly used outdoors and has
    an operator's compartment or position that would expose the person
    operating it to an environmental condition that could be hazardous
    to their health or safety, the compartment or position is fitted
    with a roof or other structure that will protect the person from the
    environmental condition and is constructed from non-combustible or
    fire-resistant material;

  - [(o)]{.lawlabel} any hook with which the equipment is used or
    equipped has

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} if it is used for lifting persons, a
      spring-loaded latch that is locked and pinned in the closed
      position before use to prevent the connecting shackle from
      dislodging from the hook, or

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} in any other case, a spring-loaded latch or
      other equally effective means of preventing the load from falling
      off the hook;

  - [(p)]{.lawlabel} any self-locking eye hooks with triggers with which
    the equipment is used or equipped are designed to prevent the
    trigger from being accidentally activated;

  - [(q)]{.lawlabel} if there is a risk that the person operating the
    equipment or a person being lifted, moved or positioned by it could
    be struck by an intruding, falling or flying object or a shifting
    load, it is equipped, if feasible, with a structure that is
    constructed from non-combustible or fire-resistant material and that
    will, under all foreseeable conditions, protect the person from that
    risk;

  - [(r)]{.lawlabel} any place on the equipment to which an employee
    requires regular access, including any operator's compartment or
    position, is equipped with a safe means of access and egress that

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} does not require the employee to jump,

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} would permit the emergency evacuation and rescue
      of the employee, and

    - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} can accommodate the employee's body dimensions
      while they are wearing personal protective equipment;

  - [(s)]{.lawlabel} any item used in any operator's compartment or at
    any operator's position on the equipment provides an adequate range
    of adjustability to accommodate the person using it;

  - [(t)]{.lawlabel} the placement and design of any displays and
    controls on the equipment do not hinder or prevent the person
    operating the equipment from doing so safely;

  - [(u)]{.lawlabel} any pendant control with which the equipment is
    equipped is not hung or supported solely by its electrical wiring;

  - [(v)]{.lawlabel} any wire rope drum or sheave with which the
    equipment is equipped has a spooling device or other device to
    maintain the wire rope in the groove;

  - [(w)]{.lawlabel} all loads handled by the equipment are secured as
    necessary to prevent them from sliding or falling in a manner that
    would present a risk to the health or safety of any person;

  - [(x)]{.lawlabel} if the equipment is operated remotely, it is
    operated at a safe distance from the load being lifted;

  - [(y)]{.lawlabel} all tools, tool boxes, spare parts or other items
    carried on the equipment are securely stored;

  - [(z)]{.lawlabel} the equipment is not left unattended unless
    adequate measures have been taken to prevent it from moving;

  - [(z.1)]{.lawlabel} if the person operating the equipment does not
    have a clear and unobstructed view of the load and the area in which
    it is being operated, including the area through which the load is
    being moved, that person is directed by a competent person
    designated by the employer as a signaller who

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} is clearly recognizable as a signaller,

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} has a continuous view of the person operating
      the equipment and remains in that person's line of sight,

    - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} has a clear and unobstructed view of the load
      and the area in which the equipment is being operated, including
      the area through which the load is being moved, or, if that is not
      feasible, a continuous view of another signaller who has a clear
      and unobstructed view of, as the case may be, the load or the
      portions of the area that are not within the first signaller's
      view, and

    - [(iv)]{.lawlabel} has no duties other than signalling while the
      equipment under their direction is in motion;

  - [(z.2)]{.lawlabel} any fuel tank, compressed gas cylinder or similar
    container containing a hazardous substance mounted on the equipment
    is

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} located or protected so that it is not hazardous
      to the health or safety of any employee who operates or rides on
      the equipment,

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} connected to fuel overflow and vent pipes that
      are located to ensure that fuel spills and vapours

      - [(A)]{.lawlabel} cannot be ignited by hot exhaust pipes or other
        hot or sparking parts, and

      - [(B)]{.lawlabel} are not hazardous to the health or safety of
        any employee who operates or rides on the equipment, and

    - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} marked on its caps or covers as to its
      contents; and

  - [(z.3)]{.lawlabel} the equipment is not used in an area in which it
    may come into contact with an electrical cable, pipe or other supply
    line, structure or other thing that could, if struck, constitute a
    hazard to the health or safety of persons unless the person
    operating the equipment and, if applicable, the signaller have been
    informed of

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} the hazard and its location, and

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} the distance that must be maintained to avoid
      accidental contact with the thing that constitutes the hazard.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Exception to rated or reduced capacity

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Despite paragraph (1)(f), the materials handling
  equipment may be used to handle a load in excess of its rated capacity
  or reduced capacity for the purposes of testing and inspection.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Protection against contact

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} If the employer is unable to determine with
  reasonable certainty the location of the hazard referred to in
  paragraph (1)(z.3) or the distance referred to in subparagraph
  (1)(z.3)(ii), or if it is necessary for the materials handling
  equipment to be used in closer proximity than that distance, the
  materials handling equipment may be used in the area only if

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} every electrical cable with which there is a risk
    of coming into contact is de-energized;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} every pipe or other supply line containing a
    hazardous substance with which there is a risk of coming into
    contact has been shut down and drained; and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} every other thing that could, if struck, pose a
    hazard is protected against impact from the equipment.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Cranes and hoists

- **[[122]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-122
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Every employer must ensure, with
  respect to each lift by a crane or hoist that is carried out at a
  workplace under its control, that

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} a competent person inspects the load before the
    lift to ensure that it is adequately secured by means of appropriate
    loose lifting gear;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} tag lines or similar devices are used to control
    the load's swing unless their use poses a hazard to the safety of
    any person;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} the person operating the crane or hoist does not
    leave the load suspended from the equipment while they are not at
    the controls;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} the load is safely landed and stabilized before
    being detached; and

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} only persons who are essential to the conduct,
    supervision or safety of the lift are, while it is in progress, in
    the area in which it is being carried out.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Area marked and secured

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} For the purpose of paragraph (1)(e), the employer
  must ensure that all approaches to the area in which the lift is being
  carried out are posted with universally recognized warning signs
  prohibiting access by unauthorized persons and are secured to prevent
  inadvertent access.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Presence of non-essential persons

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} A person who operates a crane or hoist must not start
  a lift if a non-essential person is in the area in which the lift is
  being carried out. If such a person enters that area while a lift is
  in progress, the person operating the crane or hoist must immediately
  take measures to mitigate the risk to all persons, discontinue the
  lift as soon as it is safe to do so and not resume the lift until the
  area is cleared of non-essential persons.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Crane near helicopter deck

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} Every employer must ensure that, when a helicopter is
  landing or taking off, any crane at a workplace under its control that
  could pose a physical or visual hazard to the helicopter or its crew
  remains stationary and, if feasible, has its boom stowed.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Offshore pedestal crane

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} Every employer must ensure that every offshore
  pedestal crane used at a workplace under its control

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} is equipped with

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} appropriate travel limiting devices for its boom,
      hoist, blocks and slewing mechanism,

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} a device for measuring and indicating the weight
      of its load,

    - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} a device for measuring and indicating its boom
      extension or load radius, if its rated capacity varies on that
      basis,

    - [(iv)]{.lawlabel} a device for accessing anemometer readings, if
      the load that it is able to safely handle or support is
      susceptible to being reduced by wind, and

    - [(v)]{.lawlabel} a gross overload protection system, if it is used
      to move persons or things to or from a supply vessel; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} has posted conspicuously and, if feasible, inside
    the crane control cab, the location of all approved laydown areas
    and restricted areas, the limitations indicated under subsection
    120(2) and the chart referred to in paragraph 121(1)(d).

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Manually operated hoist

  [(6)]{.lawlabel} Every employer must ensure, with respect to any
  manually operated hoist used at a workplace under its control, that

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} it is equipped with a mechanism to hold the load at
    the desired height; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} if it is equipped with a crank handle and not
    equipped with automatic load brakes,

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} it is equipped with a means of preventing the
      crank handle from slipping off the crank shaft while in use, and

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} no load is lowered on it unless the crank handle
      has been removed from the shaft or the hoist has otherwise been
      designed to eliminate the risk of a person being struck by the
      handle.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Wire rope clips

**[[123]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-123 .sectionLabel}** Every employer must
ensure that any wire rope clips used at a workplace under its control
are

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} of sufficient strength to withstand the full weight

  and

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Mobile equipment

  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Every employer must ensure, with

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} any load that is transported by it while raised or

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Blind corners

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Forklift load

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} no part of a load that is transported on a forklift

    carriage, back rest or back rest extension of the forklift.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Personnel transfer

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the loading and landing areas are clear of all

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the personnel transfer device is raised and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} there is continuous communication between both

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} a fast rescue boat and crew are available nearby

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} each person assigned to plan, manage, participate

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} made of corrosion-resistant material suitable

  system that conforms to the Airworthiness Manual published by the
  Department of Transport or a properly fitted immersion suit that
  conforms to paragraph 46(b).

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Signalling

- **[[127]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-127
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Every employer must ensure, before
  any materials handling equipment is used at a workplace under its
  control, that

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} all persons at the workplace know the hand signal
    for "emergency stop"; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} if the equipment is to be used in circumstances
    that will require the use of a signaller,

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} a code of hand signals is established, and

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} every signaller and every person operating the
      equipment is instructed in the use of that code.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Emergency stop signal

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Every person who operates materials handling
  equipment at a workplace must obey the signal for "emergency stop"
  given by any person.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Other means of communication

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} If it is not feasible for a signaller to use hand
  signals to communicate to a person operating materials handling
  equipment, including due to the distance between them, the employer
  must ensure that

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the signaller and the person operating the
    equipment are provided with a primary and backup telephone, radio or
    other device that provides the signaller with a continuous means of
    communicating with the person operating the equipment while the
    equipment is in use;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} any radio codes used by the signaller to
    communicate with the person operating the equipment are included in
    the code referred to in paragraph (1)(b); and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} if the signalling is to be done by means of a
    two-way radio system, it is operated on a dedicated radio channel.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Copy of code

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} The employer must ensure that a copy of the code
  referred to in paragraph (1)(b) is kept readily available for
  examination by all persons at the workplace.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Signaller not understood

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} Any person who does not understand a direction given
  to them by a signaller must consider it to be a direction to stop.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Inspection

- **[[128]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-128 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  competent person who carries out the thorough safety inspection
  referred to in subparagraph 87(1)(e)(ii) in respect of materials
  handling equipment must be independent of the operator and the
  employer.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Increased frequency

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Every employer must ensure, despite subparagraph
  87(1)(e)(ii), that

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the thorough safety inspection referred to in that
    subparagraph is also carried out in respect of materials handling
    equipment

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} before the equipment is used at the workplace for
      the first time,

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} before it is returned to service after repairs
      or modifications have been made to its load carrying components,
      and

    - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} before it is returned to service after being in
      contact with an electric arc or current; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} all loose lifting gear, personnel transfer devices
    and safety devices that are used in conjunction with personnel
    transfer devices are subject to the thorough safety inspection
    referred to in that subparagraph at least once every six months.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Identification system

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} The employer must implement, at every workplace under
  its control, a system that facilitates the identification of materials
  handling equipment that is due for inspection.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Instruction and training

**[[129]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-129 .sectionLabel}** The instruction and
training that every employer must provide to an employee who uses
materials handling equipment in the course of their work includes
instruction and training on the effects of environmental conditions on
the equipment's safe and proper use.

## [PART 25]{.HLabel1}[Confined Spaces]{.HTitleText1} {#h-1330722 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Evaluation

- **[[130]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-130 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  risks to which a person in a confined space is exposed are prescribed
  risks for the purpose of paragraph 205.02(2)(a) of the Act and every
  employer must ensure that, before any work begins at a workplace under
  its control, a competent person evaluates the workplace and makes a
  record of all confined spaces in it.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Re-evaluation

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The employer must ensure that each workplace is
  re-evaluated by a competent person at least once every three years, as
  well as on the creation or elimination of a confined space, and that
  the competent person records any changes in respect of the confined
  spaces at the workplace from the last evaluation.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Identification

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} Every employer must ensure that each confined space
  at a workplace under its control, other than a confined space that has
  been made inaccessible with bolted blind flanges, is visibly
  identified at each point of access as

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} being a confined space;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} being a space to which access is restricted to
    authorized persons; and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} containing a danger.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Occupational health and safety program

**[[131]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-131 .sectionLabel}** Every occupational
health and safety program must, with respect to the various confined
spaces at the workplace and the types of work that could be carried out
in them,

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} identify the personal protective equipment that is to
  be used or worn by employees in the confined space;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} set out measures to be taken to prevent the
  entanglement of lifelines and other equipment used by employees in the
  confined space;

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} identify the atmospheric hazards that may be present
  in the confined space and the equipment to be used for atmospheric
  testing and monitoring in the confined space;

- [(d)]{.lawlabel} address the calibration and testing of the equipment
  referred to in paragraph (c) and the appropriate frequency of
  atmospheric testing;

- [(e)]{.lawlabel} set out a plan for responding to emergencies in the
  confined space that, among other things,

  - [(i)]{.lawlabel} identifies the situations that would trigger
    emergency response procedures,

  - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} identifies the equipment, including personal
    protective equipment, to be used or worn in carrying out those
    procedures,

  - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} sets out procedures for ensuring the immediate
    evacuation of the confined space when an alarm is activated or if
    there is any potentially hazardous change in the atmospheric
    concentration of a hazardous substance, oxygen or another flammable,
    explosive or combustible substance, and

  - [(iv)]{.lawlabel} sets out procedures for retrieving persons from
    the confined space, including alternate procedures that will ensure
    they can be retrieved safely if an obstruction or other condition
    makes the use of certain retrieval procedures or equipment unsafe
    for them or for those carrying out the retrieval;

- [(f)]{.lawlabel} identify the means by which persons inside and
  outside the confined space are to communicate among themselves,
  including in an emergency;

- [(g)]{.lawlabel} identify all resources necessary for ensuring the
  health and safety of employees in the confined space, including by
  setting out the manner of determining the number of attendants whose
  presence is necessary at the confined space;

- [(h)]{.lawlabel} set out the manner in which persons designated under
  paragraph 133(1)(i) are to be notified of the time and location at
  which their assistance might be required; and

- [(i)]{.lawlabel} provide for the regular conduct of emergency response
  drills and exercises.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Work permit

- **[[132]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-132 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A
  work permit is required for any occupation of a confined space at a
  workplace.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Additional contents

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} In addition to the information that is required to be
  set out in a work permit under subsection 53(1), the following
  information must be included in a work permit for the occupation of a
  confined space:

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the method of entering and exiting the confined
    space;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the escape route from the confined space, which, if
    persons are required to use a self-contained breathing apparatus or
    airline respirator, must allow for escape before the air supply is
    expected to be exhausted; and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} a record of all persons signing in and out of the
    confined space.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Validity

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} A work permit for the occupation of a confined space
  ceases to be valid 12 hours after the most recent testing conducted
  under subsection 134(2).

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Posting and updating

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} The employer must ensure that a copy of the work
  permit is posted at every entrance to the confined space for the
  duration of its occupation and is updated as new information referred
  to in paragraph (2)(c) or paragraph 53(1)(i) becomes available.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Entry and occupation requirements

- **[[133]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-133
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Every employer must ensure that no
  person enters or remains in a confined space at a workplace under its
  control unless

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the person is knowledgeable about the provisions of
    the occupational health and safety program that pertain to confined
    spaces;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the person is wearing a full body harness to
    facilitate their retrieval or, if wearing the harness would pose a
    greater risk to them than not wearing one, measures are in place to
    ensure that the person can be retrieved safely in accordance with
    the alternate procedures referred to in subparagraph 131(e)(iv);

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} the opening to be used for entry into and exit from
    the confined space is sufficiently large to allow safe passage of
    persons wearing personal protective equipment;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} any mechanical and electrical equipment that is in
    the confined space but not required to carry out any work there is

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} disconnected from its energy source, and

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} isolated, locked out and tagged in accordance
      with Part 27;

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} all sources of ignition are eliminated, if a
    flammable, explosive or combustible substance is present in the
    confined space;

  - [(f)]{.lawlabel} any liquid in which a person may drown or
    free-flowing solid in which a person may become entrapped has been
    removed from the confined space;

  - [(g)]{.lawlabel} engineering controls are in place to prevent any
    inadvertent discharge from any source, including a pipe or other
    supply line, that may be hazardous to the health or safety of any
    person in the confined space;

  - [(h)]{.lawlabel} measures have been taken to ensure that, if an
    atmospheric hazard arises while the confined space is occupied, the
    confined space will be continuously ventilated;

  - [(i)]{.lawlabel} persons have been designated to respond to any
    emergency that may arise in the confined space and have been
    notified of the time and location at which their assistance may be
    required;

  - [(j)]{.lawlabel} sufficient equipment referred to in paragraph
    131(a) and subparagraph 131(e)(ii) is provided as close as feasible
    to the entrance to the confined space for use by the persons
    referred to in paragraph (i); and

  - [(k)]{.lawlabel} a drill has been completed simulating an emergency
    rescue from the confined space.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Isolation of piping

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The engineering controls referred to in paragraph
  (1)(g) must, with respect to a pipe containing a hazardous substance
  or a substance under pressure or at a high temperature, consist of a
  blank or blind in conjunction with valves or other blocking seals that
  are secured in the closed position --- using a positive mechanical
  device that is designed to resist being opened inadvertently, other
  than as a result of excessive force --- to prevent the substance from
  reaching the blank or blind. The employer must ensure that the pipe is
  clearly marked to indicate the location of the blank or blind and that
  the valves or seals are clearly marked as being closed.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Unauthorized entry

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} The employer must ensure that adequate barriers are
  erected to prevent unauthorized entry to the confined space.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Sign in and out

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} Every employer must ensure that every person entering
  and exiting a confined space signs in and out.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Atmosphere

- **[[134]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-134
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Every employer must ensure, if
  feasible, in respect of every occupied confined space at a workplace
  under its control --- and every area whose atmosphere may be affected
  by, or may affect, the atmosphere in an occupied confined space ---
  that

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} no person's exposure to a hazardous substance in
    the atmosphere exceeds the threshold limit value for that substance,
    as adjusted if necessary to reflect the length of time the person is
    in the confined space or area, or the biological exposure index for
    that substance, without regard to any protection that may be
    afforded to the person through the use of personal protective
    equipment;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the concentration of oxygen in the atmosphere is
    not less than 19.5% and not more than 22.5%; and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} the concentration of any other flammable, explosive
    or combustible substance in the atmosphere is less than 10% of its
    lower explosive limit.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Testing

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The employer must ensure that a competent person
  conducts atmospheric testing, and records the results, at times and
  frequencies appropriate to the hazards in the atmosphere, including

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} before each time the confined space becomes
    occupied, unless the atmosphere in the space was, while the space
    was unoccupied, continuously monitored for any accumulation of
    contaminants that could pose an immediate threat to life or that
    could interfere with a person's ability to escape unaided from the
    space and that monitoring shows no irregularities;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} after any change in the work or to the confined
    space that may affect its atmosphere; and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} in any case, no less frequently than every 12 hours
    while the confined space remains occupied.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Testing from outside

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} The employer must ensure that the competent person
  does not enter the confined space to carry out testing unless they
  have first carried out preliminary testing of its atmosphere from
  outside the space.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Continuous monitoring

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} In addition to the testing required under subsection
  (2), the employer must ensure that the atmosphere in the confined
  space is continuously monitored for any accumulation of contaminants
  that could pose an immediate threat to life or that could interfere
  with a person's ability to escape unaided from the confined space and
  must ensure that all persons in the space are alerted to any such an
  accumulation with sufficient warning to be able to exit the space
  safely.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Lifeline

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} If it is not feasible to comply with subsection (1),
  the employer must ensure that every person in the confined space who
  wears a full body harness in accordance with paragraph 133(1)(b) has
  securely attached to it a lifeline that is secured outside the
  confined space and is monitored and controlled by an attendant, unless
  the risk of using the lifeline would pose a greater risk to the person
  than not using it.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Attendants

- **[[135]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-135
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Every employer must ensure that
  attendants are stationed outside and near all entrances to each
  confined space at a workplace under its control while the space is
  occupied to

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} maintain a record of all persons entering and
    exiting the confined space and communicate that information among
    themselves;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} maintain communication with and monitor the safety
    of persons in the confined space; and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} provide emergency assistance to persons in the
    confined space and summon additional assistance if needed.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Means of communication

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The employer must ensure that attendants are provided
  with a means of communicating continuously with persons in the
  confined space, of communicating with other attendants at the confined
  space and of summoning additional assistance.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}No entry

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} Attendants must not enter the confined space.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}No other duties

  [(4)]{.lawlabel} The employer must ensure that attendants are not
  assigned any duties beyond those referred to in subsection (1) while
  stationed outside a confined space.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Multiple entrances

  [(5)]{.lawlabel} If a single attendant is responsible for monitoring
  more than one entrance to a confined space, the employer must ensure
  that they are stationed in the location that best allows them to
  perform their duties in respect of each of those entrances.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Instruction and training

- **[[136]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-136 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  instruction and training that every employer must provide to employees
  whose work relates to confined spaces at a workplace under its
  control, including employees whose work involves entering, evaluating,
  attending at, supervising persons in or carrying out emergency
  response procedures in relation to a confined space, includes

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} training on the legislation applicable to confined
    spaces, including as it pertains to rights and duties;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} training on the identification of confined spaces;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} training on and practice in the assessment of risks
    associated with confined spaces, including the particular risks of
    carrying out hot work in confined spaces;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} training on the issuance and use of work permits
    for the occupation of confined spaces;

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} an overview of the operation of personal gas
    monitoring devices;

  - [(f)]{.lawlabel} training on atmospheric testing, including practice
    in selecting appropriate testing methods and equipment;

  - [(g)]{.lawlabel} training on methods to safely ventilate or remove
    unwanted substances from confined spaces;

  - [(h)]{.lawlabel} training on the measures required under paragraphs
    133(1)(d) and (g) for isolating energy and substances;

  - [(i)]{.lawlabel} training on methods of emergency response; and

  - [(j)]{.lawlabel} training on and practice in the selection and use
    of appropriate personal protective equipment and rescue equipment in
    a confined space.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Frequency

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The training required under subsection (1) must be
  provided to every employee before the first time they do any work
  relating to confined spaces at the workplace and then at least once
  every three years.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Emergency response

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} The employer must also provide any employee who may
  be required to carry out emergency response procedures in relation to
  a confined space with training and instruction in

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} appropriate emergency response procedures for that
    confined space; and

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} first aid at a level appropriate to the types of
    situations that may arise in that confined space.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Completion of work

**[[137]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-137 .sectionLabel}** Every employer must
ensure that, once work in a confined space at a workplace under its
control is complete, a competent person verifies that all persons have
left the confined space and all tools, equipment and other material not
intended to remain in the confined space have been removed.

## [PART 26]{.HLabel1}[Hot Work]{.HTitleText1} {#h-1330834 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Risks

**[[138]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-138 .sectionLabel}** The risks arising from
hot work are prescribed risks for the purpose of paragraph 205.02(2)(a)
of the Act.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Work permit

- **[[139]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-139 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A
  work permit is required for all hot work carried out at a workplace.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Content --- circumstances

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The circumstances referred to in paragraph 53(1)(e)
  that must be set out in the work permit include

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the location where the hot work is to be carried
    out, in particular, relative to any areas referred to in subsection
    26(2);

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the presence of any flammable, explosive or
    combustible material; and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} the presence of any material that could produce
    toxic or flammable vapours.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Content --- procedures

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} The work procedures referred to in paragraph 53(1)(f)
  that must be set out in the work permit must identify, among other
  things, the tools and equipment to be used in carrying out the hot
  work.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Requirements

- **[[140]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-140
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Every employer must ensure that no
  hot work is carried out at a workplace under its control unless

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the atmosphere in the work area and any adjacent
    area that may be affected by the hot work is continuously monitored
    for flammable, explosive or combustible substances, if there is a
    risk of them being present in those areas, and all persons in those
    areas are alerted if there is a risk of any of those substances
    being present in concentrations exceeding the values referred to in
    paragraphs (b) and (c);

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the atmospheric concentration of oxygen is less
    than 22.5%;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} the atmospheric concentration of any other
    flammable, explosive or combustible substance is less than 5% of its
    lower explosive limit;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} all potential sources of flammable, explosive or
    combustible substances have been identified, isolated and locked
    out;

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} the work area and any adjacent area that may be
    affected by the hot work are free of all materials that could
    produce a toxic, flammable, explosive or combustible vapour when
    heated;

  - [(f)]{.lawlabel} a competent person maintains a fire watch patrol;
    and

  - [(g)]{.lawlabel} firefighting equipment appropriate to all fire
    hazards that may arise is readily accessible.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Welding, cutting and allied processes

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The employer must ensure that welding, cutting and
  allied processes are carried out, to the extent feasible, in
  accordance with the requirements set out in CSA Group standard W117.2,
  Safety in welding, cutting, and allied processes.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Use of gas

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} The employer must ensure that, if gas is used in the
  carrying out of hot work,

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} all hose lines or pipes that convey gases to the
    burner, and all couplings, are clearly identified to ensure they are
    not interchanged;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} only standard fittings are used and those fittings,
    as well as any regulator or automatic reducing valve on the
    equipment being used, are designed for the gas being used;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} safety devices that prevent the reverse flow of
    fuel, gas, oxygen or air from the torch end of the equipment being
    used to the supply lines and that prevent a flame from burning back
    from the torch end into the supply lines are used;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} all gas cylinders, piping and fittings are located
    to prevent them from being damaged or are otherwise protected
    against damage;

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} all regulators and associated flexible connecting
    hoses are tested for leaks, using a substance that is not oil-, fat-
    or grease-based, immediately after being connected to a gas cylinder
    or other gas supply;

  - [(f)]{.lawlabel} while the hot work is being carried out, a person
    is stationed in a location that allows them to immediately cut off
    the gas supply in an emergency;

  - [(g)]{.lawlabel} the gas supply is immediately cut off if a leak is
    detected during the test referred to in paragraph (e) or during the
    hot work and no further work is carried out until the leak has been
    repaired and another test has been carried out to verify the success
    of the repair;

  - [(h)]{.lawlabel} all parts of the equipment being used are free from
    defects, leaks, oil and grease;

  - [(i)]{.lawlabel} the torch is ignited only with a device that is
    designed for that purpose; and

  - [(j)]{.lawlabel} hot metal parts and electrode stubs are disposed of
    or otherwise dealt with when not in use in a manner that dissipates
    heat and minimizes the potential for ignition and fire.

## [PART 27]{.HLabel1}[Hazardous Energy]{.HTitleText1} {#h-1330871 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Definitions

**[[141]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-141 .sectionLabel}** The following
definitions apply in this Part.

[[electrical hazard]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[electrical hazard]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means a danger of electric
    shock, arc flash burn, thermal burn or blast injury resulting from
    contact with electrical equipment or failure of that
    equipment. ([risque associé à l'électricité]{.DefinedTermLink
    lang="fr"})

[[hazardous energy]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[hazardous energy]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means any energy that can
    harm a person. ([énergie dangereuse]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

[[limited approach boundary]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[limited approach boundary]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means

    - [(a)]{.lawlabel} in respect of an exposed energized electrical
      conductor,

      - [(i)]{.lawlabel} if it is part of an alternating current system,
        the distance set out in column 2 of Schedule 1 that corresponds
        to the conductor's voltage in column 1, and

      - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} if it is part of a direct current system, the
        distance set out in column 2 of Schedule 2 that corresponds to
        the conductor's voltage in column 1; and

    - [(b)]{.lawlabel} in respect of an exposed energized circuit part,

      - [(i)]{.lawlabel} if it is part of an alternating current system,
        the distance set out in column 3 of Schedule 1 that corresponds
        to the part's voltage in column 1, and

      - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} if it is part of a direct current system, the
        distance set out in column 3 of Schedule 2 that corresponds to
        the part's voltage in column 1. ([seuil d'approche
        limite]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

[[restricted approach boundary]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[restricted approach boundary]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}, in respect of
    an exposed energized electrical conductor or circuit part, means

    - [(a)]{.lawlabel} if it is part of an alternating current system,
      the distance set out in column 4 of Schedule 1 that corresponds to
      the conductor's or part's voltage in column 1; and

    - [(b)]{.lawlabel} if it is part of a direct current system, the
      distance set out in column 4 of Schedule 2 that corresponds to the
      conductor's or part's voltage in column 1. ([seuil d'approche
      restrictif]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Occupational health and safety program

**[[142]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-142 .sectionLabel}** Exposure to hazardous
energy, including as a result of the unexpected start-up of any
equipment, machine, device or system or as a result of contact with or a
failure of electrical equipment, is a prescribed risk for the purpose of
paragraph 205.02(2)(a) of the Act and every occupational health and
safety program must

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} set out, for each piece of equipment, machine, device
  and system at the workplace that may present such a risk,

  - [(i)]{.lawlabel} the nomenclature by which it is to be identified,
    which must be consistent with any associated design documents,

  - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} detailed procedures for de-energizing it and
    isolating its energy source using an energy-isolating device at all
    possible locations, both local and remote, and

  - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} detailed procedures for verifying and testing
    that the de-energization and isolation are complete;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} set out detailed procedures for securing and removing
  lockout devices and for affixing tags or signs to those devices;

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} set out the method by which persons in the vicinity
  of any equipment, machine, device or system are to be notified of its
  lockout;

- [(d)]{.lawlabel} set out procedures for the orderly transfer of
  control of lockout devices between outgoing and incoming employees
  during shift or personnel changes;

- [(e)]{.lawlabel} set out measures for ensuring that, before any
  equipment, machine, device or system that has been locked out is
  re-energized, all persons are clear of --- and have been instructed to
  remain clear of --- the area in which they would be at risk of
  exposure to hazardous energy;

- [(f)]{.lawlabel} set out procedures for the inspection and testing of
  electrical equipment and circuits, including the selection of
  appropriate testing equipment, having regard to the electrical code to
  which the workplace is designed;

- [(g)]{.lawlabel} set out procedures for maintaining the integrity of
  any electrical equipment's insulation and its enclosure;

- [(h)]{.lawlabel} set out procedures for all work involving hazardous
  energy, including in relation to

  - [(i)]{.lawlabel} the selection of appropriate tools,

  - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} the use of personal protective equipment and other
    protective devices, and

  - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} communication with persons in the vicinity of the

- [(j)]{.lawlabel} identify the arc flash boundary for every piece of

- [(l)]{.lawlabel} set out procedures for carrying out work involving

- [(m)]{.lawlabel} set out procedures for responding to emergencies

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the cylinder's valves are kept closed at all times

  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Every employer must ensure that all

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} are not

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} rolled on their sides,

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} subjected to rough handling, or

    - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} moved using a lifting magnet or sling;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} are protected from

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} exposure to corrosive materials or

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} exposure to excessive heat or fire, and

    - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} falling and impact;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} if they are not equipped with appropriate lifting
    mechanisms, are lifted only while held by a suitable cradle,
    platform or other device;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} are transported in a manner that will prevent
    damage to them and their components, including by

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} being fastened securely in an upright position,

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} having in place a protective cap or other means

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} are stored

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} securely in place, using securing devices capable
      of withstanding a fire,

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} in a well-ventilated storage area where the
      cylinders are not exposed to temperature extremes, in accordance
      with the specifications of the cylinder manufacturer and the
      person who supplied the gas,

    - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} with the cylinders grouped by type of gas and
      the groups arranged having regard to the gases they contain,

    - [(iv)]{.lawlabel} with full and empty cylinders separated,

    - [(v)]{.lawlabel} at a safe distance from all operations that
      produce flames, sparks or molten metal or that would result in
      excessive heating of the cylinder, and

    - [(vi)]{.lawlabel} with all protective devices with which they are
      equipped in place.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Signage

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The employer must ensure that signs are posted in a

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Occupational health and safety program

  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Every employer must ensure, with

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} only competent persons designated by the employer

    to explosives;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} only persons who are directly involved in the

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} detonators are not stored with any other explosive

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} containers in which explosives are stored are

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} constructed and located in a manner that allows
      them to be safely jettisoned in an emergency; and

  - [(f)]{.lawlabel} a competent person maintains and keeps in a readily

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} the competent person's name,

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} the name of the person who stored, removed,

    - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} the date of the storage, removal, use,
      destruction or transfer,

    - [(iv)]{.lawlabel} the type and amount of explosive stored,
      removed, used, misfired, destroyed or transferred, and

    - [(v)]{.lawlabel} particulars of the explosive's use, intended use,
      destruction or transfer.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Retention of register

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The employer must retain the register referred to in

## [PART 31]{.HLabel1}[Hazardous Substances]{.HTitleText1} {#h-1331085 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Definitions

**[[154]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-154 .sectionLabel}** The following
definitions apply in this Part.

[[fugitive emission]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[fugitive emission]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means a hazardous product
    in any form that escapes into the workplace from processing
    equipment, emission control equipment or a product. ([émission
    fugitive]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

[[hazardous waste]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[hazardous waste]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means a hazardous product
    that is intended to be recycled, recovered or disposed of. ([résidu
    dangereux]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

[[product identifier]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[product identifier]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}, in respect of a
    hazardous substance, including a hazardous product, means its brand
    name, chemical name, common name, generic name or trade
    name. ([identificateur de produit]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Occupational health and safety program

- **[[155]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-155
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Exposure to hazardous substances is
  a prescribed risk for the purpose of paragraph 205.02(2)(a) of the Act
  and the associated control measures set out in the occupational health
  and safety program must be commensurate to the risks associated with
  each hazardous substance present at the workplace.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Contents

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Every occupational health and safety program must set
  out procedures for

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} managing the introduction of new hazardous
    substances into the workplace;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} identifying and substituting, to the extent
    feasible, non-hazardous or less hazardous substances for more
    hazardous substances used at the workplace;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} ensuring that all safety data sheets and other
    documents containing hazard information with respect to hazardous
    substances at the workplace are kept up to date;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} developing and implementing any medical monitoring
    program that may be required under paragraph 156(1)(c);

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} identifying and implementing means, potentially
    including protective reassignment, of eliminating all workplace
    exposure to a [[respiratory sensitizer]{.dfn}]{.DefinitionRef} or
    [[skin sensitizer]{.dfn}]{.DefinitionRef}, as those terms are
    defined in section 8.4 of the [Hazardous Products
    Regulations](/eng/regulations/SOR-2015-17), for any employee who is
    or is likely to be sensitized to that substance;

  - [(f)]{.lawlabel} informing employees of the hazards posed by any
    substance that is a [[germ cell mutagen]{.dfn}]{.DefinitionRef}, as
    defined in section 8.5 of the [Hazardous Products
    Regulations](/eng/regulations/SOR-2015-17), or [[toxic to
    reproduction]{.dfn}]{.DefinitionRef}, as defined in section 8.7 of
    those Regulations, to which they may be exposed at the workplace;
    and

  - [(g)]{.lawlabel} determining levels of safe exposure to the
    substances referred to in paragraph (f) for persons who are pregnant
    or breastfeeding or intend to conceive a child and identifying and
    implementing means, potentially including protective reassignment,
    of ensuring that those levels are not exceeded in respect of any
    employee who has advised the employer that they are such a person.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Investigation and assessment

- **[[156]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-156 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  employer must, for the purpose of investigating and assessing
  potential exposure to hazardous substances under paragraph 205.022(f)
  of the Act, before the work that gives rise to the potential exposure
  begins,

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} obtain from a competent person designated by it, in
    consultation with the workplace committee or coordinator, as the
    case may be, a signed written report that

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} addresses the following factors in respect of
      each hazardous substance to which employees may be exposed:

      - [(A)]{.lawlabel} the substance's chemical, biological and
        physical properties,

      - [(B)]{.lawlabel} the routes of exposure to the substance,

      - [(C)]{.lawlabel} the acute and chronic effects on health of
        exposure to the substance,

      - [(D)]{.lawlabel} the manner in which the substance is produced,
        stored, used, handled and disposed of at the workplace,

      - [(E)]{.lawlabel} the control methods used to eliminate or reduce
        the employees' exposure to the substance, and

      - [(F)]{.lawlabel} the quantity, concentration or level of
        substance to which the employees may be exposed, including, in
        the case of an airborne chemical agent, whether that
        concentration is likely to exceed 50% of the threshold limit
        value for that agent referred to in paragraph 157(1)(a), and

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} sets out the competent person's recommendations
      regarding compliance with the provisions of the Act and these
      Regulations respecting hazardous substances, including
      recommendations in respect of sampling, testing and medical
      examinations of employees;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} if the report referred to in paragraph (a)
    recommends the medical examination of employees,

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} obtain from a physician with specialized
      knowledge of the hazardous substance to which the employees may be
      exposed a written opinion, to be retained with the report, as to
      whether the medical examination is necessary, and

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} if it is confirmed that the examination is
      necessary, obtain the results of a medical examination of each
      employee, carried out by a physician acceptable to that employee
      and at the employer's expense, indicating whether the employee is
      fit to be exposed to the substance and, if so, any restrictions
      that ought to be imposed on their exposure; and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} assess whether and to what extent medical
    monitoring of employees is necessary and, if the employer determines
    that it is necessary --- or if requested by the Board under
    paragraph 205.066(1)(f) of the Act --- implement a program for the
    medical monitoring of the employees.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Combined effect

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} If two or more hazardous substances have a similar
  toxicological effect on the same target organ or system, their
  combined effect must be considered for the purpose of the
  investigation and assessment, using the additive mixture formula set
  out in the American Conference of Governmental Industrial Hygienists
  publication TLVs and BEIs: Based on the Documentation of the Threshold
  Limit Values for Chemical Substances and Physical Agents & Biological
  Exposure Indices.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Testing methodology

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} If it is likely that the concentration of an airborne
  chemical agent referred to in clause (1)(a)(i)(F) exceeds the
  threshold limit value for that agent referred to in paragraph
  157(1)(a), the concentration must be determined using a test that
  conforms to the United States National Institute for Occupational
  Safety and Health's NIOSH Manual of Analytical Methods, if such a test
  exists for that agent.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Employer obligations

- **[[157]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-157
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Every employer must ensure, in
  respect of each workplace under its control, that

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} no employee's exposure to a hazardous substance
    exceeds the threshold limit value for that substance, as adjusted if
    necessary to reflect the length of the employee's work period, or
    the biological exposure index for that substance;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} automated detection and warning systems are in
    place, if feasible, to alert employees of any potential exposure to
    a hazardous substance;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} hazardous substances are stored

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} in an area, designated by the employer for that
      purpose, that is

      - [(A)]{.lawlabel} designed and constructed to provide for the
        safe containment and protection of its contents,

      - [(B)]{.lawlabel} clearly identified by appropriate signage,

      - [(C)]{.lawlabel} designed and maintained --- including through
        the provision of adequate ventilation and lighting --- to allow
        for its safe occupancy and the safe movement of employees,
        equipment and material, and

      - [(D)]{.lawlabel} designed and equipped to permit effective
        emergency response having regard to the nature of each substance
        being stored, including, if any of the substances is flammable
        or combustible, by being equipped with a suitable fire
        suppression system,

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} in containers that are designed and constructed
      to protect persons from the substances' hazardous effects, and

    - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} in a manner that ensures that

      - [(A)]{.lawlabel} the substances and their containers cannot
        readily fall, become dislodged, suffer damage or be exposed to
        extreme temperatures, and

      - [(B)]{.lawlabel} if the mixing of multiple substances would
        create a health or safety hazard to persons, those substances
        are prevented from mixing in the event of container leakage,
        breakage or other similar circumstance;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} any storage area designated under subparagraph
    (c)(i) in which a flammable or combustible substance is to be
    dispensed or transferred also meets the following criteria:

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} its ventilation conforms to the applicable
      provisions of National Fire Protection Association publication
      NFPA 30, Flammable and Combustible Liquids Code,

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} its exhaust air is discharged outdoors, to an
      area in which the exhaust will not pose a risk to the health or
      safety of any person, and the storage area is provided with makeup
      air,

    - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} any makeup air duct that passes through a fire
      separation is equipped with a fire damper that is fitted to close
      automatically on detection of fire or the arming of a related fire
      suppression system, and

    - [(iv)]{.lawlabel} any doors to or within the area are
      self-closing;

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} hazardous substances are removed from storage and
    used in as small a quantity as is feasible;

  - [(f)]{.lawlabel} any hazard posed by a hazardous substance,
    including as a result of its production, storage, handling, use or
    disposal, is confined to as small an area as is feasible;

  - [(g)]{.lawlabel} signs warning of the presence of hazardous
    substances are posted in conspicuous places, including all access
    points to the area in which the substances are present;

  - [(h)]{.lawlabel} any production, storage, handling, use or disposal
    of a hazardous substance is done in accordance with the safety data
    sheet for that substance, if any, or another document containing
    hazard information in respect of that substance;

  - [(i)]{.lawlabel} any handling, storage or use of a hazardous
    substance that is at risk of igniting from static electricity
    conforms to the National Fire Protection Association publication
    NFPA 77, Recommended Practice on Static Electricity;

  - [(j)]{.lawlabel} any words or symbols that identify the contents of
    a container as a hazardous substance are removed once the container
    has been completely cleaned of that substance;

  - [(k)]{.lawlabel} if an employee's skin, hair or clothing is likely
    to become contaminated by a hazardous substance in the course of
    their work,

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} a shower is available to them, outside of the
      accommodations area, for the purpose of decontamination, and

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} they are allowed sufficient time during their
      normal working hours to use the decontamination shower or other
      cleaning facilities;

  - [(l)]{.lawlabel} appropriate emergency eye-wash stations and showers
    that conform to and have been installed in accordance with
    ANSI/International Safety Equipment Association (ISEA) standard
    Z358.1, American National Standard for Emergency Eyewash and Shower
    Equipment are provided in any work area where a person's eyes or
    skin may be exposed to a hazardous substance, having regard to the
    risk of exposure and the hazard information for that substance;

  - [(m)]{.lawlabel} no person enters any accommodations area while
    wearing clothing that is likely to have been contaminated by a
    hazardous substance, other than a space within that area that has
    been designated by the employer for the removal of contaminated
    clothing;

  - [(n)]{.lawlabel} any person who handles, cleans or disposes of
    clothing at a workplace does so in a manner that minimizes exposure
    of persons to hazardous substances, including by

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} storing clothing that is wet or likely to have
      been contaminated with a hazardous substance separately from
      clothing that is not wet or contaminated, and

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} laundering clothing that is likely to have been
      contaminated with a hazardous substance separately from other
      clothing;

  - [(o)]{.lawlabel} any use of a device that is capable of emitting
    energy in the form of electromagnetic waves conforms to the
    applicable safety code, including any addendums, published by the
    Department of Health;

  - [(p)]{.lawlabel} any non-destructive testing activity that involves

    Department of Natural Resources' National Non-Destructive Testing
    Certification Body;

  - [(q)]{.lawlabel} every piping system that contains a hazardous
    substance is

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} designed to control static electricity,

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} fitted with valves or other safety devices to
      ensure its safe operation,

    - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} marked using any method, including
      colour-coding or signage, to identify the hazardous substance it
      contains and, if applicable, the direction of the flow, and

    - [(iv)]{.lawlabel} despite paragraph 87(1)(e), inspected before it
      is placed in service and then at least once a year; and

  - [(r)]{.lawlabel} if an employee is carrying out work on a piping

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} the following engineering controls are fitted on

      - [(A)]{.lawlabel} a blank or blind, in conjunction with valves or

        prevent the substance from reaching the blank or blind,

      - [(B)]{.lawlabel} a double block and bleed system, consisting of
        two valves or other blocking seals that are secured in the
        closed position and located on each side of a valve or other
        mechanism that is secured in the open position to allow for
        bleed-off between the two seals, or

      - [(C)]{.lawlabel} another engineering control that has been
        approved by a professional engineer,

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} the location of any blank or blind referred to
      in clause (i)(A) is clearly marked on the pipe and all valves and
      other seals and mechanisms referred to in clause (i)(A) or (B) are
      clearly marked to indicate the position they are in, and

    - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} any engineering control referred to in clause
      (i)(B) or (C) is monitored for leaks throughout the work.

## [PART 32]{.HLabel1}[Diving]{.HTitleText1} {#h-1331240 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Definitions

**[[164]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-164 .sectionLabel}** The following
definitions apply in this Part.

[[decompression table]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[decompression table]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means a table or set of
    tables that shows a schedule of rates for safe descent and ascent
    and decompression stop times, having regard to the breathing mixture
    to be used by a diver during a dive. ([table de
    décompression]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

[[dive contractor]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[dive contractor]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means an employer that
    exercises direction and control over diving operations at a
    workplace. ([entrepreneur en plongée]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

[[dive team]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[dive team]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means all divers, standby divers,
    dive support personnel and dive supervisors on a dive
    project. ([équipe de plongée]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

[[dive safety specialist]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

:   [[dive safety specialist]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means a person
    designated under subsection 168(1). ([spécialiste de la sécurité en
    plongée]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Occupational health and safety program

**[[165]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-165 .sectionLabel}** The risks associated
with diving operations are prescribed risks for the purpose of paragraph
205.02(2)(a) of the Act and the occupational health and safety program
in respect of a workplace from which a dive project is carried out must
include

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} procedures for consulting with employees who perform
  a variety of roles in the diving operations, including members of the
  dive team, with respect to the management of risks to divers' health
  and safety;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} procedures for obtaining the agreement of the dive
  safety specialists designated in respect of the dive project with
  respect to the hazards identified, the risks assessed and the hazard
  control measures to be implemented;

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} procedures for safely carrying out each task
  associated with the dive project, including with regard to the
  equipment to be used;

- [(d)]{.lawlabel} procedures for ensuring divers' safe and controlled
  entry into and exit from the water;

- [(e)]{.lawlabel} procedures for carrying out decompression in a manner
  that will minimize decompression sickness or other adverse effects on
  divers, including having regard to repetitive factor and residual
  inert gases;

- [(f)]{.lawlabel} procedures for treating decompression sickness,
  including decompression sickness that results from planned or
  unplanned omitted decompression, and communicating with a specialized
  dive physician in respect of that treatment;

- [(g)]{.lawlabel} procedures for responding to hazardous weather or
  water conditions;

- [(h)]{.lawlabel} procedures for aborting and resuming dives;

- [(i)]{.lawlabel} procedures for calculating --- in a manner that
  allows for leakage, waste and other unplanned depletions --- the
  quantities of breathing mixtures required by divers, including for
  both primary and secondary use and for therapeutic treatment;

- [(j)]{.lawlabel} procedures for storing breathing mixtures that, among
  other things, identify a single Canadian or international standard to
  be used for the colour-coding of all gas cylinders and quads or other
  banks associated with the dive project;

- [(k)]{.lawlabel} procedures for providing breathing mixtures to
  divers;

- [(l)]{.lawlabel} procedures for ensuring that all materials or objects
  introduced into or used in diving bells or compression chambers do not
  contain or produce gases or vapours that may be harmful to divers;

- [(m)]{.lawlabel} procedures for maintaining divers' thermal balance
  and comfort, including by heating their breathing mixtures if
  necessary and ensuring the continued supply of heat in the event of
  any failure of the primary thermal control system;

- [(n)]{.lawlabel} procedures for installing barriers or isolating
  energy sources as necessary to protect divers from contact with
  hazards;

- [(o)]{.lawlabel} procedures for ensuring that the dive contractor is
  made aware of any seismic work being carried out in the vicinity of
  the workplace that may pose a risk to divers' health or safety and for
  communicating with the persons carrying out that seismic work;

- [(p)]{.lawlabel} procedures for assessing seabed or seawater
  contamination levels in areas in which contamination is a known
  hazard; and

- [(q)]{.lawlabel} if the workplace is a dynamically positioned vessel,

  - [(i)]{.lawlabel} procedures for responding to changes in its station
    keeping status,

  - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} procedures for operating in close proximity to
    marine installations or structures or other physical obstacles,

  - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} procedures for guarding against thruster wash and
    suction effect,

  - [(iv)]{.lawlabel} procedures for preventing equipment entanglement,
    and

  - [(v)]{.lawlabel} procedures for repositioning the vessel that
    address, among other things, the maximum increments for
    repositioning and heading change while divers are in the water.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Emergency response plan

- **[[169]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-169 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} The
  emergency response plan developed under section 18 in respect of a
  workplace from which a dive project is carried out must include
  provisions developed by the dive contractor --- in consultation with
  the dive safety specialists for the project and, as the case may be,
  the installation manager referred to in section 193.2 of the Act or
  the offshore construction manager and dive vessel master --- that

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} set out procedures for responding to all vessel or
    dive system emergencies that have the potential to compromise
    divers' safety;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} set out procedures for responding to chamber system
    emergencies, including fire, loss of pressure, atmospheric
    contamination and life-support system malfunction;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} set out procedures to be followed in the case of
    any loss of communication;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} set out procedures for ensuring that any emergency
    at the workplace does not impede the provision of life support to
    divers, including during evacuation, recovery, decompression and
    observation for decompression sickness;

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} set out procedures in relation to the rescue of a
    diver;

  - [(f)]{.lawlabel} if the dive project involves saturation diving, set
    out procedures

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} for locating and recovering a lost diving bell,

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} for responding to the loss of atmospheric
      pressure within a diving bell, and

    - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} in relation to emergency hyperbaric evacuation,
      including the recovery and transport to a hyperbaric reception
      facility of self-propelled hyperbaric lifeboats, their reception
      at that facility and the replenishment of resources on the
      lifeboats; and

  - [(g)]{.lawlabel} address any other matters that are necessary for
    preparing for and responding to emergencies that have the potential
    to compromise divers' safety.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Procedures

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The dive contractor must ensure that detailed
  emergency response procedures covering all reasonably foreseeable
  emergencies are readily available to all persons at the workplace who
  may have a role in carrying them out.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Availability of plan

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} In addition to conforming to subsection 18(3), every
  dive contractor must ensure that the emergency response plan for the
  workplace from which the dive project for which it exercises direction
  or control over diving operations is carried out is made readily
  available to all persons, including those not at the workplace, who
  may have a role in responding to a dive emergency.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Emergency drills and exercises

**[[170]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-170 .sectionLabel}** The plan established
under section 30 for any workplace from which a dive project is carried
out must include provisions, developed by the dive contractor, requiring
the conduct of exercises and drills with respect to all reasonably
foreseeable dive emergencies, including

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} diver evacuation drills --- including, if the dive
  project involves saturation diving, drills involving the boarding of a
  self-propelled hyperbaric lifeboat --- to be conducted prior to the
  first dive being carried out under the dive project and then at least
  once a month;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} exercises involving the simulation by the members of
  the dive team of the procedures for dealing with a diver who has
  suffered injury or decompression sickness, including communication
  with a specialized dive physician, to be conducted at least once a
  month;

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} if the dive project involves the use of dynamic
  positioning equipment, drills completed on the diving vessel
  simulating the loss of dynamic positioning capability, to be conducted
  at least once a month;

- [(d)]{.lawlabel} if the dive project involves saturation diving,

  - [(i)]{.lawlabel} drills involving the location and recovery of a
    lost diving bell, to be conducted prior to the first dive being
    carried out under the dive project and then at least once every
    three months, and

  - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} drills involving the launch and manoeuvring of
    self-propelled hyperbaric lifeboats, to be conducted at least once
    every six months; and

- [(e)]{.lawlabel} drills or exercises in respect of all other
  reasonably foreseeable diving emergencies, to be conducted at least
  once a month.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Dive project plan

- **[[171]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-171
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Every dive contractor must, in
  respect of each dive project for which it exercises direction or
  control over the diving operations, in consultation with the dive
  safety specialists for the project and, as the case may be, the
  installation manager referred to in section 193.2 of the Act or the
  offshore construction manager and dive vessel master, establish,
  maintain and implement a written dive project plan that sets out, in
  detail, all operational and safety elements of the proposed dive
  project, including

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} a description of each dive to be carried out that
    includes an indication of

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} the diving technique to be used,

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} the tasks to be carried out,

    - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} any specialized equipment to be used,

    - [(iv)]{.lawlabel} the estimated and maximum time to be spent at
      each depth,

    - [(v)]{.lawlabel} the number of divers involved, and

    - [(vi)]{.lawlabel} the hours each diver will be expected to work,
      including the frequency and duration of their breaks;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the composition of the dive team and the
    qualifications and any specialized training required of its members;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} the hierarchy of command for the project;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} a list of legislation, standards and codes of
    practice that are applicable to any aspect of the dive project;

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} a list of all vessels to be used in the dive
    project, including rescue vessels to be on standby;

  - [(f)]{.lawlabel} the decompression tables to be used;

  - [(g)]{.lawlabel} the types of equipment, including personal
    protective equipment, that are to be worn or used by members of the
    dive team and the quantity of each that is required to ensure
    sufficient availability for standby divers;

  - [(h)]{.lawlabel} procedures, approved by a specialized dive
    physician, for carrying out the medical checks referred to in
    paragraphs 172(2)(b) and (3)(b);

  - [(i)]{.lawlabel} schematic diagrams indicating, for each vessel to
    be used, the distance at various depths from a diver to the vessel's
    propulsion system components and other hazards to the diver and
    their umbilical, as well as the corresponding safe umbilical
    lengths;

  - [(j)]{.lawlabel} a description of the diving system and any dynamic
    positioning equipment to be used;

  - [(k)]{.lawlabel} a description of the potential failure modes of the
    diving system and any dynamic positioning equipment to be used, the
    consequences of such failures and the mitigation measures to be
    taken, including an indication of which of the system's or
    equipment's components require redundancy, as determined on the
    basis of a failure modes and effects analysis;

  - [(l)]{.lawlabel} schedules for inspecting the diving system and its
    components and the positions of those responsible for carrying out
    those inspections;

  - [(m)]{.lawlabel} a description of all subsea lifts planned;

  - [(n)]{.lawlabel} the means of communication to be used among members
    of the dive team and employees on the bridge, at the dive control
    station and at the dynamic positioning control station, and to
    support the provision of medical and emergency response services,
    including secondary means to be used in the case of a failure of the
    primary means or a loss of power, and procedures to be followed in
    the case of a total loss of communication;

  - [(o)]{.lawlabel} a copy of the emergency response plan developed in
    respect of the workplace under section 18;

  - [(p)]{.lawlabel} the method by which the dive project plan is to be
    communicated to the dive team and any other persons who may be
    affected by the plan;

  - [(q)]{.lawlabel} procedures for managing any changes that require
    deviation from the plan; and

  - [(r)]{.lawlabel} any other information that is necessary to plan for
    safe diving operations.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Dive team

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} For the purpose of paragraph (1)(b), the composition
  of the dive team must be determined having regard to the risk
  assessment carried out in accordance with the occupational health and
  safety program and that team must include

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} no fewer than two dive supervisors on shift at the
    dive control station at all times during a dive, with the exception
    of breaks, during which one supervisor may be replaced at the dive
    control station by another competent person;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} sufficient dive support personnel to support the
    divers and operate and maintain all equipment; and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} in the case of surface-supplied diving, sufficient
    divers to ensure the availability of standby divers who satisfy the
    requirements set out in paragraph 172(2)(c).

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Means of communication

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} All means of communication referred to in paragraph
  (1)(n) must be dedicated and continuous and, if used between a dive
  supervisor and diver, must

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} have sufficient sound quality to permit breathing
    and speech to be clearly heard without distortion;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} if the diver is using a breathing mixture that
    contains a substance that distorts the voice, be equipped with a
    voice descrambler; and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} be equipped with a recording device that
    continuously records all transmissions while a dive is in progress.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Dive contractor obligations

- **[[172]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-172
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Every dive contractor must ensure,
  with respect to all diving operations under its direction and control,
  that

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the diving system used conforms to the annex to
    International Maritime Organization Resolution A.831(19), Code of
    Safety for Diving Systems, 1995;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} each member of the dive team and the pilot of any
    remotely operated vehicle being deployed conforms to the applicable
    competencies set out in CSA Group standard Z275.4, Competency
    standard for diving, hyperbaric chamber, and remotely operated
    vehicle operations;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} each diver and dive supervisor holds a valid
    standard first aid certificate or advanced first aid certificate and
    a valid certificate in first aid oxygen administration;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} each diver has been certified, within the 12-month
    period ending on the last day of the diving operation, as being
    medically fit to dive by one of the following physicians and has
    confirmed that their medical condition has not changed since their
    most recent certification:

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} a physician who is licensed to practise medicine
      in Canada and meets the competencies of a Level 1 Physician set
      out in CSA Group standard Z275.4, Competency standard for diving,
      hyperbaric chamber, and remotely operated vehicle operations, or

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} a specialized dive physician who bases their
      certification on their review of a medical fitness certification
      issued in a jurisdiction outside of Canada within the same
      12-month period;

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} a specialized dive physician is readily available
    at all times to provide medical advice from a remote location in
    Newfoundland and Labrador and to be transported to the workplace, if
    necessary, to provide medical treatment, including to a diver in a
    compression chamber;

  - [(f)]{.lawlabel} any person performing first aid on a diver has
    unimpeded access to a means of communicating with the specialized
    dive physician;

  - [(g)]{.lawlabel} appropriate equipment is available at the workplace
    to permit the specialized dive physician, from a remote location, to

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} communicate directly with a diver inside a
      compression chamber,

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} observe and examine a diver inside a compression
      chamber by means of visual and auditory aids, and

    - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} use available monitoring or clinical assessment
      technologies on a diver;

  - [(h)]{.lawlabel} the data transfer rate at the workplace is
    sufficient to permit continuous monitoring of a person inside a
    compression chamber and to allow the results of ongoing medical
    testing, such as electrocardiograms, to be transferred to the
    specialized dive physician, as determined through testing before the
    start of the diving operations;

  - [(i)]{.lawlabel} breathing mixtures that conform to CSA Group
    standard Z275.2, Operational safety code for diving operations or
    European Committee for Standardization (CEN) standard EN 12021,
    Respiratory equipment --- Compressed gases for breathing apparatus
    are available in the quantities calculated in accordance with
    paragraph 165(i);

  - [(j)]{.lawlabel} each diver has independent primary and secondary
    breathing mixture supplies, each of which can be isolated from the
    supplies of other divers;

  - [(k)]{.lawlabel} breathing mixtures are available to divers at a
    rate appropriate to the depth and circumstances of the dive but no
    less than 62.5 L per minute;

  - [(l)]{.lawlabel} breathing mixtures are stored in compressed gas
    cylinders that have been certified by a competent person who is
    independent of the operator, dive contractor and manufacturer as
    being safe for that use;

  - [(m)]{.lawlabel} the applicable colour code referred to in paragraph
    165(j) is posted in a conspicuous place in all breathing mixture
    storage areas;

  - [(n)]{.lawlabel} the oxygen content of each breathing mixture is
    analyzed by a member of the dive team on receipt of the mixture and
    immediately prior to each dive for which that mixture is to be used
    and any breathing mixture found to contain more than 25% oxygen by
    volume is handled as if it were pure oxygen;

  - [(o)]{.lawlabel} if a remotely operated vehicle is deployed while
    divers are in the water, there is a dedicated and continuous means
    of communication between the dive supervisor and the vehicle's pilot
    and a monitor at the dive control station displays the same picture
    as seen by the pilot;

  - [(p)]{.lawlabel} if a dive is being carried out from a dynamically
    positioned vessel,

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} the vessel is equipped with

      - [(A)]{.lawlabel} an indicator that continuously displays its
        station keeping status,

      - [(B)]{.lawlabel} a visual and audible alarm system that warns of
        station keeping status changes, and whose alarms are visible and
        audible on the bridge, at the dive control station and in any
        other location where knowledge of such a change would be
        important for ensuring diver safety, and

      - [(C)]{.lawlabel} a fixed means of communication between the
        vessel's bridge and the dive control station and between the
        dive control station and the dynamic positioning control station
        that is capable of working even in the event of a total loss of
        power to the vessel, and

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} there is a dedicated and continuous means of
      communication between the dive control station and the dynamic
      positioning control station for the duration of the dive and
      employees at each station inform those at the other station
      immediately of any changes in operational circumstances;

  - [(q)]{.lawlabel} divers' breathing patterns are continuously
    monitored and their activities continuously observed and recorded
    for the duration of each dive;

  - [(r)]{.lawlabel} every diver's location in the water is continuously
    monitored for the duration of each dive;

  - [(s)]{.lawlabel} effective means of assisting and recovering divers
    are available for the duration of each dive;

  - [(t)]{.lawlabel} any dive during which a diver loses thermal balance
    or there is a failure of a thermal control system is immediately
    suspended and all divers are returned to the diving bell, if safe,
    or to the surface, even if the loss or failure is expected to be
    temporary;

  - [(u)]{.lawlabel} decompression is carried out only in accordance
    with the applicable decompression table identified in the dive
    project plan, except in extenuating circumstances and in
    consultation with a specialized dive physician;

  - [(v)]{.lawlabel} no diver travels by air within 24 hours after a
    dive or while suffering from decompression sickness, unless approved
    by a specialized dive physician; and

  - [(w)]{.lawlabel} the medical report associated with each diver's
    certification under paragraph (d) is readily available, in the case
    of an emergency, to members of the dive team who hold a diving
    medical technician certificate and to the specialized dive physician
    referred to in paragraph (e), in an official language understood by
    that physician.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Surface-supplied diving

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} If the diving operation involves surface-supplied
  diving, the dive contractor must also ensure that

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} there is, at all times, at least one member of the
    dive team who holds a valid diving medical technician certificate
    and is not on a mandatory rest period on the surface and readily
    available to provide assistance to the divers;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} medical checks are carried out by a member of the
    dive team who holds a diving medical technician certificate, or by a
    medic under the direction of the specialized dive physician, on each
    diver at the beginning and end of each shift during which they dive;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} except in the case of an emergency, each standby
    diver has had 12 consecutive hours of rest since their most recent
    dive and has no residual inert gas in their tissue as calculated in
    accordance with the applicable decompression table set out in the
    dive project plan;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} no dive is carried out at pressures greater than 50
    msw or if the partial pressure of oxygen exceeds 1.4 ATA;

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} sufficient double-lock deck compression chambers
    that have an inside diameter of at least 1.524 m and that can
    accommodate all divers who need to undergo decompression at any one
    time, as well as all other persons needing to be in the chamber with
    the divers to carry out the decompression procedures or provide
    medical care to them, are available at the workplace to allow for
    decompression in accordance with the applicable decompression table
    identified in the dive project plan; and

  - [(f)]{.lawlabel} if diving occurs from a light dive craft, the time
    needed to transport a diver from the surface to the deck compression
    chamber or medical room on the primary vessel from which the light
    dive craft is deployed does not exceed 15 minutes.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Saturation diving

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} If the diving operation involves saturation diving,
  the dive contractor must also ensure that

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} each diver holds a valid diving medical technician
    certificate;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} medical checks are carried out by a member of the
    dive team who holds a diving medical technician certificate, or by a
    medic under the direction of the specialized dive physician, on each
    diver immediately before they enter the compression chamber and
    immediately after they exit it after decompression;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} at least two diving bells are available, each of
    which

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} is capable of sustaining the lives of the divers
      in it and protecting them against hypothermia for at least 24
      hours,

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} is equipped with an emergency locating device
      whose signals the marine installation or structure from which the
      dive operation is carried out, and all rescue vessels on standby,
      are equipped to receive and interpret,

    - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} has suitable protective devices fitted to its
      main umbilical to control loss of atmospheric pressure in the
      diving bell if any of the components in the umbilical are
      ruptured, and

    - [(iv)]{.lawlabel} has its internal atmosphere continuously
      monitored for contaminants and oxygen and carbon dioxide levels by
      both a primary and secondary monitoring system for the duration of
      each dive, with the data displayed both in the diving bell and at
      the dive control station, and the oxygen and carbon dioxide levels
      being recorded at least hourly;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} the relative humidity in all living chambers is
    maintained between 40% and 60% at all depths, regardless of the
    number of divers in the chamber;

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} no pressurization is scheduled to last more than 28
    days; and

  - [(f)]{.lawlabel} a hyperbaric evacuation system that includes the
    following is readily available for the evacuation and reception of
    all divers:

    - [(i)]{.lawlabel} a hyperbaric reception facility, and

    - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} self-propelled hyperbaric lifeboats that are
      equipped with a life support package sufficient to sustain the
      lives of the divers and for which a mating trial with the
      reception facility has been conducted.

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Dive record

- **[[173]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-173
  .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} Every dive contractor must make and
  sign a record that sets out, in respect of each dive carried out under
  its direction or control,

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the date and location of the dive;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the names of all divers, standby divers and dive
    supervisors;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} the task carried out;

  - [(d)]{.lawlabel} a list of the tools and equipment used that
    includes, in respect of each piece of equipment that is part of the
    diving apparatus, its type and serial number;

  - [(e)]{.lawlabel} the breathing mixture used;

  - [(f)]{.lawlabel} the time the diver began their descent from the
    surface;

  - [(g)]{.lawlabel} the maximum depth attained;

  - [(h)]{.lawlabel} the time spent at the maximum depth;

  - [(i)]{.lawlabel} the time the diver began their ascent from the
    maximum depth;

  - [(j)]{.lawlabel} the time the diver reached the surface;

  - [(k)]{.lawlabel} the surface interval, in the case of a repetitive
    dive;

  - [(l)]{.lawlabel} the type of decompression carried out and the
    decompression table used;

  - [(m)]{.lawlabel} the environmental conditions during the dive; and

  - [(n)]{.lawlabel} any remarks, including with respect to any unusual
    occurrences during the dive.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Retention of record

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} The dive contractor must retain the record for five
  years after the day on which the dive is completed.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Retention of recordings

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} The dive contractor must retain all recordings
  referred to in paragraphs 171(3)(c) and 172(1)(q) for 48 hours after
  the diver has returned to the surface or living chamber, as the case
  may be, or any longer period that is necessary to enable the operator
  to investigate an occupational disease, accident, incident or other
  hazardous occurrence under subsection 205.017(2) of the Act.
