## Fredericton Airport Zoning Regulations {#fredericton-airport-zoning-regulations .Title-of-Act}

[SOR]{.abbr title="Statutory Orders and Regulations"}/81-452
:::

[AERONAUTICS ACT](/eng/acts/A-2)

Registration 1981-06-08

Regulations Respecting Zoning at Fredericton Airport

P.C. 1981-1520 1981-06-04
::::

::::::: section
::: {.section .order}
His Excellency the Governor General in Council, on the recommendation of
the Minister of Transport, pursuant to section 6 of the [Aeronautics
Act](/eng/acts/A-2), is pleased hereby to approve the annexed
[Regulations respecting Zoning at Fredericton
Airport](/eng/regulations/SOR-81-452) made by the Minister of Transport.
:::

## [Short Title]{.HTitleText1} {#h-871202 .Part}

**[[1]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-1 .sectionLabel}** These Regulations may be
cited as the [Fredericton Airport Zoning
Regulations](/eng/regulations/SOR-81-452).

## [Interpretation]{.HTitleText1} {#h-871204 .Part}

- **[[2]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-2 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} In these
  Regulations,

  [[airport]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[airport]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means the Fredericton Airport, in
      the county of Sunbury, in the Province of New Brunswick;
      ([aéroport]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

  [[airport reference point]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[airport reference point]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means the point
      described in Part I of the schedule; ([point de repère de
      l'aéroport]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

  [[approach surface]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[approach surface]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means an imaginary
      inclined plane that extends upward and outward from each end of a
      strip, which approach surface is more particularly described in
      Part III of the schedule; ([surface d'approche]{.DefinedTermLink
      lang="fr"})

  [[Minister]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[Minister]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means the Minister of Transport;
      ([ministre]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

  [[outer surface]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[outer surface]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means an imaginary surface
      located above and in the immediate vicinity of the airport, which
      outer surface is more particularly described in Part IV of the
      schedule; ([surface extérieure]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

  [[strip]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[strip]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means the rectangular portion of the
      landing area of the airport including the runway prepared for the
      take-off and landing of aircraft in a particular direction, which
      strip is more particularly described in Part V of the schedule;
      ([bande]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

  [[transitional surface]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm}

  :   [[transitional surface]{.dfn}]{.DefinedTerm} means an imaginary
      inclined plane that extends upward and outward from the lateral
      limits of a strip and its approach surfaces, which transitional
      surface is more particularly described in Part VI of the schedule.
      ([surface de transition]{.DefinedTermLink lang="fr"})

- [(2)]{.lawlabel} For the purposes of these Regulations, the assigned
  elevation of the airport reference point is 53 feet above sea level.

## [Application]{.HTitleText1} {#h-871215 .Part}

**[[3]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-3 .sectionLabel}** These Regulations apply to
all the lands, including public road allowances, adjacent to or in the
vicinity of the airport, that consist of

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} the lands within, and

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} the lands directly under that portion of an approach
  surface that extends beyond

the outer limits described in Part II of the schedule.

## [General]{.HTitleText1} {#h-871220 .Part}

**[[4]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-4 .sectionLabel}** No person shall erect or
construct on any land to which these Regulations apply, any building,
structure or object or any addition to any existing building, structure
or object, the highest point of which will exceed in elevation at the
location of that point, any

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} approach surface;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} outer surface; or

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} transitional surface.

## [Natural Growth]{.HTitleText1} {#h-871225 .Part}

**[[5]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-5 .sectionLabel}** Where an object of natural
growth on any land to which these Regulations apply exceeds in elevation
any of the surfaces referred to in section 4, the Minister may make a
direction that the owner or occupier of the land on which that object is
growing remove the excessive growth.

## [Disposal of Waste]{.HTitleText1} {#h-871227 .Part}

**[[6]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-6 .sectionLabel}** No owner or occupier of any
land to which these Regulations apply shall permit that land or any part
of it to be used for the disposal or accumulation of any waste, material
or substance edible by or attractive to birds.

## [SCHEDULE]{.scheduleLabel}[(ss. 2 and 3)]{.OriginatingRef} {#h-871230 .scheduleLabel}

### [PART I]{.HLabel1}[Description of the Airport Reference Point]{.HTitleText1} {#part-idescription-of-the-airport-reference-point .SchedHeadL1}

The airport reference point is a point distant 500 feet measured
southwesterly at right angles to the centre line of runway 15-33 and
distant 500 feet measured northwesterly at right angles to the centre
line of runway 09-27, the point having co-ordinate values of
N. 769,959.37 feet and E. 991,257.19 feet with reference to the New
Brunswick Horizontal Control System.

### [PART II]{.HLabel1}[Description of the Outer Limits of Lands]{.HTitleText1} {#part-iidescription-of-the-outer-limits-of-lands .SchedHeadL1}

The boundary of the outer limits of lands is a circular area having a
radius of 13,000 feet the centre of which coincides with the airport
reference point, except where any portion of C.F.B. Gagetown falls
within the above described radius, the boundary of the outer limits of
lands is the boundary of C.F.B. Gagetown as shown on Department of
Public Works Plan No. S-352.

### [PART III]{.HLabel1}[Description of the Approach Surfaces]{.HTitleText1} {#part-iiidescription-of-the-approach-surfaces .SchedHeadL1}

The approach surfaces, shown on Department of Public Works Plans S-352,
S-352A, S-352B and S-352C dated July 20, 1977 and prepared for the
Department of Transport, are surfaces abutting each end of the strips
associated with the runways designated 09-27 and 15-33 and described as
follows:

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} a surface abutting the end of the strip associated
  with runway approach 09,

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} a surface abutting the end of the strip associated
  with runway approach 27,

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} a surface abutting the end of the strip associated
  with runway approach 15, and

- [(d)]{.lawlabel} a surface abutting the end of the strip associated
  with runway approach 33,

consisting of an inclined plane having a ratio of 1 foot measured
vertically to 50 feet measured horizontally, rising to an imaginary
horizontal line drawn at right angles to the projected centre line of
each strip 1,000 feet measured vertically above the elevation at the end
of each strip and 50,000 feet measured horizontally from the end of each
strip, the outer ends of each imaginary horizontal line being 8,000 feet
from each projected centre line.

### [PART IV]{.HLabel1}[Description of the Outer Surface]{.HTitleText1} {#part-ivdescription-of-the-outer-surface .SchedHeadL1}

The outer surface, shown on Department of Public Works Plan S-352 dated
July 20, 1977 and prepared for the Department of Transport, is an
imaginary surface located at a common plane established at a constant
elevation of 150 feet above the assigned elevation of the airport
reference point, except where that common plane is less than 30 feet
above the surface of the ground, the imaginary surface is located at
30 feet above the surface of the ground.

### [PART V]{.HLabel1}[Description of the Strips]{.HTitleText1} {#part-vdescription-of-the-strips .SchedHeadL1}

The strips, shown on Department of Public Works Plan S-352 dated
July 20, 1977 and prepared for the Department of Transport, are
described as follows:

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} the strip associated with runway 09-27 is 1,000 feet
  in width, 500 feet being on each side of the centre line of the runway
  and 7,400 feet in length, 200 feet extending beyond each end of the
  runway; and

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} the strip associated with runway 15-33 is 1,000 feet
  in width, 500 feet being on each side of the centre line of the runway
  and 6,400 feet in length, 200 feet extending beyond each end of the
  runway.

### [PART VI]{.HLabel1}[Description of Each Transitional Surface]{.HTitleText1} {#part-videscription-of-each-transitional-surface .SchedHeadL1}

Each transitional surface, shown on Department of Public Works
Plan S-352 dated July 20, 1977 and prepared for the Department of
Transport, is a surface consisting of an inclined plane having a ratio
of one foot measured vertically to seven feet measured horizontally at
right angles to the centre line and centre line production of each strip
and extending upward and outward from the lateral limits of each strip
and its approach surfaces to an intersection with the outer surface or
another transitional surface of an adjoining strip.
