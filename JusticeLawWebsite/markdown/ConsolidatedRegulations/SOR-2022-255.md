## [Constitutional Questions]{.HTitleText1} {#h-1380988 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Filing of notice

- **[[1]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-1 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A party
  who wants to challenge the constitutional validity, applicability or
  operability of a provision of the [Canada Pension
  Plan](/eng/acts/C-8), the [Old Age Security Act](/eng/acts/O-9), the
  [Employment Insurance Act](/eng/acts/E-5.6), Part 5 of the [Department
  of Employment and Social Development Act](/eng/acts/H-5.7), the
  [Canada Disability Benefit Act](/eng/acts/C-3.45) or the rules or
  regulations made under any of those Acts must file a notice with the
  Tribunal that sets out

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} the provision that will be challenged;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} the material facts relied on to support the
    constitutional challenge; and

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} a summary of the legal argument to be made in
    support of the constitutional challenge.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Service of notice

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} At least 10 days before the date set for the hearing
  of the appeal or application, the party must serve notice of the
  challenge on the persons referred to in subsection 57(1) of the
  [Federal Courts Act](/eng/acts/F-7) and file a copy of the notice and
  proof of service with the Tribunal.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Failure to file proof of service

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} If proof of service has not been filed in accordance
  with subsection (2), the Tribunal may, on its own initiative or on the
  request of a party, postpone or adjourn the hearing.

## [Format of Hearing]{.HTitleText1} {#h-1380999 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Format of hearing

- **[[2]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-2 .sectionLabel}** [(1)]{.lawlabel} A
  Tribunal hearing must be held in one of the following formats, as
  requested by the appellant:

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} in writing;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} by teleconference, videoconference or other means
    of telecommunication;

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} in person.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Exception --- other parties

  [(2)]{.lawlabel} Despite subsection (1), the Tribunal may allow any
  other party to participate in a hearing by a means other than the
  format requested by the appellant if the Tribunal determines that it
  is warranted based on the needs and circumstances of that party.

- [Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Exception --- circumstances

  [(3)]{.lawlabel} Despite subsection (1), the Tribunal may hold a
  hearing, in whole or in part, in a format other than that requested by
  the appellant if the Tribunal determines that the requested format

  - [(a)]{.lawlabel} would not allow for a full and fair hearing;

  - [(b)]{.lawlabel} would raise security or health concerns that cannot
    be mitigated to the Tribunal's satisfaction; or

  - [(c)]{.lawlabel} would be impractical in extraordinary circumstances
    such as fire, flood, epidemic, natural disaster, political turmoil,
    act of terrorism or major accident.

## [Confidentiality of Proceeding]{.HTitleText1} {#h-1381013 .Part}

[Marginal note:]{.wb-invisible}Private hearing and restricted access

**[[3]{.sectionLabel}]{#s-3 .sectionLabel}** The Tribunal may, on
request or on its own initiative, hold a hearing, in whole or in part,
in private and restrict public access to all or part of the appeal
record if it determines that reasonable alternative measures cannot
adequately mitigate any of the following serious risks that could result
from a public hearing and public access:

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} a serious risk that the disclosure of personal
  information, such as medical information, would cause undue hardship
  to a person that outweighs the societal interest that the hearing and
  appeal record be public;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} a serious risk to the life, liberty or security of a
  person;

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} a serious risk to the fairness of the proceeding that
  outweighs the societal interest that the hearing and appeal record be
  public;

- [(d)]{.lawlabel} a serious risk to public security.
