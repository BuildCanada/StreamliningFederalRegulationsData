## Order Declining to make an Emergency Order for the protection of the Killer Whale Northeast Pacific Southern Resident Population {#order-declining-to-make-an-emergency-order-for-the-protection-of-the-killer-whale-northeast-pacific-southern-resident-population .Title-of-Act}

[SI]{.abbr title="Statutory Instruments"}/2018-102
:::

[SPECIES AT RISK ACT](/eng/acts/S-15.3)

Registration 2018-11-14

Order Declining to make an Emergency Order for the protection of the
Killer Whale Northeast Pacific Southern Resident Population

P.C. 2018-1352 2018-11-01
::::

:::: section
::: {.section .order}
Whereas the following recovery planning measures have been taken under
the [Species at Risk Act](/eng/acts/S-15.3) with respect to the Killer
Whale ([Orcinus orca]{.otherLang lang="la"}) Northeast Pacific southern
resident population (SRKW):

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} the Recovery Strategy for the Northern and Southern
  Resident Killer Whales ([Orcinus orca]{.otherLang lang="la"}) in
  Canada, published in 2008 and amended in 2011;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} the Action Plan for the Northern and Southern
  Resident Killer Whale ([Orcinus orca]{.otherLang lang="la"}) in Canada
  and the Multi-species Action Plan for Pacific Rim National Park
  Reserve of Canada, published in 2017;

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} the Multi-species Action Plan for Gulf Islands
  National Park Reserve of Canada, published in 2018; and

- [(d)]{.lawlabel} the Recovery Strategy for the Northern and Southern
  Resident Killer Whales ([Orcinus orca]{.otherLang lang="la"}) in
  Canada (proposed), identifying additional areas of critical habitat
  and published on September 4, 2018;

Whereas, on May 24, 2018, the Minister of Fisheries and Oceans and the
Minister of the Environment announced that they had formed the opinion
that the SRKW faces imminent threats to its survival and recovery and
identified three key threats to the population, namely prey
availability, acoustic and physical disturbance and environmental
contaminants;

Whereas, having formed that opinion, the Ministers recommended under
subsection 80(2) of the [Species at Risk Act](/eng/acts/S-15.3) that the
Governor in Council make an emergency order;

Whereas measures under the [Species at Risk Act](/eng/acts/S-15.3) are
in place to protect and address the threats to the SRKW, including the
prohibition in section 32 against the killing, harming, harassing,
capturing or taking of an individual of an endangered species and the
[Critical Habitats of the Northeast Pacific Northern and Southern
Resident Populations of the Killer Whale ([Orcinus orca]{.otherLang
lang="la"}) Order](/eng/regulations/SOR-2009-68), made in 2009, which
triggered the prohibition in subsection 58(1) of the [Species at Risk
Act](/eng/acts/S-15.3) against the destruction of any part of the
critical habitat of these two populations;

Whereas other measures have been taken, continue to be taken and will be
taken by the Government of Canada and other organizations to address the
three imminent threats to the survival and recovery of the SRKW,
including

- [(a)]{.lawlabel} with respect to prey availability,

  - [(i)]{.lawlabel} fisheries closures for recreational finfish and
    commercial salmon fisheries in key foraging areas from June 1 to
    September 30, 2018, partial, varying closures in the mouth of the
    Fraser River from June 1 to September 30, 2018 and potential further
    closures at times and places to be established before the 2019
    fishing season based on information available at that time,

  - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} a reduction in total fishery removals for Chinook
    salmon of 25-35% in 2018 and potential further reductions to be
    established before the 2019 fishing season based on information
    available at that time,

  - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} habitat restoration to increase Chinook
    productivity,

  - [(iv)]{.lawlabel} aerial coverage of the SRKW and their critical
    habitat and increased enforcement capacity,

  - [(v)]{.lawlabel} development of the Southern BC Chinook Strategic
    Planning Initiative (CSPI) to support the recovery and rebuilding of
    the Chinook salmon population,

  - [(vi)]{.lawlabel} finalization of a renegotiated Chinook chapter of
    the Pacific Salmon Treaty, and

  - [(vii)]{.lawlabel} increased hatchery production;

- [(b)]{.lawlabel} with respect to acoustic and physical disturbance,

  - [(i)]{.lawlabel} fishery closures in key foraging areas and
    reductions in fishery removals, which contribute to reducing the
    impacts associated with disturbance from vessel noise,

  - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} amendments to the [Marine Mammal
    Regulations](/eng/regulations/SOR-93-56) to establish 200 metres as
    the minimum approach distance for killer whales in all Canadian
    fisheries waters in the Pacific Ocean and British Columbia, which
    came into force on June 22, 2018,

  - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} enhancement of enforcement capacity for the
    [Marine Mammal Regulations](/eng/regulations/SOR-93-56) and the
    [Species at Risk Act](/eng/acts/S-15.3),

  - [(iv)]{.lawlabel} voluntary slow-downs of commercial vessels in Haro
    Strait and other zones,

  - [(v)]{.lawlabel} voluntary trial lateral displacement of commercial
    vessels within the shipping lanes in the Strait of Juan de Fuca away
    from foraging areas,

  - [(vi)]{.lawlabel} seeking of new expanded legislative authorities
    under the [Canada Shipping Act, 2001](/eng/acts/C-10.15) to regulate
    the impacts of vessels on the marine environment,

  - [(vii)]{.lawlabel} conservation agreements or memoranda of
    understanding with key stakeholder groups on vessel noise mitigation
    measures to formalize and expand on voluntary measures,

  - [(viii)]{.lawlabel} proposal to extend existing requirements for
    automatic identification systems to smaller commercial vessels to
    improve safety and collision avoidance while also strengthening the
    ability of government to better target dynamic management measures
    based on traffic density,

  - [(ix)]{.lawlabel} improvements to whale detection tools and alerting
    procedures,

  - [(x)]{.lawlabel} development of noise management plans with industry
    for quieting the marine environment,

  - [(xi)]{.lawlabel} deployment of hydrophones to acquire baseline data
    to help develop noise reduction targets, and

  - [(xii)]{.lawlabel} research on technical solutions for quieting
    ships;

- [(c)]{.lawlabel} with respect to environmental contaminants,

  - [(i)]{.lawlabel} regulatory measures governing the release of
    deleterious or toxic substances under the [Metal and Diamond Mining
    Effluent Regulations](/eng/regulations/SOR-2002-222), the [Pulp and
    Paper Effluent Regulations](/eng/regulations/SOR-92-269), the
    [Wastewater Systems Effluent
    Regulations](/eng/regulations/SOR-2012-139) and the [Prohibition of
    Certain Toxic Substances Regulations,
    2012](/eng/regulations/SOR-2012-285),

  - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} strengthening of regulatory controls for five
    persistent organic pollutants, including two flame retardants
    (polybrominated diphenyl ethers (PBDEs) and hexabromocyclododecane
    (HBCD)) and three oil and water repellents (perfluorooctane
    sulfonate and its salts and precursors (PFOS), perfluorooctanoic
    acids and its salts and precursors (PFOA) and long chain
    perfluorocarboxylic acids and their salts and precursors
    (LC-PFCAs)), as outlined in a notice of intent published in October
    2018, with publication of the proposed regulatory amendments in Part
    I of the [Canada Gazette](http://www.gazette.gc.ca/) anticipated for
    winter 2020, subject to consultations,

  - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} upgrades to wastewater treatment with the
    construction in Victoria and Vancouver of two new wastewater
    treatment plants expected to be operational by January 1, 2021 and a
    third in Vancouver expected to be operational by January 1, 2031,
    and

  - [(iv)]{.lawlabel} research for the purpose of monitoring and
    measuring contaminants in the SRKW, their habitat and their main
    prey; and

- [(d)]{.lawlabel} with respect to all of those threats, expansion of
  research to inform future decision-making, including research

  - [(i)]{.lawlabel} on SRKW health and condition,

  - [(ii)]{.lawlabel} on the effectiveness of threat mitigations, and

  - [(iii)]{.lawlabel} on the foraging success of the SRKW in known key
    foraging areas and on prey availability over time;

Whereas the above measures, considered together, will contribute to
abating the imminent threats to the survival and recovery of the SRKW;

And whereas social, economic, policy and other factors, and the broader
public interest, have also been considered;

Therefore, Her Excellency the Governor General in Council declines to
make an emergency order pursuant to section 80 of the [Species at Risk
Act](/eng/acts/S-15.3)[[Footnote
